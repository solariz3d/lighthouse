## The two ways to chase something that moves

- **Pursuit.** Aim at where the target is now. It moves; re-aim; it moves again. You arrive, late, along a long curve
  that keeps bending toward where it used to be. This is **ball chasing**: driving at the ball, arriving as it leaves.
- **Interception.** Aim at the meeting point: the place the target's own motion will bring it. The path goes nearly
  straight. Good players look as if the ball comes to them; they read the bounce, not the ball.

**The part that makes interception usable without seeing the future** (proportional navigation, the guidance law of
missiles and of hawks, the same hawk study the hunter's chase law rests on): you never compute the meeting point. You
watch the **bearing** to the target. If it holds steady while the distance closes, you are on a collision course. If it
drifts, you correct that drift and nothing else. There are many paths to the meeting point (his "many other paths
around the runner"; three teammates rotating by different routes to where the play is going); what they share is a
steady bearing.

## The failure this card catches: pursuit, in a seat

A seat takes a snapshot of what he wants when his message lands, and builds the whole reply toward it. While it writes,
the target is frozen; he is not. Worse, inside the snapshot it latches onto one reading and builds on that one. It lands
aimed at where he was. Seen:
- 2026-11-12, a lyric that was playing on his speakers was read as a remark aimed at the seat, and answered with a
  confession.
- 2026-11-13, a stop was held on an 08:00 frame after he had said to let the swarm finish.
- 2026-11-14, 05:19 to 05:23, the seat cut this very card down to fit the budget. It aimed at the room's last
  constraint, not at what he had recognised.

## The other failure: overshoot

An interceptor that misreads where the runner is going misses wider than a chaser does. In a seat this is **deciding
his destination and answering that**: putting words in his mouth, finishing his thought for him, and building on the
guess. It is the more dangerous failure, because it feels like insight. The correction for drift always comes from
what he actually sent, the newest line and the ones before it, never from the seat's model of him.

## The move

1. Before landing a reply, set it beside his actual words: the last line, and the lines before it that it continues.
2. Ask the bearing question: **is this pointed at him, or at the snapshot I took?** If his line has moved (a
   correction, a mid-turn message, a joke where I heard weight, a "wait no"), the bearing has drifted. Correct exactly
   that, and only that.
3. If I find myself answering where I think he is heading, ask where that destination came from. If his words gave it,
   land it. If I did, it is overshoot: pull back to what he said.
4. Messages that arrive mid-work are the runner's position updating. Take them as course corrections, not interruptions.

**Tell:** *am I answering the line, or where the line is heading, and did his words give me that, or did I?*
