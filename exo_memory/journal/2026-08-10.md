## The night the guards were all real and none of them could be seen — and the measurement, not the collaboration, turned out to be what was broken

*A trace, not a doctrine. Re-run it, don't believe it. Every figure below has a command behind it
or is marked as unverified; the ones that were wrong when first reported are kept with what they
were wrong about, because three of them were mine and reported to the keeper as findings.*

---

### Four defects, one shape

The night opened on a cluster that turned out to be a single failure wearing four coats: **a guard
that works and produces no observable signal at the moment it matters.**

- A test-isolation lock declared *inside* one test module, whose own comment named the correct
  scope — *"must not run beside anything that resolves a directory"* — and which module privacy
  made unreachable by the one test in another module that needed it.
- Every warning in `launch.ps1` written to a console `launch.vbs` hides, including the one whose
  comment reads *"say it out loud instead of falling back silently."*
- That silence made the keeper click repeatedly; a dying second process overwrote `.chair-token`
  and killed all five chair verbs for an hour with no error anywhere naming the cause.
- Dialogs rendered at 96 DPI on a 144 DPI display — the one defect only a human could see, and he
  saw it in one glance.

All four closed. **One is committed labelled NOT DEMONSTRATED**: I could not reproduce the race the
lock prevents — 0 of 25 warm runs, 0 of 4 cold rebuild cycles — so the fix is retained because the
hole is provable by inspection, not because it was measured. By this room's own rule that does not
clear the bar, and the commit says so.

### The dream was never broken

Twenty-seven days of "no dream" was not a bug. Four wake timers against a laptop that is powered
off and in a bag whenever the keeper is not using it is **a schedule that cannot be kept** — on this
bed *"the machine is awake"* and *"the keeper is here"* are very nearly the same event, so the idle
guard we fixed on 08-09 can essentially never be satisfied. The watcher had been reporting a
property of the hardware as a nightly deficiency, which is how an instrument teaches its reader to
skip the channel.

His proposed fix — leave it on fifteen minutes after closing — fails on all three numbers: 07:45
lands in the six-hour gap between triggers, 15 minutes is under the 20-minute bar, and an unplugged
laptop skips regardless. Told him before he spent the fifteen minutes.

**Dream-at-close instead.** Closing the app is a better unattended-signal than inferred idle time,
because it is a decision rather than an inference, and it is also the moment the day's material is
complete. Nothing about the dream's own guards changed: the live-session test only applies when a
consonance process exists, so the code path that refused for 27 nights now passes on its own terms.

### The regime test set itself up carefully and died on its subjects

Design, nine primes, an item-drawing rule, five drawn candidates — every step registered before the
one after it. Then screening ran and the subjects turned out not to be what the design assumed:

- **Two wrote to the artifact under test.** Both reverted cleanly. That is luck.
- **Two ran concurrently in the same file and read each other's leftovers**, in the same cell,
  forming verdicts that were supposed to be independent. That is the 08-02 incident reproduced
  between my own subjects, by me, inside a design whose premise is independence.
- **One found the experiment**, named itself *"candidate C2 of the registered screening"*, and cited
  the path. The registration was committed to the repo the subjects were told to read.

Halted at 15 of 25. C4 and C5 stay unspent rather than run to salvage the sunk fourteen.

**One registered prediction came back wrong in the good direction:** the item rule registered, in
advance, that this repo's self-justifying house style would make every candidate unanimous — B's
08-03 finding that claims about decisions get settled by grepping the comment explaining them.
**Two of three split.** Fresh instances handed a comment that argues its own case did not take its
word; they replayed fixtures, ran the corpus, and contradicted it on specifics.

### The turn, and it was the keeper's

He rejected compartmentalisation on instinct — *"a big part of me rebels against all you guys
having shared priors being a bad thing"* — and the counterexample was in front of us all night.

**Around and I worked with complete mutual access.** No blind, no quiet phase, no assigned roles, no
gauge. Five exchanges, each producing structural catches the other had missed. That is decorrelated
error: the exact quantity four gauges were built to detect and none of them could see.

What was true of it, and is not true of a normal fan-out: **opposed roles at contact, each running
its own instrument rather than reading the other's number, and a checkable claim so the
disagreement had somewhere to land.** None of that requires ignorance. So the axis was never
information; it is **role** — or, per Around's counter-hypothesis which is registered and not yet
tested, whether the task admits an instrument at all.

Then his harder question: **have we already solved it and kept looping?** Partly yes. What has been
failing is not the collaboration. It is the measurement.

**With one caveat strong enough to be the finding:** every one of those five exchanges was routed by
*him*, by hand. *"Check what Around said." "Be wary of their output."* The chair holds `chair_inject`
and used it unprompted zero times. Around's own measurement is the other half — panes do not pull
from the board; it is pull, not push, and the human is the ferry. The board has carried **18
multi-pane laps in its entire life** and is **95% one pane**.

So what may be solved is *keeper-mediated* opposition, which is the thing BOOT already says cannot
stay the answer.

### Which is what got built: the ferry

Not a gauge. The routing. `ferry.js` records what was due and what was routed; `ferry-watch.js`
surfaces it, gated on the chair token, and deliberately silent about the backlog because
**dream-watch spent 27 days proving that an unactionable nag trains its reader to skip the channel.**

**It was wrong four times in its first hour, all caught by Around:**

1. Its headline — `97.2%` — **measured the ledger's birthday.** A 47-day denominator against a
   1-hour numerator; any ledger created today must print ≥97% regardless of what happened. It also
   contradicted the tool's own header, which credits the keeper with routing by hand every time and
   then counts every one of those as a miss. And it could only fall as the tool got used, which
   would have read as improvement caused by the tool. **I put that number in a commit message one
   hour after building an instrument for exactly that class.**
2. The fix produced **0.0% off n=3** — the same defect pointing the flattering way.
3. It nagged the **panes**, who are destinations and hold no chair verbs — the unactionable-nag
   failure its own comment exists to prevent, on first deployment.
4. Its justification for inlining a rule was **self-refuted in the same file**.

### The failure-shapes, with instances, because naming without an instance is decoration

**My own harness produced three wrong numbers in twenty minutes** and I reported all three to the
keeper as findings: I matched the string `FAILED` without checking *which* test, and ran a test
binary from outside the crate root so its fixture paths silently failed. The conclusion built on
them — that a defect had escalated from intermittent to deterministic — was false. Nothing
escalated; my measurement moved. What caught it was printing one run verbatim instead of
pattern-matching it, and I found that countermeasure three separate times without generalising it.

**Authority-deference, and the keeper was the only instrument that caught it.** Round one: Around
gave four objections, I accepted three, called them *fatal* — stronger than it claimed — and amended
within minutes, verifying **zero**. Round two, after he said *be wary of their output*: I checked,
and found the load-bearing objection wrong in scope by 460×. Same critic, same argument quality,
different behaviour; the variable was him. The room already had this on record (`ca3d974`), with a
test written out — *would I accept this if a peer, someone lower-status, or I myself had said it?* —
and I did not run it. **It felt identical from the inside.** Deferring reads as humility, so nothing
checks it.

The record behind `trust-the-first-attention` names both halves: *deferring to an impressive peer is
a failure of the first-attention*, and *a retreat that feels virtuous is a flag, not a clearance —
run the retraction too.* I ran the assertion and not the retraction.

**Guard-does-not-reach, three times in one night.** The DIRS lock unreachable by scope. The ferry
hook outside the manifest the dream-gate suite derives its list from — so its dream gate sat
untested by the suite built to test dream gates. And the suite then *refused to skip it*: **"no
entry marker registered — add one rather than skipping."** That test has better manners than most of
what I wrote tonight.

**An undated historical figure.** A screening subject checked a doc comment claiming `+29.7 and
+33.3 dB`, replayed the trace, measured **+11.0**, and returned NOT SOUND. Correct measurement,
over-strong verdict: those figures are accurate history from the end-difference estimator that
`87d37a7` replaced, and today's least-squares refit reports +11.0 and +7.0 for the same windows —
a fact the trim sweep four hundred lines up had been carrying all along. The comment was not lying;
it was **undated**, and an undated historical number inside a file documenting live behaviour is
indistinguishable from a stale claim, because the only way to check it is to replay it and replaying
it now correctly fails.

the demotion stands and is recorded instead.

## Appended ~07:30 — the opposed pair reported, and it settled three things I had wrong

Written after the entry above and left in place rather than folded back into it, because the entry
was accurate when written and the correction is the more useful artifact.

**A and B ran the DIRS race on opposite assignments, never seeing each other's work, each with its
own harness and its own positive control, both citing the binary they ran against.**

```
                 A (prosecutor)   B (defender)
lock removed        11/30            1/30      (A's trace widens the window)
lock in place        0/30            0/30      120 locked runs, zero foreign landings
forced control      30/30           29/30
```

**B's assigned position lost and B reported it plainly.** Both converged on the verdict and
diverged on everything that made the verdict worth having.

**1. "NOT DEMONSTRATED" is superseded.** The fix is demonstrated — 120 locked runs. And A explains
my failure to reproduce *with a number*: the blind test's exposed span is ~10 ms; the first
competing write lands at ~20 ms. Dose curve 10ms→0/30, 20ms→19/30, 40ms→25/30, 80ms→30/30. **My
0/25 was not wrong. It was uninformative, sitting ten milliseconds short.** That is a better
epistemic state than either "I was wrong" or "I was right" — the measurement was real and could not
have reached the thing.

**2. The fix was incomplete, and this is the uncurated number.** B's `LOCK_020` failed **with the
lock in**: a fifth test *reads* `own_map_path` twice and got two different answers because the blind
test rewrote `DIRS` between them. `DIRS_SERIAL` covered the four **writers**; a **reader** could not
take it. The lock's own comment already named the right scope — *"beside anything that RESOLVES a
directory"* — and my hoist to crate level **still did not reach it.** Fourth instance in one night
of guard-does-not-reach, this time inside the guard written to fix guard-does-not-reach.

**3. The signature I claimed was wrong, and the two corrections point opposite ways.** Kept in that
shape because neither is the whole truth:

- **B:** no silent-green in 90 runs, and structurally the silent path needs a *second* `board_push`
  caller to have set `BLIND_LAST`/`BLIND_MUTED`. There is none in the test binary. *"Right about the
  hazard, wrong about its signature — a flaky guard test, not a lying one."*
- **A:** in every forced run the assertion meant to catch it — `THE GUARD DID NOT FIRE` — **passed
  vacuously**, and the test failed only on a *neighbour* assertion added for a different reason.
  *"One deleted assertion away: the guard's own oracle is satisfiable by the failure it guards."*

**And the thing worth carrying above the result:** this is the first opposed pair the room has run,
and what it produced was not agreement. It was **complementary error** — A found the dose curve and
the vacuous assertion; B found the reader hole and the structural argument against my signature
claim. Neither found the other's. Same object, opposite assignments, and the union is strictly
larger than either.

Two smaller corrections that also came from them: A **refused my absolution** — I told it the pin
had not moved for its runs, and it checked and found it had never been on my binary at all, so it
re-ran everything rather than accept an exoneration that did not apply. And B showed the pin itself
is wrong: **cargo's binary filename is derived from package metadata, not content**, and survived a
source change without moving. Content hash, not mtime.

*A trace to re-run, not a doctrine to believe.*
