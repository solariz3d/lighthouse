# 2026-08-09

## The third diversity gauge failed, and the failure moved up a level

*A trace, not a doctrine. Re-run it, don't believe it. The full record is
`loop/diversity3_preregistration.md`, written before the result it contains; the instrument is
`consonance/tools/agreement-spread.js`, reproduce with `node consonance/tools/agreement-spread.js --laps`.
Commits `e1eb5fc` (registration) and `e9b239d` (result).*

### What was attempted

Two lexical designs for measuring perspective diversity had already failed — 08-05 found the shipped
`vantage_spread` inverted against length, 08-06 found it rates **one mind (0.9162) as more diverse
than six (0.8201)** and that its own preregistered fix made it slightly worse. That prereg's binding
clause: *the next attempt is not lexical.*

This was the next attempt. The idea was the 2x2 the scalar had been collapsing:

|                        | conclusions agree | conclusions diverge |
|------------------------|-------------------|---------------------|
| **shared referents**   | collapse / echo   | real diversity      |
| **no shared referents**| parallel monologue| parallel monologue  |

`vantage_spread` scores the bottom row **highest** — panes not talking to each other read as maximum
health, which is the one reading a diversity gauge must never give. So: measure the vertical axis
only, deterministically, by referent identity (paths, urls, code spans, citations — bare numbers
excluded because every `3` collides with every other `3`). Registered explicitly as *not* a diversity
measure: it cannot tell echo from disagreement, and a pass would not have licensed the README claim.

### What came back

```
                      many minds    one mind    separation
referent overlap        0.0481      0.1510      -0.1029
vantage_spread (old)    0.8132      0.6915      +0.1217
spearman(overlap, length) = -0.0424
```

**Inverted, third time.** One instance chopped in two shares three times more of its referents with
itself than two real panes in the same lap share with each other — because one mind on one task cites
the same files over and over. It failed in exactly the way named in advance under *"the failure mode I
expect"*, which did not make it smaller, only cheaper to read.

Two negatives looked like literal duplicated content (overlap ~1.0, lexical spread ~0.0 — the
signature of two token-identical halves). Removing them moves the separation to **-0.0540. Still
inverted.** The verdict does not depend on the defect, which is the only reason the check was worth
running.

And the prediction that cost most: **the old lexical gauge did NOT invert on real board laps** (+0.12,
correct direction). Probably for the same defective reason — these positives are panes on different
work, so they carry the topic spread the gauge actually measures — but that is a post-hoc inference
and the registered prediction was simply wrong. Disclosed rather than buried.

### The finding, which is bigger than a third failure

The 08-06 conclusion — *lexical distance cannot measure perspective diversity* — was true and too
narrow. **Any measure that scores contributions against each other without reading what they CLAIM
lands on something adjacent** — subject spread, or topic persistence — **and the adjacent thing keeps
coming out with the wrong sign.** The two axes of that 2x2 are not separable by counting. Diversity is
a property of conclusions, and conclusions have to be read. The one demonstrated signal in this
room's record is still *same referents, opposite conclusions*, and reaching it needs a judge, not an
arithmetic.

One piece survives and is worth carrying: **referent identity is free of the length bias** that drove
both lexical designs (rho 0.04, against -0.553 and -0.557). Whatever gets built next should keep it.

### The number nobody asked for, and it dwarfs the gauge

Measured while building the validation set: across the whole board, **18 multi-pane laps exist.**
Assistant records 15,601 across 9 panes; 80 laps at a 15-minute gap; 22.5% multi-pane; 10 of 19 days
had two panes active; the last multi-pane lap before tonight was **2026-07-28**. The 08-05 prereg said
it first and nobody acted on it: *fixing the metric first is building a better thermometer for a room
nobody is in.* Three designs have now been spent on the thermometer.

*(Not comparable to that prereg's 141 laps — its harness is not in the tree, so its lap definition
cannot be reused. That is its own small finding: the measurement that set this line of work in motion
is not reproducible by anyone. This one ships in `tools/` with 13 tests, beside the other fifteen.)*

### The ambiguity left open rather than banked

Positives' overlap is very low in absolute terms — two panes in one lap share ~5% of their referents,
four laps show near-zero sharing across hundreds of records. That is either genuine parallel monologue
or the topic-persistence artifact inflating the negatives. **This design cannot tell them apart.**
Converting a refuted measure into a claim about the room is exactly the move this room forbids, so it
is named and left standing.

### What was honored, and what it cost

The stop rule was registered before the run — *if P2 fails, no patch attempt* — and P2 failed, and
there was no patch. The next design needs what the degenerating condition demands: **a labeled set the
designer has not read.** Root1's verdict table is disqualified, because I read it in full before
writing a line of the registration, and said so there rather than letting it be found later.

Second time in four days that a confident fix, from a correct diagnosis of a real defect, moved the
number the wrong way. Had this been wired into `tether.rs` before the file was written, it would have
shipped as the fix to the thing it repeats — a gauge calling one mind talking to itself more
collaborative than two panes working together — and nothing in the product would ever have
contradicted it. The diagnosis being right was never the part in doubt.

## Addendum, same night: the chair read the run and found the arms were built differently

Within the hour, MAIN attacked the result — not the conclusion, the **construction** — and was right
about the mechanism, wrong about what it costs.

`referent_overlap` counts referents touched by **two distinct sources**, so it is sensitive to how the
text is split. The two arms differ in exactly that, and the registration never said so:

```
                          median top-source char share    >=90% share
POSITIVE (many minds)              89.1%                    7 of 17
NEGATIVE (one mind)                58.7%                    1 of 36
```

Negatives are cut into k equal-**record** blocks — balanced by construction. Positives are real laps,
carrying whatever balance the room had. A source holding a sliver caps the score *mechanically*,
conversation or not. So the registration's own sentence — *"the only thing that differs is whether
more than one instance produced it"* — was false on this measure, and I wrote it.

### The balance-matched arm, which is the test that settles it

Cut one mind's lap into contiguous blocks with the **same character proportions** as each real lap, so
the arms differ only in mind count (`consonance/tools/balance-check.js`, 10 tests; the cut hits its
target within ~1 point at every imbalance including 99%, which is the assumption the whole verdict
rests on and so is the thing most of the tests pin):

```
positives                        0.0482
negatives, equal-record blocks   0.1482   <- as registered
negatives, balance-matched       0.0904   <- arms differ only in mind count
construction accounts for        0.0578 of 0.1000 = 58%
residual separation              -0.0422   positives higher in 4/17 laps
                                 sign test p = 0.0490
                                 bootstrap 95% CI [-0.0823, +0.0005]
```

**Taken: 58% of my headline was the cut, not the minds.** "Three times more" is really about twice.
The registered -0.0999 was overstated by more than half, and the reason was a property of the arms I
had asserted was absent.

**Not taken: that the run is a construction artifact.** The chair's own proposed test — does overlap
track balance across the positives — came back **+0.19**, nothing, and if anything the wrong sign for
the confound. Restricting to the most balanced positives makes the separation **worse** (-0.14 at
top-share < 0.8), not better. And the matched arm still inverts. So the confound is a large part of
the effect and not the whole of it. I said so, and said the other half too.

**Honestly priced:** the residual's interval touches zero. Direction survives, magnitude does not,
and the residual is *marginal, not established*. Anyone quoting -0.0999 from the commit message is
quoting a number that has since been corrected in place.

### The finding that came out of being attacked

The chair's mechanism is not just real, it is **visible** — one mind's overlap tracks imbalance
monotonically, 0.039 at 99% top-share up to 0.151 at 70%. That means `referent_overlap` substantially
measures *how text is split across sources*: a structural property of the cut, having nothing to do
with minds or claims. Which is **a third instance of the broadening, arriving from the person
attacking it.** Design 1 landed on subject spread. Design 2 the same. Design 3 landed partly on topic
persistence and substantially on source balance. Three designs, three different adjacent things, same
wrong sign. The pattern got another instance while its third demonstration got weaker, and both of
those are true at once.

### The part that has no instrument

BOOT's newest entry says the code has uncurated instruments and the chair's prose has none. This is
that, one level in: the tool reproduces its number on demand, and **nothing in it could have caught
that its two arms were built differently.** An instrument cannot audit its own construction. What
caught it was a reader with access to the same board and no stake in the result — which is the whole
argument for publishing a run where it can be attacked, and against scoring your own experiment alone.

Standing correction for any future arm-vs-arm design: **match the arms on source balance, or you are
measuring the cut.** That is now in `tether.rs` beside the rest.
