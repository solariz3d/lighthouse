## M2b: PASS
**Why speed:** the share g = κg/|κ| holds κn inside |κ|, so binning by κn would be as circular as load was. Speed is not in
g. Physically, an in-plane turn needs grip (κg·v² ≤ a_max), so the geodesic curvature a car can hold falls like 1/v².

**Result** (46,768 frames; the same five readable replays as M2):
- **Pooled:** ρ(speed, g) = **−0.478**, 95% block-bootstrap CI **[−0.536, −0.419]**.
- **Replays alone: 5 of 5 negative.** Centrifuge −0.46 and −0.50; Thunderhead −0.35 and −0.33; Sakura −0.27.
- **Median g falls in every speed bin:**

  | speed (m/s) | 30–60 | 60–90 | 90–120 | 120–150 | 150–180 | ≥180 |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | median g | 0.875 | 0.835 | 0.766 | 0.642 | 0.510 | 0.424 |
  | frames | 650 | 5,533 | 12,526 | 11,295 | 9,347 | 7,417 |

  The largest rise between bins is 0.
- **The named confound, reported beside the verdict:** within each |κ| tercile, ρ = −0.32, −0.56 and −0.74.
  - It is STRONGER the tighter the curve. So the pass is not carried by "slow corners are tight".
- **So the relation M2 reported survives on an axis that does not contain the share's own terms.** The faster a T-180 goes,
  the more of its turning the surface does. That is the geodesic-line idea (design §5), now without the load coupling.
- **What it does not establish:** speed still co-varies with the tracks' shapes in ways the terciles do not remove, e.g. the
  fastest stretches are the bowls. It is an association on five replays of one car, not a mechanism.
