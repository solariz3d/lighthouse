### THE CAUSE (registered)

The snap is **the steering rule on a polyline**, not the aim:

1. **`chase/chaser.cpp:284`:** `err` is the bearing to the path's next point minus the heading.
   - `:298-300`: `heading += clampf(err, ±turn_rate·dt)`. That is a bang-bang steer.
   - At a path vertex the heading pivots at the full turn rate (a constant 261–353°/s).
   - Between vertices `err` is exactly zero, so the heading is frozen.
   - Two motions only, full turn or none, which is what a machine does.
2. **The path is the navigation's string-pulled polyline** (`chaser.cpp:186-195` `plan_to` → `nav::Query::find_path`).
   - Its vertices are the corners.
   - Past the last corner it is one or two dead-straight segments to the target.
3. **Each replan aims that polyline at the target** (`chase/run.cpp` `Run::replan`; the target is the player's observed position,
   or a point ahead for the interceptor).
   - So once round a corner the chaser runs a ruler line at where it was told the player is.
   - The next replan pivots it onto a new ruler line.

**Ruled out by the same data:**
- **A heading set outright:** the turn is always bounded, at most 353°/s.
  - The direct assignments in `run.cpp` (`:824` reprieve, `:843` off stage) are not in these corners: all 9 are in Pursue on the
    mesh.
- **Perfect aim at the player:** the mean angle to him over the second is 17–42°, not 0. The line points at where it was told he
  was.
  - **So "the angle of the line to the player over the first second should not be zero" is already true in his run, and is not
    what reads as AI.**
  - What reads as AI is the line itself: dead straight 63–72 % of the second after a full-rate pivot.
  - **I add two measures beside the plan's:**
    - the pivot (seconds above 30°/s, and its peak);
    - the dead-straight share.
  - The plan's angle stays measured too.

**What would falsify this cause:**
- After the build makes the heading's turn continuous (a turn rate that follows a curvature with no frozen segments and no
  saturated pivots), the replays of the same seed and inputs still show more than 50 % of the second dead straight after a
  corner, or pivots at the full rate. Then the straightness comes from somewhere else, the plan cadence or the target, and I
  will say so.
- **The unwelcome outcome, named:** the organic version costs catch power. Curved, lagged, wandering pursuit could let the
  bots outrun every kind, and the speed model's registered ratios (`chase/SPEED_MODEL.md`) could fail. That would have to be
  reported, not tuned away by weakening a row.
