## 1 · THE METHOD, fixed before any track was run (written 00:4x; nothing in §2 onward existed then)

Before this section was written, the tool was run only on synthetic surfaces (`test/water.test.js`, §1.8). No track
surface or replay had passed through it.

### 1.1 The surface
- **What counts as road:** the reader's drivable meshes, by `read_track.cjs`'s rule:
  - the `IS_VALID_TRACK=1` surface keys;
  - no pit meshes;
  - underside skins skipped unless they are the road.
- **Normals:** each triangle's normal is kept on the car's side by continuity with the last contact. The nearest
  triangle must turn by less than 45° from the last contact. This is how the reader follows walls and inversions.
- **The lip** is the mesh's own boundary: an edge used by one triangle, after welding vertices at 5 mm, as
  `envelope.cjs` does.

### 1.2 The integrator and time step
- **Semi-implicit Euler with projection.** Each step:
  1. `v += g·h`; `p += v·h`.
  2. Take the nearest road triangle.
  3. **At 2 cm above the surface or below it, it is CONTACT:** snap onto the surface and remove the velocity along
     the normal (an inelastic, unilateral constraint; the normal force is whatever this takes).
  4. **Higher than 2 cm, it is airborne:** a crest that curves away faster than gravity can follow lifts the particle
     off, as it would a car.
- **The step h:** at most 4 ms, and an exact divisor of the replay's dt, so every sample is evenly spaced. At dt 0.03 s,
  h = 3.75 ms, which is about 0.48 m at 460 km/h.

### 1.3 The speed
- **The verdict uses the DESIGN SPEED, 460 km/h** (`MACH6.designSpeedKmh`, `src/validate/limits.js:37`), held
  constant while on the road.
  - The thrust is taken to cancel drag and gravity's component along the path.
  - Gravity's sideways component still acts, as does every normal force.
  - In the air there is no speed hold: the flight is ballistic.
- **This is geometry-only.** No replay number enters the prediction.
- **Beside it, NOT the verdict:** the same run with the speed held at the replay car's speed at the nearest replay point
  (`--replay-speed`). This separates "wrong speed" from "wrong model".

### 1.4 The sheet and "the predicted line"
- **The sheet:** 21 particles, released at the read's first station (the start line).
  - They are spread evenly by ARC LENGTH along the surface, from 1 m inside the right edge to 1 m inside the left
    (the read's `wr` and `wl`), so a bowl's walls get their share.
  - Each starts at 460 km/h along the read's direction, projected onto its own triangle.
- **The predicted line is the CENTRE particle** (the 11th of 21, released on the read's centre).
  - It is chosen from the geometry alone.
  - The particle nearest the replay at the start was NOT used as the predictor, because choosing it would take a
    number from the replay.
- **Beside it, NOT the verdict:**
  - the sheet's BEST particle (the lowest off-line share): an upper bound on what the sheet contains;
  - the RE-RELEASE protocol: a fresh sheet released every 1,000 m of the read, each ridden 1,000 m. It maps every
    region of the lap, not only the stretch before a single release spills. It gives a stitched centre-particle
    off-line share over the whole lap.

### 1.5 The lip
- **A particle beyond a boundary edge by more than 1 cm is off the road.**
- **Crossed SIDEWAYS** (the crossing direction is less than cos⁻¹ 0.7 ≈ 46° off square to the velocity), the
  particle is **over the lip**. It flies ballistically:
  - if it is back on road within 0.25 s, the event is a lip excursion (a small gap between meshes, or a brief hop);
  - otherwise it is a **SPILL**, recorded at the crossing point, and the particle ENDS there.
- **Crossed FORWARDS,** it is a take-off (a jump). The particle flies and continues if it lands on road within 5 s,
  otherwise it is LOST.

### 1.6 Comparing the line
- **Progress** = the read's nearest station, as `coverage.cjs` finds it. The last progress is used as a hint, so an
  overpass does not snap the particle to the road above.
- **For every road station of the read (every 4 m):**
  - take the first sample of the particle past that station;
  - measure its 3-D distance to the replay line, using only replay points within ±300 m of the same progress (the
    nearest point, refined onto the two segments beside it).
- **A station the particle never reached** (because it spilled or was lost first) counts as OFF.
- **Off-line share** = OFF stations / all road stations. **The line fails if it is > 10%.**
- **The replay line:** car 0's frames that are moving (> 5 m/s) and not teleports, by `loads.cjs`'s filters. Every such
  frame counts, so a replay with more than one lap contributes all of its laps.

### 1.7 The loads
- **The predicted loads use `loads.cjs`'s method on the particle's own path**, sampled at the replay's dt:
  - ±4-frame smoothing, then a second difference;
  - the load is (a + g)·n / g along the contact normal (in flight, the last contact normal).
- **p50 and p99 are taken over the centre particle's whole ride.**
- **The replay's p50 and p99 are `loads.cjs`'s figure,** recomputed by the same code inside `water.cjs`. The Sakura
  27-03 figure must reproduce FINDINGS §3c (p50 5.87, p99 34.76), or the comparison is not the same instrument.
- **The load fails if** |pred − replay| / replay > 0.25 for p50 or for p99.
- **Beside it, NOT the verdict:** the replay's loads over only the stretch of progress the centre particle covered.
  This gives a same-stretch comparison if the particle spills early.

### 1.8 Checked before any track (synthetic surfaces)
**`node --test --test-concurrency=4 test/water.test.js` → 4/4:**
- on a 30°-banked ring (R 300 m) at the balanced speed (tan θ = v²/gR), the particle holds its radius within 1 m for
  two laps. Water finds zero cant deficiency;
- at 1.5× that speed it climbs and spills over the outer lip;
- the load there is 1/cos θ;
- on a plane it runs straight.

**A real bug was caught and fixed there, before any track.** The first lip rule said: nearest point > 0.3 m to the side
means off the road. A particle crossing the lip moves a few cm per step, so it was snapped back onto the edge every
step and could never spill. The rule is now the mesh's own boundary edges.

### 1.10 What each outcome would mean, said now
- **A FAIL on Sakura** is the expected-hard case for a first form: a frictionless particle with no steering, ridden for
  21 km from one release. Small errors compound, and one spill ends the predicted line.
  - A FAIL with a good re-release share says water is right locally and the single release is the weak part.
  - A FAIL on both says water alone is not the line on Sakura.
- **Thunderhead is expected to depart** (§11: grip does more of the turning there). I will report where and by how much,
  and not count it as evidence against water on the surface-riding tracks.
- **A PASS would say the line is geometric on that track at the design speed.** It would not say how a driver reaches
  the line, and it would say nothing about loads beyond p50 and p99.
