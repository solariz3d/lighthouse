## 2. What row B measured, and what it did not

The darkest tenth of a grainy picture sits below the picture's true dark level, because grain puts dark pixels below
it. A filter that takes the grain away raises those pixels without lifting the true dark.

**Measured, not argued** (`scratchpad filter/settled.txt`, a temporary block never committed): the two still views
**settled**, 432 frames before their 24, when the grain is gone (over 8 levels per frame: 0.02-0.03%). Each cell is
the darkest tenth in 8-bit levels.

| view | 24 frames from a forgotten history, off / on | settled, off / on | the filter's lift, settled |
|---|---|---|---|
| spawn, hand light | 4.52 / 5.00 | 5.09 / 5.14 | **+0.06** |
| spawn, lamps alone | 1.41 / 1.63 | 1.71 / 1.79 | **+0.08** |

- **Settled, the filter lifts the true dark by 0.06 to 0.08 levels**, inside the 0.25 margin.
- In the grainy frames without the filter, the dark sits 0.30 to 0.57 levels below its own settled level. The filter
  brings it most of the way to the settled level and does not pass it.
- **So:** by the measure I registered, the filter lifts the dark. By the measure that answers his worry, "loses the
  darkness we want", it keeps the dark where the game's own converged picture has it. **The registered measure was the
  wrong instrument** (my registration's fault, not the filter's), and I do not change it after seeing the result.
