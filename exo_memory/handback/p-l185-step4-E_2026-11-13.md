## 2. Three causes found and fixed. Each was changed after the first measured run, and each is named in the code

1. **The motors' size.**
   - Jolt's frequency mode sizes a spring by its own two bodies (SpringSettings `FrequencyAndDamping`). A shoulder at
     "5 Hz" was far softer against the whole arm below it.
   - Measured: a running pose, held still and placed exactly on its target, sagged **0.85 m**; at six times the
     frequency it held at 0.06 m.
   - Fixed: each motor is now `StiffnessAndDamping`, with k = I·ω² and c = 2ζ·I·ω. Here I is the inertia about the
     joint of every body below it: point masses at each body's centre, plus each body's own largest principal moment.
   - **The registered 8 Hz trunk and 5 Hz arms, and the damping ratios 1.0 and 0.5, are unchanged.** They now mean
     what they say.
   - The held pose now holds within **0.046 m**, from either start.
2. **Placement.**
   - A pose already moving (9 m/s from the first tick) left the bodies, placed by `SetPose` with no velocity, behind.
     They were flung: the hand was 0.19 m off by the third tick.
   - Fixed: on the step after a placement, each body on motors takes its target's velocity. In the game this is the
     hunter appearing mid-run.
3. **The solver.**
   - The ragdoll's own system now iterates **30 velocity and 10 position steps** (Jolt's defaults are 10 and 2).
   - The elbow's 95th percentile went from 0.13 m to **0.066 m**; 60 steps or two collision steps gave nothing more.

## 3. Ruled out, by measurement, not argument

- **The target mapping.** Each constraint's rotation in constraint space equals its target to 0.0–0.6° at the start
  of a held pose (printed from `GetRotationInConstraintSpace` against `GetTargetOrientationCS`).
- **The target's direction and sign.** q⁻¹ was no better, and forcing w positive gave the same numbers to the digit.
- **Sudden target jumps.** The largest turn of any upper target in one tick over the chase is **8.2°**, at the stop.
  The arms' frames never flip.
- **The joint limits.** A cone opened to 0.99π changed the 95th percentiles by under 3 mm.
- **Self-collision.** All of it off changed under 5 mm.
- **Position-only motors** (no velocity fed forward) were worse: hand 0.655 m against 0.575 m.
- **Stiffer.** Three times the stiffness is unstable: the arms go to 2.7 m. So the arms sit near the limit of what
  this solver can hold, and stiffness is not the lever.

## 4. The method question (the chair's, or his)

- **What the rows show:** a chain of four small dynamic bodies (upper arm, forearm, hand capsule, hand sphere), on
  swing-twist motors hanging off keyed bodies, cannot follow a 10 m/s arm swing here. The trunk and the upper arm can.
- **The plan already names the other mechanism:** Jolt's soft-keyframe drive (`SoftKeyframedRigTest.cpp:40-91`).
  Each body's velocity is set toward its keyframe every step, and collisions still act. Per-bone strength is then a
  blend between that velocity and the body's own (Overgrowth's weights), and is stable for any chain.
  inferred: it would pass R4.3 at full strength and give follow-through at partial strength. Not measured.
- **Options, one line each:**
  - **(a) The whole upper body on the soft keyframe.** Registered rows unchanged; the mechanism named as changed.
  - **(b) Motors for the trunk and upper arms, soft keyframe for the forearms and hands.**
  - **(c) Stop step 4 here.** The keyed ragdoll stays as it is on main.
- **I would take (a).** One mechanism is simpler than two. Its strength dial is the per-bone weight the plan asks
  for, and step 5's reaction becomes a strength drop on the same dial.
- **What would change my mind:** if (a) cannot give R4.4's follow-through at a strength that also passes R4.3. That
  is the same registered unwelcome outcome, met by the second mechanism.
