## 1 · Why a hill through the pitch rate cannot be local (the premise, measured)

**The probe:** `scratchpad/d186/probe.js` → `probe.txt`, under the lock, on landed 17c2301.
- **Setup:** a level straight of 2 km; a bump H·(1 − S₂(|s − s₀|/r)); Δκv = h″ put on the kv control points at their knot
  averages.
- **Height and pitch return to zero:** the pitch change at the end is 1e-17 rad.
- **The end moves back along the track:**

| hill | the end moves |
|---|---|
| 1 m, r 100 m | 8.9 mm |
| 5 m, r 100 m | 22.3 cm |
| 10 m, r 200 m | 63.8 cm |
| 2 m, r 50 m | 46 m; the pitch never returns: 20 m knots cannot hold h″ in a 100 m window |

**The cause is arc length.** s is the road's arc length. A hill makes the road longer over the same s, so its horizontal
projection shrinks by ∫(1 − cos h′) ds. That is second order in the slope, so no choice of κv removes it.
- The theory for 5 m / 100 m is 0.36 m. The measured 0.22 m is lower because the projection on 20 m knots smooths the bump.
- A second fault: 1 − S₂(|x|) is only C2 at its centre, so h″ would have a kink there. A pitch-rate hill would need a C4 bump.

**Offsets are local by construction.** `h` and `l` are VALUES, like φ, w and r. Where they are zero with zero slope, the adapter
returns the geometry's own sample object: nothing is recomputed, so nothing can differ. Reference 10 §4 has the derivation.

**Corrections, in order (each would have given a false verdict):**
1. My runner's first "12 of 12" was FALSE. The peak's position was `undefined` in every case, so the r/2 check compared against
   NaN and passed silently. I found it by instrumenting the run, and the runner now fails on NaN (`!(|at − s₀| ≤ r/2)`).
2. The cause was a real bug in `offset.js`. The geometry's samples are LAZY objects: `s`, `seg` and `roll` are getters on their
   prototype, and my `{ ...x }` copy dropped them. It now keeps the prototype and every own descriptor, and a unit test holds it
   (mutant O2).
3. **kvec in a lifted sample must be curvature per metre of ROAD.** Inside a hill s is not arc length, so dT′/ds is divided by
   |dpos′/ds|. My own unit test first expected h″, which was wrong. The graph curvature is h″/(1 + h′²)^{3/2} (WIKI-CURV), with
   vertical part h″/(1 + h′²)². Code and test are both fixed.
4. **The peak normalisation** first read the change at s₀. At r = 20 the widened bump's top sits off s₀ on uneven knots, so the
   peak was 5.40 m for 5 m asked (inside B's 10%, but not what was asked). It now reads the largest change over the window used,
   and peaks are 5.0000 ± 0.0001 m.

## 3 · What is in `src/core/sculpt.js` now

**The D185 API is unchanged:** `sculpt(doc, { channel, s0, r, delta })` is the engine.

**New: `brush(doc, { mode = 'hill', channel, s0, r, delta, sharp = false })`**, with `MODES`:

| mode | channel | does |
|---|---|---|
| **hill** (the default) | `h` | raises or lowers the road; `delta` is the PEAK in metres (normalised, §2 item 4) |
| **swerve** | `l` | moves the road sideways; `delta` is the peak in metres, + = left |
| **value** | `phi`, `w`, `r` (and `h`, `l`) | a local value brush, as D185 |
| **rate** | `kh`, `kv` | "turn or climb harder from here on". The track changes downstream. On a CLOSED track it re-closes at once with close's `edited` set to the brushed pieces; a failed re-close leaves the track OPEN and says so |

**What it refuses, by name:**
- a rate channel under any other mode (`BAD_CHANNEL`);
- a hill or swerve with no offset channel (`NOT_YET`);
- an unknown mode (`BAD_MODE`).

**The widening now reads the spans UNDER the window** (A's catch, correct), iterated to a fixed point because a widened window can
reach coarser spans.

**`sharp: true`** refines the knots under the brush first (A's `refineKnots`), so the brush acts at the asked radius. Its measured
cost (§4) is why it is opt-in.

**`src/core/offset.js` (new; a proposal for A's adapter):** `offsetSamples(samples, at)`.
- `pos′ = pos + h·ŷ + l·R`, with R the gravity frame's horizontal left, and T′ from dpos′/ds.
- The frame is rebuilt the geometry's way with the SAME roll.
- kvec′ is dT′/ds divided by the speed.
- A sample with no offset and no slope is the SAME object.
- Reference 10 §4 has the derivation and the known answers.

## 4 · Widen or refine under a narrow brush (`scratchpad/d186/compare.js` → `compare.txt`, under the lock)

On test 3's 30 brushes (track 0; W⁺ = W ± 2 ORIGINAL spans):
- **W**, widen to 3 spans (mine);
- **R**, A's `refineKnots` over [s₀ − r − 3h, s₀ + r + 3h] to h = r/3, then the brush at the asked r.

| | channel values differing outside W⁺ | geometry moved past W⁺ | κ-change kink rule FAIL |
|---|---|---|---|
| **W** | **0 of 30** | **0 of 30** | **0 of 30** |
| **R** | 15 (every r = 20 case; up to 2.7e-5) | 9 (value brushes at r = 20: 18–70 µm) | 6 (φ, w, r at 25% and 50%, r = 20) |

**Why R moves things:** the knots are SHARED by all channels. Refining re-quantises the new control points of kh and kv too,
so the line past the brush moves, even for a bank or width brush.

**So:** widening stays the default, and refinement is the explicit `sharp` option with this cost stated. The chair's queued
"fold refineKnots into the brush" crossed with this measurement; I did not fold it in as the default, and say why here and on the
board.
