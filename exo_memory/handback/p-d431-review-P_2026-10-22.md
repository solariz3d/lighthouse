## 2. Does the metric do what §3 says? Yes

Read `src/core/close.js` at a519bb9 `gramMetric` (:131-163) and the step (:337-341):

- Per channel, over the free columns: W = Σ_pieces Σ_samples w·(a aᵀ + ℓ²·a′ a′ᵀ) + GRAM_EPS·I, with a(s) the column shares of the channel's value at s and a′ its slope, both through `expand` (so a joint's linked points carry into the piece after). That is ∫ (Δ)² + ℓ²(Δ′)² ds by the midpoint rule every 2 m, as §3 says.
- a′ is per metre: `tools/piecewise.cjs basis` `d1` differentiates in the knot parameter, and `document.js:70 knotVector` is in metres (`[0,0,0,0, …knots, length ×4]`). So ℓ = 50 m is a length, as the comment says.
- Which pieces the Gram reads (:138 `e.some(terms => terms.some(([c]) => at.has(c)))`) is the same predicate as `keptPieces` (:reached), so the Gram covers exactly the pieces the close rebuilds: the window, and the piece after a mid-lap window through its two linked points (the seam tries). A fixed column is 0 both in the Gram and in `winvRow`'s output.
- Cholesky W = LLᵀ then forward/back substitution: W⁻¹·row. W is SPD by construction; the `CLOSE_SINGULAR` throw is unreachable without a NaN channel, and would surface by name if it were.
- The step: `WJ = W⁻¹Jᵀ`, `M = J·WJ`, `δ = Σ WJ_i y_i` — δ = −W⁻¹Jᵀ(JW⁻¹Jᵀ)⁻¹r, the minimiser of δᵀWδ under Jδ = −r. The rows J are untouched, so the lap closes to the same bars; only which change is taken differs. §3 is accurate.
- Built once per `close()` call, before the iteration loop (:309). Cost is small (a few hundred samples × ≤ 4 basis terms per piece per channel; one n³/3 per channel with n a window's points).
- Cross-channel weighting (kh in rad/m against w in m against phi in rad) is as arbitrary as the old diagonal metric's; not a regression, noted.
