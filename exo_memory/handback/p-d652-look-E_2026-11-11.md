## 1. What the look is, in one paragraph

- The pictures he means are **empty places built for people**. They are lit by artificial light that is still on, shot on a cheap
  camera at an ordinary height, and they feel familiar and wrong at once.
  - The roots are the 2019 Backrooms post and r/LiminalSpace (Aug 2019).
  - The feeling has a coined name, *kenopsia*: a place that is usually busy, now empty.
- The **nostalgia-bait** side is the same emptiness with a period attached: dead malls, hotel and indoor pools, school halls, an
  XP-era PC. It is softer and warmer.
- The **creepypasta / analog-horror** side is the same places seen through a failing medium (tape, broadcast, bad JPEG), with
  something in them.
- Every source agrees on the core traits: emptiness, fluorescent or tungsten light, yellowed tones, grain or low-quality capture,
  transitional places, and retro fittings. They disagree on where the edge is (r/LiminalSpace's own rule: "it's the combination of
  transitional elements", not any empty photo).

## 2. The traits, as things our renderer and generator can turn

| area | trait | what makes it read |
|---|---|---|
| **light** | colour temperature | Fluorescent cool-white (~4000 K, slightly green) in offices, schools and the facility. Warm tungsten (~2700-3000 K) in hotels and apartments. Mixed, never graded clean. |
| | the fixture | Long tubes in grids, flat and shadowless from above, with even, dull falloff. Liminal light is everywhere and from nowhere, not pools. |
| | wrongness | One tube dead or flickering in a row of good ones. A lit room with no one to light it for. |
| | overexposure | Blown-out lamps and windows, a white sky through glass, a camera flash's hot near wall and black depth. |
| **materials** | surfaces | Drop-ceiling tiles, low-pile carpet with a busy repeat pattern, beige or mono-yellow wallpaper, small square tile (pools), terrazzo and brass (malls), lino and lockers (schools). |
| | wear | Water stains, a labelscar where a sign came off, faded paint. Clean enough to be in use, never ruined. |
| | gloss | Wet tile, polished mall floors and water reflect. In the pool rooms that is most of the picture. |
| **camera and lens** | FOV | Phone or digicam wide (~70-80° horizontal photo), held at chest or eye height, level. Not a game FOV. |
| | capture | Grain, JPEG blocking, soft focus, slight chromatic fringe, an orange date stamp (digicam), on-camera flash. |
| | tape (analog horror) | VHS chroma bleed, tracking lines, head-switching noise at the bottom, timestamp, interlace, 4:3. |
| **composition** | emptiness | No people, no clutter, nothing moving. |
| | repetition | The same doorway or column, again and again into fog. |
| | wrong scale | A ceiling too high, a corridor too long, a pool room the size of a cathedral, a door at the wrong height. |
| | thresholds | Doorways, stairs, escalators and corridors: places for passing through, never for staying. |
| **sound** | room tone | The fluorescent hum (mains 60 Hz, so a 120 Hz buzz and its overtones from magnetic ballasts, North America), HVAC air, the flicker's tick. |
| | space | Long reverb in big tiled rooms, dead air in carpeted ones, water lapping, distant muzak through ceiling speakers ("mallsoft"). |
| | absence | No voices. Silence carries information when the hum cuts out. |

## 3. What the built renderer has, and what is missing (39e03db)

checked: `render/renderer.h:28-105`, `render/src/renderer.cpp:214-226` (ForLook), `render/shaders/dz.hlsl:510-567`,
`core/scene.h:34-81`, `gen/src/gen.h:221-230`, `gen/src/biome.cpp`, `audio/audio.h`, `audio/mixer.cpp:586-588`.

**Has:**
- Soft shadows from sized lights (`Light::radius`).
- Point and cone lights, with a range and a knee.
- Emissive materials (the lamp fixtures).
- One bounce of indirect light, ambient occlusion, and temporal accumulation (TAA).
- Fog toward the void, and bloom with a threshold.
- An ACES curve (Film) and the Dreamzone-RT grade (Horror): contrast, saturation, warmth, split tone, lifted blacks, vignette.
  - Chromatic aberration exists, but only in Horror.
  - Film grain exists but is **0 in both looks**. It is only on a slider in the render viewer (`render/view/main.cpp:540`).
- F2 switches Film ↔ Horror. Those are the only two looks.
- The camera FOV has a speed widen (`game/camera.cpp:148`).
- Per-biome kits: each biome gets its own material ids and lamp colour (`kit_palette`).
  - Numbers are set only for the apartment (warm 1.0/0.86/0.62) and the hotel (1.0/0.78/0.50).
  - Facility, mall, pool rooms, school and chaos are "named, and waiting for their numbers" (`biome.cpp`).
- Sound: chaser voices, footsteps, landing thumps, a looped ambience bed from a WAV, and the reprieve's hum (three steady sines,
  "a room with something running in it").

**Missing:**
- **Textures.** A `Material` is one flat albedo and an emissive colour (`renderer.h:43-46`). There is no carpet pattern, tile grid,
  wallpaper, ceiling tile or stain. This is the biggest gap for this look: most of its recognisability is surface pattern.
- **Reflections / gloss.** The trace is diffuse only: no specular, no mirror, no water (grep of dz.hlsl for
  reflect/rough/specular → none). The pool rooms and mall floors need it.
- **Long lights.** A fluorescent tube is a line; the renderer's lights are points or cones with a radius.
- **Lamp flicker or failure over time.** Lamps are static (`SetStaticLights`).
- **Any capture look.** No digicam or VHS pass: no JPEG blocking, chroma bleed, tracking noise, interlace, date stamp, or
  flash-on-camera.
- **Room acoustics.** No reverb or echo in the mixer (grep → none). There is no hum per lamp and no muzak; the only hum is the
  reprieve's.
- **Generator traits.** There is no wrong-scale rule (a too-tall room, a too-long hall), and there are no numbers for five of
  seven biomes.

**Cost note (from my L161 look hand-back, `p-l161-look-E_2026-11-11.md`):**
- The worst building views were already at 2.8-2.9 ms against a 2.78 ms budget, so anything that adds rays is the expensive kind.
- **Free or near free** (post or CPU side, no new rays): grain, a capture pass (digicam or VHS), flicker (a light's colour changing
  over time), colour temperature per kit, FOV, and the generator's scale and repetition.
- **Costs rays:** reflections (pool, mall floor), a camera flash as a light, and many more lights in view.
- **Costs content work:** textures (a texture path into the renderer, plus the art).
- inferred: a flash light per pixel adds a shadow ray, like any loose light (`kMaxLights` loose lights are "considered at every
  pixel", `renderer.h:48`). Not measured.

## 4. The sub-genres, a line each, with references

Links found 2026-11-11 by web search. I did not open every page, and community sites change.

**Liminal spaces.** Real photographs of empty transitional places (corridors, waiting rooms, stairwells, parking lots, offices),
lit and unpopulated.
- [Liminal space (aesthetic), Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liminal_space_(aesthetic))
- [r/LiminalSpace](https://www.reddit.com/r/LiminalSpace/): the community, from Aug 2019; its bot account @SpaceLiminalBot
- [Know Your Meme: Liminal Spaces / Images With Elegiac Auras](https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/cultures/liminal-spaces-images-with-elegiac-auras-dreamcore)
- [Hyperallergic: How Liminalism Became the Defining Aesthetic of Our Time](https://hyperallergic.com/how-liminalism-became-the-defining-aesthetic-of-our-time)
- *The Exit 8* (2023, KOTAKE CREATE): one underground corridor that loops, where you play by noticing what is wrong. No link
  verified.

**The Backrooms (liminal horror).** The same places, made endless and inhabited. A photo plus one paragraph in 2019, then a
fiction (my L161 hand-back, `p-l161-backrooms-E_2026-11-11.md`, covers its lore).
- [The Backrooms, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Backrooms)
- [Kane Pixels' Backrooms series (fan wiki)](https://kanepixelsbackrooms.fandom.com/wiki/Backrooms_(series)): built in Blender,
  made to look like 1990s camcorder tape
- [Dread Central: "The Backrooms" found footage](https://www.dreadcentral.com/news/421920/the-backrooms-is-a-found-footage-nightmare-freaking-out-the-internet)
- [Anemoiapolis (itch.io)](https://q-andrew.itch.io/anemoiapolis): a liminal-space game from the 2020 forum ideas

**Dreamcore and weirdcore.**
- Dreamcore is the dream remembered: soft, bright, pastel, nostalgic and odd but not threatening.
- Weirdcore is the memory glitching: low-quality photos, strange angles, captions, eyes, and dread.
- References:
  - [Weirdcore aesthetic, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weirdcore_aesthetic)
  - [Screenshot Media: inside weirdcore, "nostalgia of the unknown"](https://screenshot-media.com/?p=22297)
  - [Adobe: what is weirdcore](https://www.adobe.com/uk/express/learn/blog/what-is-weirdcore): its "dreamcore is a fantasy you
    just woke up from" contrast
  - [Yume Nikki, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yume_Nikki): 2004, the dream-exploration ancestor. You fall asleep in
    your bed and walk dream worlds, with no combat and no game over. Item 3's bed-to-dreamzone is close to this, so keep it a
    reference only.

**Analog horror.** Horror told through degraded broadcast and VHS: emergency alerts, instructional tapes, timestamps, and wrong
faces.
- [Analog horror, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analog_horror)
- [Know Your Meme: Analog Horror](https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/analog-horror)
- [Know Your Meme: Local 58](https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/subcultures/local58): Kris Straub, usually credited as the origin
- [Dread Central: Local58 and the birth of analog horror](https://www.dreadcentral.com/editorials/534205/local58-and-the-birth-of-analog-horror)
- [Georgia Tech: "The Mandela Catalogue" and the uncanny reality of mediation](https://repository.gatech.edu/entities/publication/b26e1f62-2b28-4c91-bbf8-b980031ea435)

**2000s mall and pool nostalgia ("nostalgia bait").** Emptied consumer places from a childhood: dead malls with fountains and
skylights, hotel and indoor pools, with slowed lounge music in the air.
- [Dan Bell, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Bell): the *Dead Mall Series* on his channel, tours set to vaporwave
- [DeadMalls.com](https://deadmalls.com/): an archive since 2000, with a [dictionary](https://www.deadmalls.com/dictionary.html)
  (where "labelscar" comes from)
- [Mallsoft, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mallsoft): 1980s pop slowed and reverbed "as if from the ceiling speakers
  of an empty mall"
- [Jared Pike, Dream Pools](https://jaredpike.art/info): Blender renders of tiled pool interiors, which the Backrooms fiction
  later absorbed as the "Poolrooms"
- [POOLS by Tensori (itch.io)](https://tensori.itch.io/pools): a pool-rooms walk with no UI and no music, where the echo changes
  per room and water slows you
- [Frutiger Aero, CARI](https://cari.institute/aesthetics/frutiger-aero): the ~2004/05-2013 consumer-tech look (glossy, aqua,
  blue skies, green grass, bubbles). It is the "PC in his room" side of the period.

**Kenopsia.** The feeling, not a look: the forlorn atmosphere of a place that is usually bustling, now empty, as if the
population were negative.
- [The Dictionary of Obscure Sorrows: kenopsia](https://www.thedictionaryofobscuresorrows.com/concept/kenopsia) (John Koenig)
- [Wiktionary mirror: kenopsia](https://wiki.neonvagabond.xyz/content/wiktionary_en_all_nopic_2024-05/A/kenopsia): κενός,
  "empty"
- The design rule it gives: **show the signs of the missing people** (a lit room, chairs set out, a cart, a lane rope), never
  the people.

**The period, 2004-05** (item 70, proposed by him, not yet decided):
- **The capture devices of the time are the look's cheapest anchor.**
  - A 3-5 MP digicam: on-camera flash, JPEG blocking, a hot white balance, an orange date stamp.
  - MiniDV or a hand-me-down VHS camcorder.
  - Both are post passes, with no rays.
- **The kid's room** (items 3, 70): a CRT monitor, a beige or silver tower, an XP-era desktop with a glossy aqua UI (Frutiger Aero),
  posters, a window onto the fake world.
  - This is the one place the period should be crisp and specific.
  - The dreamzone can stay the period's *memory* of places, so it may be softer and older.
- **What the places themselves wore in 2004-05:** fluorescent T12 tubes with magnetic ballasts (still common, hence the hum and
  flicker), 1990s mall fittings, hotel carpets of the time, a computer lab of beige machines in the school.
- inferred: the analog-horror side (VHS) dates a decade earlier than the period. It suits the dreamzone as a memory, or as found
  footage. In his room, a digicam or MiniDV look fits 2004 better.

## 6. Three look directions, for him to pick

**A. "Found photo": the liminal photograph.** The dreamzone looks like someone's 2004 digicam pictures of real empty places.
- What changes first, all without new rays:
  - each biome kit's lamp colour and colour temperature (cool fluorescent vs warm tungsten), with flat overhead light and some
    lamps dead or flickering;
  - Film as the base look, with grain on, slight overexposure on lamps and windows, and the FOV narrowed toward a photo's;
  - the hum per lamp and room reverb in the audio.
- Next: textures (carpet, tile, ceiling) are what make it *recognisable*, and they need a texture path.

**B. "Tape": analog horror.** The same places seen through a failing tape that fails worse as the chase or stress climbs.
- What changes first: a third look in the F2 switch, a VHS or MiniDV pass (chroma bleed, tracking noise, interlace, a timestamp,
  4:3 option) whose strength is a dial the game can drive.
- Free in rays. It is also a natural carrier for the lore drip of item 26 (a "tape" encounter).
- Risk: it can hide the parkour readability the game needs. Keep the pass light while running.

**C. "Dream": dreamcore and the period's gloss.** Softer, brighter and stranger. The good-entity golden aura (item 26) and the rare
outside live most naturally here.
- What changes first:
  - the generator's wrong-scale rule (rooms and halls too tall and too long), plus the outside biome;
  - a brighter, pastel-leaning grade, with more bloom and lifted shadows;
  - the pool rooms and the mall as the first biomes given numbers.
- The pool rooms then want reflections, which is the costly item. Price it before promising it.

**My lean:** A first, with B as the dial that rises with danger.
- A gives every biome its identity using what the renderer already has.
- B is cheap and turns the stress system into something you see.
- C's wrong scale is a generator rule worth taking whichever he picks.
- He chooses; this is only the order I would build in.
