## Wilson AM, Lowe JC, Roskilly K, Hudson PE, Golabek KA, McNutt JW (2013) Nature 498:185-189. FULL TEXT READ (main article and print Methods).
- **Deceleration and acceleration.** "Successful hunts involved greater deceleration on average (-7.5 m s-2 versus -5.5 m s-2; P < 0.05; Fig. 3d)": per-run means of peaks. "sped up by up to 3 m s-1 and slowed by up to 4 m s-1 in a single stride." Fig. 4b caption: "Horizontal lines represent acceleration and deceleration of 13 m s-2, equating to the proposed grip limit of 1.3."
- **Lateral (centripetal).** "maximum lateral (centripetal) accelerations often exceeding 13 m s-2 at speeds less than 17 m s-1 (Fig. 4e, f; polo horses achieve 6 m s-2)." "The maximum centripetal acceleration observed was smaller at speeds greater than 17 m s-1... which may be behavioural in origin." "A cheetah running at 25.9 m s-1 with the maximal observed lateral acceleration of 13 m s-2 would have a turn radius of 52 m." "Slowing from 16 m s-1 to 4 m s-1... would drop the turn radius... from 19.7 m to 1.2 m": a **hypothetical computed from v^2/r = 13, not a measured radius.**
- **Speed.** Top speed 25.9 m/s (stride-averaged; the others 25.4, 22.0, 21.1, 20.1); mean top speed 14.9 +/- 3.4 m/s held 1 to 2 s.
- **mu.** Methods: "Coefficient of friction, mu, is the maximum achievable ratio of horizontal force... with respect to vertical force... assuming that vertical and horizontal forces are always in proportion", giving a_c,max = mu g and v_max = sqrt(mu g r). Fig. 4e/f captions: "solid curved line (mu = 1.3) represents a grip limit/coefficient of friction of 1.3"; the "mu = 0.6" line is "the 0.6 grip limit reported for polo horses." The authors' own hedge: the LFL line "seems to follow the upper bound... but confident verification would, however, require stance times or limb forces during manoeuvring."

## What range of mu the reads support (subagent)
- **Read:** wild cheetahs reached stride-level lateral accelerations of about 12 to 13 m/s^2, a few higher, at speeds under 17 m/s: a minimum grip need of about 1.25 to 1.3 under the authors' a = mu g model. Horses fit 0.6 (0.7 envelope) against measured hoof static friction of 0.58 to 0.69. `inferred:` (arithmetic) impala about 1.1, lion about 0.66, zebra about 0.57.
- So **mu above 1 is supported for a clawed, light, cursorial carnivore at brief stride-averaged peaks. `inferred:` 1.3 is a ceiling-type envelope, not a typical or sustained value.**
- **Not supported by anything read:** any measured friction coefficient; any hunter-sized body at 9 to 13 m/s; any radius under about 1 m. The radii in the paper come from GPS/IMU positions with a median error after smoothing of 0.67 m.
