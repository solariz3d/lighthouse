## 1. How it was measured (the script is beside it: `measure.js`, `session.js`, `pagelib.js`)
- The **real page** (`app/index.html`, served from a checkout) in headless Chrome, never a window, at 1400x860, in a fresh profile with the look saved in `t180.settings.theme`. The page's Extend length field is set (1,000 m etc.), the Extend ghost's **turn handle** (or its **length handle**) is found on the overlay canvas by its colour, and a **real pointer press and then trusted mouse moves** (`Input.dispatchMouseEvent`, one every ~4 ms, a sine of +-250 px for the turn, +-220 px for the length) drag it for 5 s. The first form of the drag, "turning a 1000 m straight", is the turn handle on a 1,000 m piece; "stretching" is the length handle.
- Every animation-frame callback the page runs is timed (`requestAnimationFrame` wrapped before the page's scripts). A **work frame** is one in which those callbacks ran more than 1 ms: a pointer move made the page rebuild the ghost. The table is the **script time of the work frames** (median / p95 / p99), and the share of work frames over 6.9 ms and over 4.2 ms. Chrome is run with its frame-rate limit and vsync off (`--disable-frame-rate-limit --disable-gpu-vsync`), so a fast page is not hidden by a display's cap; a capped run (60 Hz limit) was also taken (section 5) and says the same.
- **Heavy-run lock:** both slots held for the whole session (this tree's and the "no git tree" bucket's), so no other heavy run ran beside it (checked each run: the lock line printed after every case shows only my two). Four of the first dozen quick runs before the session (exploring the page) were not under the lock and are not in the table.
- n = 2 runs per look at 1,000 m, 1 run per ladder step and old release (about 130 to 270 work frames each). Honest limit: one machine, one session.

## 5. (d) The browser trace: script against Paint, Composite, Raster, GPU
Chrome tracing (`devtools.timeline, cc, gpu, viz, blink, v8`) over a 5 s drag of a 1,000 m turn in Aurora (60 Hz frame limit, about 500 frames), blur on and off; and a CPU profile of the Aqua drag.
- **Renderer main thread: 4.4 s of the 5.0 s is script** (`v8.callFunction` 4,384 ms, `PageAnimator::serviceScriptedAnimations` 2.8 s); Paint and Layout do not appear in the top rows; Raster on the worker threads is 30 to 45 ms in all (`RasterizerTaskImpl::RunOnWorkerThread`). GPU process 0.4 to 0.6 s. Compositor 0.13 to 0.29 s. So **the main thread is the whole story; CSS compositing is not.**
- **Inside the script** (CPU profile of the Aqua turn drag, 4 s): the mesh and geometry builders: `src/geom/mesh.js` 10%, `profile.js` 7%, `adapter.js` 5%, `segkey.js` 4% (hashing each chord), `path.js` 4%, `tools/piecewise.cjs` 4%, `document.js` 3%, the renderer's upload `renderer.js` 2%, handles drawing 2%; native 50% of which "(program)" 29% and the garbage collector 4%. In node (no browser) the same per-move work measures the same: `shell.candidate` 4.8 ms + `ghostFor` 13.7 + `candidateReadout` 2.2 = 20.7 ms at 1,000 m, against 20 in the page (`nodeghost2.js`). So **what the page spends is the build of the ghost of the next piece, per move: its resolve (a turn is solved by fitting eleven channels each time), 500 chords of 2 m (a mesh row per 2 m), and the numbers readout, which asks for the same piece again.**
- Mesh bytes never cross IPC (ARCHITECTURE section 9: nothing in a drag does).

## 6. What it says, plainly
- The keeper is right: **a 1,000 m piece is not smooth to drag, on a very fast machine (about 50 fps), and 2,000 m is 25 fps.** It has been like this since at least 0.3.6.
- The cost is **the ghost's own geometry, rebuilt in 2 m pieces on every pointer move**; it grows in proportion to the piece's length. It is **not** the frosted glass, not Aurora's animation, not the skins, not paint or raster.
- Ordinary pieces (up to 2,000 m) can be made to hold the refresh rate by building the drag's ghost from far fewer chords. That is the fix hand-back.

### What the quiet numbers say (against the keeper's 165 Hz display: 6.06 ms; the librarian's bar 6.0 ms; 6.9 ms for 144 Hz; 4.2 ms for 240 Hz)
- **Before D404 (v0.4.7), every look:** turn drag at 1,000 m median 18.9 to 19.3 ms, p95 25 to 26 ms; at 2,000 m median 38 to 39, p95 47 to 50; length drag 8.7 to 9.4 and 18.4 to 18.7 ms medians. - **After D404 (d404-a):** median 2.4 to 3.1 ms in every look and case; **p95 at 1,000 m 3.5 to 4.1 ms (turn) and 3.7 to 4.1 (length); at 2,000 m 3.8 to 4.3 ms (turn) and 4.3 to 4.8 (length): inside 6.0 ms in all 20 cells with at least 1.2 ms to spare.** p99 reaches 5 to 7.1 ms at 2,000 m (a few frames in 100 are over 6.0). The 240 Hz bar (4.2 ms) is not met at 2,000 m.
- **The blur and Aurora's animation, quiet:** with them off the drag costs the same (differences of 0.1 ms at the median, within the 0.1 ms run-to-run spread): not the cause. What they do cost is off the main thread: **compositor +0.25 ms a frame (0.41 on, 0.16 off), GPU flush +0.40 ms a frame (1.11 on, 0.71 off)** in Aurora; Glass's blur costs +0.25 ms in the compositor (0.41; its flush 0.61 on). Together about 0.65 ms of a 6.06 ms frame in Aurora on a GPU this fast: 11%. It is not what made the drag laggy, and it is the next place to look at 240 Hz or a slower GPU.
- **D399's textures (fc8c445), the chair's condition for 0.4.8: add nothing measurable.** Script time, v0.4.7 + fix against fc8c445 + fix, turn handle, ten cells: median difference 0.00 to 0.03 ms (one cell -0.07), p95 difference -0.37 to +0.10 ms, against a run-to-run sd of p95 of 0.10 ms (max 0.21).
