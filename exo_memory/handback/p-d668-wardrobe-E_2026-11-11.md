## 1. The style guide: what mid-2000s teen clothing looked like (for A's eye) [MINE, from the period]

**Silhouettes:**
- **wide below, loose above:** baggy or carpenter jeans, wide skate shoes, oversized tees;
- or **slim and layered:** a fitted tee over a long-sleeve, a zipped track top, slim dark jeans, the "emo" cut late in the period.

**Layering:** a tee over a thermal; an open flannel or zip hoodie over a tee; a vest over a hoodie.

**Surfaces:**
- heather grey, stone-wash and dark-rinse denim;
- screen-printed graphics, cracked with washing;
- ribbed cuffs, mesh panels on sportswear.

**Colours:**
- the period's palette: navy, charcoal, olive, burnt orange, mustard, dusty red, sky blue;
- black with one bright accent;
- pastel only on sportswear trims.

**Details that read 2005:**
- wallet chains, studded belts;
- carabiner key clips and lanyards;
- a single sweatband, wristbands;
- trucker caps worn high, beanies worn low;
- big over-ear headphones or white earbud wires;
- digital watches;
- puffer vests, cargo pockets, zip-off trousers.

**The rule for the art:**
- every graphic is ours (his moth, invented band names, abstract shapes);
- **no real brand, band, team or logo**;
- a parody brand's mark is an original shape that only *rhymes* with the period.

## 5. The rub-off progression [HIS mechanic; numbers and tables MINE]

**When:** at the end of every run. Each worn store item makes one roll.

**The chance:**
- 5 % at the start, plus 2 % per full minute survived;
- ×1.5 on a won run (the timer survived, item 66);
- capped at 30 %.
- A 10-minute won run gives each piece a 30 % roll.

**What it does, if it hits:** the item rises **one rarity**, and either:
- (a) its slot buff steps up, or
- (b) it gains a **quirk** in a free quirk slot.

It is 50/50 while a quirk slot is free; (a) only when the slots are full.

**Quirks: small, one stat each (Cyberpunk-like), drawn from the slot's table** [MINE examples]:
- **Shoes:** *Light step* (−5 % footstep noise), *Grip* (−5 % speed lost on a corner), *Spring* (+3 % jump height);
- **Legs:** *Long reach* (+5 % ledge grab reach), *Knees* (−10 % landing stun);
- **Top:** *Second wind* (stamina refills 5 % faster below 20 %);
- **Outer:** *Collar up* (−5 % stress from the hunter's sound);
- **Head:** *Ear for it* (the D666 alert tell sounds 10 % louder);
- **Glasses:** *Night eyes* (+5 % sight in dark pools);
- **Over:** *Deep pockets* (+1 trinket storage, no stat);
- **Neck:** *Charmed* (+2 % chest chance in the Park).

**Story quirks, some with a story (Darkest Dungeon-like): earned from what happened in that run** [MINE]:

| quirk | earned when | does |
|---|---|---|
| *Hospital-cold* | ran over 60 s of the run in the hospital | −5 % stress gain in the hospital biome |
| *Seen by three* | the pack's heads turned to him and he lived | the pack's alert is 0.2 s slower on him |
| *Smells of rain* | crossed the rare outside | +5 % luck outside |
| *Parked* | used 3 Parks in one run | +0.5 s before a Park's erosion starts |
| *Recognised* | reached Recognition in the run (items 60-62) | resonant only (below) |
| *Close call* | the hunter within 2 m and he escaped (A's 1.4 m and 2.3 m) | +3 % speed for 5 s after a near catch |
| *Mother's mend* | worn on the morning his mother's note mentions the patch | −3 % stress in the room |

- **Resonant** (the top tier) is reached only on a run that hit **Recognition**: the item gains the *Recognised* trait, a unique,
  stronger buff per slot.
  - It ties the wardrobe's top tier to the story's centre (item 107).
- **A negative quirk** (Darkest Dungeon has them), [MINE, his call]: on a run lost to a catch, a 10 % chance of an *afflicted* quirk
  (−3 % in its stat).
  - Cleared by washing it (the room's washer, a small cost in cash), so a bad run's mark can be taken off.

**Determinism:**
- every roll is a salted hash of (the run's seed, the run's record fingerprint, the item's unique id, the roll's purpose);
- so a replayed run gives the same rub-offs, and the save records them;
- the world never changes with a player's clothes (one seed, one world).

**Where they come from:**

- found items (marked "story" above) appear in their biome as rare finds;
- trinkets stay the strong buffs and are never clothing.

## 6. Hair under hats [HIS: "haircuts still change underneath the hat, making the hair bend in ways it should"]

**Hat classes** [MINE], six by how they press the hair:
1. **none**;
2. **beanie or watch cap:** pressed flat on the crown, spilling at the sides and nape;
3. **cap or visor:** flattened under the band, fringe pushed down or tucked, back spilling;
4. **bucket or flat cap:** crown flattened, sides pressed out;
5. **headband or bandana:** a ring pressed in, the hair puffed above it;
6. **hood:** the hair pressed and partly hidden. Hood-up tops count.

**How it is built:**
- each haircut mesh has **one shape key per hat class** (a morph target), modelled in Blender by pressing the hair under each hat's
  inner volume;
- in glTF, morph targets are standard, and cgltf reads them;
- at runtime the current hat's class sets its shape key to 1; a change of hat blends over about 0.2 s;
- the hat itself sits on the head bone; the hair mesh is skinned to the same head.
- **12 cuts × 6 classes = 72 shapes**, made once in the Blender pipeline (D659 models §2, a hair step after step 3).
- **First person:** hair and hat are both in the head's ray group (C's 0x04). They cast shadows; they never fill the view.
