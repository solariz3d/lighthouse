## The falsifiers (the plan's, kept verbatim; any one firing is reported as fired, not tuned away)

- **F1, certainty:** on 20 seeds x the three bot policies, with `run_length_s` set so overtime begins, **any** bot survives overtime past **90 s**.
- **F2, skill:** the flee policy's median overtime survival is **not** at least 25% longer than the straight-line policy's.
- **F3, the band:** the flee policy's median overtime survival is outside **15 to 45 s**.
- **F4, the clear:** a lesser chaser or the hunter is within the player's view after `overtime_clear_s`, or ends a run in Overtime.
- **F5, nothing before:** any existing chase row, fingerprint or streamed result changes with `run_length_s` = 0.

## Registered here, before any overtime sweep runs

**What is measured.** `dz-chase sweep --run-length T --max 400 --levels 1 --policies flee,line,juke --seeds 20 --first <s> --threads 2` (the three bots the plan names are flee, the straight-line policy `line`, and `juke`; `cutback` is not one of them), the table "overtime" it prints: runs that reached the timer, the median and 90th percentile of the overtime survived, how many outlived 90 s, how many are still alive at the stop, how many ended saved. T = 30 s (the timer is short on purpose: the run before it is not what is measured, and a bot caught before 30 s is not a run that reached overtime; the table reports how many reached it). Two disjoint blocks: seeds 1 to 20 (the tuning block) and 21 to 40 (the held-out block).

**The dials at first: the defaults the interface commit set (`dark_speed_start` 1.35, `dark_speed_rise` 0.02, `dark_spawn_m` 40).** F1 to F5 are read at them first and reported as they come.

**Registered expectations (inferred, from the closing speed alone):** the dark one starts about 40 m off at 1.35 x the reference speed (10.4 m/s at a 7.7 m/s reference, inferred) and gains 0.154 m/s each second; a runner at 8 m/s is closed on at about 2.4 m/s at first, so the straight-line count is about 15 to 20 s before corners and walls, less with them. My chance that the defaults leave F1 unfired: 90 %. That F2 is met (flee at least 25 % longer than line): 45 %. That F3 is met (flee median in 15 to 45 s): 45 %. That all five hold at the defaults: about 15 %. I expect to need the tuning rule below.

**The tuning rule, written before the first sweep (the plan says "A picks the values to meet the bands"; this is how, so that no value is chosen after a run).** The dials' present maxima equal their defaults, so tuning upward needs the maxima raised: `dark_speed_start` max 1.35 -> 3.0 and `dark_speed_rise` max 0.02 -> 0.1 (limits only; defaults stay). The grid: start in {0.9, 1.1, 1.35, 1.6} x rise in {0, 0.02, 0.05}, `dark_spawn_m` fixed at 40. Each of the 12 cells is run on the tuning block (seeds 1 to 20). The pick: **among cells that meet F1, F2 and F3 on the tuning block, the one with the smallest `dark_speed_start`, then the smallest `dark_speed_rise`.** The pick is then run once on the held-out block (seeds 21 to 40) and F1 to F3 are read there; **a failure on the held-out block is reported as a failure, and no second grid is run on it.** If no cell meets all three on the tuning block, that is reported (with the table) and the defaults stay; a different grid is a new registration. Why smallest: a gentler dark one that still meets "certain but skill lasts longer" leaves the room to raise it, and the other way round leaves none.

**Abuse condition (named in advance):** "it is close enough after the fact" is the degenerating move. A cell that misses a band by a second is a cell that misses it.

**The unwelcome outcomes, said in the words that would make them true:** (a) F2 fires at every cell: overtime survival does not depend on skill in this chase, only on the dark one's speed; the "you can try your best to outrun it" he asked for (item 66) is then not built, whatever the numbers. (b) The dark one is not out of sight at its first position in some runs (the far-point search found no out-of-sight site and fell back to the best one it had): counted and reported, not hidden. (c) A leaver in view past the clear (F4).

**Open flag, not fixed here (it is a design call):** the dark one cannot climb (`built=false`, and `body_tests.cpp:71` pins the Crawler as the only climber), and in overtime no climber is sent, so a player on a ledge nothing can walk to earns the overtime rate with no save for as long as they stay. Whether that is wanted is his or the chair's to say.
