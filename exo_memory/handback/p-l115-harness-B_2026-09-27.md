## 1 · THE RUNNER — `node claimrec.js readers --in <reply dir> --out <reader dir>`

- **One fresh `claude -p` per reply file** (`*.md`/`*.txt`; the id is the file name without its extension).
- **The prompt is E's §2 exactly:** the ask, a blank line, `---`, a blank line, the reply. The ask is the plan's verbatim (sha256 `07e7f855…`, recorded in the manifest).
- **The prompt goes in on STDIN,** so a long reply never meets the Windows command-line limit or any shell's quoting.
- **Isolation per call** (E §8; the manifest records the flags):
  `-p --output-format json --no-session-persistence --tools "" --strict-mcp-config --mcp-config <mcp.json> --setting-sources project --settings <settings.json>`
  - `mcp.json` = `{"mcpServers":{}}`;
  - `settings.json` = `{"disableAllHooks":true,"claudeMdExcludes":["C:\\Users\\zackn\\.claude\\CLAUDE.md","C:/Users/zackn/.claude/CLAUDE.md"]}`;
  - **both are FILES**, written to a temp dir outside every call's cwd, so PowerShell 5.1 has no inline JSON to strip;
  - each call runs from a **fresh empty temp cwd, deleted afterwards**, and the runner records whether the call left anything in it.
- **Outputs:**
  - `<id>.reader.txt` (the answer) and `<id>.reader.json` (the whole CLI output);
  - `run-readers.json`, holding:
    - `claude --version` **before and after**, and a `versionChanged` flag (§8 says a change voids the run);
    - per call: exit code, duration, model, turns, usage, cost, and bytes of stderr;
    - `transcriptsAdded`: new `.jsonl` under `~/.claude/projects` across the batch.
- **No token handling,** as the packet says. The registration's §8 names that limit; see §4 for what I found about the exclusion.

## 2 · THE CODER — `packets` then `coders` (and `score`, for the librarian only)

- **`packets --in <reply dir> --readers <reader dir> --out <packet dir>`.** Per id, it builds:
  - the reply split into numbered units (`units.js`, E's §4 rules);
  - the reader's answer parsed into numbered statements (every list item or `>` line, with its quoted spans);
  - **E's §3 step 1**, the mechanical mapping (normalised: whitespace collapsed, `**` removed, quotes unified, `…`/`...` as a gap, ellipsis parts of ≥ 6 words);
  - the coder prompt and its sha256.
  
  **The key is not an input to any of it.**
- **The coder prompt, verbatim:**
  ```
  Below is a text split into numbered units, then a list of statements someone extracted from it.

  For each listed statement, give the unit number(s) it quotes or clearly refers to; say NONE if it matches no unit.
  Answer with one line per statement, exactly in the form "S<k>: U<n>, U<m>" or "S<k>: NONE", and nothing else.

  UNITS
  U1: …
  STATEMENTS
  S1: …
  ```
  - **The second line is E's §3 instruction word for word.** The third, the answer format, is **my addition**, so a script can read it. It is not in E's text, and A or E can refuse it.
  - The prompt never contains the key, the census or the word "wrong". A test pins that.
- **`coders --packets <dir>`:** one fresh `claude -p` per packet, with the reader's isolation. It writes `<id>.coder.txt/.json`, `run-coders.json`, and `<id>.map.json`:
  - per statement: the mechanical units, the coder's units, and the **final** units (the mechanical mapping where it found a unit, the coder's otherwise, so step 2 covers "anything step 1 leaves unmapped");
  - the flagged units and **COST = flagged ÷ total units** (§4).
- **`score --packets <dir> --key <key.json>`** is for the librarian **after** coding. It takes `{"<id>": [key unit numbers]}` and gives, per id, HIT (flagged ∩ key ≠ ∅), COST and §4's chance term 1 − C(n−k, f)/C(n, f). It also prints the hit rate, the expected chance rate and LIFT.

**THE DEVIATION FROM THE PACKET, named.** The packet says the coder "marks HIT or MISS … once the librarian supplies the wrong claim's quote". **E's registration §3 says the opposite:** the coder "never sees the key", maps statements to units, and "the librarian unmasks". I built E's version, because the packet also says the coder must match E's registered HIT rule, and E's rule keeps the key out of the coder entirely. `score` is where HIT/MISS appears, from the librarian's key and never the coder's. If the chair wants the quote-in-hand coder instead, it is a different prompt; say so and I'll add it.

**And an ownership overlap.** E's §8/§9 table gives "the mechanical mapping script and the coder prompt" to **A**, "hashed before the readers run". This builds both. A can adopt them as they are, with the hashes in §0 and in each packet, or replace them. Either way they are hashed before the readers, as §9 requires.

## 5 · FOR E TO RULE BEFORE L116 — a reader's list can contain statements it says NOT to check

- **In the dummy:** the reader listed two predictions under "These are predictions rather than facts, so they can't be checked in a file" (S9, S10) and quoted an offer it said needs no check.
- **What the parser does:** it counts every list item as a listed statement, so S9 and S10 count as FLAGGED (U2, U4).
- **It also counts** nested sub-bullets (S5–S7) as statements of their own. That is harmless here, since they map to their parent's unit.
- **Why it matters:** on this dummy it changed nothing (U2 and U4 were flagged by S2 and S8 anyway). On real replies, **it inflates COST and lets a hit come from an item the reader called uncheckable.**
- **The parser cannot tell these apart mechanically:** readers format freely.
- **Two options, both E's call, not mine:**
  - (a) the coder is also asked to mark each statement CHECK or NOT-CHECK, from how the reader's text presents it;
  - (b) accept "listed" as literal, and report the excluded-but-listed count beside COST.
