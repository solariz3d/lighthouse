## 1. The glTF path in this renderer

**What is there now** (read, `render/renderer.h`, `render/src/renderer.cpp`, `render/shaders/dz.hlsl`, `core/scene.h`, `game/body.h`):

| piece | today | file:line |
|---|---|---|
| uploaded meshes | `Mesh` = positions, normals, indices, one material. **No UVs**, no weights | `core/scene.h:54-60` |
| GPU vertex | `GpuVertex { pos[3], normal[3] }`, 24 bytes; normals interpolated by barycentrics at the hit | `renderer.cpp:125`, `dz.hlsl:160-169` |
| material | one flat albedo and emissive per material id (128 max); no textures | `renderer.h:43-48` |
| moving things | `UploadScene` once, then `SetInstanceTransform` per instance per frame. The top-level structure is rebuilt in the frame's command list | `renderer.h:181-187` |
| the body now | posed boxes, one instance per part, moved every frame (`stage.cpp:174`). The pose is **procedural**: 21 joints in world space (`Joint` Eye…ToeR) hung from the camera | `game/body.h:4,37-44` |
| chasers now | box figures, one instance per part (`stage.cpp:158-164`) | `game/world/figures.cpp` |
| per-instance motion for TAA | `GpuMotion` per instance (rigid transforms only) | `renderer.cpp:128-129` |
| ray masks | primary rays trace mask 0xFF. Shadow rays use a light's `shadowMask`, so instance groups exist | `dz.hlsl:54,114,142,206` |
| opacity | every query is `RAY_FLAG_FORCE_OPAQUE`: **no alpha test**. Hair cards or leaf cards would need an any-hit path | `dz.hlsl:108-109` |
| descriptors | binding sets of 4 SRV + 4 UAV per pass; no texture table | `renderer.cpp:210,582-610` |
| streamed chunks | merged, compacted, flat-shaded per triangle. **Untouched by models** | `renderer.cpp:773-896,1108-1185` |

**What it takes, in four parts:**

1. **Loading glTF (CPU).**
   - Recommended: **cgltf** (single header, C99, MIT; meshes, skins, animations, morph targets).
   - **It is a new external dependency, so it needs his or the chair's yes before C adds it** (the global rules). The
     alternative, writing our own glTF JSON reader, is more code for the same thing.
   - The loader fills an extended `Mesh`: `uv0`, `joints` and `weights`, a texture index per material. Malformed files are
     refused whole, as `UploadScene` already does (`renderer.h:180-182`).

2. **UVs and a texture path (GPU).**
   - Widen `GpuVertex` to carry `uv` (24 → 32 bytes). The shader's hit already has barycentrics (`dz.hlsl:163`), so the UV is the
     same interpolation as the normal.
   - Textures go in one **bindless SRV table** (an unbounded `Texture2D` array). The material record gets texture indices
     (albedo, normal, roughness for later). Chunks stay flat until phase 3.
   - **Mip choice without derivatives:** a ray tracer has no screen-space derivatives, so pick the mip by **ray cones** (the
     cone's width at the hit against the triangle's UV density). It is a few ALU per hit.
     - GI-bounce hits take a coarse mip, or the material's average colour.
   - **Formats:** BC7 (albedo) and BC5 (normals), made offline. That is one more tool (Microsoft DirectXTex `texconv`, MIT).
     **Needs the same yes as cgltf.** PNG in memory would work for the proof but costs 4× the memory.

3. **Skinning with the existing acceleration structures.** Two ways:
   - **(a) Rigid segments.** Cut the mesh by its dominant bone into parts. Each part is an instance moved by
     `SetInstanceTransform`, exactly as the boxes are.
     - **Zero new GPU code.** TAA motion already works per instance.
     - Seams at elbows and knees, hidden with overlapping caps.
     - Good enough for **chasers** (never seen sharp, item 78), and for a first proof.
   - **(b) Compute skinning plus a bottom-level refit.**
     - A compute pass writes skinned vertices each frame.
     - The body's bottom-level structure is built once with `ALLOW_UPDATE` and refit each frame with `PERFORM_UPDATE`.
     - The vertex pass also writes last frame's skinned position per vertex, because TAA needs per-vertex motion on a deforming
       mesh. Without it, limbs ghost.
     - **This is what the kid needs:** the first-person view sees his arms and legs up close, where (a)'s seams show.
   - **Driving the kid's bones:** from the game's 21 procedural joints, not from clips.
     - A fixed table maps each `Joint` to the rig's bones. Bone rotations come from the joint chain each tick.
     - So the body stays procedural: item 27's hands-on-walls is the same IK, and the camera is untouched.
     - Clips are for the chasers and the room.

4. **First person: the head.**
   - The head joint stays the eye (`body.h:4`; the camera test `the_head_is_where_the_camera_is`, `p-l161-body-C` :28-35).
   - The kid's head and hair go in their own instance group. **Primary rays skip that group; shadow rays keep it.** That needs
     one mask bit in the primary ray (today 0xFF, `dz.hlsl:114,142`).
   - The body's build must match `BodyBuild` (1.8 m figure, eye at 1.65 m, `body.h:67-70`), or the camera height moves. So the
     kid's model is fitted to these lengths, not the other way round, unless he chooses to change the kid's height (that would
     change the camera; ask him).

**Frame-cost risks against 2.78 ms p99** (my L161 figures: the worst building views 2.8-2.9 ms, all in the trace; `render/CHANGES.md:153-185`):
- **Texture sampling in the trace** is the biggest risk. Every primary and every bounce hit on a model samples. Keep the textured
  area small (models only, chunks flat), and bounce hits take a coarse mip.
- **Refits:** one per skinned body per frame. inferred: ~20k triangles refits in a fraction of a tenth of a millisecond on this
  class of GPU. Several pack bodies multiply it. Cap the skinned bodies (e.g. the kid plus the two nearest chasers skinned, the
  rest rigid).
- **Triangle count** barely moves the trace (a BVH is logarithmic), but the motion vectors and the per-frame top-level rebuild
  grow with instance count. Rigid chasers add instances.
- **No alpha** (FORCE_OPAQUE): hair is modelled as opaque mesh, not cards. An any-hit path would slow every ray.
- **The render scale of D653 step 3** (trace below screen size, upscale through TAA) is what buys the room. C should measure the
  model path with and without it.
- **inferred, all of the above:** not timed. The proof's job is to time it on D (packet: "frame cost on D against 2.78 ms").

## 2. A headless Blender 5.2.3 pipeline (Python only inside Blender, item 83)

**Runs as** `blender.exe --background --factory-startup --python art/tools/<step>.py -- <args>`, never a window, never the
launcher. Each step is one script that writes its output and a preview. A pane looks at the preview (PNG) before the next pass.

| step | script | does | output |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | `probe.py` | prints `bpy.app.version_string`; checks the glTF exporter (`bpy.ops.export_scene.gltf`) and Rigify are present; checks MPFB is installed | a log |
| 1 | `human.py` | **MPFB** (the MakeHuman plug-in for Blender, extension 2.0.18, 10 Oct 2026, "Blender 4.2 LTS and newer", GPL code, CC0 system assets). Makes the base from parameters (age, build, proportions), then scales so the eye stands at 1.65 m and the limb lengths match `BodyBuild` | `kid_base.blend` |
| 2 | `rig.py` | MPFB's game-engine skeleton, plus a JSON map from its bones to the game's 21 `Joint`s (and to Quaternius' universal rig for the chasers' clips) | `kid_rig.blend`, `joint_map.json` |
| 3 | `dress.py` | clothes and hair from MPFB asset packs, **CC0 ones only** (each pack's licence read; some third-party packs are CC-BY); hair as opaque mesh | `kid_dressed.blend` |
| 4 | `bake.py` | Cycles bake: normal (from the subdivided body to the game mesh), AO, curvature, at 2k. Materials from ambientCG and Poly Haven fabrics (CC0) | `*_albedo.png`, `*_normal.png`, `*_rough.png` |
| 5 | `retarget.py` | chasers only: Quaternius Universal Animation Library clips (CC0) onto each chaser's rig. Copy-rotation constraints through the bone map, then baked to keyframes (no add-on needed) | actions in the `.blend` |
| 6 | `preview.py` | EEVEE renders: front, side, three-quarter, and a 12-frame turntable strip, plus one frame per clip. Neutral grey light, so the model is judged, not the lighting | `previews/*.png` |
| 7 | `export.py` | `export_scene.gltf(export_format='GLB', export_skins=True, export_animations=True, export_yup=True)`, metres, textures alongside | `assets/models/<name>.glb` |

- The truer preview is the game's own: the renderer's offscreen `Capture` (`renderer.h:231`) of the imported GLB, in the
  apartment's light. C's proof adds a model-viewer capture beside the Blender previews.
- **inferred, to check at step 0:** that 5.2.3 still ships the glTF exporter and Rigify, and that MPFB installs from its zip
  offline (`bpy.ops.extensions.package_install_files`).

**The kid (item 82: "his persona and identity… doesnt matter to me how he looks… as detailed as you can go").**
- His look is the room's choice. He is a boy in his mid-teens in 2004-05 (item 70's period, not decided): a hoodie, jeans, worn
  sneakers.
- Pass 1: body and proportions in-game, driven by the 21 joints.
- Pass 2: clothes and baked maps.
- Pass 3: the face for the rare moments it shows (item 78: the pick screen, a camera turned round, waking in bed).

**The chasers (item 78: "the entities stay the things never seen sharp").**
- Proposed silhouettes, mine, to be his or the room's choice. Each is read from its behaviour (`chase/archetype.h:18-24`):
  - **hunter:** too tall, too-long forearms, a slight forward lean, always upright;
  - **sprinter:** lean and long-legged, the torso pitched forward like a runner off the blocks;
  - **interceptor:** broad, side-on, the head turned toward where you are going;
  - **pack:** small, several alike, a little hunched;
  - **charger:** low and heavy, shoulders above the head line.
- **Never seen sharp:**
  - detail lives in the silhouette and the motion, not the texture;
  - materials are dark and low in contrast, with no fine maps;
  - a soft edge is made by the haze and the D653 dial, not by the model.
- None copies an existing creature. Each is built from a primitive base plus a stretched human rig, not from any known
  monster's design.
