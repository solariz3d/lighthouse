## 1 · THE RULING ON (1) — the chair's guess is accepted in the field and CORRECTED in the number

**Accepted:** `--entry ring`, and the initiator naming the seat that carried it.

**Refused:** *"the guess/map number recorded as ABSENT rather than zero."* The first half is right and
was already built; the second half would have deleted a real measurement. Detail in §1.3.

### 1.1 What was considered and rejected, because the alternatives are the argument

- **"A ring lap should not be a lap at all — the ring is already more `--stage` rows on the same
  lap."** True, and it is what L029 does: three `dispatched` rows, one lap, which is the ring
  turning again on the SAME inquiry and needs nothing. **L033 is not that.** It is a DIFFERENT
  inquiry, discovered inside the loop by the librarian. Refusing to open it makes work that happened
  unrecordable, which is the worse of the two failures. So the ledger records *more*, not fewer —
  and the packet's permission to rule the other way is declined with the reason stated.
- **A separate `--ring` flag, or `--entry` optional when the initiator is not human.** Optionality is
  refused for the reason `--guess` and `--entry` are both required already: a legitimate state must
  be SAID, never arrived at by omitting a flag. A second flag is more surface for one fact.
- **`--entry none`, matching this file's own `--guess none` / `--paths absent` idiom.** This was the
  close call. Refused on one ground, and it is the ground the packet named as the part to get right:
  in a report someone skims, `none` and `not recorded` are one word apart, and those two ARE the
  done-vs-never-started pair. `ring` cannot be misread as `?`. Word chosen for the reader, not the
  enum.

### 1.2 Why it is a value of THIS field and not a new one

The objection I started from — `entry` means *which door the USER came in by*, and `ring` is not a
door — is the same objection `chain()` already documents at length against putting a chain row in
`stage`. It does not survive contact with the report: the section's question is *how did this lap
start*, and **the ring is an answer to it.** What the objection does correctly demand is that `ring`
never be folded into either door OR into `not recorded`. It is printed on its own, and both
foldings are mutants (`RING: ... folded into the not-recorded bucket`, `RING: ... counted as door
one`). Both caught.

### 1.3 The correction to the chair's guess, and it is the load-bearing part

**A ring lap may carry a genuine prior, and L033 did** — four line-numbered paths, written before any
map. A writer that forced ABSENT would have deleted a measurement that existed.

What the chair actually wanted is that an EMPTY guess on a ring lap read as *inapplicable*, not as a
missed seal. **That is a reader's job and the machinery is already there**: it is exactly how the
report already separates a direct entry from a chair that failed to seal. So the report gains

    ring laps with no guess: N of M (...) - these read "no guess - ring lap", not a missed seal.
    INAPPLICABLE, never zero.

and a ring lap WITH a prior scores normally. Both halves are asserted:
`RING: a ring lap with no guess reads INAPPLICABLE, never a missed seal` and
`RING: a ring lap MAY carry a real prior - the writer does not force it absent`.

### 1.4 IMPACT ON THE TWO DOOR-TWO FALSIFIERS — asked for explicitly, and RUN rather than claimed

Both are read by `node consonance/tools/lap-row.js --report` off these rows.

**FALSIFIER (a direct-entry lap whose guess is sealed after the map).** Read as the fresh-map-floor
line over the door-two set. `ring` adds no member to that set and removes none: every `entry:"lib"`
row on the live ledger (L030, L031, L032) is a genuine user direct entry.
**NO IMPACT.**

**FALSIFIER (three consecutive direct-entry laps with no guess).** The run counter iterates every
valid lap and `continue`s on anything that is not `lib`, so a ring lap neither advances the run nor
**RESETS** it — the treatment a door-one lap already gets. **NO IMPACT**, and the test builds the
same four-lap ledger twice, once with the interleaved lap as `orch` and once as `ring`, and requires
the `longest such run:` line to be byte-identical while still reading `-> FIRES`. A mutant that
makes a ring lap reset the run is caught.

**And the whole report, re-derived over the LIVE 33-lap ledger before and after, differs in exactly
four lines** — the section header, the count line, and the two lines of limit (e). Every figure —
`guessed 76 · mapped 119 · in both 45`, `59.2%`, `62.2%`, falsifier 1's `4 of 10`, falsifier 2's
`27 of 33 = 81.8% -> FALSIFIER FIRES` — is unchanged. Command in §5.

### 1.5 What the third value COSTS, printed with the number rather than defended

A lap can now be relabelled `ring` to excuse a missing guess, and `guess_seal` covers the guess only.
That is a **new hole this change opened**; it is appended to limit (e) in the report's own
WHAT THIS CANNOT DISTINGUISH block and asserted by a test.

### 1.6 What is OWED and is not this file's to decide

On the ring, the librarian's return leg precedes the chair's plan — which is **door two's hazard
exactly**. So the ring rule (the librarian rings the chair the inquiry, one line, no map, before
filing) arguably should apply on a ring lap too. **I did not widen the falsifier to cover it**: it is
registered in `brief/BUILDING.md` over DIRECT-ENTRY laps, and changing what a registered falsifier
counts is that document's call, not a tool edit. Named for whoever holds it.
