# By eye on [T2]: three short stretches (pane C, L259, for the keeper)

**What this measures.** Every class-1 count so far is a script's. This asks one question: aiming by eye at the shadow track, does a person
lay these stretches in fewer pieces than the script did? Nothing is timed. Use any field the panel has (corners, rates, turn change rows,
bank, width), undo as much as you like. Only the saved file is read, from a copy.

**The app:** T-180 as installed tonight (f3cd425, pane P's install). **The track:** [T2], the 36 km one in the Reference list. Its name
is kept out of the repo: the librarian can say it, and it is in a local note on L,
`%LOCALAPPDATA%\Temp\claude\2b46c68187c81881\0845a868-38f2-4cc2-b45a-431e0c088fb1\scratchpad\l259\KEY_LOCAL.md`.

## For each stretch, A, then B, then C (one fresh document each)

1. **File ▸ New.**
2. **View ▸ Reference track…**: pick [T2], choose **Road (its kn5)** (Line is greyed: it has no AI line), Show.
3. **Type the four nudge numbers** for the stretch (x, z, up, turn) from the table. The shadow's road then starts under your start and
   runs the way your first piece points.
4. **Lay about 60 m of lead-in** from your start onto the stretch. It is not counted; use it to match the shadow's bank and climb
   (your start is level and flat, the shadow's is not always).
5. **Lay the stretch by eye** against the shadow, and carry on about 30 m past its end (that is not counted either).
6. **Save** (Ctrl+S) as **byeye-A** (then byeye-B, byeye-C). It lands as `eq-byeye-A.t180track` in your tracks folder.
   Leave the nudge as typed. If you change it, note the four numbers you end with and pass them back with the name.

The tolerances (the study's, the same for all three): position 5 m, direction 3°, bank 5°, width 1 m, peak curvature within 20% of the
track's, roll rate within 25%. The count is your pieces whose road lies along the stretch; the lead-in and the run-out are not counted.

Other parts of [T2] may show nearby in Road mode (it draws every road surface the track has); follow the one under your start.
