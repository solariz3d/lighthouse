# p-d654-audio-C — D654: an in-depth audio system for Dreamzone 3: Bodycam, geometric audio as practised, and the map onto dreamzone-3. Pane C, on D, 2026-11-11

**Packet:** the chair's D654, sent 16:19:14Z. His word (10:18, brief item 77): "there should defintelly be an indepth audio system that works like ray traced audio or whatever, look into the work of bodycam and their sound design". Plan: `librarian/2026-11-11.md:81` (55da9f5d). Tier: just do it.

**What this is:** research plus a code read of dreamzone-3 at `39e03db` (`Desktop\dreamzone-3`, read only). No code changed, nothing built, nothing run. My D651 branch is untouched.

**The answer in short:**
- Build the acoustics on the generator's own grid, not on a ray tracer.
  - The generator already knows every room (cells of 3 m by 3.5 m, by kind), every doorway (walk-graph edges flagged `doorway`) and every biome.
  - That is the rooms-and-portals model Hitman and Wwise use, given to us for free.
  - It can be deterministic, so the same paths can drive the lesser entities' hearing inside the simulation (item 65).
- Use rays only where a grid cannot answer: how big and hard the space round the ear is (reverb), and walls between a source and the ear (transmission).
  - Use a handful of Jolt rays on the CPU, as today's occlusion already does.
  - Do not use the renderer's DXR structure.
- Steam Audio (Apache 2.0) is the fallback if real early reflections turn out to be wanted. It takes our own ray tracer through callbacks.

## 1. Bodycam's sound design: what is said about it
Bodycam is Reissad Studio's Unreal Engine 5 shooter. I found no technical talk or written interview about how its audio is built. The YouTube "Dev interview" and Devlog #2 videos turned up, but I did not watch them. So the following is what the studio's own posts and the press say it does, not how.

**The studio's words (Devlog Part 2/4, "Sound Design", via a mirror of the Steam post):**
- "Organic Reverb" "analyzes the environment in real time to generate natural reverberation". Reverb comes from the space as it changes, not from presets.
- Sound is propagated "through corridors, rooms, stairwells, windows, and open areas".
- Vertical audio is improved so a player can tell above from below.
- Footsteps are named among the sounds players locate enemies by.
- Weapons were re-recorded and remixed, with cracks, fly-bys, shockwaves and suppression added.

**The v0.8 "Locked & Loaded" update (2 September 2026), as reported by TGTV (the outlet's words, not the studio's):**
- "Sound propagation and reverb are calculated live according to the space".
- "a shot fired inside a closed room no longer sounds like the same shot out in the open".
- "Doors and windows react to sound".
- "vertical source location is sharper".
- "Footsteps change with the material underfoot".
- "Sound there is information, and sometimes it is the only information available before anything becomes visible."

**The studio's short videos (titles only; not watched):**
- "The audio transition in Bodycam from indoors to outdoors will blow your mind."
- "The Sound Actually Comes From Your Camera": the listener is the body camera's microphone.

**Players:** the game is praised for its sound design among its other qualities (Wikipedia, "Bodycam (video game)"). I found no player write-up that says what in particular.

**What makes it work, as I read those sources (inferred):**
- **The space is heard.** The same sound changes with the room it is made in and the room it is heard from: reverb taken from the real geometry, and the indoor to outdoor change.
- **Sound travels the way a person could walk.** It goes along corridors, up stairwells and through openings, not straight through walls.
- **Distance and height are readable.**
- **The floor is heard in every step.**
- **Tails and cracks give range:** a long reverberant tail outdoors against a short one in a room. I found no source on gunfire tails specifically.
- **What we should NOT take:** the camera-microphone character (clipping, the mic's colour). Item 75 rejected anything that reads as footage: "it is a dream experience". Take how sound behaves in space, not the recording medium. Nothing of its assets is used or wanted.

## 2. Geometric audio as practised

| system | what it does | cost | licence | fits a custom D3D12 C++ engine? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Steam Audio** (Valve) | Occlusion, transmission, real-time and baked reflections and reverb, pathing round geometry, HRTF. Rays traced from the listener. | CPU, linear in rays x bounces x sources (Valve's GDC 2019 talk). Baking moves it to load time; GPU via Radeon Rays (OpenCL). | **Apache 2.0** since v4.5.2 (Feb 2024), whole SDK | **Yes.** It has a C API, and its scene can be `IPL_SCENETYPE_CUSTOM` with our own closest-hit and any-hit callbacks (batched versions too), so Steam Audio builds no geometry of its own. Jolt's raycast could sit behind those callbacks. It brings its own mixing chain, which would sit beside or under our `Mixer`. |
| **Microsoft Project Acoustics** (Triton) | Wave simulation, baked for static geometry on Azure, then looked up at run time. | Bake: a cloud job per level. Run time: cheap. | Code MIT, docs CC-BY. **Discontinued 1 July 2024** (notice on its Unreal Marketplace listing) | **No.** It needs static, pre-baked levels; ours are generated and streamed. |
| **Wwise Spatial Audio** (Audiokinetic) | Rooms and portals; diffraction round portal edges mapped to obstruction; reverb per room, spatialised through portals. Reflect, a separate plug-in, does early reflections. | Rooms and portals are cheap (a graph). Reflect's cost was not found. | Per title; free indie tier under a $250k production budget. **Reflect is licensed separately** (its error says to contact sales) | Possible, but it replaces our mixer with middleware and its authoring tool. |
| **FMOD** | A Geometry API: occlusion polygons with direct and reverb occlusion values; reverb zones. No path finding round corners. | Cheap. | Indie tier (fmod.com/licensing lists under $600k budget; older figures differ). | Possible, the same trade as Wwise. Its geometry occlusion is a straight line, which is what we already have. |
| **Hitman** (IO Interactive, GDC Europe 2015, Stepan Boev) | Hand-built rooms and portals; the portal graph is walked from the listener's room to find occlusion and the way sound comes. | About 275 rooms and 800 portals in an average level (the slides). Reverb through portals is spread over CPU cores (Intel's Hitman 2 case study). | Their own | The model, not the code, is what we take. |
| **Ubisoft's Siege** (Ubisoft; Louis Philippe Dion, Game Developer deep dive) | Propagation nodes over the map; the lowest-cost path from source to listener sets where a sound seems to come from; diffraction is treated as "Obstruction"; walls break and the paths change. | A graph search per sound. | Their own | The model, again: a path search on a graph we already have. |

**The pattern across them:** the shipped games that make sound come around corners use a graph of rooms and openings (Hitman, Siege, Wwise). Ray-traced reflections (Steam Audio) are an addition for the texture of a space, and they cost CPU per ray and bounce.

## 3. Dreamzone-3 at 39e03db

**What the audio is today (checked: read):**
- **Occlusion is one flag.** `audio/audio.h:23` and `:78`: a source with no line of sight is low-passed at 800 Hz and 9 dB quieter. `game/world/sound.cpp:110` sets it with one Jolt ray from the ear to the chaser's chest, each chaser, each frame.
- **No reverb or reflections:** `audio/README.md:237`, "a corridor and a hall sound the same".
- **What the mixer has** (`audio/mixer.cpp`, 1,117 lines):
  - every sound is made in code;
  - equal-power pan, behind-dulling, distance as 2 m over the distance;
  - 16 voiced chasers, footsteps, a hunter tone, a reprieve hum and tell, cues;
  - a looped WAV bed.
- **How it runs:** in blocks on the device's thread, with no allocation in `render`. A reverb belongs here.

**Where sound rays could come from:**
- **The renderer's DXR structure: no.**
  - It is on the GPU, and its answers would come back a frame or two late.
  - Every ray would be taken from a frame that already has its worst frames over the 2.78 ms budget (D651's bench).
  - It exists only where a picture is drawn. The selftest's headless run, and any simulation of hearing, have none.
- **The sim's Jolt world on the CPU: yes, for the player's ears.**
  - `sim/world.h:118` `raycast` is already used (`sound.cpp:110`) and is not part of the fingerprint.
  - **Its reach is limited:** collision exists for 1 chunk each way (`world/stream.h:47-48`), a box of 108 x 21 x 108 m round the player. That covers the 30 to 40 m audible ranges, except near the box's edges, where a ray through an unloaded chunk would read as open.
  - inferred: a few dozen rays a frame on the game thread cost far under a millisecond; not measured.
- **The generator's grid: yes, for everything that is about rooms and openings.**
  - Cells are 3 m by 3 m by 3.5 m (`gen/src/gen.h:24-25`). Each has a kind (`:251`: Lane, Cut, Room, Landing, Stair, Shaft, Well, Hall, Void, Reprieve, ...).
  - The walk graph's edges carry their length (`:336-341`), and a route knows which edges pass through a door frame (`world/route.h:34`, `:66`).
  - Rooms as acoustic volumes: a flood of open cells bounded by doorways is a room, with its size and height known exactly. A Hall with its Void above is a tall atrium; a Lane is a long narrow corridor; a Stair or Shaft links storeys.
  - **Biomes are data** ("A biome is numbers", `gen/src/biome.cpp:3`), with a kit of materials per biome. The seven are apartment, hotel, facility, mall, pool rooms, school and chaos (`gen/src/gen.h:220-229`). Acoustic numbers go there beside the lamp numbers.

**The lesser entities' hearing today (checked: read):**
- `chase/run.cpp:912-940` `Run::sense` hears a noise if the straight-line distance is inside the kind's range for that noise. The ranges are walk 4, sprint 22, slide 26 and landing 30 m (`chase/archetype.h:64-67`), times the `lesser_sense` dial.
- Through geometry, `hear_through = 0.5` of the range is kept (`:68`, `run.cpp:939`). A wall therefore halves hearing whether it is one partition or a whole floor of rooms.
- It runs in the deterministic tick, with `det_atan2` and a `Sight` interface (`chase/run.h:37`). Whatever replaces it must be deterministic too.
- The hunter does not use it: it always knows (item 65).

## 4. What it gives the game
**Chasers heard round corners and through walls, with the right muffling:**
- For each voiced chaser, two paths:
  - the way round: a search on the walk graph from the chaser's cell to the ear's, bounded at the kind's range;
  - the way through: the straight line, with a loss per wall the Jolt ray crosses.
- **The way round sets direction:** the sound comes from the last doorway on the path, not from the chaser. Its loss grows with the path's length and with each turn at a doorway (the diffraction loss, a few dB a corner).
- **The way through** is low-passed and quieter per wall.
- Mix both, or take the louder. The binary 800 Hz flag becomes a continuous cut and level.
- What the player hears: the pack coming down the next corridor, heard from the doorway it will come through.

**Footsteps that tell distance and floor:**
- The floor under each foot: the cell's material role (slab, stair, ladder) and the biome's kit. That gives carpet against tile against terrazzo against metal stair.
- Each surface is a set of numbers for the existing synthesised step (sole noise, thump frequency, decay); no files.
- A chaser's steps use the floor under it, pass through the propagation above, and carry the reverb of the room they are made in. That is how near-and-tiled reads apart from far-and-carpeted.

**Room reverb per biome:**
- A small feedback-delay-network reverb in the mixer (made in code, like everything else in it). The number of delay lines is set by the room's size and the biome's numbers:
  - **pool rooms (item 76's tiled long reverb):** long decay, bright, dense;
  - **carpeted apartment and hotel (dead air):** short and damped high;
  - **atria:** long and dark, with a pre-delay from the height;
  - **stairwells and shafts:** a tall, ringing column.
- Two reverbs, the listener's room and the source's room, the second heard through the doorway: Hitman's and Wwise's rooms-and-portals reverb.
- **The ballast hum:** a quiet positioned tone at each lit fixture near the ear. Their positions are already gathered for the light grid (`game/world/stage.cpp`, `sync`). A dead or flickering tube buzzes differently. It is propagated like any source, so a hum through a doorway is muffled.

**The lesser entities' hearing (item 65: only they hear; the hunter always knows):**
- Replace the flat `hear_through = 0.5` with the same way-round path on the walk graph, inside the sim.
  - The player's noise reaches a chaser if the path length is inside its range.
  - A wall's way-through keeps a small share for thin partitions only.
  - inferred: one bounded search from the player's cell per noise event serves every lesser chaser.
- This is deterministic (graph distances, fixed order), so replays and the bot runs stay reproducible.
- **It changes chase behaviour:** chasers will hear round corners they could not before, and stop hearing through thick mass. It needs A's levers and the dials re-measured, and the speed model rechecked. That is why it is the last step.
- **The pairing matters for fairness:** what a lesser chaser can hear, the player can hear coming. Both use the same paths.
