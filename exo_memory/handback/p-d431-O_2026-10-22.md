## 2. The cause (file:line at 6261a22)

- **`src/core/close.js:254`** — the step's metric `winv` is diagonal: each control point is its own column, weighted alone.
- **`src/core/close.js:284-286`** — the step δ = −W⁻¹Jᵀ(JW⁻¹Jᵀ)⁻¹r is then the least-norm change *in control points*.
- **`src/core/close.js:98-100`** — at a joint the next piece's first point IS the previous piece's last, and its second is c₁ = c₀ + ρ(c_{n−1} − c_{n−2}). So a
  joint's points shape two pieces' worth of curve at the price of one, and the least-norm step loads the correction onto them. The signature is in the numbers:
  `0.02, 0.55 | 0.55, 1.06` is c₁ = 0.55 + 0.96·(0.55 − 0.02).
- The generator is `src/core/finish.js` finishToStart: it lays a level route (`:136-139`) and closes it with `close(laid, { window: the new pieces })` (`:250`),
  so the whole climb (262 m on TEST 10) comes out of that windowed step.

## 3. The fix — the windowed close takes the smallest change to the ROAD

`close.js` `gramMetric`: for a windowed close only (Finish to start, the seam tries, D242's window), W measures what a change does to each channel's curve along
the road: ∫ (Δ)² + ℓ²(Δ′)² ds over the window's pieces (the B-spline Gram matrices, through the joints' links, sampled every 2 m), ℓ = 50 m, plus 1e-9 of the
plain norm. W couples only one channel's columns, so it is one small Cholesky per channel. The rows the close must meet are unchanged; only which closing change
is taken differs. **The whole-lap close is unchanged** — `wholelap.js`, four whole-lap closes (two test laps, F1 opened, TEST 10 with its last three pieces
edited): the same sha256 of the closed document on 6261a22 and a519bb9.

Not a different spline and not a one-piece closer: the curve's representation, the pieces and their knots are as they were. Whether a change of the step's
metric falls under the plan's "a different spline fit" is the librarian's to say; I read it as not.
