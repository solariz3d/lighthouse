## 2 · THE TEARDOWN BOUND, MEASURED ON REAL SEATS — and why the wait cannot use `proc_info`

**The instrument.** `scratchpad/leave/teardown/`, a Rust harness on the app's own crates, `portable-pty =0.8.1` and
`sysinfo =0.30.13`. It spawns real `~/.local/bin/claude.exe` seats as `spawn_claude_pane` does:
- ConPTY 34×120, `--session-id <uuid> --dangerously-skip-permissions`, the same env scrub
- the master held, and the Child moved into a thread that waits on it
Then it kills every seat at once through its killer and polls each pid every ~10 ms with two probes:
- `open`: `refresh_process_specifics`, which is `proc_info`'s call
- `enum`: `refresh_pids_specifics`, which is `NtQuerySystemInformation`, now `proc_listed`
It checks with `tasklist /FI "PID eq"` at 2 s, and records what the killer returned. Busy cells first accept the scratch
folder's trust dialog, then ask each seat to run `ping -n 120 127.0.0.1` through Bash, and kill it mid-call. Scored by
`node scratchpad/leave/score.js <cell.jsonl>…`:

**What it establishes:**
1. **`proc_info` cannot be step 1's probe.** Opening a process that has exited succeeds while anything holds a handle to
   it. That is the seat's own killer (held: never, 17/17 across D and L) or, for a busy seat, its orphaned bash/ping
   children (dropped or held: never, 21/21). tasklist no longer listed any of those 38 at 2 s. As §2.2 was ruled with the
   app's existing probe, **every close with a busy seat would read NOT DONE after the full bound**, whatever the seats did.
2. **The process-list probe ends them all, fast:** 63 of 63 on L within 26.5–129.9 ms, held or dropped, idle or busy.
   So `proc_listed` (main.rs:10462) is step 1's probe, and the sessions are also dropped before the wait. Its positive
   control, that it sees a running process under its own image, is a test (§3). The first "ended" never came before
   26.5 ms, so it also saw each live seat before the kill landed.
3. **The killer's return is inverted, re-confirmed 111 of 111:** "Err(… os error 299)" on every successful kill, D and L.
   It is never read (a wiring test and mutant W3).
4. **The bound: 5 s, about 38 times L's slowest.**

                         seats were …"). run_leave carries the count through to the result file. The export still runs.

**Why it is race-free, argued rather than assumed.** A spawn either raises the count before the close sets the phase, or
after:
- **Before:** the close's wait sees a count above zero. The spawn's insert lands before the count drops, so the drain
  finds the session and kills it; at the bound it is NOT DONE.
- **After:** the spawn's phase read, which comes after its increment, sees the close and refuses.

The one sequence that could escape is a phase read that comes before the increment. Mutant Y1 builds exactly that, and
a source-order test catches it (no single-threaded test can observe the order).

A failed resume followed by resume_pane's fresh spawn is two flights. The fresh one reads the phase again, so the gap
between them is not a hole.
