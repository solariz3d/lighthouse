## THE CAUSE (measured): the adapter's roll chords, not water.js

**The mechanism:**
- `src/core/adapter.js` `toSegments` hands a road piece to src/geom as chords of `segM = 2` m, with `roll0`/`roll1` set to the phi channel at each chord's ends.
- `src/geom/path.js:103` rolls each chord by its OWN smoothstep: `phi = roll0 + (roll1 − roll0)·smooth(u/L)`.
- So **the roll rate falls to zero at every 2 m chord boundary.**

**What that does to the road:**
- The phi channel is a smooth cubic, but the road's roll becomes a staircase of S-bumps.
- Its roll-acceleration ripples at about ±0.02 rad/m² (55° over 100 m).
- That curls every line along the road that runs off the centreline.

**Why every stream lifts off:**
- A stream u metres off centre feels N/m = v²·u·φ'' + g·cosφ. At 250 km/h, v² = 4,823 m²/s².
- With the ripple, the |u|·φ'' term beats g for any stream more than a few centimetres off the centreline.
- **So all 7 streams lift off, at ANY transition length.**

`inferred:` the same ripple is in the road the mesh and the export draw. It is about 3 cm of edge height at 12.9 m off centre (0.15° × 12.9 m). I did not measure the mesh.

## THE PHYSICS THAT STAYS RED (this is not a bug)

**The note's premise, "a road that only rolls, on a straight, cannot lift water off", is wrong for any stream off the centreline.**
- At a Bloss onset φ'' = 6·Δφ/L². A stream u m off centre is on a crest where v²·u·φ'' > g·cosφ.
- 55° over 100 m at 250 km/h: that is u < −3.5 m at the onset (the falling side) and u > +2.0 m at the end (the rising side, cos 55°).
- The exact-roll run lifts off exactly those streams: u −10.62, −7.11, −3.83 near s 199, and +2.64, +4.77, +7.17 by s 272.5.
- **At 100 m these 6 lift-offs are real verdicts and must survive the fix.** From ≥ ~200 m the roll is slow enough (LONG).
