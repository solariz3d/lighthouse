## 2 · What the core does, and the choices in it

**Curve model.**
- Each segment is a clothoid in heading θ (yaw about WORLD up, k linear in s) and pitch p (kp linear in s).
- T = (cos p sin θ, sin p, cos p cos θ), with θ and p exact quadratics in s.
- Yaw is about world up on purpose: "turn left" turns left on the map, whatever the pitch, which is how a document reads.
- Position is ∫T ds by 5-point Gauss–Legendre on substeps of 0.25 m or less. There is no closed form (Fresnel
  integrals). The quadrature error is below the tests' 1e-6 m closure (inferred from the fifth-order rule; the closure
  tests pass at 3.9e-10 m).
- **Say so if the document model means frame-relative curvature instead** (the coaster convention). It is one function to
  change, `tangent`/`integrate`.

**The frame.** Rotation-minimising, by double reflection: **Wang, Jüttler, Zheng & Liu, "Computation of rotation
minimizing frames", ACM Transactions on Graphics 27(1), article 2, 2008**, in f64 on the substeps.
- The start frame is level (left = up × T).
- On a closed loop, the end frame's mismatch (`twist`) is spread linearly over s.
- The explicit roll φ (smoothstep, + = left side up) is then applied about T.
- (T, L, U) is right-handed with L = U × T: the T1 markers' left.
- `bankG` (bank relative to gravity, §3 `:56`) and `grade` are per station.
- The heartline is applied by moving the road centre down U.

**The profile.** ψ(u) is linear between samples, and X(u) = ∫cos ψ, Y(u) = sign(u)·∫sin ψ in closed form per piece.
The normal is cos ψ·U − sign(u)·sin ψ·L, so a 110° left wall faces down and to the right. ψ past π/2 is ordinary data.

**Adaptive mesh.** Stations are picked from the path's samples, greedily as far apart as all three limits allow, checked
on EVERY profile vertex:
- chord error ≤ `chordErr` (default 0.02 m);
- the vertex normal turns ≤ `maxSeamDeg` (default 1°, FINDINGS.md:24);
- the step is ≤ `maxStep` (default 4 m, FINDINGS.md:23).

Across the road, samples are placed by equalising ∫(1/maxAcross + |dψ/du|/maxSeam) du. Every road segment shares one
vertex count K, so rows connect index to index across segment boundaries.

**Cells.** Runs split at gaps, material changes, `cellLength` (default 150 m) and 65,535 vertices.
- Each cell is a dummy `CELL_n` whose matrix carries the origin, holding one mesh `1<KEY>_n` (e.g. `1ROAD_0`) with
  vertices relative to that origin.
- One ksPerPixel material per surface key, with no textures.

**Folds.** margin = 1 − kvec·q at every sample and every profile vertex. Every margin ≤ 0 is reported as
`{ s, u, margin }`, and the mesh is still built so the fold can be seen.

**A measured example.** The planar stadium fixture (100 m straights, R 30 m hairpins with 20 m clothoid ends, a 60°
half-pipe turn and a 110° wall-ride turn):
- **428.496 m, 863 samples at 0.5 m → 450 mesh stations, K = 168 across.**
- **3 cells: 18,480 / 22,848 / 34,608 vertices.**
- **0 folds.** buildPath 7 ms, buildMesh 58 ms.
- `writeKn5` writes it as a 4,241,894 B kn5.

Reproduce with the one-liner in §6.

## 5 · Against INTERFACES.md: what fits, and what I could not do

**Fits:** `buildPath(segments, { step }) → { lengthM, closed, twist, samples[{ s, seg, pos, T, L, U, kvec, roll,
bankG, grade }] }`, and `buildMesh(path, segments, { maxSeamDeg, chordErr, cellLength }) → { scene, cells[{ name, s0,
s1, vertices, origin }], folds[{ s, u, margin }] }`. The extra `maxStep`, `maxAcross` and `stats` are additions.

**Differences, said here rather than diverged silently:**
1. **Self-intersection between non-adjacent cells (the BVH, §3 `:58`) is NOT implemented.** `folds[]` holds only the
   local 1 − κ·(q·N) check. That makes validation's `self-intersection` red unreachable for now.
2. **A font change is a change of shape within one row step,** not a blend. The segment list has no profile ramp field,
   so two adjacent segments with different fonts meet in one step. Say whether the document model should ramp fonts,
   i.e. emit a blend segment.
3. **`closed: true` requires the segments to close** (position within `closeTol`, default 1e-3 m, and tangent within
   1e-6). Otherwise it throws: the connector that closes a loop is `resolve`'s job (§2 `:50-51`), not the geometry's.
4. **Pitch past ±90° (an inversion)** is supported by the curve model, but no test covers a full loop-the-loop yet.
5. **`psi` samples are interpolated linearly** between the given `u`. A font that wants a circular wall should give
   enough samples; the closed form is exact for what is given.

**What changed in the core, and why each change was needed:**

1. **`extendPath(path, segments)` grows an OPEN path from its end, in place.**
   - Everything a segment needs from the past is its start state (position, heading, pitch, T, the unrolled RMF left),
     kept in `path._end`.
   - Stations are per segment, every multiple of `step` plus each boundary: exactly the set a full build picks. So an
     extension equals a full rebuild.
   - The ONE old sample it rewrites is the open end. A boundary sample belongs to the segment it starts, so it is
     re-emitted under the new segment's roll, heartline and profile. That is the seam.
   - `buildPath` was restructured the same way. The old per-sample segment lookups were O(segments) each, so the cost
     grew with the track; the restructure removes them.
2. **`extendMesh(prev, path, segments)` redoes only three things,** and keeps every other cell as it was:
   - the samples from the old open end on;
   - the station choice from the old open end, which is a forced station in both builds;
   - the LAST cell, which the tail may continue, plus the new cells.
3. **One change to the station choice was needed for exactness.** A strip ending on a segment boundary is now judged
   with its OWN segment's profile at both ends. Before, the choice made just before the old open end could depend on the
   new segment's profile, so an extension could have picked different stations from a full rebuild. This was found in
   design, not by a failure.
4. **If a new profile needs more vertices across than the track's shared K,** every row must change. `extendMesh` then
   rebuilds in full and says so in `stats.rebuilt: 'K grew'`.
5. **The build head:** `path.head = { s, seg, pos, T, L, U }` at the open end.
   - In INTERFACES.md's naming, the dispatch's N and B are **U (up) and L (left)**. I did not add N/B aliases: the file
     explains why the frame is not named N/B (Frenet axes flip at inflections).
   - `headCamera(head, { back = 15, up = 6 })` returns `{ eye, look, up, target }`: behind along −T, above along U,
     looking along T. The distances are placeholders for the app to tune (inferred, not measured).
6. **A closed loop cannot be extended** (`extendPath` throws). Its closing twist spans the whole loop, so closing or
   reopening is a full rebuild by nature.
7. **v1 is track-only:** the core emits road cells and nothing else. No environment meshes exist in it.

**Tolerance, stated in the test file's header before the tests were written:**
- An extended path and mesh equal a full rebuild: centreline and frame within 1e-9 (f64), identical station choice,
  cells and folds, and float32 vertex data within 1e-6 m.

### The design

- **A piece is a pure function of its handles plus its start state.** Each segment gets its own station grid starting at its own start (`k·step` from the segment start), and its own end sample. No station, vertex count or cell boundary is shared across a piece boundary.
- **Per-piece mesh.**
  - Each piece has its own vertex count across (K), its own adaptive stations, and its own cells, all under 65,535 vertices.
  - Vertices are stored in the piece's **local frame** at its first sample (rows L, U, T, then origin), and the node matrix carries the placement. So a saved piece is its handles, and the mesh reproduces from them.
  - Names: `CELL_<id>_<n>` holding `1<KEY>_<id>_<n>`.
- **Seams.** Where two pieces meet, a zipper strip `SEAM_<later id>` joins A's last row to B's first row, in B's frame. It is omitted when the rows coincide within 1e-9 (same font, continuous frame).
- **`rebuildPathFrom(path, segs, g)`** regrows the path from segment g's stored start state; everything before g is untouched.
- **`sculptMesh(prev, path, segs, g)`** remeshes piece g. A later piece is **reused** when its handles match (`JSON.stringify` minus id) AND its start pitch and start gravity-bank match within 1e-12. For a reused piece only its matrix changes, which is an O(1) update: no sample is read, and its boundary rows are recomputed lazily only if a seam beside a remeshed piece asks for them. Seams on both sides of every remeshed piece are redone.
- **Consequence (a changed test, stated):** `geom_grow.test.js` had asserted that "a wider tail forces K to grow for the whole track". With per-piece K that is no longer true by design, so the assertion was removed. The same test now checks that a wider tail still extends exactly (cells equal a full rebuild). This is a changed requirement, not a weakened check.

### THE LIMIT, and the §5 escalation

With the **world-up curve model** (yaw about WORLD up, plus pitch), a piece's shape in its own frame depends on its **start pitch and start bank**. So a saved piece reproduces exactly under **translation plus rotation about world up, at the same start pitch and bank**, and not under an arbitrary placement.

Tested both ways:
- **Same shape:** a different lead-in (one piece vs three, one of them a 1/40 turn) from a different start (yaw 0.7 rad at (120, 0, −40)) gives the same shape.
- **Different shape:** a ~6.9° start pitch bends the same handles differently. The test asserts a positions difference over 1e-3 m.

- **Length, curvature and wall (ψ) edits:** mesh work is flat regardless of track length.
- **A pitch edit tilts everything after it,** so with the world-up model every downstream piece is remeshed, costing about as much as a full build.
- **Path re-placement is linear in the downstream length** for every edit (substeps + samples: (88,119 − 1,929) / 390 ≈ 221 ops per downstream piece, `checked: node -e "console.log((88119-1929)/390)"`). A 402-piece length edit took 56 ms in all against a 907 ms full build (wall time, `inferred:` a shared machine, noisy).

**Escalated (the §5 question, now with a cost attached):**
- **Option 1: track-relative curvature.** Yaw about the track's own U, so the piece frame is an RMF-and-curvature pair with no world-up term. A piece would then be invariant under ANY placement, and a pitch edit would reuse every downstream piece.
- **The cost of option 1:** the curve model changes. The "yaw about world up keeps a level turn level" property goes away, so a level turn at a start pitch is no longer level. That is a design call for the keeper or B, and I did not make it.

**Also flagged: node matrices are now ROTATED** (the rows are L, U, T). INTERFACES.md §2 describes the cell `origin` as translation-only. `validateScene` and `writeKn5` accept a rotation, and the round-trip through `kn5write.js` is exercised by the mesh scene test. **Whether AC's physics honours rotated node transforms on a road mesh is UNVERIFIED**; no AC launches, per the keeper at 12:17. If it does not, the fallback is to bake world coordinates at export: same arrays, one extra transform per vertex, and the local arrays stay the saved form.
