## THE SHORT ANSWER

- **Left 4 Dead's chasers are not clever pathers.** [source: Booth] They run cheap A\* on a nav mesh and then **"move towards a 'look ahead' point farther down path"** with local avoidance, which "superposes well with mob flocking behavior". Climbing is algorithmic, from hull traces, so the horde is "always dangerous".
- **The brutality is in where and when they are spawned, not in how they path.** Mobs of **20–30** come every **90–180 s**; **75 % come from behind**; spawning is always out of sight, keyed to "flow distance". An **intensity-driven Director** runs Build Up → Sustain Peak (3–5 s) → Peak Fade → Relax (30–45 s).
- **Flow fields** (Supreme Commander 2) pay off when **hundreds to thousands share one goal**. [inferred] One player is one goal, so a flow field toward him is the same idea at any count, but it adds little at today's count of one hunter plus a few lesser chasers.
- **They Are Billions** does the opposite: [source] "every unit computes its own paths", re-planned only when a change affects that path.
- **Vampire Survivors** uses plain seek. [source, community] enemies "walk toward the player" and are pushed apart, with no pathing at all.
- **Days Gone and World War Z:** no public account of horde pathing was found. Only counts (up to 500, about 1,000) and the shape ("flow like water"; "topple over each other").
- **Where dreamzone-3 differs most:**
  - [code] It already does what L4D does for one pursuer: A\* replanned every 0.25–0.5 s, followed with a steered, corner-braked look-ahead.
  - [code] **It lacks L4D's numbers and spawn placement:** at most **one** lesser chaser alive by default; one every **24 s**; spawned at a random bearing; no crowd separation.
  - [inferred] The soft chase is more likely a population and placement problem than a pathing one.

## 1. Left 4 Dead (his ideal)

Primary source: Michael Booth (Valve), "The AI Systems of Left 4 Dead", GDC 2009, Valve's own deck, read as text (`pdftotext`).

**How they path** [source, quoted from the deck]
- "A\* through Navigation Mesh", which "creates jagged path".
- Path optimization (collapsing nodes) was rejected as the main method: "Nontrivial CPU cost at path build time", "Paths are recomputed often in the game", "Following path directly looks robotic".
- **"Reactive Path Following":** "Move towards 'look ahead' point farther down path"; "Use local obstacle avoidance". Its goods: "(Re)pathing is cheap"; "Local avoidance handles small physics props, other bots, corners, etc"; "Superposes well with mob flocking behavior"; "Resultant motion is fluid". Its cost: "Can avoid off path too much, requiring repath". And: "Reactive Path Following is used for all actors in Left 4 Dead."
- **Climbing:** "To ensure the Common Infected horde are always dangerous, they have the ability to rapidly climb". "Climbing is algorithmic, using a similar technique to local obstacle avoidance".
  - The steps: a test for obstacles ahead; "Find Ceiling" (a hull trace up); "Find Ledge" (hull traces lowest to highest for the first clear one); "Find Ledge Height"; "Find Ledge Forward Edge"; then "Pick closest match animation from dozens of motion captured climb animations of various height increments".

**How many, and when** [source, quoted]
- "Mobs … A large group (20-30) of enraged Common Infected that periodically rush the Survivors", "Created at randomized intervals (90-180 seconds on Normal difficulty)".
- "Mob size grows from minimum just after spawn to maximum after a duration".
- Wanderers: "Common Infected that wander around in a daze until alerted". They are a count N per area, created when the area enters the **Active Area Set** and deleted when it leaves. N is zeroed "When an area becomes visible to any Survivor" and "When the Director is in Relax mode".
- **"Flow Distance":** "Travel distance from the starting safe room to each area in the navigation mesh". It answers "is this spot ahead or behind the Survivor group".
- **Placement:** "75% of Mobs come from behind, since wanderers and Special/Boss Infected are usually engaged ahead of the team". Specials use "valid area in the AAS not visible by the Survivor team". "Boomers spawn ahead, since they are slow and can't chase well".

**The Director** [source, quoted]
- "Estimate the 'emotional intensity' of each Survivor". It rises with damage, incapacitation, being pulled off ledges, and "When nearby Infected dies, inversely proportional to distance". It decays toward zero, but "Do NOT decay Survivor Intensity if there are Infected actively engaging the Survivor".
- "Build Up: Create full threat population until Survivor Intensity crosses peak threshold."
- "Sustain Peak: Continue full threat population for 3-5 seconds after Survivor Intensity has peaked."
- "Peak Fade: Switch to minimal threat population ('Relax period') … Peak Fade won't allow the Relax period to start until a natural break in the action occurs."
- "Relax: Maintain minimal threat population for 30-45 seconds, or until Survivors have traveled far enough toward the next safe room".
- "Boss Encounters NOT affected by adaptive pacing".
- Inspired by Counter-Strike: "Constant, unchanging combat is fatiguing", "Long periods of inactivity are boring".

**What makes it fair or brutal** [inferred, from the above]
- **Brutal:** the mob arrives in numbers, from behind, unseen at spawn, climbing anything. Nothing waits on a path the player can exploit: every agent repaths cheaply and steers.
- **Fair:** the Relax period is real (no new mobs, wanderers zeroed), and Peak Fade lets a fight finish first. The pressure has a shape the player can feel.

**One idea for Dreamzone:** spawn by **flow distance**, behind or ahead of the player's direction of travel, out of sight, in **groups**, with mob size rising from a minimum to a maximum. Not one chaser at a random bearing.

## 2. Flow fields: Supreme Commander 2 and Planetary Annihilation

**Supreme Commander 2** [source: Elijah Emerson, "Crowd Pathfinding and Steering Using Flow Field Tiles", *Game AI Pro* ch. 23, the free chapter, read as text]
- The problem: "each unit in Supreme Commander would move along a fixed, one-way A\* path". On collision "the existing code would stop the units and wait for the conflict to resolve, rather than rebuilding a new path". "This behavior repeats across a thousand units".
- The method: sectors of 10×10 grid squares of 1 m. Portals cross sector boundaries. A\* runs over the portal graph, then three fields per sector: cost, integration ("total integrated cost" plus "line of sight" flags) and flow (a direction per square).
- Steering: "if the agent doesn't have a valid flow field, it should steer to the next portal position. Once the agent has a flow field, it should look for an LOS flag to steer to its goal; otherwise, it should use the specified flow field direction". Blend new directions as squares are crossed.
- The payoff: "moving hundreds to thousands of individual agents across massive maps", with obstacle avoidance, flocking and physics "without the heavy CPU burden of repeatedly rebuilding individual paths". "Agents move instantly despite path complexity".
- Flow fields are cached and shared by every agent with the same goal.

**Planetary Annihilation** [source: secondary only]
- PC Gamer, on Uber's developer walkthrough: flow fields "create a sort of dynamic movement system that lets the AI cleverly bypass obstacles".
- PA's forum threads debate the term. Uber's own post was not found.

**Where flow fields beat per-agent A\*** [inferred]
- They win when many agents share one goal. One field toward the player serves them all, and every agent "moves instantly".
- They lose when goals differ per agent (an interceptor aiming ahead, a flanker), or when the map is 3D with many storeys. Our nav is Recast's polygon mesh, not a grid.
- **For Dreamzone at today's counts (one hunter plus at most a few lesser chasers):** per-agent A\* every 0.25–0.5 s is cheap enough. A flow field becomes worth it only if he wants L4D-sized groups (20–30) all converging.

**They Are Billions** (Numantian Games), his item 128
- [source: Numantian, "Development Update v.0.8.2", via DevTrackers] "every unit computes its own paths to reach its targets"; units are "truly independent" with "their own AI".
- [source: same] Paths used to be recomputed whenever anything on the map changed, causing frame drops. Now only when a change affects the path: "if a wall in their path is destroyed or built the path won't be recomputed."
- [source: same] Infected "ignore" walls unless blocking, then try to destroy them. Swarms can split into several paths, and single units leave a swarm if they "have seen or heard something".
- [source: the same update via ModDB] "ultra optimized … especially the pathfinding".
- [inferred] **Brutal by count and persistence:** a wave keeps coming. **Fair:** your walls shape the path, and a unit only peels off for something it sensed.
- **Where no developer account exists, said plainly** (the chair's 06:47:55Z addition):

  - **The unit counts** are not published by the developers. The update says only that the game "manages more units in real time" than many others. "Thousands" or "billions" is the game's framing, not a measured figure.
  - **Anti-clumping and swarm steering** are not published.
  - **Players dispute the quality:** a 2021 Steam thread says the AI fails to path properly around anything, and a 2018 thread asks how waves are determined, without a developer answer (search results).

**Vampire Survivors**

## The ideas Dreamzone could take, as options for him and A

1. **L4D's numbers and placement** [source → inferred]: raise `lesser_max` and spawn in **groups**, placed by flow distance, **behind more than ahead** (L4D: 75 %). Ahead only for slow kinds (L4D's Boomer rule).
   - A bot test: the 150-seed paired method, with flee-never-caught as the headline number (the plan's context: 52 → 62 of 96 since D664).
2. **L4D's look-ahead** [source]: steer toward a point further down the path, not the next corner, with local avoidance between chasers. That is what makes a group read as alive, and it "superposes" with flocking.
3. **L4D's algorithmic climb for every chaser** [source]: hull-trace ledge finding, so a pack is dangerous anywhere, not only the dedicated climber. [inferred] It cuts against his rule that parkour is the escape ("the entity has to route around", item 7), so it should be per kind.
4. **A flow field toward the player** [source: Emerson], only if groups grow to L4D sizes. Not needed at one-to-three chasers.
5. **They Are Billions' rule** [source]: replan only when a change touches the path. [inferred] Not needed here: our replan is already cheap and timed.
6. **Keep the Director**; it is already L4D's. [inferred] **Make the hunter respect it**, the way L4D's bosses are exempt but rare. That is a choice for him.
