## 1. The five concepts, one line each

1. **Hairpin T:** the T and the 180° turn as one stroke. The stem drops from the crossbar into a hairpin and climbs back up toward the accent dot (the car). The risk: it may read as the letters "Ti".
2. **Hairpin sigil:** the hairline ring the header already draws (`index.html:41`, the h1's `::before`), with a hairpin inside and the accent dot at the apex of the turn. It carries the in-app mark onto the taskbar.
3. **Crossover loop:** one closed circuit crossing itself on a bridge, a figure-eight tilted as if banked, with the car as the accent dot. It says "track", not T-180. At 16 px the bridge is lost and it comes close to an infinity sign.
4. **Protractor T:** a T inside a half-circle on its diameter, a protractor reading 180°, with the arc in the accent. The most literal "T-180". The risk: an umbrella.
5. **T180 monogram:** the brief's own idea, a T over "180" in DIN-like strokes with the 0 in the accent. **It fails the 16 px bar**, since the digits blur, and it's shown as the honest test.

**Each concept also has a wordmark for the `<h1>`**, shown as live text in `--display` in mock 44 px header bars, in SIGIL and each skin:
- (1) "T—180", with the dash as a short accent bar;
- (2) today's h1, with this sigil replacing the ring-and-dot;
- (3) a leading accent dot, "T-180", a hairline divider, "TRACK BUILDER" dimmed;
- (4) "T-180°", with the degree sign in the accent;
- (5) a two-line lockup, "T180" over "TRACK BUILDER".

**Brand marks:** every concept copies no existing brand mark. Each is built from a T, a hairpin, a ring, a figure-eight circuit or DIN-like digits, in the app's own tokens. The page says so per concept.
- inferred: (3) is a common circuit shape and (4) a common protractor shape; I know of no brand mark that is either. I didn't search trademark registers.

**My lean, for the librarian (the pick is the keeper's): (2) or (4).**

- (4) reads "T-180" most directly at 16 px.

## 2. How they were drawn, and the tools

- **The tools: node v24.14.1 (its zlib) and Microsoft Edge (headless), both already on the machine.**
  - ImageMagick, Inkscape, rsvg-convert and resvg are absent. `C:\Windows\system32\convert` is Windows' disk tool, not ImageMagick.
- **One list of strokes per concept** (segments, arcs, rings, dots, a polyline) writes both the SVG master and the PNGs. The PNGs come from an 8×8 supersampled rasteriser with the PNG writer `make-icons.js` uses. So the SVG and the PNGs can't drift apart.
- **Deterministic.**
  - checked: two runs → the same `sha256` over every png, svg and the page (`e87244b2…`).
- **The SVG masters, checked in a second renderer:** headless Edge drew the five SIGIL SVGs, and they match the PNG rasters by eye, including the bridge's gap in (3).
  - checked: `SC/d395/svgcheck.png`, opened.
- **At render size no stroke is thinner than 1.15 px**, so hairlines thicken at 16 px and stay hairlines at 32 px and up. The page says so. The SVG masters keep the design widths.
- **Palettes, read at `0e50b42`:**

  - each skin's `--bg` / `--text` / `--accent` from `app/themes/<name>.css`.
  - The taskbars are #202020 (dark) and #f3f3f3 (light). Those are approximate Windows 11 shades, chosen for contrast.
