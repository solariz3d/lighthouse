3. **'wall-swerve', a new AMBER** (`limits.js` `wallSwerve: { tiltDeg: 60, sideG: 1, holdG: 1 }`; `index.js`). It fires per line where three
   things hold:
   - the line's surface tilts more than 60° from level (its normal against vertical: the bank on a plain road, the wall's own angle on a
     cupped one);
   - the line's curvature in the road's plane (κ⃗·l over the margin, which is about dθ/ds·sin φ for a climb change on a wall) asks more
     than 1 g sideways at the design speed;
   - the load into the road (fN_g, without the downforce, the same load D515 compared against) is under 1 g.

   The worst figure is the sideways g. The words say the 1 g is an estimate, not a known failure.

  checked: bands.txt, h −14.7 cm at 20–25 g, then −19.4 / −19.8 / −20.1 / −20.4 / −20.9 cm from 25 to 50 g. The plateau starts in the
  25–30 g bin; inside 20–25 g the suspension is still moving.
- **40 g: supported, at the cautious end of O's 37–40 g.**
  checked: bands.txt, box over the mesh 0.7 cm at 40–45 g; 10% of frames under the mesh at 46 g, 57% at 47 g. peaks.txt, measured minus
  predicted +6.2 to +9.2 g at the ten peaks, while the p50 over all frames above 600 km/h is +0.20 g.
  inferred: the under-prediction is measured only at the peaks (56–63 g measured), so 40 g predicted ≈ 47 g measured holds only if the same
  gap holds at 47 g. Nothing in peaks.txt shows it there.
- **The wall-swerve's 1 g sideways: not supported by any measurement.**
  - D515's one case asked 3.7 g (predicted) and swerved 3.16 g (measured), and the car recovered. Nothing near 1 g was driven, so the 1 g
    sits well inside the one survived case.
  - My row 3b puts the 1 g edge at a 1.0–1.2° climb change over 100 m on an 85° wall at 819 km/h, a gentle change.
  - inferred: expect this amber on most climb changes on a near-vertical wall at speed.
  - The rule's other condition (under 1 g into the road) holds on almost any such wall: D515's straight 85° wall ran 0.1–0.4 g. So the
    sideways ask is the only part of the rule that discriminates.
  - Raising sideG to 3 g, the measured case, is the alternative. The keeper sees both in Part 2.
