## 2. What makes Night-Runners read as stylized hyperrealism

**Limits first.**
- I could not look at its images: my tools read text, not pictures.
- PCGamingWiki and NamuWiki refused the fetch (403).
- No developer interview or technical breakdown exists that I could find.
- So what follows comes from:
  - the Steam page;
  - a review that names the visual tricks ([Geek Sleep Rinse Repeat, 2024-02-29](https://geeksleeprinserepeat.com/2024/02/29/night-runners-is-for-all-those-tokyo-xtreme-racer-fans/));
  - a Steam discussion ([NRP vs Tokyo Xtreme Racer](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2707900/discussions/0/596264973809843567/));
  - [Steam reviews](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2707900/reviews/) and [Metacritic user reviews](https://www.metacritic.com/game/night-runners-prologue/user-reviews/).
- **Each trait is marked by its source.** Where I add general technique, it is marked as such.

**What the sources say it does:**
- **Low-resolution, "PS2-style" environments, with high-detail cars.** "A mixture of lower res/retro graphics to much of the
  environments… the cars have higher quality textures and a lot more detail" (review). The contrast is the trick: the eye trusts
  the detailed thing and fills in the rest.
- **Withheld detail on people.** NPCs "all have blurred faces and that lower-res PS2 look" (review). It never shows the thing the
  eye is best at catching as fake.
- **Thick atmosphere.** The page itself sells "the thick atmosphere of smog and secondhand cigarette smoke". Players mention "low
  draw distance". Haze hides the far geometry and makes depth read as air.
- **Speed blur.** "Visuals become blurred and the music becomes clear the faster you go" (discussion). Detail drops as intensity
  rises.
- **A CRT and VHS filter "on a lot of the game".**
  - This is the part he has ruled out for us. It does real work there: it unifies low-res textures into one picture.
  - We need something else to do that unifying job (below).

**inferred, general technique for this kind of look** (not documented for Night-Runners):
- texture detail taken from photographs, at modest resolution;
- light with believable colour (sodium orange, fluorescent green-white, warm tungsten) against deep shadow;
- a filmic tone curve with soft, bloomy highlights;
- a narrow palette;
- surfaces that are wet or glossy enough to double the light;
- a slightly soft overall image, so nothing is crisp enough to look "CG".

**What it leaves out** (sources plus inferred):
- crisp faces and close-up people;
- far detail;
- clean, saturated "game" colour;
- sharpness.
- inferred: the realism is mostly in the *light and the air*, not in polygon detail.

## 3. Each trick on dreamzone-3 39e03db: has, missing, cost

Cost classes, by my L161 rules:
- **free:** numbers or a post pass (~0.03 ms, by analogy with the measured tone pass);
- **ALU:** more maths inside the trace pass. Not free in the worst rooms, which already run 2.8-2.9 ms against a 2.78 ms budget;
- **new rays:** over budget unless something else is cut;
- **content:** art or a pipeline.

checked: `render/renderer.h:28-105`, `render/shaders/dz.hlsl`, `render/CHANGES.md:150-185` (1080p 0.68 ms vs 1440p 1.33 ms on
the test scene), `core/scene.h:54-60` (a Mesh has positions, normals and indices, **no UVs**), `gen/src/biome.cpp` (kit numbers
for the apartment and hotel only).

## 5. The first three build steps, one stretch of the apartment

Order chosen so each step pays for the next, and so he sees a change at every step.

**Step 1: light and air.**
- **Cost:** free (numbers, plus a tight halation in post).
- **What:**
  - the apartment's rooms warm tungsten (~2700 K) and its corridor cool fluorescent (~4000 K, a touch green);
  - one lamp in five dead;
  - fog density up a little, its colour taken from the lamps;
  - chroma 0 and grain 0 in the base look;
  - a warm halation round each lamp;
  - Film as the base curve.
- **He would see:** the apartment stops reading as "lit boxes" and reads as rooms lit by real bulbs, with air between him and the
  far end. Warm doorways off a cool hall, darkness where a bulb is out.

**Step 2: surfaces.**
- **Cost:** ALU in the trace (world-space procedural patterns, no textures or UVs needed yet).
- **What:** per material role, a pattern made in the shader from world position:
  - low-pile carpet with a repeat on the slabs;
  - painted wall with faint roller variation and a darker band near the floor;
  - drop-ceiling tile grid;
  - a door frame with wood grain.
  - Plus a low-frequency grime and darkening field, so no two metres of wall are identical.
- **He would see:** surfaces with texture under the light, where today they are flat plastic. The same coordinates later carry
  the fear layer's "flowing" and "mirroring", so step 2 is also the canvas for the dream effects.
- **The risk is the budget.** The worst building views are already over, which is why step 3 follows at once.

**Step 3: the soft picture.**
- **Cost:** free to negative (render scale plus dither; the trace cost falls with pixels).
- **What:**
  - trace at ~0.75 of the screen and upscale through the existing TAA;
  - blue-noise dither before the 8-bit output;
  - fog and bloom tied to speed, so detail drops as he sprints.
- **He would see:** a slightly soft, unified picture where nothing is crisp enough to look CG, and a smoother gradient in the dark
  (no banding). It is Night-Runners' "lower-res world" done by the renderer instead of a CRT filter. The time it frees pays for
  step 2.
- inferred: the saving is ~0.75² ≈ 56 % of the trace's pixels. On the measured scaling (1080p 0.68 vs 1440p 1.33 ms) that would
  take the worst rooms well under budget. Not timed.

**After these three:** the dial wiring (section 4), then a sheen on floors (ALU), then real reflections only where they carry a
biome (the pool rooms, new rays). The model path for chasers and the body (content) is its own lap (item 73).
