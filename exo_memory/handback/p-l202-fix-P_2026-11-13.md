## 1. The cause, checked against the code at 901d3c39

- `idleSampleTree` (heavy-run.js:358-362) sums CPU over the processes alive in the holder's tree at that sample. The check
  at :442 is `s.cpu - base.cpu < idleCpuS`.
- When a child that used CPU exits between the baseline and the sample, its time leaves the sum. The delta shrinks or goes
  negative, and anything under 1.0 s reads as idle.
- The librarian's cause is right, and I found no other. The recorded figures are negative: A's sweep -47.67 s, C's stomp
  run -504.44 s. Only a fall in the sum produces a negative figure.

## 5. Not covered: a specific limit, stated so it is not mistaken for proof

The fix stops false idles that come from a *fall* in the sum.

- A tree whose work is all done by short children has a different blind spot. If each child starts and exits between two
  one-minute samples, it is never seen at all, and the sum can rise by under 1 s while the tree works. That needs no fall,
  so this fix does not touch it.
  - inferred: from `idleSampleTree` sampling only live processes.
  - A ctest running many short test binaries is the shape that could hit it. Today's measured holders ran children longer
    than a minute (dz-chase cells, dz-world, world_app_tests).
