## The cause (checked, beyond the chair's reading)
- **Where the lean comes from:** `game/world/world_app.cpp` gather_poses drew `lean = atan(speed × turn / g)` from the chase's turn rate every tick. That rate carries D664's sway (`chase/chaser.cpp`, two sines, 0.31 and 0.77 Hz) and steps where the chase plans again. The per-frame interpolation only blends between ticks.
- **Why a lean dead zone failed:** in his run the sway alone leaned the hunter from −9° to +23° on a straight, because the lean scales with speed (10–12 m/s there).
  - checked: a dead zone of 4°, then 6°, on the lean left his run at 11 sign changes on straights (0.75 a second).
  - So the dead zone has to be on the **turn rate**, which does not grow with speed.
- **The turn rates in his run** (checked, `scratchpad/l173/his_lean.csv`, the turn taken over a quarter second):
  - straights: median 0.06 rad/s, 90th percentile 0.82 (replan steps);
  - corners: median 2.65 rad/s.

## The fix (`game/world/world_app.cpp` WorldApp::ease_poses, picture only)
- The turn rate is taken back out of the chase's lean (`tan(lean) g / speed`) and followed by a critically damped spring of 6/s (about 0.36 s).
- It loses a **dead zone of 0.5 rad/s**.
- What is left becomes a lean again at the chaser's speed, followed by a second spring of 6/s **capped at 100°/s** (kLeanMaxRate).
- **The look** follows the chase's through its own spring of 9/s.
- Poses keep their state per chaser id and are cleared with the poses at a run's start and end.
- **The switch:** `WorldApp::set_ease_poses(false)` draws the lean as the chase gives it, which is the 682fb36 drawing. It serves the test's control and the captures.
- **The body's yaw (heading)** is not eased. I did not measure it as jittering, and it is the travel direction, so a lag there would show the body facing off its line.
