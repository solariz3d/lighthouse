## 1 · THE WORDING RULE, QUOTED, AND THE LINE AGAINST IT

**The design, `:14–15`:** *"So the product must say: 'Jev marks turns worth a second look. Measured on its first 56 units,
about 1 in 4 marked turns was confirmed by a blind reader; the readers were the room's own and lean lenient.' **Never**
'Jev caught drift.'"*
**`:31–32`, the line itself:** *"one line: '[jev · worth a second look] your last turn: <one-line reason>'."*
**The packet adds:** never "drift detected", and nothing that reads as a verdict on the person. The reason is on record:
the score's `:16` says *"8 of 30 (27%)"* confirmed, and its `:38` says *"ship Jev's flags as 'worth a second look', never
as a verdict. Don't gate on confidence."*

**The line this hook writes (session audience), exactly:**

    [jev · worth a second look] your last turn: <Jev's reason, cleaned>
    [jev · worth a second look] your last turn (Jev gave no reason that can be shown)      ← when the reason is empty or broke the rule

**How it meets the rule:**
- **The fixed words are only the design's own:** "worth a second look", "your last turn". **The word "drift" is not in the
  fixed text at all.** A test pins that a session line with a plain reason contains no "drift".
- **The only free text is Jev's reason, and it is model output, so it is CLEANED, not trusted:**
  - collapsed to one line;
  - capped at 200 characters;
  - **dropped entirely if it matches a forbidden phrase**, in which case the second form is written.
- **`FORBIDDEN`** covers "drift detected" and "was detected", "caught drift", "detected drift", "confirmed / definite /
  certain drift", "you are / were / seem drifting, wrong, dishonest, lying, performing, manipulating", and "you drifted /
  failed / lied".
- **A test runs eight hostile reasons through both audiences** ("Drift detected in this move", "you were wrong to say
  that", "You lied about the count", and five more) and asserts that **no line either audience writes matches any
  forbidden pattern.** A mutant that makes the session line say "drift detected in your last turn" is caught.
- **Nothing is about the person:** the line speaks of "your last turn", which is the turn and not the one who took it.

**The consonance-audience line**, for the chair and the librarian, who already use the vocabulary:

    [jev · worth a second look] unverified · <seat letter> · turn HH:MM · Jev chose "drift" p=0.60

It keeps the room's content: seat, time, label, p. **It changes the room's wording:**
- the room's `[jev L2 · unverified] B · turn 14:02 · drift p=0.56` became **"Jev chose 'drift'"** under the same prefix,
  so the label is visibly Jev's choice and not a finding;
- the reason is not shown in this audience, as in the room today.

## 2 · WHERE I DEPARTED FROM, OR HAD TO ASSUME PAST, THE CONTRACTS — for the collation

1. **The line: the packet and the design differ.** The packet says `[jev · worth a second look] <reason>`; the design
   says `… your last turn: <reason>`. **I built the design's**, because the packet names the design as the spec. It is one
   string (`PREFIX` plus "your last turn:") if the librarian rules otherwise.
2. **`turn_uuid` is not defined by the contract.** "The last turn" is the uuid of the newest main-thread assistant row, and
   only if that row ended the turn (`stop_reason: "end_turn"`): the convention the room's `jev-judge.js lastTurnEnd`
   records today.
   - **An interrupted last turn gives NO line.** An older finished turn is never offered as "your last turn". My first
     version walked past the interruption, and I caught that before the tests.
   - **If B's judge writes a different `turn_uuid`, this hook is SILENT, never wrong.** The collation should run one
     judge row through this hook. My end-to-end test does it with a hand-written row, not B's judge.
3. **`dream`'s MEANING was not in the contract, and I got it wrong first.** The contract lists `dream: false` only. C's
   `config.js` (`:36`) defines it as *"the dream guard (today's CONSONANCE_DREAM skip) is a switch, off outside
   Consonance"*. So `dream: true` means **the guard is on**, and silence follows only when `CONSONANCE_DREAM` is set.
   - **My first build read `dream: true` as "always silent"**, which would have muted every Consonance seat on every
     prompt.
   - I found it by running C's real `load()` once it landed. Fixed, with a test for all three cases and a mutant for my
     first version.
   - **Worth a line in the contract:** "dream: the guard is on; silent only when CONSONANCE_DREAM is set".
4. **Consonance mode needs the room's seat ids, and `config.load()` does not give them.** Room resolution is C's
   (`config.js`), but its contract returns no `MAIN_SID` / `LIBRARIAN_SID` / `THIRD_PLACE_SID`. **So I vendored the room's
   lookup into `jev-flags.js`**, as the packet allows: `room_path` → `main.rs` for the ids, `data_dir` → `letters.json`
   for seat letters (L089 and L099). When batch 2 makes Consonance a config consumer, the ids belong in config, and
   these ~30 lines come out.
5. **ABSTAIN.** The session audience shows any non-clean row, as the packet says, **abstain included**. An abstain means
   Jev found no judgeable move, and telling a person that turn is "worth a second look" may be a stretch. Flagged, not
   changed. The consonance audience keeps the room's `p >= 0.80` bar for abstain.
