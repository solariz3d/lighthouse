## §3 Part 1 (C): the core — d421b-c, 5b1c49a on ea54ac3

Read whole: `git diff ea54ac3 5b1c49a` (4 files, +400 −12: `src/core/sculpt.js`, `CHANGELOG.md`, `src/core/README.md`, the new

**Verdict:** it can land. All three departures in C's §7 are right by the design's intent; two of them correct the design's letter, and I say
so below so the record carries the correction against me, not against C. Two small things to add (items 4 and 5), one seam for O (item 6).

**The three departures, each judged by intent:**

1. **The area-keeping factor instead of plain L0/L1: right, and the design's letter was wrong.** The intent (§3.2) is "the corner's total turn
   and climb are what they were and the piece is simply longer". Plain L0/L1 on every control point cannot give that, because a piece's first
   two control points are the joint's (the previous piece's end value and slope, which the stretch must not change), so they stay at the old
   rate and the corner turns short, C's measured 4.6%. The design did not see that the START joint pins two points; it only worked the end
   joint. C's g keeps ∫kh and ∫kv exactly and holds both end slopes, which also leaves the piece after the run with its own start, so nothing
   past the run changes but its place. The price, which C's hand-back does not state and should: with both ends pinned at the old rate and only
   the middle scaled, a uniform corner is no longer uniform after a big stretch. On a 200 m corner at 1.5× (13 control points, 20 m spans), the
   middle points go to about 0.61 of the old rate where 1/1.5 = 0.67 would keep it flat: the corner turns a little harder at its ends than in
   its middle, by about a tenth. inferred, by hand from C's own formula (the end weights of a clamped cubic are 3h/4 each side); C's rows
   check the integral, not the flatness. For stretches near 1× it is invisible; for 2× and beyond the keeper may feel a corner that "bulges" at
   its ends. It is inherent to keeping both joints and I would ship it, with one line in the CHANGELOG entry or the hint. A one-span piece
   (n < 5) keeps its rates instead of its bend, so a 20 m corner stretched to 40 m turns twice as much; also inherent, also worth one line.
2. **Knots scaled, not `evenKnots(L1)`: right, and the design named the wrong helper.** `evenKnots` makes one span per 20 m, so its COUNT
   changes with L, and the control points would have to be resampled; the design's own intent in the same sentence is "the same control points
   on a longer piece keep the same SHAPE per metre", which is exactly scaling the knots at the same fractions. C read the intent past the
   letter. The consequence C names (spans over 20 m after a big stretch) has one effect worth knowing: the brush widens its window to three
   spans, so a 20 m piece stretched to 400 m makes every later brush on it at least 1,200 m wide. A later lap can refine a heavily stretched
   piece back to 20 m spans (`refineKnots`, as the sharp brush does); not now.
3. **A run quantised piece by piece: right.** The document's quantum is 4 dp of a metre, so a run of k pieces lands within k × 5e-5 m of the
   length asked; a single piece is exact, which is what row 6 was written for. If the exact total ever matters (a typed length on a run), one
   line gives the rounding remainder to the run's last road piece. Not needed for a drag.

**The rest, ranked:**

4. **checked — the sharp brush fix is right.** A piece that touches `[lo, hi]` only at its end or start was handed a zero-width range; `<=` and
   `>=` leave it alone. The refinement it loses is of a span the window does not reach, so the result is the same where the brush acts.
5. **checked — the `sculpt()` split (`widened`, `checkReach`, `isLinked`, `settle`) changes no behaviour**: the two throws are verbatim and the
   anchors M10, M25 and KB1 still occur once (C's §3), and the two harnesses that anchor there ran whole, 52/52 and 61/61. A reader of
   `settle` should know it now serves two callers; its header says so.
6. **checked — a seam with O.** O's `stretched` loops `stretchFn(d, i, len × f)` over the run's pieces, one call and one `checkDoc` each, and
   rebuilds each joint from the already-stretched piece before; C's `stretch(doc, { from, to }, L1)` does the run in ONE call with one
   `checkDoc` and the same joint rule. On a 50-piece lap a 5-piece run costs O five document checks a frame where one would do. O should call
   the run form; the single-piece form stays for one piece. (Noted in §2 item 6 as the next thing to make smaller; this is the concrete cut.)
7. **checked — Smooth's one-pass rule is the design's, and the joint rule is right.** The later piece's second point is rebuilt as
   x₁ = x₀ + (cₙ₋₁ − cₙ₋₂)·h_b/h_a, which is the equal-slope condition 3(x₁ − x₀)/h_b = 3(cₙ₋₁ − cₙ₋₂)/h_a; the passes at λ/⌈λ⌉ ≤ 1 keep
   each Jacobi step non-expansive, so a long drag cannot overshoot. A window with fewer than five free points moves nothing and says so.
8. **checked — the suite's one failure is not this change.** C's A/B (release-frame row 4 at p95 15-18 ms on base and on 5b1c49a alike, with
   two other seats' heavy runs holding the slots) is the right way to show it, and it is the row A's parked D407b owns. The landing runs it
   with the slots free, as C says.
