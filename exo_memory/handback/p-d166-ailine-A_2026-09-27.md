## 4. The speed profile and the 110° wall-ride — what it does, and why

- **The profile is one constant speed, 300 km/h,** the middle of C's 250–350 km/h band, where the jump holds at both
  landings (`jumpCheck`).
  - Slower misses the jump.
  - The 28.66 m turns need 17–34 g across the band (v²/R, C's arithmetic), which the walls carry, not the floor. So
    nothing needs braking.
- **In each turn the line climbs the outside (right) wall** to where the wall's slope equals the frictionless balance
  angle φ = atan(v²/(R g)), with R the line's own radius.
  - On the **110° wall-ride** that is **about 87°**: below the part that overhangs (> 90°), where the wall alone holds
    the car. Test: steepest point 87 ± 2°, under 90°, more than 3 m up.
  - On the **60° half-pipe** the balance angle is past the top, so the line is **capped at 50°** (10° below the top),
    and grip does the rest.
- **The easing is three passes of a box average over 60 m in all.** My first version was one pass over 30 m, and the
  line's horizontal radius dropped to **4.25 m** on straight B: the ramp's ends were kinks (inline `node -e` run
  before the test). Three passes over 60 m give **17.6–32.5 m** on the straights and **18.9–21 m** in the turns.
- **The line is back on the floor centre before the jump.** Test: the lip and landing points are within 1 mm of the
  centreline.
- **`--ai-mode floor`** keeps the line on the floor centre as a **control**. If the wall line fails, a floor run tells
  "AC rejected the file" apart from "AI does not ride walls" (ARCHITECTURE §11.3 lists the second as open).
