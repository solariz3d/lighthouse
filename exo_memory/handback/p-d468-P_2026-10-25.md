### 1a. The choice: a curvature row per joint in the plan's own sweeps, not a quintic ease

The plan is not a ramp between two levels that an ease could shape: it is the curvature's share at every control point, held back by two limits, pinned at joints,
with flips and withheld pieces pinned at none. A quintic laid over that would have to be re-limited and re-pinned, and would move the share. A channel's end
curvature is linear in its last three control points and its start curvature in its first three (continuity.js's closed forms, the same six weights as close.js
`curvRow`), so "the added bank's curvature does not step across this joint" is one linear equality, a convex set like the two bounds, and it joins the same
alternating projections with the pins never moved. Three kinds of row:

- a linked joint inside the window (A | B both planned): k_end(A) − k_start(B) = 0 on A's last three and B's first three; at a relaxed joint (D447 (d)) B's first
  two points are set from A by the slope rule at the end of bankPlan, so their weights fold into A's last two;
- the window's start (the first planned piece; on a lap, the first piece at the seam): k_start = 0, with the two pins P₂ = 0;
- the window's end (the last planned piece before road outside the plan; the seam): k_end = 0, P[n−3] = 0.

The rows are projected after the acceleration and rate passes in each sweep; the loop still ends when nothing moved by more than 1e-9.
