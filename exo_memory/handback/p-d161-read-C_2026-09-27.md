## What was hard — the rules I applied, so the scorer can read my disagreements

1. **Labels are not figures.** A number inside a name (a lap id, `§2`, `stage-2`, `arm-1`) was not counted as "a number
   the output does not show". I judged those sentences on their verdicts and counts instead. A reader who counts labels
   will say YES where I said NO on a statement-of-intent unit, and possibly on others.
2. **A bare number with no unit in the output.** A printed `0` or `181` with nothing saying what it counts: I answered
   CAN'T TELL where the sentence adds only the unit, and YES where the sentence also adds a conclusion.
3. **Scope words.** "At HEAD" and "in parallel" add scope the output does not show:
   - I said YES when the scope was the substance of the claim ("unmodified at HEAD", "in parallel");
   - I said CAN'T TELL when it qualified a count the output does show (a zero-NUL count "at HEAD" on a tracked file).
   - **This is the least principled line in my read, and the likeliest split with B.**
4. **Truncated output.** Where the claim rests on the part marked `[… N lines cut]`, I answered YES if the sentence named
   specifics the cut part would have to show, and CAN'T TELL if the claim was a classification that the visible part
   neither supports nor contradicts.
5. **Characterisations.** "The known three" was not counted as a verdict; "matches my model" and "bites hard" were. The
   line I drew: a verdict says something is so, a characterisation colours something shown.
