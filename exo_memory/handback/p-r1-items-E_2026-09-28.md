## 2 · The downforce ray: a gap in the physics road is RED

**What changed:**
- **New `src/validate/raygap.js`.** Its inputs are the drivable physics meshes in world space (the export's built
  scene: `1<KEY>…` not WALL), welded at 5 mm. A boundary edge (used by one triangle) is a GAP when the car's own ray
  finds road again beyond it:
  - probes are at 0.1, 0.25, 0.5 and 1.0 m across the edge;
  - at each, the ray is cast from 0.4 m above along the surface normal, 1.0 m long, downward (a ray–triangle hit).
  - **The figures are C's, from the installed car's `script.lua:424-433`:** the ray sits 1.0 m ahead of the car's
    origin, starts 0.4 m up, and is 1.0 m long. So "within the ray's reach" is 1.0 m, and road more than 0.6 m below
    is beyond it.
  - They live in `MACH6.downforceRay` in `limits.js`, with that source.
- **RED `downforce-ray-gap`,** with its source in SRC (FINDINGS.md:110; research §4). In plain words:
  - "a gap in the road under the car's downforce ray: the car loses all its downforce over it";
  - `validate(..., { roadMesh })` runs it;
  - `src/export/fromwords.js` passes the built road, so **every export is checked**;
  - without a mesh (the live editor), the check is listed in `notChecked`, never passed silently.
- **Jumps note the downforce step:** every checked jump carries `downforceStep: { aheadM, note }`, with the note "the
  downforce drops to zero as the car's downforce ray, 1 m ahead, passes the lip, and returns in one step when it finds
  the landing".
  - **Its size is NOT given:** C's "about 2.4 g at 100 m/s" rests on UNVERIFIED arithmetic (the script's speed unit),
    so only the fact and its source are carried.

## 3b · ui_track.json "width" (the chair's added item, 01:0x)

**Was:** `"width": ""`, always, because nothing passed a width to `uiTrack`. That empty string is the root of B's NaN
(B's CORRECTION 01:1x in p-d182-plike-reg-B).

**Now:** the road's width as AC tracks write it, a whole number of metres with "m" (the sample loop gives `"32m"`).
- **Which statistic: the LENGTH-WEIGHTED MEDIAN, over the road segments, of each segment's edge-to-edge width ACROSS
  ITS SURFACE** (the profile's u span, floor plus both walls; u is arc length across, as `src/geom/profile.js`
  defines it). Jump flights are left out. `src/export/fromwords.js` `roadWidthM`. Why:
  - **edge to edge along the surface** is how the track reader measures an AC track's width (`tools/read_track.cjs`,
    1 m points across, walls included), so the builder's figure means what the corpus's and FINDINGS' widths mean
    (Sakura 28.7, Rainbow 50);
  - **the median by length** is the width most of the lap has: a short wide apron, a font blend or one sculpted word
    does not move it, as it would move a mean;
  - **not the dominant font's width:** a font is a starting shape, and width and wall are per-word handles the user
    sculpts.
- **The platform test** (`scripts/build_platform_test.js`, which writes its own description) now writes its fixed
  width, 2·WF + 2·LW = `"26m"`. Before, it wrote `""` too.
- **The sample loop:** 5 of its 8 words are 32 m across (bowl, 16 m floor + 2 × 8 m walls) and 3 are 20 m (flat). By
  length, 735 of its 1,035 m are 32 m wide, so the field is `"32m"`. The length-weighted mean would have said 28.5 m ((300 × 20 + 735 × 32) / 1,035; first written here as "27 m" without computing it, corrected before the ring).

**Tests: `node --test --test-concurrency=4 test/export_width.test.js` → 3/3.**
- **The sample loop's exported field** is non-empty, matches `^\d+m$`, parses to a finite number, and lies **within
  0.5 m** of an INDEPENDENT figure. That figure is the length-weighted median of (floor + 2 × wall) taken from the
  document's own word handles, not from the export's profile code. The bound is 0.5 m because the field is rounded to
  whole metres.
- **Median, not mean:** 900 m at 20 m plus 100 m at 120 m gives 20, where the mean would give 30. A flight has no
  width; no road gives null.
- **The platform test's two variants** write `"26m"`.
