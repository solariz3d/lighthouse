## THE CONTRACT FOR C (unchanged in kind, one addition)
`extend(doc, { length, transition, targets, … })`: `transition` is a **number of metres, as before**, OR a **per-channel map** `{ w: 20, phi: 'start', c: 12, … }` (channel names as `D.CHANNELS`: kh kv phi w r c h l). A channel missing from the map uses the piece's length (the whole-piece blend, as today). **Addition you may use:** the value `'start'` means the short ramp at the start and the core computes it (`startRampM(length, knotM)`, exported: the piece's first knot span, at most 20 m, the whole piece if shorter), so C need not know the knot layout. C may equally send numbers in metres: a value must be in (0, length] (a value past the length is refused, so for a piece shorter than 20 m send `'start'` or `min(20, length)`). A map key with no target in `targets` is ignored. Bad map → `CoreError` code `BAD_TRANSITION` (unknown channel, ≤ 0, > length, NaN, a string other than `'start'`). Everything else (`first`, `targets`, the first piece's typed-from-s=0 behaviour from D194a) is untouched.

## The finding that shaped the build: the least-squares fit rings, and knots do not cure it
The plan said "pass Lt[ch] to channelFn" and clamp the ringing. Measured first (`scratchpad/d195/probe1.js`, `probe2.js`; the piece is fitted by `D.fitChannel`, cubic B-spline, knots every ≤ 20 m):
- w 31 → 12 on a 100 m piece: a **20 m ramp (one knot span) fits to 1.55 m off the ideal, overshoots the target by 1.05 m and undershoots the start by 1.13 m, with control points 5.1 m outside [12, 31]**; 10 m: 2.7 m off; 5 m: 4.4 m off, 7.2 m of control-point overshoot. A ramp of 1.5 spans is less bad (0.16 m past) and 2 spans 0.45 m: the error depends on where the ramp's end falls against the knots, it does not vanish.
- Extra knots inside the ramp shrink it but never remove it: at 4 subdivisions of a 20 m ramp, 0.28 m past the target; at 8, 0.07 m. The Bloss blend has zero slope at both ends but its curvature jumps at u = 1, which a C2 cubic spline follows by ringing.
- Clamping the fitted control points into [start, target] (D190's V5 move) stops the overshoot but the fitted **hold was not flat**: 12.35 against a target of 12 at twice the ramp.
So a short-ramp channel is **not fitted**: its control points are the ideal ramp read at the **knot averages** ξᵢ = (uᵢ₊₁+uᵢ₊₂+uᵢ₊₃)/3 — the variation-diminishing approximation, **SOURCED** (ref 10 §2, LYCHE-MORKEN Def. 5.25, eq. (5.30); exact for a line) — the joint's first two control points are left alone (C1 by construction), every other one is clamped into [start, target, those two] (**SOURCED**, ref 03 §1b, WIKI-BSPLINE: the curve lies in the convex hull of its control points), and the ideal ramp is compressed to R − 2R/4 = R/2 (the curve at s is fed by control points about half a ramp ahead), so **the curve reaches its target by R and is exactly the target after**. Monotone control points give a monotone curve (the derivative's coefficients are their differences over positive spans): **derived, not sourced**, and sampled in the tests. The piece carries **4 extra knots** inside the ramp (`rampKnots`, `RAMP_KNOTS = 4`; the even knots they would crowd are dropped). Numbers for a channel with no short ramp (a number transition) are **byte for byte what they were** (the fit path is untouched).

**The price, stated:** the ramp is smoothed. The curve stays within **11.9% of the change** of the ideal ramp it is built from (Bloss over R/2), measured on four shapes (`scratchpad/d195/probe5.js`), pinned in the tests at 15%.
**Ramp length, justified:** `'start'` = the first knot span: 20 m for a piece ≥ 40 m, L/⌈L/20⌉ for shorter (100 → 20, 45 → 15, 30 → 15, 25 → 12.5, 15 → 15, 10 → 10). A ramp shorter than a knot span is representable here (unlike with the fit) because the knots are added inside it; the span is the natural unit the panel and the fit share, and 20 m matches the chair's "≈ one knot span". A ramp as long as the piece (a short piece with `'start'`) is not "short": it takes the ordinary whole-piece path, identical to the number transition.

## NOT MET, and why (a finding for the chair, not a defect of `extend.js`): the 1 mm SEAM standard at the joint into a fast ramp, for a LEGACY piece
- **Joints are C1** (value and slope, tested, `checkDoc` passes). **The mesh seam is not within 1 mm**: measured (`probe3.js`, the mesh's own last row of the previous piece against the first row of the ramped one, B's method) w 31 → 12 with `{ w: 'start' }` on a 100 m piece: **262 mm** at the joint; 451 mm on a 30 m piece; 443 mm half-pipe 31.5 → 12 on 45 m; 151 mm for 31 → 20. Inside the ramp the width steps between neighbouring 2 m segments reach **4.8 m** (6.0 m on a 45 m piece).
- **Cause (checked in `adapter.js`):** a legacy piece draws each 2 m segment with ONE profile at its middle width (`profileAt(family, mid.w, mid.r)`, no blend), so any width change is a staircase of width steps at segment boundaries, and the first segment's width (at 1 m) already differs from the joint's. It is the existing behaviour, made large by a fast ramp: **the same change as a number transition over 100 m gives a 2.8 mm joint gap and 0.57 m steps; over a 15 m piece 106 mm** (`probe3n.js`, `probe4.js`; the seal's own fixtures F4 and F8 already have 0.29 m and 0.86 m steps between segments). A cup piece draws a width change as a chord (D190 `cupSegmentsRuns`) and has no such steps.
- **Not fixable in `extend.js`:** for the joint into the piece to be within 1 mm the first segment's mid-width must be within ~2 mm of the joint's, which no short ramp satisfies. The fix belongs in `adapter.js`: draw a legacy segment whose width changes as a CHORD (profile at its end, `blend` from the profile at its start, like the cup's chord segment). That would change how EVERY legacy piece with a width change renders, so **row 5a's F4 and F8 digests would change** ("legacy renders as before" is the seal). I did not do it: it needs your ruling (regenerate the fixtures, or gate the chord on a step threshold that F4/F8 stay under — 0.29 and 0.86 m against the ramp's 4.8 m — which is a hack). **Options for the chair:** (a) accept the legacy staircase for this feature and tell the keeper the ramp will look stepped; (b) the chord for legacy segments, re-baselining F4/F8; (c) gate it. My recommendation: (b), as its own lap, because the stairs are already wrong at F4's 0.29 m and this feature makes them impossible to miss.
