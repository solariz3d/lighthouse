The control must first be run to show it equals E's sweep within noise (flee never caught near 62 of 96).
- **Seeds.** **150 seeds, paired, in two disjoint blocks:** `--first 1` and `--first 151`. `dz-chase` is deterministic (the L162 control reproduced the previous round's table exactly), so the only noise is which seeds were drawn; **a bar counts only if both blocks clear it with the same sign.** Normal, 300 s, the current movement numbers, `--threads 4`.
- **The command.** `dz-chase sweep --seeds 150 --first <1|151> --levels 1 --policies line,flee,cutback --max 300 --threads 4 [--speed "<field>=<value>"]` (the policies exist: `bots.h:33`; `--first` exists: `main.cpp:95`).
- **The catch test (restoring catch power).** Read off the sweep's survival table, same seeds in both arms: (i) the **share of flee runs not caught** by 300 s (control about 65 %, E's 62 of 96); (ii) the **Line median survival**. *Pass:* the not-caught share falls by **at least 10 points** and the Line median by **at least 15 %**. *Falsified:* the share falls by under 5 points, or the Line median by under 7 %. (Chosen above L161's measured spread: ratios under about 1.15 were not distinguishable from 1 at 150 seeds.)
- **The cutback test (making the juke pay).** The sweep's `paired cutback vs line` line: **the median of the per-seed ratio** (cutback survival over Line survival on the same seed). *Pass:* **at least 1.30**, and at least the control's value plus 0.25. *Falsified:* under the control's value plus 0.10. (The L162 bar was 1.5 and was never reached; 1.30 is registered because catch power is being restored at the same time, which lowers every survival.)
- **The organic-pursuit guard.** `chase_tests` rows `pursuit_tests.cpp:92` and `:117` stay green, and the **wall-hit rate** (the sweep's "where chasers caught on the world" block) is at most twice the control's. A lever that wins by undoing D664 is not a win.

- **Two instrument gaps, to be closed before any run** (both small, both in `chase/tools/dz-chase`): (a) the sweep pairs **policies within an arm** but cannot pair **an arm against the control**; a per-run CSV (`seed, policy, survival_s`) is needed for the lever-against-control ratios named below. ## 4. The levers

### P1. Lead the player's position (NextBot `ChasePath::PredictSubjectPosition`)
- **Source.** `NextBotChasePath.cpp:20-156` (`e1f1fd42`). Beyond `GetLeadRadius` the bot heads for the subject's position (`:30-34`; the radius is 500 units, `NextBotChasePath.h:65-68`, about 9.5 m at 1.905 cm a unit, `inferred:` the unit conversion). Inside it: `leadTime = 0.5 + range / runSpeed` (`:41`), `lead = leadTime * subjectVelocity` (`:44`), and **if the subject is moving toward the bot, only the perpendicular part of that velocity is used** (`:47-60`). The lead point is clipped to walkable space (`:67-76`) and dropped if it would put the bot over a cliff (`:141-145`).
- **Dreamzone now.** The hunter aims at `obs.pos` (`run.cpp:735`); only the interceptor and the pack lead (`:1020-1037`). The observation already carries a velocity (`k.push(now, where, player_vel_)`, `run.cpp:702, 707`; `Knowledge::Obs`).
- **The lever.** `lead_range` (m, 0 = off) and `lead_base_s` (0.5): the hunter's re-plan target becomes `obs.pos + flat(obs.vel) * (lead_base_s + range / top)`, clipped by `ahead_on_mesh` (`run.cpp:395`, already written), with NextBot's toward-velocity rule. **Code or idea:** idea (Valve licence); about 15 lines of ours.
- **Why it can serve both questions.** `inferred:` a lead raises the catch on a runner who holds a line (it cuts corners off the player's route) and **commits the chaser to an extrapolation**, which a cut makes wrong: the juke pays for a reason that does not depend on a wall. The toward-velocity rule is NextBot's guard against exactly the reversal, so the cutback gain is from the lead's error on a turn **away**, not toward.
- **Catch test.** Arms: `--speed "lead_range=10"`. Expected: the pass bar (about 60 % likely; the observation is stale by up to `hunter_update_s` early in a run, `dials.h:33`, so the lead is built from old velocity until the player is in sight: `inferred:`).
- **Cutback test.** Same arm. **I expect a partial pass: the ratio rises toward 1.15 to 1.25, not 1.30 (about 35 % to clear 1.30 alone).** Falsifier as in section 3.

### P2. A body that keeps its velocity through a turn (NextBot ground locomotion; DetourCrowd's integrate)
- **Source.** `NextBotGroundLocomotion.cpp:159-182`: the bot has a **velocity vector**; on the ground, **sideways friction** acts on the component of velocity across its move direction and forward friction only when it slides backwards (`:171-178`); `NextBotGroundLocomotion.h:160-168`: forward friction 0.0, **sideways friction 3.0** (a second-order decay, so a half-life of about 0.23 s if read as a rate per second, `inferred:`); `:170-173`: max yaw rate 250 degrees a second; `:262-270`: acceleration 500 units a second squared (about 9.5 m/s squared); acceleration is governed so it fades near the desired speed (`.cpp:424-436`). The turn itself is `FaceTowards` (`.cpp:1413-1439`): the facing turns at most the yaw rate; **the velocity does not turn with it.** DetourCrowd's `integrate` (`DetourCrowd.cpp:58-74`) is the same idea in vector form: the new velocity moves toward the wanted one by at most `maxAcceleration * dt`.
- **Dreamzone now.** `chaser.cpp:376-378`: velocity is always along the heading; the body has inertia in its **speed** (acceleration, braking, the turn's cost, `turn_drag`) and in its **heading** (the eased turn), but **not in its direction of travel**. This is the answer I could not find in L161: finite braking (`brake_scale`) changed nothing because a chaser that is turned is already travelling the way it faces.
- **The lever.** `side_friction` (per second, 0 = the body as it is; NextBot 3.0): the chaser keeps a lateral velocity component, carried when the heading changes and bled off at that rate; each tick the position moves by `heading * speed + side * lateral`, then snaps to the mesh as now (so a wall turns it aside, as now). **Code or idea:** idea (Valve licence; DetourCrowd's integrate is zlib but it is five lines and our body is not a crowd agent).
- **A mechanism row (not a bot test), registrable now:** a chaser at top speed whose path turns 90 degrees at 8 m ahead; the lateral distance of its feet from the path's inside corner one second after the corner is **at least 1.0 m with `side_friction` 3.0 and under 0.4 m with it off** (my estimate; `inferred:` not measured). It must still pass `pursuit_tests.cpp:92` and `:117`, because those measure the heading's turn, not the travel.
- **Catch test.** This lever **costs catch power** (a drifting chaser is slower round a corner). Registered as a **price test:** the Line median falls by no more than 15 % and the flee not-caught share rises by no more than 5 points. Alone it is not meant to pass the catch bar. **Pair it with P1 or P3** (arms `P1+P2`, `P3+P2`).
- **Cutback test.** Arm `--speed "side_friction=3"`. This is the lever the whole cutback question points to: **I expect the best chance of any lever to clear 1.30 (about 45 %)**, and the control-plus-0.25 bar as well. The falsifier is section 3's. If it fails with the mechanism row passing, the dodge is not in the body at all, and the bots cannot measure it.

### P3. A floor on corner speed (NextBot `PathFollower::AdjustSpeed`)
- **Source.** `NextBotPathFollow.cpp:115-131`: desired speed = `runSpeed + |curvature| * (walkSpeed - runSpeed)` at the closest point of the path (`:126-130`); with the defaults (`NextBotGroundLocomotion.h:251-260`, run 150, walk 75) **the slowest it is ever asked to go in a corner is half its run speed**, a graded slowdown with a floor.
- **Dreamzone now.** `corner_speed(now, angle)` (`chaser.cpp:302`) can ask for the creep speed 1.2 m/s at a sharp corner (`kCreep`), and a corner is summed over 3 m of path (`:294-302`). `inferred:` this is where the 858 m was given back.
- **The lever.** `corner_floor_speed` (share of top, 0 = off; NextBot's is 0.5): `through = max(through, floor * cap)`. A body that cannot make the turn at that speed meets the wall and slides on (`chaser.cpp:394-411`, no stand). **Code or idea:** idea.
- **Catch test.** Arm `--speed "corner_floor_speed=0.5"`. Expected to **pass** (about 65 %): it attacks the measured cause directly. The guard to watch is the wall-hit rate; sliding on a wall costs speed through `kScrapeLoss` (`chaser.cpp:410`), so the floor may be partly paid back.
- **Cutback test.** Same arm. **I expect it to lower the cutback ratio** (a faster-cornering chaser closes the gap a cut opens). Reported as the price; no pass is expected.

### P4. Look ahead along the path, skip corners that can be cut (NextBot path following; DetourCrowd's corridor)
- **Source.** NextBot `NextBotPathFollow.cpp:463-523`: with a `m_minLookAheadRange` set (`:469`; **the default is off, `-1`**, `:48`), goal points nearer than the range are skipped to the next segment when the next point is directly reachable and not uphill (`:473-509`). DetourCrowd: `optimizePathVisibility` (`DetourPathCorridor.cpp:310-352`, called at `DetourCrowd.cpp:1117` with `pathOptimizationRange`) tightens the corridor toward a point a set distance ahead by a visibility test, and `calcSmoothSteerDirection` (`DetourCrowd.cpp:104-133`) steers between the first two corners (`:112-130`), so the line rounds a corner before it. `updateTopologyOptimization` (`:907-936`) does a local re-search every 0.5 s for one agent at a time.
- **Dreamzone now.** The path is the funnel path, which hugs a corner's inside; `chaser.cpp:310-312` records that an early turn onto the way out "scraped" and cost time. D665 summed the corner's turn (`:294-302`). `inferred:` a look-ahead that skips redundant points would lower the turn asked at each corner, and with it the braking.
- **The lever.** `skip_range` (m, 0 = off): when the next-but-one point can be reached in a straight line over the mesh (a walkability ray over `nav::Query`; `inferred:` `nav/` would need to expose one, since `ahead_on_mesh`, `run.cpp:395`, steps the mesh instead), and the corner it removes is under a set angle, aim at it. **Code or idea:** the Detour corridor functions are zlib and could be read as the reference; they work on a `dtPathCorridor` we do not hold; so **idea**.
- **Catch test.** Arm `--speed "skip_range=6"`. Expected: **half a pass** (Line median down 8 to 12 %, below the 15 % bar; about 30 % to pass). **Cutback:** expected neutral (ratio within 0.1 of control).

### P5. Re-plan by how far the target has moved, not by a clock (NextBot `ChasePath::IsRepathNeeded`)
- **Source.** `NextBotChasePath.h:115-126`: re-path when the subject is further than `0.33 * range` from the path's end (`toleranceRate`, `:121`): **the closer the bot gets, the more accurate its path must be**; a minimum 0.5 s between re-paths (`:233-234`); a failed path retries at `0.005 * range` seconds (`:250`). DetourCrowd re-plans a target at most once a second (`TARGET_REPLAN_DELAY`, `DetourCrowd.cpp:943, 1029`).
- **Dreamzone now.** A clock: every `hunter_replan_s` (`run.cpp:655`), 0.50 s falling to 0.15, whatever the range.
- **The lever.** `repath_tolerance` (0 = the clock, as now; NextBot 0.33): re-plan when the observed target has moved more than `tolerance * range`, with a floor interval of 0.15 s. **Code or idea:** idea.
- **Catch test.** Arm `--speed "repath_tolerance=0.33"`. Expected: a **small** catch gain at close range (about 25 % to pass), from sharper aim near the player.
- **Cutback test.** Same arm. **A conflict:** a chaser that re-aims faster when close is the chaser a cut does not fool. **Expected to lower the ratio.** Registered so the price is on record; I would not ship it for catch power alone.

### P6. Separation among chasers (DetourCrowd steering; RVO2/ORCA)
- **Source.** DetourCrowd: a separation steer that pushes a desired velocity away from neighbours inside `collisionQueryRange` with weight `separationWeight * (1 - (dist/range)^2)` (`DetourCrowd.cpp:1214-1250`), velocity-sampled obstacle avoidance (`:1273-1303`) and a hard overlap resolve (`COLLISION_RESOLVE_FACTOR`, `:1324, 1365`); the slowdown near the goal (`:1204-1208`, speed scaled by distance to goal over twice the radius) is **unconditional in the code and would slow a chaser as it reached the player** (a thing to switch off, not copy). RVO2: `Agent::computeNewVelocity` (`Agent.cc:270`) builds one ORCA half-plane per neighbour from a `timeHorizon` (`:539`) and solves a small linear program (`:612-615`) for the velocity nearest the preferred one.
- **Why it only partly applies.** ORCA assumes agents that **share the burden of avoiding each other**. The player is not such an agent, and a pursuer wants to reach the target, not avoid it. The lever is **only for chaser-with-chaser separation** (the pack's three bodies, sprinters, a horde).
- **Dreamzone now.** None (section 1). `inferred:` bodies that follow one navigation path stack into a single file; the pack's roles (`run.cpp:1024-1037`) are the only spread.
- **The lever.** `separation` (weight, 0 = off) with a 4 m range: a lateral nudge to each chaser's aim away from neighbours of its group, never toward the player. **Code or idea:** the separation formula is idea (one line of arithmetic); RVO2 and DetourCrowd's sampler as code would add a dependency (RVO2) or a different movement owner (Detour), for a result the simple weight gives.
- **Catch test (needs several chasers).** Arm `--speed "separation=1"` with the director opened up (`--dial "lesser_max=6"`), same bar. Expected: **a catch gain on flee and Line** (spread bodies leave fewer gaps), about 40 % to pass. **Cutback:** a runner that cuts away from one chaser cuts toward its neighbour: **expected to lower the ratio.** `inferred:` this is the lever the lesser-chaser arc (hear, investigate, scare) will want, not the hunter's.

### P7. A flow field from the player (flow-field tiles)
- **Source.** The crate's README (`docs.rs/crate/bevy_flowfield_tiles_plugin`): the world in sectors, each with a **cost field, an integration field and a flow field**, joined by portals; the field serves **all agents heading for one goal** (the "basic flow fields" post says it suits many agents with one destination, and that vectors are along 8 directions only and agents do not avoid each other). The crate lists as limits: agents are pushed off the field by collisions, steering is out of scope, and changing a cost field regenerates the affected portals. `inferred:` Emerson's chapter says the same; I did not read it.
- **Dreamzone now.** One A* per chaser per re-plan (`run.cpp:648-657`), against one target: the case a flow field is for. `checked:` the sweep prints `paths a second` (about 5 in the L161 runs; `cost` block of `dz-chase run`).
- **The lever.** A field from the player's mesh cell over the walk graph or the navigation polygons, each chaser reading its gradient. **It is not a catch or a cutback lever:** the field gives each chaser the same route A* gives it, with the staleness moved from the path to the field. Its value is **scale** (a horde of 40 reading one field), which is the lesser-chaser arc's and E's *They Are Billions* question, not these two.
- **Registered as a non-inferiority and a cost test, not a pass test.** (a) The catch and cutback numbers of section 3 are within noise of the control (the Line median within 5 %, the flee not-caught share within 3 points, the cutback ratio within 0.1). (b) With `lesser_max` at 40, **path searches per second** (the `Run::cost.paths` counter, not wall time; **no timing runs**) fall by at least a factor of 2.
- **Code or idea:** idea. The one open-source implementation I read is Rust, MIT or Apache, and would be read, not used.

## 5. What I would take, in this order
1. **P3 first** (a floor on corner speed): it attacks the measured cause (858 m given back at corners), is a four-line change, and its price (a lower cutback ratio) is on record before it runs.
2. **P2 with P1** (a body that keeps its velocity, and a lead): the only pair that addresses the cutback in the body, and the lead supplies the catch power the drift costs. Register the arms `P1`, `P2`, `P1+P2`, `P3+P2`.
3. **P4** if P3 leaves the Line median short.
4. **P5 and P6 only if asked for:** both lower the cutback ratio; P6 is for the lesser chasers' arc.
5. **P7 when the horde is built,** not before.
- `inferred:` the cutback may be un-fixable by the body, in which case no bot arm clears 1.30 and the keeper's play is the only judge. L161 and L162 reached that point with five levers; the difference this time is that one lever (P2) gives the chaser the thing it lacks, a travel direction that is not its facing.
