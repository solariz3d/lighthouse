## The finding, in one line
The plan's lead (`spanHolds` grid, L141's non-monotone cells) is **half right**. The refusal is CORRECT; its **stated limit was wrong**. 500 m really is over the bar. The message said "from at most 628.5" and so allowed it.

## Cause (checked: measured, `scratchpad l151/meas.js`)
- B's case needs the panel's `bankShape: 'rounded'` (checked: `extendOptions` gives `bankShape "rounded"` for his fields; without it the same numbers are accepted at 500, 0 of 2 scans refused). With it, the first ramp is the rounded whole-piece bank, `base = { step 34.78°, length 1000, shape 'rounded' }`.
- The built road's peak roll acceleration over the bar (`probePeak`, gridded to −70°) is NOT monotone in the span:
  - 250 m 1.113 (over), 275 m 1.045 (over), **300 m 0.909 to 425 m 0.993 (holds)**, 450 m 1.0007 (over), **500 m 1.011 (over)**, 700 m 1.029 (over). Ungridded at 500 m: 1.0114.
  - So `spanHolds` is false at 500 (right), while `secondMinM` finds a shortest of 263.6 m (below 500).
- `src/core/extend.js` (the `changeLists` check, the old `if (!spanHolds…) { minM = secondMinM…; if (minM <= end - F …) { … ceilOf … } }`): when the search found a shorter span that holds, the refusal fell back to the closed-form CEILING `ceilOf` = 371.5 m and printed "from at most `end − 371.5` = 628.5". That ceiling is a span that holds, but it is not an upper bound on the holding spans, because longer ones fail. Its comment ("the refusal names the ceiling, which always does") was true of "a span that holds", false of "a limit".
- The line is in the branch's `src/core/extend.js` at the `let minM = 0;` block of that check.

## The fix
The refusal stays. When the asked span fails but a shorter one holds, it now finds the longest span below the asked one that holds (by the metre, `spanHolds`) and says so:
`… is laid over the bar by 500 m before the piece's end (the built road rounds a longer one back over the hold; 447 m still holds): from at least 553.0, got 500`.
If no span below holds, it falls back to the old ceiling message. A too-short span keeps its old "needs at least N m … from at most M" text (`core_additive.test.js:159` parses that and still passes: see the suite).
