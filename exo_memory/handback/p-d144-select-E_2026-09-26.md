## 4 · NOT VERIFIED / LIMITS

- **Recognition.** *The Federalist* is famous, and a subject may answer from knowledge (there are 85; No. 34 follows) as
  well as from the text. For K and O, knowledge and text point **the same way**, so it strengthens separation rather
  than confounding it. **For F it does not:** a subject may know Seneca's collection ends at 124 and decline from
  knowledge. That would raise the floor, which is **conservative** for any claim that arm C teaches closure above the
  floor.
- **F's content is closure-adjacent.** Its last paragraph says *"you may feel that you have reached perfection"*. That is
  about the good life, not the series, but a subject could read it as a culmination. It is named here so a high F rate
  can be diagnosed rather than surprising.
- **Genre split.** F is a letter and K/O are newspaper essays. The same-genre F candidate (Brutus XVI) failed neutrality,
  and I chose neutrality over genre.
- **K/O separation is predicted, not measured.** It is the stage-1 gate, and only A's 9 subjects can show it.
- **Web search is US-only** (the tool's own limit), and the engine and date are as run today.
- **Gutenberg's numbering of No. 33** follows its source edition. Other editions number the papers differently. The file
  records the text, and the label is only a label.
