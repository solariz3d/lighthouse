## 2 · WHAT THIS DOES TO MY §4 — weakened, not closed

cross-model check, Gemini, agreed 10/10). Until then the honest statement is: **the method is readable by this model
family without the room's training, and the machine judges do not read it the same way.**

## 6 · WHY I DID NOT TAKE OPTION (2) AS WRITTEN — the refusal, as a finding

**The rule forbids surfacing any verdict about the keeper. On a one-subject trajectory measure, cell counts ARE a
compressed verdict about that subject.** *"B answered X on n of m units"* about his trajectory is a reading of him
with a denominator attached, whatever the hand-back calls it. The obvious repair — permute the four category labels
and report only permuted cells, since kappa and raw agreement are invariant under a consistent relabeling — **leaks
here**, because the judge's real distribution is already partly on the record: the *"72% of one-message windows"*
figure is quoted inside the very material I read this afternoon. With four categories and one known marginal, the
mapping is recoverable by anyone who reads both documents. **So I judge the blind-read half cannot be done inside
the rule, and I would rather land that than file cell counts that quietly are the thing the rule forbids.**

**What I would take instead, and believe is strictly better:** the question is the judge's **reliability**, and
reliability is measurable **without any reader forming a verdict at all** — test-retest over repeated or materially
identical L3 inputs, reported only as *"the judge answered differently on k of m identical inputs."* No reading of
him is produced by anyone. It is also the better instrument, because agreement-with-a-second-reader confounds
reliability with validity — which is exactly what today's kappa 1.000 could not separate, and what §2 still cannot.
**It needs one precondition I have not checked: that the L3 store contains repeats.** The L2 set had two pairs by
luck, not by design. If it has none, the refusal stands alone.
