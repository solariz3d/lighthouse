The structure:
- `kinetic_chain(BodyPose&, ChainPose)` is the one function every body goes through. It takes any pose standing along
  its heading.
- `chain_pose(lean, pitch, look, phase, run, follow)` turns lap 1's signals into each segment's angles.
- `KineticChain` holds the follow-through springs, stepped inside `DrawnChaser::step`.
- `ChaserPose::follow` carries them to the stage.
- lap 1's rigid roll at `game/model_rig.cpp:447-463` (add21fe) is gone; apply_motion calls the chain instead.

Lap 1's tilt and look are read as signals and are unchanged.

B can pose the player's body through the same function: build a BodyPose from `game/body.cpp`, call `chain_pose` with
the player's signals and an empty `ChainFollow` (or a `KineticChain` of its own), then `kinetic_chain`.
`game/body*` is untouched.

     - the trunk on the pelvis: Pelvis to Chest (the bones pelvis, spine_01 and spine_02) and Chest to Neck
       (spine_03), with the shoulders and arms riding it;
     - the head on the neck: Neck to Head (neck_01 and head).

     full share.
   - inferred: the pelvis mesh, weighted to the pelvis bone, banks at the trunk's 0.35 and not the hips' 1.0.
     Unchecked by eye.
2. **The signal is lap 1's tilt.** Followed. `chain_pose` reads `DrawnChaser::lean()` and `pitch()`, not the turn rate.
3. **The bank shared down the chain.** Followed from **one** of the two cited sources:
   - The first game, as cited (:6655 pelvis `pelRoll + bank`, :6662 torso `bank * 0.35`, :6669 head `- bank * 0.30`):
     hips 1.0, trunk 0.35, head −0.30.
   - **Overgrowth's numbers are not a bank share.** checked: `og_movementobject.cpp:2680-2685` (fetched in L176) →
     `key_transforms[kHipKey].origin += foot_balance_offset * 0.7f` and the same for the chest (0.5), head (0.4) and

   - Those are **translations** of the IK keys' origins, applied only `if (balancing)`, i.e. while the character

   head all of it.
   - The first game's torso share is `gz * 0.45` (:6662), within 2° of ozz's at the 1.3 rad limit.
   - Valve's torso aim (eyes − feet) is not added: it is a facing rule, and lap 1 already has Valve's facing.
5. **The counter-rotation.**
   - The structure follows the first game: pelvis `pelTw` against thorax `torTw` (:6613-6614), and the head taking
     `torTw * 0.55` (:6667). The arms already swing opposite the same-side leg in `gait_pose`.
   - The sign is anatomical: the pelvis turns toward the side opposite the leg that swings forward, which is the same
     as saying that hip comes forward.
   - **The amplitudes come from two measured studies:**
     - Pelvis: transverse range 9.96 to 16.13° (Perpiñá-Martínez et al. 2023, *Int J Environ Res Public Health*
       20(4):3631, PMC9961938, Table 1; men and women at three running speeds). checked: Europe PMC full text →
       "Pelvic rotation (°) 10.64 (2.47) … 13.21 (2.98) … 9.96 (3.76) … 15.74 (3.99) … 13.60 (3.68) … 16.13 (3.42)".
     - Thorax: axial range 22.6 ± 3.5° in men, 34.5 ± 7.0° in women (Ford et al. 2013, *Med Sci Sports Exerc*
       45(6), doi 10.1249/MSS.0b013e3182825aca; collegiate runners, treadmill, 3.58 m/s, contact to toe-off).
       checked: the Europe PMC abstract. **The full text was not read**: the figures are the abstract's own, and its
       method and tables are unread (his item 165). If the abstract's numbers hold in the paper, the thorax amplitude
       stands; it is the one chain figure resting on an abstract.
   - **The amplitudes used** take the middle of each range (ours): pelvis (9.96 + 16.13) / 4 = 6.52° each way (written
     as 0.11345 rad = 6.50°), thorax (22.6 + 34.5) / 4 = 14.28°.
   - **The first game was over both ranges:** 0.20 rad = 11.5° and 0.32 rad = 18.3° each way.
   - **Limits of the studies:** Ford's figure is over stance only, not a whole stride, so it is a floor on the stride's
     range. Neither study gives the pelvis–thorax phase.
   - The phase (opposite) comes from the first game. The literature (abstract level) says it tends toward opposite as
     speed rises: in phase at slow walking, toward anti-phase fast. The amount scales with the gait's run (speed / 6),
     so it fades toward a walk.
   - Preece et al. 2016's abstract says the pelvis "lag[s] slightly behind" the thorax in running. That is a timing
     detail not modelled here.
6. **Follow-through.** Followed, with one extension marked ours.
   - The trunk and head follow their shares on the first game's critically damped spring, restated (`SpringStepF`
     :6420-6425: omega = 2/dur), with durations 0.13 s (:6617) and 0.22 s (:6618).
   - The first game springs the **pitch** only. Springing the bank too is ours.
   - Its spring targets also carry a nod (cos 2φ) and the body's bounce. Our gait has no bounce spring yet, so both are
     left out (ours).
   - The pelvis is the root and takes its share at once.
7. **Feet.** Unchanged from lap 1. The legs are reached again to the planted feet after the hips move (`reach_leg`), as
   the first game aims its legs at planted feet.
   - The thighs' twist follows the pelvis frame: `p.right` now carries the pelvis twist. The first game's legs ride
     `pelY`, the pelvis's yaw with its twist.
8. **One function for every body.** Done, as above. The lesser chasers draw boxes and do not use it.

limb overspeed, asymmetric leg phase, and the head pinned down with no stabilisation (dreamzone-rt :6352-6356).

first game's detuning on top, with the player's body staying human? Nothing of it is built.

are built so each body can differ:

built.
