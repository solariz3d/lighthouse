## 1. The documented ground (what the game can quote or lean on)

**The institute and Hemi-Sync:**
- [DOC] Robert Monroe (1915-1995), a radio executive, wrote *Journeys Out of the Body* (1971) (L4, L5).
- [DOC] His institute began as the research and development division of Monroe Industries (1958, accelerated learning). It was
  named The Monroe Institute and moved to a larger property in 1977. It is in Faber, Virginia (S1, S2).
- [DOC] Monroe received the first of several patents for what became Hemi-Sync in 1975 (S2).
- [DOC] Hemi-Sync is binaural beats, e.g. 170 Hz in one ear and 174 Hz in the other for a 4 Hz beat. Its "frequency-following"
  claim is contested: Stone et al. 2002 found no EEG change with air-conducted headphones (L6).

**The Army and the memorandum:**
- [DOC] The US Army's GRILL FLAME / CENTER LANE program sent personnel to Gateway training at the Monroe Institute of Applied
  Sciences: a seven-day seminar, and a CENTER LANE operative in August 1983 (S3).
- [DOC] The Secretary of the Army was briefed on INSCOM's involvement with the institute on 5 January 1984 (S3).
- [DOC] "Analysis and Assessment of Gateway Process", LTC Wayne M. McDonnell, 9 June 1983, was written at the Army's request to
  evaluate the Gateway Experience's "mechanics and ultimate practicality" (S3, L2, L3). It leans on Itzhak Bentov's biomedical
  models (S3).
- [DOC] Its words:
  - "the Absolute" (¶18);
  - "intervening dimensions" (¶18) and "intermediate dimensions" (¶21);
  - "click out" (¶19-20);
  - the "cosmic egg" (¶24-25) and the "energy balloon" (¶29);
  - Focus 10, 12, 15 and 21, the last "the last and most advanced" (¶30);
  - "intelligent, non-corporal energy forms" (¶38 J).
  - "Bubble" is not in it (L2, L3).
- [DOC] The Army's psychic intelligence unit (later "Stargate", 1978-95) was closed in 1995 after the finding that "no
  discernible benefit had been established" (S4).

**Monroe's later books:**
- [DOC] Monroe's Locales I-III (1971). Later in his books and courses, Focus 22-27: Focus 23 the recently dead and confused,
  24-26 the "Belief System Territories", 27 "the Park" and its Reception Center. These are secondary sources, uneven (L4, L5).

**What the game must not claim:** that Hemi-Sync works as a technology, that any real person did what a character does, or that
the institute pays anyone.

## 2. The lore ties, each marked

**T1. The dreamzone is the "intervening dimensions."**
- [DOC] The memo's in-between, between time-space and the Absolute.
- [INV] Every run is a walk through that in-between, shaped by the dreamer's own mind.
- [INV] The chaser is what lives there and feeds.
- [INV] The walls are a mercy: beyond them is the "beautifully dark and ominous void" of item 29 (his), the in-between seen
  bare.

**T2. Each character's biomes are a Belief System Territory.**
- [DOC] Monroe's later term, for places shaped by one fixed belief (L4, L5; later, secondary).
- [INV] A character's biome set is their territory: the kid's grief makes his apartment, school, mall, hospital and facility
  (D658).
- [INV] Item 74's "every other character could have their own style of biomes" is then the same rule: each mind builds its own.

**T3. A reprieve is a piece of "the Park."**
- [DOC] Focus 27 "the Park", the way-station (later Monroe, secondary).
- [INV] Reprieves are places the chaser cannot enter because they belong to the Park, not to the dreamer.
- [INV] That is also why reprieves look nearly alike across characters: they are the one shared architecture.

**T4. Energy.**
- [DOC] The memo describes consciousness and matter as energy, with the "energy balloon" as protection (¶29).
- [INV] Item 67's second currency, **energy**, is literally what the dream is made of. A successful escape (item 69) brings some
  back.
- [INV] The golden aura of item 29 is the energy balloon made visible: the paper's own image, ¶29, not a new one.

**T5. Focus levels as the late reveal.**
- [DOC] Focus 10-21 in the memo.
- [INV] Hidden until a character "knows of Gateway" (items 29, 49).
- [INV] Then the meditation chair's menu is arranged by Focus level, and a level is a depth tier of the run.
- [INV] Focus 21 is the deepest the chair reaches. The memo's "last and most advanced" is the ceiling no character passes.

**T6. The click out.**
- [DOC] ¶19-20.
- [INV] The sound and cut at the moment the good god rips you out of the evil god's jaws (item 30). A run that is "saved" ends
  in a click out.

**T7. The cheque, and why a dream pays cash (item 69's open question).**
- [DOC] A state program once paid people to see things at a distance, and it was closed in 1995.
- [INV] **Someone kept paying.** A cheque arrives after a successful run, for the run's cash, with the dreamer's name, an amount,
  and no payer.
  - Paper only, no letterhead.
  - The signature is a scrawl that never resolves, whatever the zoom.
- [INV] No story ever names the payer. Each narrows the field (§4), and the player is left with two or three candidates and a
  feeling.
- [INV] Item 84's rule is the design: the absence is the point, as the father's is.

**T8. The illicit site (item 67).**
- [INV] A website that trades trinkets for cash and energy "to another human who knows the importance of it".
- [INV] Its sellers are other dreamers. Its look is an early-2000s forum and storefront (item 70's period).
- [INV] Its oldest threads quote the memo's paragraph numbers without saying what they are from. A player who knows will see it.
  [DOC: the paragraph numbers are real, L2]

**T9. Hemi-Sync in the room.**
- [DOC] Binaural beats exist and the institute makes them; the effect claims are contested (L6).
- [INV] The writer's tapes (item 71) are binaural recordings on cassette.
- [INV] **The game never claims they work.** In the fiction they work *for him*, and the kid never needed them.
- [INV] That asymmetry is a question the story keeps open: was it the tapes, or the man?

**T10. The two gods.**
- [DOC] The memo speaks of "non-corporal energy forms" and of protection against "lower energy levels" (¶29, ¶38 J). It does not
  sort them into good and evil (L161).
- [INV] The sorting is his (items 28, 30, 47): an evil god that plays with its food and a good god that mirrors it when the
  equilibrium breaks.
- [INV] The lore never says what either is. The kid's story lets it be his love for his father, or his father (item 47). Other
  stories add other readings, and none wins.

**T11. Why "Recognition" cannot last (item 62's question).**
- [INV] Three answers to offer him. All can be true in the fiction.
  1. **Equilibrium (his item 30):** the evil god cannot let it stand, and throws everything (item 61). The state lasts exactly as
     long as the dreamer can hold the signal against that.
  2. **The click out is transient by nature** (memo ¶19-20, [DOC] wording; [INV] use). A dreamer who stayed would not come back
     to the bed.
  3. **The body calls:** the sleeping body needs its owner back. The writer's story is where this one is learned.

## 3. The characters: four, plus two DLC (item 72), each a save with a global level (item 68)

Each has: a life path, a backstory, a way in, a unique buff (item 68: "unique buff… could synergize with a build"), and a biome
set (item 74). **All [INV]**, except where his items fix a point.

### C1. The kid (his, items 46-47, 70; the first arc, item 72)

- **Life path:** a teenager in 2004-05 (item 70's period, not decided). He lives with his mother (never seen, item 31) in an
  apartment.
- **Backstory:** his father is gone, murdered, never said (item 46). The photo on the desk, his mother's knocks, dad's watch as
  a trinket (item 47).
- **Way in:** sleep, naturally (items 3, 71: "the kid could naturally"). Access and no cash.
- **Unique buff (proposed): "Natural."**
  - He enters the dream already moving: a running start.
  - His stress falls a little faster while out of the chaser's sight. Children recover.
- **Biome set:** D658 (`dreamzone-3/docs/BIOMES.md`: apartment, hotel, school, mall, hospital, facility).
- **What he does not know:** anything. He is the player's ignorance made a person.

### C2. The writer (his, item 71)

- **Life path:** an established author of paranormal non-fiction, failing. A high-rise loft over a city, savings draining, no
  energy.
- **Backstory (proposed):**
  - He built a career on other people's experiences and never had one.
  - He has read everything: the memo, Monroe's books, the forums. He contacts the institute to train (item 71).
  - [INV] His research files are the player's encyclopedia, with mystery kept.
- **Way in:** the tapes, sitting in the chair. Later, sleep (item 71).
- **Unique buff (proposed): "Research."**
  - Designed pieces and lore objects show a faint mark at the edge of view when near.
  - The first time he sees any chaser type, its tell is labelled.
- **Biome set (proposed), his territory of ambition and emptiness:**
  - the loft, an office tower of publishers, an empty bookstore, an airport (book tours), a television studio after hours;
  - **the hotel**, shared with the kid (§4).
- **What he knows:** the documents, but not what they feel like. **What he lacks:** the thing the kid has without trying.

### C3. The night nurse (new)

- **Life path:** a nurse on nights at a city hospital, then in hospice care. A divorced parent, always tired, the one who sits
  with people at the end.
- **Backstory:**
  - She has been beside many deaths and talks to the dying, though she does not believe it does anything.
  - [INV] One night in the early 2000s she was on shift in the hospital the kid waits in. She was behind the door he could not
    open (D658's hospital designed piece).
  - She does not know him; he never saw her.
- **Way in:** exhaustion. She falls asleep in the on-call room between rounds. It is a short, broken sleep, so her runs start
  in the middle of things.
- **Unique buff (proposed): "Bedside."** Reprieves calm her faster, and a pack chaser that loses sight of her stays lost longer.
  She knows how to be still.
- **Biome set (proposed):** the hospital from the staff side (corridors without signs, the break room, the morgue corridor
  kept dim and never entered), a hospice house, a 24-hour diner, a laundromat, the parking garage at 4 a.m.
- **Tie to Monroe's later terms:**
  - [DOC: Focus 23, "the recently dead, confused" (later, secondary)]
  - [INV] Her deepest tier is where she meets the people she sat with, still confused. It is the only place in the game where the
    dead speak, and only in fragments.

### C4. The analyst (new; a fictional character, not based on any real person)

- **Life path:** a retired Army intelligence analyst, born in the late 1940s. Once a believer, then a skeptic, now neither.
- **Backstory:**
  - [DOC] Army personnel attended Gateway training in 1983 (S3).
  - [INV] He was one of the fictional attendees in this game's world. He went in sure it was nonsense, came out unable to say
    what happened, and spent twenty years not talking about it.
  - [INV] The program around him ended in 1995 as the real one did ([DOC] S4), and he did not.
- **Way in:** the technique, Focus 10 to 21, practised for decades. He enters at will but is old: his runs are short.
- **Unique buff (proposed): "Coordinates."** Once a minute, for a breath, he sees the direction of the hunter through the walls.
  It is the game's nod to the program's purpose, with no claim that it was ever real.
- **Biome set (proposed):** a windowless office, a barracks, a motel on a highway, a veterans' hall, a training centre that is
  not the real campus ([INV]: generic, unnamed).
- **What he might be:** one of the cheque's candidates. He has the means, the reason and the silence. No story confirms it.

### DLC1. The detective (new)

- **Life path:** a homicide detective whose one unsolved case is the kid's father. Retired early.
- **Way in:** insomnia. Hypnagogic sleep at the kitchen table among the case files.
- **Unique buff (proposed): "Trail."** Chasers' recent paths show as faint marks on the floor for a few seconds, like scuffs. He
  reads where things went.
- **Biome set:** the precinct, an evidence room, an interview room, a parking structure, the street where it happened. That
  street is the rare **outside** (item 26), the one place it means something.
- **Why DLC:** his story says the most about the father, so it must come after the kid's has stayed silent long enough. It still
  shows only files with lines blacked out, never the act (item 46's rule).

### DLC2. The mother (new; the one the kid never sees)

- **Life path:** a widow raising a son alone in the apartment. Two jobs. She knocks on his door and he does not always answer.
- **Way in:** grief, and later, deliberately, the tapes. She found them in the writer's book (§4).
- **Unique buff (proposed): "Knock."** Once a run, she can call the kid's good god toward her for a few seconds: the golden aura
  answers her.
- **Biome set:** her workplace, the apartment from the parent's side (his door always closed), a church, a courthouse corridor,
  the grocery store at closing.
- **Why DLC:** the knocks of item 31 heard from her side. And the mimic of item 31 ("an entity invading… mimicking eventually
  players mom") becomes unbearable once you have been her.

## 4. One intertwining structure (item 68: "a chaotic intertwining narrative", playable in any order past the unlock caps)

**The shape (proposed): one night, seen from every side, and one object that travels.**
- **The night:** the night the father died. Every character was somewhere that night:
  - the kid waiting;
  - the nurse on shift;
  - the detective called out;
  - the mother on the phone;
  - the writer in a hotel on a book tour in the same city;
  - the analyst in a motel, practising.
- **No story shows the night.** Each holds one corner of it, and the corners only fit when you have several.
- **The object:** the father's watch (item 47), a trinket that moves between saves.
  - It is the kid's heirloom.
  - It appears for sale on the illicit site in the writer's story (who sold it? someone who knew its worth).
  - It sits in a hospital property bag in the nurse's story.
  - It is entered as evidence, then released, in the detective's.
  - The player follows it and never sees the hand that moved it.
- **The shared places:**
  - **The hotel** is in four characters' sets (the kid, the writer, the analyst, the detective). It is where their territories
    overlap, so it is where one character can glimpse another's trace: a room number, a cart, a coat.
  - **Reprieves** are the Park, shared by all (T3).
- **The payer:** never named. Each story removes a candidate or adds one.

**What each story discloses about the others** (the table he asked for):

| story | discloses about… | how, never said directly |
|---|---|---|
| **the kid** | the dreamzone itself; that sleep alone can reach it; the two gods | play; the rescue; dad's watch |
| **the writer** | the documents behind everything (memo, Focus levels, Belief System Territories); that the kid's ease is rare | his files, the forum, his failure to get in at first |
| **the nurse** | what was behind the hospital door; that the dreamzone touches the dead (Focus 23) | the staff corridors; fragments from the people she sat with |
| **the analyst** | that someone has paid for what dreamers see, for decades; why the cheques exist at all | his silence; an old pay stub with the program's name blacked out; the motel |
| **the detective (DLC)** | that the father's case was never closed; what kind of man the father was | case files with lines blacked out, the street outside |
| **the mother (DLC)** | what the kid's silence cost her; that the mimic in his room learned her knock | the closed door from the hall; her own invasions |

**Unlocks (item 68):**
- The writer unlocks a quarter through the kid's arc. His files make the kid's lore make sense.
- The nurse unlocks a quarter through either.
- The analyst unlocks at a global level, because his story spoils the most.
- The DLCs come after the analyst.
- No order is required past that; any story can be played to its end alone.

**The ending they share (proposed):**
- Every story ends at the same place: Focus 21's ceiling, the deepest the chair goes.
- What each character finds there is different, and the player who has finished several sees that the good god is shaped
  differently for each: a father, a stranger who sat with you, a reader, a son.
- [INV] So the god's nature (item 47) stays plural and open, and never resolves into one answer.
