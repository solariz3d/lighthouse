**How the models are posed:**
- `game/model_rig.h:1-17`: the game's 21 procedural joints (`game/body.h` Joint) are retargeted onto the skin by aiming each bone between two joints (`Rig::palette`, `:67`).
  - The kid is posed from my L161 `Body` (`game/world/stage.cpp:298`).
- **The hunter:**
  - It is posed by `gait_pose` (`game/model_rig.h:89-93`; `game/model_rig.cpp:372-429`). The header calls it "Not animation: a stand-in until the chasers have clips".
  - Only the **first** hunter is a model (`stage.cpp:265-273`). Every other chaser is still a box figure, placed by position and heading (`stage.cpp:276-278`).
- **The stand-in gait:**
  - Legs swing by sine about the hips with a bent knee, and arms swing against them (`model_rig.cpp:395-427`).
  - The phase advances by distance (`gait_phase_per_metre`, `:366-370`, called at `stage.cpp:269`), so on flat ground the step length is tied to speed.
  - The feet are placed from the pelvis, not against the floor. inferred: on stairs or a slope they go through the steps or hang above them.

**What the renderer gets per chaser:** `ChaserPose` (`game/world/world_app.h:78-89`) gives position, heading, speed, mode, gait, look (head yaw) and lean.
- The gait says Mesh, Ladder, Drop, Recover, Climb or Off (`chase/chaser.h:57-65`). Recover is "after a drop, or after meeting a wall at speed" (`:61`).
- The chase itself knows a link's endpoints and progress (`chase/chaser.h:88-89`: `link_from`, `link_to`, `link_tick`, `link_ticks`). **These are not passed to the renderer.** A climb's height and how far through it the chaser is never reach `ChaserPose`.

- Legs and arms reach by `two_bone` with a hinge (`body.cpp:53`, used at `:461`, `:476`).
- Its moves are `BodyMove` (`game/body.h:49`): Run, Air, Slide, Vault, Climb, WallRun, Ladder, Dive, Roll.

## 1. Left 4 Dead's infected
**What the sources say:**
- **Who animates what.** source: Valve's AI slides (Booth, GDC 2009) split the bot into components:
  - Locomotion "owns moving the actor to a new position in the environment (collision resolution, etc)";
  - Body "owns animation state";
  - Vision and Intention.
  - Behaviours hear "OnAnimationActivityComplete, OnAnimationEvent".
  - So the movement code moves the infected, and the animation follows it and reports back.
- **Path following.** source, the same slides:
  - "Reactive Path Following" moves toward a "look ahead" point farther down the path, with local obstacle avoidance.
  - It "is used for all actors in Left 4 Dead", and it "superposes well with mob flocking behavior".
  - Following an optimised path directly "looks robotic".
- **Climbing.** source, the same slides:
  - "Climbing is algorithmic, using a similar technique to local obstacle avoidance."
  - **The test sequence:** a periodic test for an obstacle ahead; a hull trace for the space above; hull traces "from lowest to highest" for the first clear one (the ledge); a trace down for its exact height; then traces stepping back to find the ledge's forward edge.
  - **The animation:** "Pick closest match animation from dozens of motion captured climb animations of various height increments."
  - This is the one place the slides speak of the infected's animation directly.
- **Size and pacing.** source: a mob is "a large group (20-30)", every 90-180 s on Normal, and 75% come from behind.
- **Blended locomotion in the engine.** source: Valve's wiki, "Blend sequence":
  - Source models blend directional run cycles by pose parameters: `move_x` / `move_y` (9-way), or `move_yaw` (8-way, earlier games).
  - inferred: Left 4 Dead's infected used this. Not confirmed for them by a source I found.

**What I didn't find in a source:**
- how the infected's stumbles, shoves and hit reactions are layered;
- how many run cycles they have.
- So: no source claim about Left 4 Dead hit reactions here.

**What Dreamzone could take (inferred):**
- **The split is already ours.** `chase/` moves every chaser deterministically, and the renderer only poses it. That is Locomotion against Body.
  - It must stay that way: a replayed run must reach the same fingerprint (L162). So animation follows the simulation and never drives it. No root motion.
- **The climb is the clearest lesson.** Left 4 Dead does not make one climb fit every height. It keeps dozens of captured climbs at height steps and picks the nearest.
  - Our chase already knows the height (`link_to - link_from`) and the progress (`link_tick / link_ticks`).
  - It would need to hand them to the renderer: add them to `ChaserPose`.

## 2. Motion matching
**What the sources say:**
- source: Clavet, "Motion Matching and The Road to Next-Gen Animation", GDC 2016 (For Honor). The GDC Vault lists it; the summaries below are from secondary coverage, and I did not watch the talk.
  - The method searches every frame across a large motion-capture database for the frame that best matches the current pose and the wanted future trajectory, and jumps there. The talk called it brute force.
  - It came out of Büttner and Clavet 2015.
- source: Holden et al., "Learned Motion Matching", SIGGRAPH 2020 (Ubisoft La Forge):
  - plain motion matching's memory "scales linearly with the amount of data used";
  - the learned version keeps network weights instead;
  - foot locking and IK are applied afterwards to polish.
- source: Holden's "Code vs Data Driven Displacement" (theorangeduck.com) is about the question that matters here: whether the code or the animation data moves the character. It comes with source code (the orangeduck Motion-Matching repository). I read only its index entry, not the article.
- source: Bollo, "Inertialization", GDC 2018 (Gears of War): transitions are done as a post-process from the old pose's offset and velocity, not by evaluating both animations through a blend.
  - My L161 body already does a form of this (`body.cpp:387-388`).

**What it would need here (inferred):**
1. **A database of captured motion:** minutes of running, turning, stopping and starting, on this skeleton.
   - There is none. The GLBs hold no clips, and the loader reads none.
   - Licences for public datasets not checked.
2. **A feature search every frame per character.**
3. **A displacement rule:** our chasers are moved by code, so matching must follow code-driven displacement, with the clip's drift corrected toward the simulation's position. That is the case Holden's article is about.
4. **A foot-lock and IK pass** after it.

**Fit:** good for one hero character with a lot of data, poor for a pack on our budget.
- The data is the cost, not the code.
- The chasers' motion is already smooth by design: D664's organic pursuit eases turns (`chase/chaser.h`, `turn`).
- So a blend of a few loops would show most of the gain for far less.

## 3. Procedural feet: foot IK and stride warping
**What the sources say:**
- source: Epic's "Pose Warping" documentation (Animation Warping plugin):
  - **Stride warping** "modif[ies] the spacing between foot positions" so an authored stride fits the capsule's actual speed. It takes a per-leg foot definition and a stride scale.
  - **Orientation warping** twists the body toward the direction of travel.
  - Foot placement against the floor is not in that page; Epic does it in Control Rig.
- source: Rosen, "An Indie Approach to Procedural Animation", GDC 2014 (Overgrowth), per the GDC Vault description: fluid, interactive animation from "very few key frames" plus simple procedural techniques.

**What we have (checked above):** stride warping by construction, with no floor under the feet.
- **Stride:**
  - The hunter's gait sets its phase by distance (`stage.cpp:269`), so it can't slide on the flat.
  - My body ties contact length and duty factor to speed (`body.cpp:126-131`, `:239-240`).
- **What's missing:** a per-foot floor height.
  - My body plants on the player's own height (`body.cpp:218`).
  - The hunter plants on nothing (`model_rig.cpp:404-411`).

**What it would need (inferred):**
1. **A floor height under each planted foot:**
   - either a short ray or hull probe down at the foot's planned spot (the simulation's world has the geometry);
   - or the height from the walk graph.
2. **Using it for the plant:**
   - the planted foot's height and slope go into the existing `two_bone`;
   - the hips sink by the lower foot, as my body already does for reach (`body.cpp:411-427`).
3. **The same for the hunter's `gait_pose`:** it then needs the floor, plus the foot plant and hip sink my body has. Today `gait_pose` is pure maths on rest joints.

- **Cost:** a few probes per character per frame on the CPU, and no assets. This is the cheapest of the three.
