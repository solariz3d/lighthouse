## What is in the game now (checked: read at cab3530)
**The models:**
- **The models carry no animation at all.** checked: the GLB JSON headers of `content/models/hunter.glb` and `kid.glb` give `animations 0`.
  - The hunter has 6 meshes and one skin of 53 joints.
  - The kid has 9 meshes and 54 joints: the same plus `eye`.
- **Skeleton:** MPFB's `game_engine` skeleton, with the bone names of the Unreal mannequin family: `pelvis`, `spine_01..03`, `clavicle_l`, `upperarm_l`, `lowerarm_l`, `hand_l`, fingers, `neck_01`, `head`, `thigh_l`, `calf_l`, `foot_l`, `ball_l`.
- **The loader reads no clips.** `model/model.h:1` loads "meshes, materials, images and a skin". A pose comes in only as a palette (`palette_from_local` / `palette_from_world`, `model/model.h:94-96`).

**How the models are posed:**
- `game/model_rig.h:1-17`: the game's 21 procedural joints (`game/body.h` Joint) are retargeted onto the skin by aiming each bone between two joints (`Rig::palette`, `:67`).
  - The kid is posed from my L161 `Body` (`game/world/stage.cpp:298`).
- **The hunter:**
  - It is posed by `gait_pose` (`game/model_rig.h:89-93`; `game/model_rig.cpp:372-429`). The header calls it "Not animation: a stand-in until the chasers have clips".
  - Only the **first** hunter is a model (`stage.cpp:265-273`). Every other chaser is still a box figure, placed by position and heading (`stage.cpp:276-278`).
- **The stand-in gait:**
  - Legs swing by sine about the hips with a bent knee, and arms swing against them (`model_rig.cpp:395-427`).
  - The phase advances by distance (`gait_phase_per_metre`, `:366-370`, called at `stage.cpp:269`), so on flat ground the step length is tied to speed.
  - The feet are placed from the pelvis, not against the floor. inferred: on stairs or a slope they go through the steps or hang above them.

**What the renderer gets per chaser:** `ChaserPose` (`game/world/world_app.h:78-89`) gives position, heading, speed, mode, gait, look (head yaw) and lean.
- The gait says Mesh, Ladder, Drop, Recover, Climb or Off (`chase/chaser.h:57-65`). Recover is "after a drop, or after meeting a wall at speed" (`:61`).
- The chase itself knows a link's endpoints and progress (`chase/chaser.h:88-89`: `link_from`, `link_to`, `link_tick`, `link_ticks`). **These are not passed to the renderer.** A climb's height and how far through it the chaser is never reach `ChaserPose`.

**My L161 body:**
- Its feet are planted by the stride phase on a **flat plane at the player's feet height** (`game/body.cpp:218`: `ground = player.position.y`). It does not probe the floor under each foot.
- Legs and arms reach by `two_bone` with a hinge (`body.cpp:53`, used at `:461`, `:476`).
- Its moves are `BodyMove` (`game/body.h:49`): Run, Air, Slide, Vault, Climb, WallRun, Ladder, Dive, Roll.
- Its targets are eased by inertialization (`kJumpBase`, `kSettleTime`, `body.cpp:25`, `:30`, applied at `:387-388`).
- **The IK hook I named in L161:** `HandOverride` at `game/body.h:85` (it was `:79` at L161; the file has grown). Set by `set_hand_overrides`, `:104-105`; blended in at `game/body.cpp:377-379`. Still unused.

**Cost already measured (checked: my D663 map line, from its timing session):**

## 1. Left 4 Dead's infected
**What the sources say:**
- **Who animates what.** source: Valve's AI slides (Booth, GDC 2009) split the bot into components:
  - Locomotion "owns moving the actor to a new position in the environment (collision resolution, etc)";
  - Body "owns animation state";
  - Vision and Intention.
  - Behaviours hear "OnAnimationActivityComplete, OnAnimationEvent".
  - So the movement code moves the infected, and the animation follows it and reports back.
- **Path following.** source, the same slides:
  - "Reactive Path Following" moves toward a "look ahead" point farther down the path, with local obstacle avoidance.
  - It "is used for all actors in Left 4 Dead", and it "superposes well with mob flocking behavior".
  - Following an optimised path directly "looks robotic".
- **Climbing.** source, the same slides:
  - "Climbing is algorithmic, using a similar technique to local obstacle avoidance."
  - **The test sequence:** a periodic test for an obstacle ahead; a hull trace for the space above; hull traces "from lowest to highest" for the first clear one (the ledge); a trace down for its exact height; then traces stepping back to find the ledge's forward edge.
  - **The animation:** "Pick closest match animation from dozens of motion captured climb animations of various height increments."
  - This is the one place the slides speak of the infected's animation directly.
- **Size and pacing.** source: a mob is "a large group (20-30)", every 90-180 s on Normal, and 75% come from behind.
- **Blended locomotion in the engine.** source: Valve's wiki, "Blend sequence":
  - Source models blend directional run cycles by pose parameters: `move_x` / `move_y` (9-way), or `move_yaw` (8-way, earlier games).
  - inferred: Left 4 Dead's infected used this. Not confirmed for them by a source I found.

**What I didn't find in a source:**
- how the infected's stumbles, shoves and hit reactions are layered;
- how many run cycles they have.
- So: no source claim about Left 4 Dead hit reactions here.

**What Dreamzone could take (inferred):**
- **The split is already ours.** `chase/` moves every chaser deterministically, and the renderer only poses it. That is Locomotion against Body.
  - It must stay that way: a replayed run must reach the same fingerprint (L162). So animation follows the simulation and never drives it. No root motion.
- **The climb is the clearest lesson.** Left 4 Dead does not make one climb fit every height. It keeps dozens of captured climbs at height steps and picks the nearest.
  - Our chase already knows the height (`link_to - link_from`) and the progress (`link_tick / link_ticks`).
  - It would need to hand them to the renderer: add them to `ChaserPose`.

## 2. Motion matching
**What the sources say:**
- source: Clavet, "Motion Matching and The Road to Next-Gen Animation", GDC 2016 (For Honor). The GDC Vault lists it; the summaries below are from secondary coverage, and I did not watch the talk.
  - The method searches every frame across a large motion-capture database for the frame that best matches the current pose and the wanted future trajectory, and jumps there. The talk called it brute force.
  - It came out of Büttner and Clavet 2015.
- source: Holden et al., "Learned Motion Matching", SIGGRAPH 2020 (Ubisoft La Forge):
  - plain motion matching's memory "scales linearly with the amount of data used";
  - the learned version keeps network weights instead;
  - foot locking and IK are applied afterwards to polish.
- source: Holden's "Code vs Data Driven Displacement" (theorangeduck.com) is about the question that matters here: whether the code or the animation data moves the character. It comes with source code (the orangeduck Motion-Matching repository). I read only its index entry, not the article.
- source: Bollo, "Inertialization", GDC 2018 (Gears of War): transitions are done as a post-process from the old pose's offset and velocity, not by evaluating both animations through a blend.
  - My L161 body already does a form of this (`body.cpp:387-388`).

**What it would need here (inferred):**
1. **A database of captured motion:** minutes of running, turning, stopping and starting, on this skeleton.
   - There is none. The GLBs hold no clips, and the loader reads none.
   - Licences for public datasets not checked.
2. **A feature search every frame per character.**
3. **A displacement rule:** our chasers are moved by code, so matching must follow code-driven displacement, with the clip's drift corrected toward the simulation's position. That is the case Holden's article is about.
4. **A foot-lock and IK pass** after it.

**Fit:** good for one hero character with a lot of data, poor for a pack on our budget.
- The data is the cost, not the code.
- The chasers' motion is already smooth by design: D664's organic pursuit eases turns (`chase/chaser.h`, `turn`).
- So a blend of a few loops would show most of the gain for far less.

## 3. Procedural feet: foot IK and stride warping
**What the sources say:**
- source: Epic's "Pose Warping" documentation (Animation Warping plugin):
  - **Stride warping** "modif[ies] the spacing between foot positions" so an authored stride fits the capsule's actual speed. It takes a per-leg foot definition and a stride scale.
  - **Orientation warping** twists the body toward the direction of travel.
  - Foot placement against the floor is not in that page; Epic does it in Control Rig.
- source: Rosen, "An Indie Approach to Procedural Animation", GDC 2014 (Overgrowth), per the GDC Vault description: fluid, interactive animation from "very few key frames" plus simple procedural techniques.

**What we have (checked above):** stride warping by construction, with no floor under the feet.
- **Stride:**
  - The hunter's gait sets its phase by distance (`stage.cpp:269`), so it can't slide on the flat.
  - My body ties contact length and duty factor to speed (`body.cpp:126-131`, `:239-240`).
- **What's missing:** a per-foot floor height.
  - My body plants on the player's own height (`body.cpp:218`).
  - The hunter plants on nothing (`model_rig.cpp:404-411`).

**What it would need (inferred):**
1. **A floor height under each planted foot:**
   - either a short ray or hull probe down at the foot's planned spot (the simulation's world has the geometry);
   - or the height from the walk graph.
2. **Using it for the plant:**
   - the planted foot's height and slope go into the existing `two_bone`;
   - the hips sink by the lower foot, as my body already does for reach (`body.cpp:411-427`).
3. **The same for the hunter's `gait_pose`:** it then needs the floor, plus the foot plant and hip sink my body has. Today `gait_pose` is pure maths on rest joints.

- **Cost:** a few probes per character per frame on the CPU, and no assets. This is the cheapest of the three.

## Added 00:51 (his items 129 and 130): how the animation carries momentum
His test is what the look-behind (V) shows mid-sprint: pursuers that swing wide and slosh around corners behind him, with their weight visible.
- **Whether they actually swing wide** is the path's job (A, `chase/`).
- **What the animation can do** is make the weight read. Four parts, each against cab3530.

### The key finding: the chase already computes the lean and the head's look, and the hunter's model draws neither (checked)
**What's computed:**
- **Lean into the turn:** every chaser gets one from its centripetal acceleration, `atan(speed × turn rate / g)` (`game/world/world_app.cpp:510-512`), interpolated per frame (`:525-526`).
- **The head's look:** it leads the body toward the player (`chase/chaser.cpp:430-434`). With no target it drifts back to the heading (`:355-358`).
- **The turn rate itself:** eased in and out, never a pivot (`chase/chaser.cpp:346-352`).

**Why none of it shows:**
- The hunter is posed by `gait_pose(rest, pos, heading, speed, phase)` (`game/world/stage.cpp:271`). That call carries **no lean and no look**.
- checked: `git show cab3530:game/model_rig.cpp | grep -i look` finds nothing. The only lean in `gait_pose` is a forward lean from speed (`model_rig.cpp:380-381`).
- So the comment at `game/world/world_app.h:86-88`, "the models' head bone turns by it", is not true at cab3530.
- The box figures can't show it either: they are one mesh placed by position and heading (`stage.cpp:276-278`).

**What it would take (inferred):**
- Pass `lean` and `look` into the pose.
- Then:
  - roll the whole body about its travel direction by the lean, from the feet up;
  - set the outside foot wider;
  - turn the head and chest by `look - heading`, the head most.
- **Cost:** code only, a few lines in `gait_pose`, no assets.
- **Why first:** from behind, a body leaning into a curve with its head already round the corner is the plainest sign of momentum. The numbers for it exist and are thrown away.

### 1. Leaning into turns
- **Ours:**
  - **My body** leans by its move, eased by `kLeanTime` 0.08 s (`game/body.cpp:28-29`): forward in a sprint, back 38° in a slide (`:308`). It has no side lean into a turn.
  - **The hunter:** no side lean, as above.
- **Sources:**
  - source: Epic's orientation warping turns the body toward the direction of travel. That is a warp applied to clips we don't have.
  - inferred: for our procedural poses the lean is direct. Roll the pose about the travel axis by the angle the chase already gives.
- **What it would need:** in `gait_pose`, a roll by `lean`.
  - **The pivot is the feet**, so the hips shift to the inside and the feet stay under the line of travel. Without that it reads as a tilt, not a lean.
  - inferred: my body could take the same from the player's own turn rate, computed from `player.velocity` between ticks (`body.cpp:220-221`). The camera stays untouched, as in L161.

### 2. Stumbles and overshoot
**What exists:**
- checked: the chase has a stumble state of sorts. `Gait::Recover` is "after a drop, or after meeting a wall at speed" (`chase/chaser.h:61`), counted by `wall_hits` (`:96`).
- inferred: a corner taken too fast does not stumble. It brakes for the corner (`chase/chaser.cpp:305-308`), and a turn takes speed off by the angle turned (`:364-367`).
- checked: **the chaser always travels along its facing** (`chase/chaser.cpp:376-378`: the step is `heading × speed`). There is no slip.

**What the sources say, and what I infer from them:**
- source: Booth's slides on reactive path following, already cited: a look-ahead point plus local avoidance, so the line is fluid and not the optimal one.
- inferred: the overshoot he wants is that line drifting outside the corner. That comes from the path (A).
- inferred: the animation sells a slide wide in three ways, all over a path that already swings wide:
  1. the head and chest already round the corner (the look) while the hips still travel outward;
  2. the lean;
  3. a planted outside foot that pushes off.
- **A real skid**, facing and travel apart, would need the chase to carry a velocity separate from its heading. That changes `chase/` and its fingerprints: A's call, not the animation's.

**What it would need for a stumble:**
- `ChaserPose` would carry its Recover state with time-in-state.
- A stumble pose would need the same kind of time-in-state. My body has none: its moves are Run, Air, Slide, Vault, Climb, WallRun, Ladder, Dive and Roll (`body.h:49`).
- Options:
  - procedural: the trunk pitching forward, a catching step, the arms out;
  - one or two clips (option C above).
- inferred: a stumble only reads if the hunter does it when he sees it. From the look-behind that means after a missed corner, which needs (A)'s overshoot to exist first.

### 3. Feet under deceleration
**What exists:**
- **Braking:** at the body's `brake` rate, 14 m/s² by default (`chase/archetype.h:43`; `chaser.cpp:361`).
- **The hunter's legs:** its stride is phase per metre, so braking shortens the stride with distance. But the legs keep the running swing with no braking pose (`model_rig.cpp:396-412`), and nothing in the pose reads acceleration.
- **My body:** its duty factor and contact length follow speed (`body.cpp:126-131`), not acceleration.

**What braking would show (inferred):** the trunk leaning back against it, a long front step planted ahead of the hips, and the knee bending as the foot takes the load.

**What it would need:**
- the chaser's acceleration in `ChaserPose` (or the speed change between ticks, which the renderer already has as speed);
- a backward lean scaled by deceleration over g;
- the next planted foot set further ahead.

**Feet that stay put:**
- My body already holds a planted foot where it was put (`body.cpp:243-265`).
- The hunter's feet don't hold anywhere: they are a function of phase.
- A planted-foot hold in `gait_pose`, together with option A's floor probe, is what stops a braking body from skating.

### 4. Speed tied to the gait
**What exists (checked):**
- **The hunter:** one gait.
  - Its stride rate is tied to distance (`stage.cpp:269`).
  - Its swing grows with speed up to 6 m/s (`model_rig.cpp:379`: `run = speed / 6`, clamped).
  - So it can't overstride or slide on the flat. But there is no walk-to-run change of shape, just one shape growing.
- **Its run-up:** the chase's `run_up` adds speed on a long straight and bleeds it in turns (`chaser.cpp:362-370`). That's the momentum in the speed. It isn't in `ChaserPose`, so the pose can't show the hunter "getting going".

**What source and inference say:**
- source: Epic's stride warping fits the stride to the actual speed. Ours does it by construction.
- inferred: momentum reads from the cadence and stride growing over a run-up, then collapsing in a corner. To show it, `ChaserPose` would carry `run_up` (or speed over top speed), and the gait would lengthen its stride and lean further with it.

### A short order for him (options, not a design)
1. **Pass lean and look to the hunter's model** and draw them. Cheapest: the numbers already exist.
2. **Braking lean and planted feet** (with option A's floor probe).
3. **A run-up and stumble on show:** `run_up` and Recover passed to the pose.
4. **A real slide**, facing and travel apart: only if A's path work wants it, since it changes `chase/` and its fingerprints.

Each is checked the same way: look-behind captures of a hunter rounding a corner, before and after. None of this was rendered this lap.
