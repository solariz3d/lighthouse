## 3. What it says

1. **(a) and (b) read alike, so the lead as framed is dead.**
   - Hidden-BelowNormal-capped (A) and plain (B) both lose the 1 ms sleep in 43 to 45% of calls.
   - Hidden at Normal with no cap (C) and the noon conditions (D) read the same.
   - So no start flag, priority or cap separates them.
2. **But the request IS being ignored, in every arm, by the process.**
   - In A to D, a p90 of 15.8 to 16.0 ms is the default 15.6 ms tick (coarsest 156,250 × 100 ns), and a share near 45% of sleeps fall
     on it.
   - This happens while the system's current resolution reads 1 ms. So it is per process, not the machine's timer.
   - E differs from A by one call only, `SetProcessInformation(ProcessPowerThrottling)` with `ControlMask =
     PROCESS_POWER_THROTTLING_IGNORE_TIMER_RESOLUTION, StateMask = 0`. In E, 0 of 9,000 sleeps went over 4 ms, and the pacer drew every
     slot.
   - The page's words for that control (SetProcessInformation, Remarks, updated 2025-07-01, fetched 02:1xZ): "After explicitly
     disabling `PROCESS_POWER_THROTTLING_IGNORE_TIMER_RESOLUTION`, the system remembers and honors any previous timer resolution request
     by the process."
3. **The size matches the benches.**
   - The no-work pacer drew 93.9% to 95.8% of its slots in A to D.
   - C's and E's benches drew 95.9% to 97.0%.
   - inferred: a bench frame has work in it, so fewer `Sleep(1)` calls fall in each slot and fewer slots are lost. The direction and
     size agree; the mechanism is the pacer's reset at :75 after a 15.6 ms sleep.
4. **What is not established:**
   - The page's Windows 11 sentence is about "a window owning process" that is occluded, minimized or non-visible. None of these
     processes owns a window. Yet the request is ignored for them.
   - Whether Windows treats a windowless, silent process as "non-visible and non-audible" by the same rule is what the numbers say,
     not what the page says.
   - Why the share is about 45% and not all is not known.
