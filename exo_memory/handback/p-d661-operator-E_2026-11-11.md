## 1. The documented ground for a Soviet side

- [DOC] **US interest was driven in part by reports of Soviet research.**
  - The US Army's Medical Intelligence Office prepared "Controlled Offensive Behavior - USSR" for the DIA. Its author is Captain
    John D. LaMothe; it was published July 1972 with information to 31 January 1972; task T72-01-14.
  - It has a part on parapsychology in the Soviet Union, and its stated purpose was countermeasures to protect US and allied
    personnel.
  - Source: CIA reading room / The Black Vault, Stargate collection (S5).
- [DOC] **A 1970 best-seller fed the fear of a "psychic arms race":** *Psychic Discoveries Behind the Iron Curtain*, Ostrander and
  Schroeder (Prentice-Hall).
  - Pentagon officials examined it.
  - Its link to the US programs is "not a clear one" (S6).
  - US work began earlier, e.g. the CIA's funding of remote-viewing experiments at SRI under Carl Duckett, its Deputy Director for
    Science and Technology from 1966 (S6).
- [DOC] The US unit ran to 1995 and closed on "no discernible benefit" (D661, S4). The Army sent personnel to Gateway training in 1983
  (D661, S3).
- **Not claimed:** that the KGB ran such a program, what it found, or that anything in the game happened. In the fiction the
  operator's unit is [INV], a small, never-named section, as the US side's own names were changed again and again.

## 2. The operator

**Era:** **1983-1986.** It is the memo's own decade and the middle of the arms-race fear above. Proposed; item 97 lets each character keep
his own era.

**Life path [INV]:**
- born in the early 1950s in a provincial town;
- a conscript, then a career soldier;
- picked out in the late 1970s for a reason never stated (his reports of dreams that came true? the story never says);
- moved into a small unit attached to the security service. The unit is a cellar under an ordinary barracks, not a laboratory.
- He is not a scientist and not a mystic. He is a soldier given an impossible order: go there and bring back what is useful.

**Backstory [INV]:**
- He believes in nothing but the unit. The unit believes in results.
- He has been inside the dreamzone more often than anyone and understands it least.
- His handlers want maps, coordinates and things they can use. What he brings back is the Park, a golden light, and something that
  hunts.
- The reports he files say "nothing of value". That is item 98's "the military trying to control it, something that cannot be truly
  comprehended".

**His room** (item 98):
- living quarters in a barracks basement, possibly a bunker (his "not sure" kept open);
- an iron bed, a footlocker, an exercise mat, a bare bulb;
- a desk with a stack of **fliers**: mimeographed supply notices and requisition lists. He browses them for his own cosmetics (uniform
  pieces, a fur hat, a scarf from home);
- **no PC and no internet.**

**Progression (item 98):**
- **abilities:** he meditates, as the kid eventually does. His "chair" is a metal stool facing the wall, the unit's drill;
- **trinkets:** through the unit's **supply**, a counter where he trades cash and energy (item 67's two currencies) for objects the
  unit has recovered and cannot explain;
- **the story it tells:** a state that keeps a ledger of what it does not understand.

**Way in [INV]:** neither the kid's sleep nor the writer's tapes. **The drill:** a timed breath count in a dark room, on order.
- He enters at will, which no one else can.
- He cannot choose where he lands or how long he stays; the unit's stopwatch decides that.
- So his runs start on command, which is exactly how a run starts. The game's frame becomes his fiction.

**Unique buff, proposed: "Discipline."**
- His stress builds **25 % slower**.
- At full stress, his Virtue roll (item 32's 25 %) is **35 %**: a trained mind breaks less often.
- He is the steady one, not the fast one.

**Biome set [INV], his territory:**
1. **the barracks:** long corridors of bunks, a drill hall (an atrium);
2. **the interception post:** a room of radio sets and headphones, in rows, all hissing;
3. **the rail depot in snow:** platforms, carriages, a roundhouse (it gives the biome field a hall with tracks);
4. **the canteen hall:** long tables, a serving hatch;
5. **the apartment block of his childhood:** prefabricated, stairwells that repeat;
6. **the parade square in fog:** his rare "outside".

Kept away from the show's marks: no mall, no numbered children, no other-world copy of a town.

**What his story discloses (to the others):**
