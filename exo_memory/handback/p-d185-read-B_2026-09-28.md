### 2.1 · The geometric rule is VOID: it fails with no brush at all
- I ran the same rule on the UNBRUSHED base, at the same windows: it **fails 20 of 30**, on every 25% and 75% window.
- On a curving base, the step between consecutive discrete-κ samples is the base's own curvature slope, so the rule reads
  WHERE the window sits, not what the brush did.
- **A rule that fails its own control cannot discriminate.** This confirms E's finding (E §3.1: "20 of 30", and 19 of 30 on the
  brushed track; mine is 19 of 30 too). The defect is mine.

### 2.2 · The κv rigid clause is WRONG for the builder's frame, and the core is exactly local
- **Registered BEFORE running the check, with its falsifier** (`kv_diag.js` header): after the window, the heading θ is
  unchanged and the pitch p is the old pitch plus ONE constant. It is falsified if θ changes, or if p − p_old varies by more than
  1e-9 rad.
- **Result on all 6 κv brushes** (1,734–6,006 samples each):
  - max heading change **4.4e-16 rad**;
  - pitch offset constant to a spread of **≤ 8.0e-16 rad** (offsets 0.034–0.097 rad).
- **So the core is local in its CHANNELS, to machine precision.**
- But in the gravity frame (references/02 §2: T = (cos p sin θ, sin p, cos p cos θ)), adding a constant to p is not a rigid motion
  of a curving track: it rescales the horizontal by cos(p + Δp)/cos p. My sealed "downstream moved rigidly" was true for κh (a yaw
  about world up IS a rotation: 2.2e-12 m) and false for κv. **E reported 3a passing on all 30.** The difference is that E did not
  apply the rigid clause the way it was sealed; my measure did, and it fails 6/6.

### 2.3 · A DESIGN finding in 2.2, for the chair and A/E (not a code defect)
**A κv brush, as the spec defines it, does not make a "hill".** Adding Δ·f to the pitch RATE leaves a permanent pitch offset
∫Δκv (here 2–5.6°), so the WHOLE rest of the track tilts, 0.5–4 km of it. The spec's "push a hill up" is a local rise and fall
of height, which needs a pitch-rate bump with ZERO net integral (up, then down), or a sculpt of height itself.
- The κh brush has the same shape: it rotates the whole rest of the track by ∫Δκh, rigidly. Close then pulls it back, spreading
  the fix over the parts not edited last.
- Whether that is what "sculpt" should feel like is a UX call; it is the keeper's test.
