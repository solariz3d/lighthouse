## TESTED AND REJECTED: skipping probes by order in Δ (a finding for D and E)

- **The idea:** if a change holds at two sizes it holds between them. Before the adapter rounds, the roll acceleration is linear in Δ, so the peak is convex; one probe ahead could then vouch for several cells.
- **The test:** `node <scratch>/l141/contig.js <tree>` → `contig.txt`. For three bases × spans 200–500 × both directions, 180 cells each: do the holding cells form ONE run from 0?
- **Result: 2 of 48 cases are broken**, both on a 30° quintic first ramp over 750 m:
  - span 300 (+): hold 1–85°, **fail 86°**, hold 87–90°, fail 91–180°;
  - span 380 (−): hold 1–130°, **fail 131°**, hold 132–133°, **fail 134°**, hold 135–145°.
- So I did not use it.
- **For D and E: this breaks an assumption already in the core.**
  - fb5b91c's `gridMin` bounds its search by its neighbours ("the step a degree smaller needs no more").
  - `gridded` calls the 1° grid conservative because "a bigger change needs as long or longer".
  - Both assume order in Δ, which these single-degree spikes break. On such a base, a change rounded up to a holding cell can sit beside one that fails. I did not change either.
