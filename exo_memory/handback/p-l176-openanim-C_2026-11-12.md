## 1. Overgrowth (Apache-2.0): the closest match to our rig
**How it leans (source).** The lean comes from the body's **velocity and acceleration**, not its turn rate.
- **`HandleAccelTilt`** (`Data/Scripts/aschar.as:7921-7940`): on the ground and moving, `target_tilt = velocity × 0.5 / scale`. Added to it, `accel_tilt` is the per-frame velocity change, low-passed by `mix(…, accel_tilt, pow(0.95, frames))`.
- **`UpdateTilt`** (`:4236-4241`): the drawn tilt eases toward that target by `pow(0.9, frames)` on the ground (0.95 in the air).
- **What it gives (inferred):** at 120 frames a second those are time constants of about 0.16 s on the acceleration and 0.08 s on the tilt. The body leans into a turn (centripetal acceleration), back when it brakes and forward when it starts, all from one signal.

**How the feet meet the floor (source):** `Source/Objects/movementobject.cpp:2540-2686` (foot IK).
- **The probe:** a swept sphere from 0.2 m above each foot to 0.6 m below (`:2619-2621`).
- **The ground height:** limited to ±0.1 m from the previous frame's, then eased toward it by `pow(0.8, frames)` (`:2627-2630`).
- **The foot's tilt:** turned to the ground's normal, eased the same way (`:2646-2660`).
- **One foot on nothing:** with ground under only one foot, the other is moved by the same offset (`:2673-2677`).
- **Balancing:** the hips, chest, head and arms take a share of the feet's offset (0.7, 0.5, 0.4, 1.0; `:2680-2685`).
- **The stepping planner** is in `aschar.as:11726-11831` (HandleFootStance): feet planted until the body moves far enough, then each steps in a sine arc.

**How it climbs (source):** `Data/Scripts/ledgegrab.as`.
- **The grips:** each hand and foot is a `MovingGrip` (`:29-111`). It moves from grip to grip on a straight line plus a sine arc (`:97-111`). It bears weight on a "sin valley" while moving, `1 − sin(progress × π)` (`:85-95`).
- **The rhythm** is set at the start (`:139-145`): hands at phases 0 and 0.5, feet at 0.1 and 0.6. Hands lift 0.1 m; feet pull 0.1 m off the wall.
- **The body offset** is the weight-weighted average of the grips (`:248-289`). It blends from where the grab began by `transition_progress` (`:183`, `:289-293`).
- **Each hand** probes the ledge's surface with a swept sphere along down-and-in, and keeps its last grip when the probe finds nothing (`:197-215`).
- **The lagged velocity** is low-passed by `pow(0.95, frames)` (`:172`).

**Turning a facing (source):** `Data/Scripts/interpdirection.as:25-41`. Directions blend by a normalised lerp. When the two are nearly opposite (dot < −0.8), the turn goes through a perpendicular axis, so a 180° turn never collapses through zero.

**What fits our rig (inferred):**
- **The hunter's lean (item 146, and L173's open dead zone):** Overgrowth draws the lean from the acceleration of the velocity, and we could too: from the chaser's position change per tick, low-passed, then the tilt eased.
  - It would replace L173's chain: turn rate taken back out of `tan(lean)`, a spring, then a dead zone (`game/world/world_app.cpp:581`).
  - **It brings braking and starting lean for free:** L169's items 129–130, which we haven't built.
  - **It does not by itself remove the sway.** At 11 m/s the sway's lateral acceleration is still a few m/s² (inferred from L173's figures). So it still needs a soft knee or the sway reduced at its source in `chase/` (A's).
  - It is the better signal for a lean because it's the physical one.
- **The un-eased yaw:** `InterpDirections`' trick for near-opposite facings is the one thing an eased yaw needs that a plain angle spring gets wrong. Our angle springs wrap at π (`wrap_angle`), so a heading spring at 180° picks a side arbitrarily.
- **Feet:** the per-frame clamp (±0.1 m) and ease on each foot's ground height are exactly what L170's floor probe lacks.
  - The hunter's `gait_pose` has no memory (`game/model_rig.cpp:408` apply_motion), so a foot can jump a step's height in one frame (my L170 "not verified").
  - The body's floor probe (`game/body.cpp:209`) is eased only by the targets' inertialization.
  - The ground-normal foot tilt is new to us.
- **The climb (L161's weak spot, `game/body.cpp:332-335`):** our climb holds both hands on the ledge while the body rises. Overgrowth's grips give:
  - **a rhythm:** staggered hand and foot phases;
  - **weight:** the body's offset is the grips' weighted average, so it sags toward the hand that holds;
  - **arcs** for the moving limb;
  - **a surface probe per hand** with a remembered grip, which our `HandOverride` hook (`game/body.h:93`) was built to take.
  - This is the strongest single lesson in the sources for the climb.

## 2. ozz-animation (MIT)
**Foot IK, in order (source):** `samples/foot_ik/sample_foot_ik.cc:113-136`.
1. A ray down from 0.5 m above each ankle (`:96-97`, `:182-205`).
2. **The ankle target on a slope** (`:207-255`): the hit point plus the hit normal times the foot's height, then corrected along the slope so the foot lies on it, not in it.
3. **The pelvis offset** (`:257-285`): moved down by the most any ankle must drop, so the lowest foot reaches and the other is reached by IK.
4. Two-bone IK, then an aim for the ankle (`:287+`).

**The two-bone knobs (source):** `include/ozz/animation/runtime/ik_two_bone_job.h:85-100`.
- **`soften`:** softens the last degrees before the chain goes straight, so a knee doesn't snap at full reach.
- **`weight`:** fades IK in and out.
- **`pole_vector`** and **`twist_angle`:** set the knee's direction.

**Look-at over a chain (source):** `samples/look_at/sample_look_at.cc:106-160`. The aim is spread from the spine to the head, each joint given a weight (`:145-149`); the last joint always takes 1 so the head ends on target.

**What fits (inferred):**
- **Feet:** our L170 `apply_motion` already sinks the hips for the lower foot. It doesn't put the ankle on the slope's normal, and it has no `soften`. Our `reach_leg` (`game/model_rig.cpp:387`) clamps the leg straight at 99.99% of reach: the snap ozz avoids.
- **The look (L173's 619°/s):** ozz spreads a turn over several joints with weights. We split it two ways, chest 35%, head the rest (`apply_motion`), and also ease it with one spring (`world_app.cpp:563`). A three- or four-joint spread (spine_02, spine_03, neck, head) at lower weights each would make a fast look read as a turn of the whole upper body, not a whipping head. inferred: the 619°/s worst is a head-rate figure, not a body one.
- **The first-person body (item 147, look-back clipping into the neck and shoulder):** the same weighted chain answers part of it.
  - Our glance turns the chest a fixed 50° and the head the rest (`game/body.cpp:145-153`).
  - inferred: the clipping is the camera meeting the kid's own shoulder and collar as the chest lags the view. A chain that turns the shoulders further with the view, or one that drops the shoulders from the eye's ray group in the glance (as D663 did for the head), would clear it.
  - The sources don't address a first-person camera inside its own body. That part is mine.

## 3. Daniel Holden (MIT): springs, inertialization, motion matching
**Exact springs (source):** `orangeduck/Motion-Matching/spring.h`.
- `damper_exact`, by **half-life** (`:9-21`), for float, vec3 and quaternion (shortest slerp, `:19-21`).
- `simple_spring_damper_exact`: a critically damped spring solved in closed form, so exact at any frame rate (`:63-110`), with `halflife_to_damping` (`:41-44`).
- `decay_spring_damper_exact` (`:115-150`).
- Inertialization's transition and update (`:162-211`).

**More springs (source):** `orangeduck/Spring-It-On`.
- `velocityspring.c:5`: a spring that tracks a goal moving at a set velocity.
- `doublespring.c:5`: two springs in series, giving a response with no kink in its rate.
- `controller.c:5-45`: a spring-driven character, with its velocity, acceleration and predicted trajectory.
- `timedspring.c`, `extrapolation.c`, `tracking.c` (titles only; not read this lap).

**Motion matching:** the `Motion-Matching` repository (`database.h`, `controller.cpp`, `lmm.h`). It is the example code for "Code vs Data Driven Displacement" (L169). Its data is LAFAN1, so **its data can't ship**.

**What fits (inferred):**
- **L173's springs:**
  - **The integration:** ours is a semi-implicit step at the fixed 240 Hz tick (`world_app.cpp:566`), which is fine at a fixed tick.
  - **The half-life form** would make the tunables seconds he can read ("the lean takes 0.2 s to halve the gap"), not an ω.
  - **The rate cap:** I capped the lean's rate with a hard clamp (kLeanMaxRate, `:562`), which puts a kink in its acceleration where it binds. A double spring removes that kink. A velocity spring reaches the goal at a bounded speed by construction.
- **The look and the yaw:** a quaternion (or unit-direction) damper with shortest-path slerp is the right tool for both. It never takes the long way round, unlike an angle spring at ±π. That would let the body yaw be eased safely, which L173 left alone.
- **Lean from acceleration** (Overgrowth's signal) pairs with `controller.c`'s spring character: the eased velocity's acceleration is a clean lean signal.
- **Motion matching:** as L169 found, out of reach without our own data. The study-only LAFAN1 is what his code runs on.

## 4. Godot (MIT) and O3DE (Apache-2.0 / MIT): blend spaces, briefly
**How Godot does it (source):** `scene/animation/animation_blend_space_2d.cpp`.
- Blend points sit in a 2D space; they are Delaunay-triangulated (`:490-515`).
- At run time the triangle holding the blend position gives three barycentric weights (`:645-700`, `_blend_triangle`), or one point in its discrete modes (`:648`, `:678-685`).

O3DE: licence only; its EMotionFX blend space was not read.

**What fits (inferred):** our `Body` blends whole moves by one weight each (`BodyMove`, eased by kBlendTime). A 2D space suits locomotion within one move, if he wants more shapes: run poses at points of (speed, lateral acceleration), weighted barycentrically. Procedurally that means a few parameter sets (stride, lean, arm swing, crouch) at the corners of a triangle mesh, not clips. It's the structure Option C of L169 would need once any clips exist.
