## 0. The answer first

**A props catalogue plugs in cleanly, but the first PAID pack is not worth building yet.** Five pieces of plumbing it needs do not exist:

1. **Ground.** Nothing in the export stands on the ground. The only ground is the preview's editor grid (§3).
2. **LODs.** Every kn5 mesh is written with lodIn 0 and lodOut 0 (§4).
3. **Compressed textures.** DDS is uncompressed on purpose, about 5.6 MB per 1024² map (§4).
4. **A merge class for props.** Without one, every placed prop is its own draw call (§4).
5. **Props in the preview.** The renderer draws the track only, with no instancing (§2).

Each of these is free-tier builder code. The pack is content poured into them.

My recommendation (moderate confidence):
- **Step 0:** build the plumbing with two free race-furniture props that ARCHITECTURE already promises. These are the start gantry and a sector board (`docs/ARCHITECTURE.md:157`).
- Neither needs ground, because both hang on the road.
- Drive the result in AC.
- **Step 1:** build the paid pack (§5) only once that works and part 1's budgets are known.

Two things would change my mind:
- A's part 1 says AC instances a repeated `models.ini` model cheaply. Then the props could ship as their own kn5 and most of the cost in §4 goes away.
- The keeper wants the pack mainly for screenshots, not for driving. Then the LOD and draw-call costs matter less.

## 1. Where a prop catalogue plugs in

### 1a. Placement by station and offset: the code already has it

- **The surface point.** `surfaceAt(path, segments, s, u)` returns the point on the road surface at distance s along the road and arc length u across it, with its normal (`src/markers/place.js:50-62`).
  - It is the surface the mesh builds, including a font transition's blend (`place.js:7-12`, `profileAt` :34-39).
  - It returns `span: [u0, u1]`, the cross-section's two edges (`place.js:61`).
- **A placed frame.** `placeMarker` turns that point into a frame: position h above the surface along its normal, forward along the road in the surface plane, and left completing a right-handed frame. It hands back the kn5 matrix too (`place.js:64-69`).
  - A prop needs exactly this frame. Today's markers are the same thing with a dummy node instead of a mesh (`src/markers/index.js:56`).
- **Anchoring that survives edits.** Markers are anchored to a word id plus metres into it, so editing upstream carries them along (`src/markers/layout.js:16-19`, `anchorS` :80-85).
  - On an equation-core track the anchor is a piece id plus `along` (`src/core/document.js:258-264`, the spawns block).
  - **A prop row should use the same anchor:** `{ kind, piece|word, along, u | edge, offsetM, h, yawDeg, mirror }`, plus a repeat rule `{ everyM, fromAlong, toAlong }` for barriers and lights.
- **Where it lives.** Spawns established that an absent field leaves an old file byte for byte the same (`document.js:255-258`; `checkSpawns` :266-282; `normSpawns` :283-289). A `props` block should follow that rule: validate, quantise, and say nothing in a file that never had one.
- **A missing anchor.** It should be said and not refused, as for spawns: "Pieces are NOT required to exist here … The export says so" (`document.js:264`). The marker checks turn a missing anchor into a named red (`src/markers/checks.js:43`). For a prop, I'd make it amber, not red, because a lost tree must not block an export.

### 1b. Snapping to the road edge: easy to compute, hard to mean

- **The edge itself.** It is `span[0]` (right) or `span[1]` (left) of `surfaceAt` (`place.js:61`; the gates already use it, `layout.js:98-99`).
- **The catch.** On a T-180 profile the edge is the TOP OF THE WALL, not a kerb.
  - The cross-section's turning angle may pass 90° (a wall past vertical) or 180° (a tube's roof) (`src/geom/profile.js:3`, :11).
  - So the surface normal at the edge can point sideways or down, and "h above the surface" there stands a prop out of the wall, not up.
  - On a closed tube there is no edge.
- **What snapping needs.** A prop needs its own up rule:
  - **(a) "on the surface":** the surface normal. This is right for paint, chevrons on the wall face, and lights in a tube.
  - **(b) "on the edge, standing up":** world +y at the edge point, pushed out by `offsetM` along the horizontal part of L. This is right for barriers and light poles on an open road.
  - **(c) "under the road":** down from the underside skin. This is for pylons (§3).
- The marker checks already measure "points out of the surface" with a 15° bar (`checks.js:78`). A prop check would reuse that bar per up rule.

### 1c. Mirroring: two separate things

- **The placement mirror** maps u → −u, or the edge to the opposite span edge. Spans are not always symmetric, so it must be by edge and not by u.
  - The gate placement already does the left/right pair this way (`layout.js:98-99`, the L gate at `span[1] − inset`, R at `span[0] + inset`).
  - The check that L sits left of R (`checks.js:60-70`) is the pattern for a mirrored pair.
- **The mesh mirror** (an asymmetric prop such as an arrow board) flips the prop's local left axis.
  - That reverses winding, and kn5 stores CCW triangles as given (`src/export/kn5write.js:10-12`). So a mirrored copy must also swap two indices per triangle, as `underskin.js:79` does when it turns the road over.
  - Forgetting this draws the mirrored prop inside-out under backface culling. That is inferred: the culling state of AC's track shaders is part 1's.
- **Mirror on the piece side.** "Mirror" exists today only for saved pieces (`app/core/coreshell.js:1044-1051`, `PC.insert(…, { mirror })`). A prop row's `mirror: true` is new.

## 2. What the preview would need to show props

- **Today the preview draws the track only.** "The track only: no environment" (`app/preview/renderer.js:3-4`).
  - Its batches come from the road mesh (`app/preview/trackmodel.js:33`, `batches.js:21`).
  - The start, grid and pits are drawn as a 2-D canvas over the view, not as 3-D (`app/core/spawnslayer.js:1-8`).
  - The old markers panel's paint meshes were never wired to the preview: "Neither is wired yet" (`app/markers/index.js:9-12`). No listener for `t180:markers` exists outside `app/markers` (grep, §7).
- **What it needs:**
  1. **A props pass in the renderer.** It would draw each prop mesh once, with its placements' matrices.
     - The renderer has no instancing today (0 files under `app/preview` mention it, §7). It is WebGL1 or 2 (`renderer.js:9`).
     - Without instancing, 500 placed props are 500 draws or one re-merged buffer per edit.
     - The renderer's precedent is merge-by-chunk (`renderer.js:144`, `CHUNKS`). That works for static props, but every edit that moves a piece re-uploads every chunk it moved (`renderer.js:31-36`).
  2. **The AC look comes almost free.** The renderer draws exported materials with the ported AC shaders (`renderer.js:15-19`).
     - Only `ksPerPixel`, `ksPerPixelNM` and `ksMultilayer` are ported (`app/preview/acshaders.js:1`, :109).
     - Alpha test IS supported: `discard` below `ksAlphaRef` when the material is alpha-tested (`acshaders.js:52`; uniforms `renderer.js:311`). So card foliage can preview.
     - `ksTree`, which ARCHITECTURE names (`docs/ARCHITECTURE.md:95`), is not ported. A pack that uses it previews wrong until it is.
  3. **Picking and dragging.** `app/core/spawndrag.js:11-16` is the pattern: `pickStation` turns the cursor into a path distance. A prop drag also needs u (across) from the cursor. That is new, a ray onto the surface.
  4. **The frame bar.** The previewer's bar is 6.0 ms p95 at 165 Hz, held by `app/test/drag-frame.test.js` (`docs/ARCHITECTURE.md:230-232`). The measured headroom after D404 is 3.5–4.8 ms on 1–2 km drags (`:241`).
     - Inferred: a props pass must fit in about 1–2 ms, or be dropped while a handle is held, as the ghost is coarsened today (`:237-238`).
  5. **Ground in the preview.** The preview's ground grid at y = 0 is editor UI, "not scenery" (`app/preview/look.js:12`; `renderer.js:3-4`). It cannot double as a ground, because the export has none (§3).

## 3. The missing ground: the largest builder-side gap

- **No ground in the export.** Nothing the export writes is ground. The scene is the road cells, the underside skin, the catch, the markers, the paint and the pit lane (`src/export/fromwords.js:283-303`).
  - The road is "one single-sided, zero-thickness shell" (`src/export/underskin.js:12`).
  - No file in `src/export` mentions a ground (grep, §7).
- **What T-180 tracks look like.** They float, loop, tube and fall hundreds of metres. My D444 lap ends 431 m below its start (`exo_memory/map/O.md`, D444 entry).
- **The consequence.** A tree, a grandstand or a pylon has nothing to stand on. A pack needs either:
  - **(a) A ground plane** at a level the user sets, with a check that the road never passes through it. The self-intersection BVH exists for the road (`fromwords.js:29-33`), so the check has a home, but a plane under a 431 m drop is either far below most of the lap or cuts through it.
  - **(b) Props that hang on the road.** These are gantries, sector boards, edge barriers, lights on the wall top, chevrons on the wall face, and ring gates over the road.
    - Plus **pylons**, which run from the underside skin (0.5 m under the road, `underskin.js:32`) down to the ground plane. ARCHITECTURE names "supports and pylons for floating track … ground under grounded sections" as open (`docs/ARCHITECTURE.md:286-287`).
- **Why the first pack should be mostly (b).** The ground is a design question for the keeper, not a catalogue entry.

## 4. What the export would need to write props

### Naming
- **Physics is a leading digit.** "THE ROAD is every physics mesh named `<digit><KEY>` whose KEY is not WALL" (`src/export/markers.js:14`; `isDrivable` :62).
- **Two other things read that name:**
  - The track map is rasterised from the drivable meshes (`src/export/trackfiles.js:127-131`).
  - Validation's downforce-ray check reads them as the road (`fromwords.js:234`).
- So a wrongly named prop becomes road, on the map and in validation. Inferred: it would also be driven on.
- **Proposed names:**
  - **Visual props:** `PROP_<kind>_<n>`, no leading digit. This follows the precedent of `UNDERSKIN_…`, which is scenery because it has no digit and no `1ROAD` (`underskin.js:19-21`).
  - **Collidable props:** `1WALL_PROP_<kind>_<n>`, which follows the test export's end wall `1WALL_T180_END` (`fromwords.js:90`).
  - The soft-collision block covers `1ROAD?` only (`trackfiles.js:23`), so a WALL prop is a hard wall. Whether AC's own `WALL` surface is right is part 1's.
- **Never `seam` in a prop name.** `weldSeams` and `findFins` take any mesh named `…seam…` for a road zipper (`src/export/acready.js:145`, :109). `underskin.js:20` and `catch.js:101` both rename around it.
- **Never an `AC_` prefix.** `flattenForAc` keeps an `AC_` node's matrix as a marker (`acready.js:63`), and duplicate `AC_` names refuse the export (`fromwords.js:306-307`).

### Materials and textures
- **The format.** The scene's material is `{ name, shader, alphaBlend 0|1|2, alphaTested, depthMode, props[], samplers[] }`, with textures as named Buffers (`src/export/scene.js:5-8`, validated :48-67). `writeKn5` embeds every texture in the kn5 (`kn5write.js:110`). Foliage needs nothing new in the format.
- **Texture size is the cost.**
  - Textures are written as **uncompressed DDS** with full mips, "Uncompressed on purpose" (`src/texture/dds.js:1-8`).
  - Measured by formula (§7): one map of 512² is 1,398,228 bytes, 1024² is 5,592,532 and 2048² is 22,369,748.
  - Inferred: ten props, each with a 1024² diffuse and a normal map, add about 112 MB to every exported kn5.
  - Precedent that size is felt: "a full-size copy of a tube doubled the kn5" (`underskin.js:35-36`).
  - Block compression (BC1/BC3) means a new encoder. `dds.js` reads back only 32-bit uncompressed levels (`dds.js:79`), and the comment says normal maps cannot be compressed at all (`dds.js:7-8`).
- **Solid fills.** `ensureDiffuse` fills any material without `txDiffuse` with a solid 4×4 grey (`acready.js:193-207`). A prop with no texture still renders lit, which is useful for a first untextured gantry.

### Shadow casters and LODs
- **Shadows are a flag per mesh** (`scene.js:96-97`; `kn5write.js:83`).
  - Precedents: the underside skin casts (`underskin.js:75`), and the catch and the paint do not (`catch.js:69`, `src/markers/paint.js:44`).
  - Gantries, pylons and grandstands cast. Chevrons, cards and decals do not. Inferred: foliage cards casting is a shadow-map cost in AC, which is part 1's to bound.
- **LODs are not written.** `writeKn5` writes `layer 0, lodIn 0, lodOut 0` for every mesh: "the values both reference tracks use for nearly every mesh" (`kn5write.js:16`, :99).
  - A props pack needs `lodOut` per mesh at least (a tree gone at 300 m). That is a new optional mesh field, validated in `scene.js` and written at `kn5write.js:99`.
  - ARCHITECTURE asks for LODs (`docs/ARCHITECTURE.md:216`).

### Draw calls, merging and vertex budgets
- **Everything is flattened.** `flattenForAc` bakes every mesh into world space under identity (`acready.js:32-67`). The kn5 has no instancing, so every placed copy costs its full vertex count in the file.
- **Props are not merged.** `mergeForAc` joins only the road, the skin and the catch (`MERGE_CLASSES`, `acready.js:233`), so props would be one draw call each.
  - The precedent that this costs frames: 15,271 meshes and "the user's frame rate dropped" (`acready.js:13-16`).
  - A `PROP_<kind>_` class keyed by material and flags fits `mergeForAc`'s design as is (`acready.js:244-253`). The cap is 32,768 vertices and 400 m per chunk (`:238`), and a mesh may hold at most 65,535 vertices (`scene.js:18`).
- **The alternative.** Props in their own kn5 (or one kn5 per prop), listed in `models.ini`.
  - `modelsIni` already takes several kn5 files and writes `POSITION`/`ROTATION` for each (`trackfiles.js:87-93`).
  - Whether AC instances a repeated model cheaply, and whether ROTATION can carry a banked frame, are **part 1's**. If yes, the road kn5 stays as it is and props become a second file.

### Files and re-export
- **Re-export cleanup.** It removes only paths listed in the previous marker (`src/export/layouts.js:24-27`; `fromwords.js:385`). A props kn5 or a textures folder must be listed there, or a re-export leaves the old one behind.
- **The lap hash.** It is "the SHAPE alone" (`fromwords.js:117-131`), so visual props rightly do not reset ghost laps. A collidable prop that moves changes the lap without changing the hash. Inferred: rare enough to say once and not to fix first.
- **No CSP config today.** "this exporter writes none today" (`layouts.js:20-21`). Night lights in CSP need a new file, and its contents are part 1's.

## 5. The smallest first pack: ten props and one environment

The pack is ordered so the first five need no ground. It is "smallest worth paying for", not cheapest: each prop is there because a T-180 track lacks it today.

1. **Start/finish gantry.** It is promised as "optional race furniture" (`docs/ARCHITECTURE.md:157`) and anchors on `AC_TIME_0`, which every export already places (`layout.js:99`). It is the first thing a driver sees. It spans the cross-section, so it needs the span (`place.js:61`) and no ground.
2. **Sector board** (×2, at `AC_TIME_1/2`). Also promised at :157. The sector gates exist (`layout.js:174-185`), so placement is automatic.
3. **Edge barrier / catch fence** (repeating, collidable `1WALL_PROP_…`). ARCHITECTURE names "catch walls and barriers" (`:286`). It is the one prop with a safety meaning, because a T-180 leaving the road has nothing to hit today.
4. **Wall-top light** (repeating, up rule b). It gives night tracks something to drive by. Its CSP light config is part 1's, but the mesh is ours.
5. **Corner chevron board** (on the wall face, up rule a, mirrored by edge). It shows the turn's direction at speed. It is the asymmetric prop that proves the mesh mirror (§1c).
6. **Speed ring / arch over the road** (repeating). ARCHITECTURE §8 wants "how often repeated objects pass per second, at the design speed" (`:210-211`). A ring every N m is that instrument, and it needs no ground.
7. **Support pylon** (up rule c, from the underside skin down to the ground). "supports and pylons for floating track" (`:286`). It is the first prop that needs the environment's ground plane, and it makes a floating track read as built.
8. **Grandstand** (start straight, grounded). The cheapest "this is a venue" signal. Only where the start straight is near the ground.
9. **Pit garage block** (beside the pit lane). The pit lane and its boxes exist (`src/export/pitlane.js:1-12`; `pitboxes.js`), and the boxes have nothing behind them.
10. **Tree card** (alpha-tested billboard, grounded). It is the cheapest ground cover, and the preview can already alpha-test it (`acshaders.js:52`). Sakura's "tree tunnels" are the precedent for close scenery that sells speed (`docs/FINDINGS.md:141`). Its cost is overdraw: "Watch foliage overdraw" (`ARCHITECTURE.md:215`).

**The one environment: a ground plane and a horizon ring, matched to the Skyline look.**
- **The ground plane** is a tiled plane at a user-set level, refused where the road passes through it. Every grounded prop (7–10) needs it.
- **The horizon ring** is a low distant silhouette mesh. The sky itself is CSP's weather (part 1).
- **The match:** the builder's six looks are preview skins only. Each names a GLSL sky drawn at infinity (`app/preview/sky/index.js:1-4`; styles `:30`, `none` plus six). None reaches the export. "Skyline" (`app/themes/skyline.css:16`, `--sky-style: sun`) is the plain-daylight one, so the first environment should match what the user already previews in it.

## 6. What is risky or expensive, named

1. **No ground (§3).** It is a design decision before it is code, and a T-180 lap's height range makes one plane wrong for some laps. **Highest risk.**
2. **Texture weight (§4).** About 5.6 MB per 1024² map uncompressed, in every export, unless a BC encoder is written (new, lossy, and normal maps excluded per `dds.js:7-8`).
3. **Draw calls (§4).** A `PROP_` merge class is cheap to add. Without it, a 500-tree track repeats D260. If props get their own kn5 via `models.ini`, the cost moves to whatever AC does with repeated models (part 1).
4. **Preview frame bar (§2).** The renderer has no instancing, the bar is 6.0 ms, and the headroom is about 1–2.5 ms on ordinary drags. The props pass must be cheap or yield during drags. This is checked by `drag-frame.test.js`, which runs under the heavy-run lock.
5. **The kn5 writer is "prove it" tier.** LOD fields, a prop merge class and a mirrored mesh all touch `kn5write.js`/`scene.js`/`acready.js`, the files every export goes through. Mutation runs apply.
6. **Physics by name (§4).** One wrong prefix puts a prop on the map, in validation and on the drivable set. A check that every prop name is `PROP_…` or `1WALL_PROP_…` belongs in `validateScene`'s caller, the way `AC_` duplicates are refused (`fromwords.js:306-307`).
7. **Paid content travels.** A pack's textures are embedded in the user's exported kn5 (`kn5write.js:110`), and a shared track carries them. The builder's existing pack format is a shareable text file with images embedded: "ONE shareable file … import packs other users made" (`src/doc/packs.js:1-2`). A paid pack built the same way is shared the same way. Licensing is part 1's, but this is a builder-side fact the business model has to meet.
8. **Edge semantics on walls past vertical (§1b).** The "edge" of a half-pipe or tube is not where a barrier goes. Up rules a/b/c are needed before any edge prop, or barriers will stand out of overhanging walls.
