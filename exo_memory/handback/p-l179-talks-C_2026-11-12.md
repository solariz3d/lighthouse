## 2. The horde games: how a crowd's bodies show momentum cheaply
- **Variation per body:**
  - **press,** Days Gone: the horde is eight meshes with "size, height, speed, etc." varied.
  - **read in half 1,** Valve: `ENTINDEX_PLAYBACK_RATE`, "played back at different rates based on entindex" (`NextBotBodyInterface.h:73`, Source 1 SDK licence: study only).
  - Two independent mentions of the cheapest trick: no two bodies step in time.
- **Group and individual:** **press**, Days Gone and World War Z: bodies "move as one collective horde" or "break off as individual units". This is behaviour, not animation; nothing readable says how it is drawn.
- **Momentum after a hit:** **read**, Techland (The Beast, 2025): "Enemies now use active ragdolls, meaning their bodies continue to react to momentum even after being struck". The same post says their locomotion was refined so the apex chasers "overcome obstacles seamlessly", and that the work "led to a complete overhaul of animation and physics in the C-Engine".
  - inferred: active ragdolls are not cheap. That's a physics-driven body, not a pose trick.
- **Level of detail:** the Assassin's Creed Unity talk exists and is about exactly this (AI level of detail for thousands of characters). **Not readable here.**

**What fits our rig (inferred):**
- **Per-chaser variation** in stride rate, swing and lean gain: a few numbers seeded per chaser id in `gait_pose`. Cheap. Sourced in two places (Valve's code, Days Gone press).
- **Active ragdolls:** out of scope for a procedural rig without a physics body per chaser.
- **Level of detail:** relevant once the pack has models (only the first hunter does today); no readable source for how.
