## 1. The premise, in one paragraph [HIS 103, 105, 106]

- **The purchase.** Spring 2005. A sixteen-year-old buys a used PC off a sketchy forum.
- **What is on it.** Rips of old Gateway tapes and a homemade program that plays them.
- **The first run.** One night, out of curiosity, he runs it. Something answers. After his first run, a cheque comes under the door.
- **The owner.** Whoever owned the PC before him went somewhere and did not come back, and the dreamzone is full of his traces.
- **The sale was not chance.** The owner was a stranger connected to the institute, an agency or some party, and he knew the boy's
  affinity. The PC found him.
- **At home.** He lives with his mother, who works nights. He talks every night to an online best friend. The seller will not let go.
- **The heart.** The PC room is the heart of the story.

## 3. The documented ground the story stands on

- [DOC] **The memo** is "Analysis and Assessment of Gateway Process", US Army INSCOM, LTC Wayne M. McDonnell, 9 June 1983.
  - Its pages carry **"Approved For Release 2003/09/10"** (CIA reading room CIA-RDP96-00788R001700210016-5, and copies on The Black Vault
    and the Internet Archive).
  - **Page 25 is missing** from the CIA's web version and The Black Vault's copy (search summaries, 2026-11-11).
  - So in 2005 it is two years public, and a page is famously absent. That is exactly the kind of thing a forum passes round.
- [DOC] Monroe's Gateway program and Focus 10, 12, 15 and 21 (the memo ¶30, L161); Focus 15 "no time", Focus 21 "outside of the
  boundaries of time-space".
- [DOC] The Army sent personnel to Gateway training in 1983 (D661 S3). The US unit ran to 1995 and closed on "no discernible benefit"
  (D661 S4). A 1972 DIA report on Soviet research fed the US fear (operator file S5).
- **Never claimed:**
  - that any agency or the institute did anything in this story;
  - that the tapes work;
  - **that the game contains the real tapes' audio** (they are a real, sold product). In the fiction they are "rips"; what plays is our
    own sound (D654).

## 7. The previous owner [HIS 105: a stranger tied to the institute, an agency or some party, who knew his affinity; identity MINE]

- **Who.** Invented, no real person. **Elias Wren**, born 1951.
  - In the late 1980s he did contract data work for a government-funded consciousness study. It is never named and never said to be any
    real agency's.
  - He took the institute's Gateway course in the 1990s. [DOC: the institute ran such courses.] [MINE: him in one.]
  - When the study's money stopped (mirroring the real 1995 closing, never naming it), he kept going alone, with a PC, the rips and a
    program he wrote.
- **How he knew the boy:** he saw him first *in there*, a boy who walks in by sleep with no tapes at all. He needed the tapes; the boy
  does not. He tracked the boy to the forum and listed the PC where he would find it.
- **Where he went:** past Focus 21. His last room is in the facility biome, chapter 4.
- **His traces, in order:**
  - the sticky note (ch 1);
  - his voice at a rip's end (ch 2);
  - his notebook pages, one per Focus level;
  - his forum threads from 2003, quoting the memo the week it was released;
  - his chair, in his last room, facing a wall with the boy's name on it.
- **Why the tapes worked for him and sleep works for the boy:** the story never says (D661 T9's question, kept open).

## 9. The four endings [HIS 105-106: rescue, become the seller, destroy the drive, or all by play]

1. **Rescue.**
   - He reaches the owner's last room at Focus 21 and brings back whoever can be brought: the owner, or his friend, or neither if he
     was too late.
   - The Signal is seen fully.
   - The cheques stop. His mother knocks; he opens.
2. **Become the seller.**
   - He lists the PC, now his, on the forum for the next boy or girl with the affinity.
   - The game's last screen is his own post: "any progress?"
3. **Destroy the drive.**
   - He wipes it, and the dreamzone closes to him.
   - The last cheque arrives anyway, with no amount.
   - His mother's knock. Morning.
4. **All by play.**
   - Each ending is reached by what the player did through the runs (who he answered, what he kept, what he sold, how often he went
     in), not by a final menu.
   - Endless mode (item 28) opens after any of them.

## 10. The hoodie, the darned cuff and the watch band, now that the father's death is not required [item 102; options MINE]

- **The hoodie:** still his father's, a man's size on a boy.
  - The father is **gone, not dead**: left when the boy was twelve, a card at Christmas. He took nothing but left the hoodie.
  - Grief by absence stands, and nothing in it needs a death. *Or, if he would rather the hoodie carry the new story: it came with
    the PC, in the box, and it is the owner's. I lean to the father's; it gives the boy a life before the PC.*
- **The darned cuff:** his mother's hand, unchanged. It still says she is there, on the nights she is not.
- **The pale wrist band:** his own cheap digital watch stopped the night of his first run, at the minute he went in, and he stopped
  wearing it.
  - The trinket that later fills the band is **the owner's watch**, found in the dreamzone. It fits his wrist exactly.
  - It is the undatable wristwatch of the 1985 ledger, the object that proves time does not run straight there (item 97).
- **So:** the redesign sheet's three directions all stand. Only what the band and the hoodie *mean* moves.
