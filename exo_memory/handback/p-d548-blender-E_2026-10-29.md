## 1. What landed

- **`src/export/gltf.js` `toGlb(scene, { name, ai, closed, pieces })`.** A glTF 2.0 binary of the export's OWN scene: the very one the kn5 is written from.
  - **Each kn5 mesh** is a node of the same name. Positions and normals are the same Float32 values (compared to the float in the test). The uv is written as the kn5 writes it: `(u, 1 − v)`, the same values, since both kn5 and glTF put v = 0 at the top and the scene puts it at the bottom.
  - **Indices** are the kn5's own unsigned shorts. The plan said uint32. The scene's rule makes every mesh's indices a Uint16Array (`scene.js`: at most 65,536 vertices a mesh), so 16-bit is both valid glTF and exactly the kn5's. A 32-bit branch remains for a mesh that would need it, though the scene never builds one.
  - **Grouping is one node per piece per kind**, the piece first: `p07_road` (a gripped piece's `1GRIPnnn_` chunks included), `p07_underskin`, `p07_catch`, and `pitlane_road`. A mesh of no piece (the paint, a test end wall) sits at the top under its own name. The groups carry no transform. This gives both "select piece 7 by name" and "node count = the kn5's": every kn5 mesh is still its own named node, under its group.
  - **The AC_ markers** are empties with the kn5's matrices. kn5's row-vector matrix, translation in 12–14, is number for number glTF's column-major one.
  - **The AI line** is `ai_fast_lane`, one LINE_LOOP on a closed lap or LINE_STRIP on an open one. It is left out of a pieces subset, because it is the whole lap's.
  - **One material per surface**, same name, metallic 0 and roughness 1, with the diffuse texture embedded as **PNG**, decoded from the scene's own 32-bit DDS, level 0 (`ddsLevel`, then `encodePng` with node's zlib). A texture it cannot read is listed in `skipped` and named in the README.
  - **It refuses a mesh under a moving parent** (`GLTF_NOT_FLAT`). The export's scene is flat after `flattenForAc`, and a writer that guessed a transform would move the road.
- **`src/export/blender.js` `writeForBlender(ex, { dir, pieces, ai })`.** Writes `<name>.glb` and `README.txt` (what each node is; the Blender → FBX → ksEditor route; keep the 1ROAD / 1GRIP / AC_ names).
  - `pieces: ['p2']` writes only those pieces, and the markers within 10 m of their bounds.
  - No Blender run and no AC folder: the FBX step is Part 2's.
- **`src/export/fromwords.js`:** the scene the kn5 is written from is now named `acScene` and returned beside the kn5. The kn5's bytes do not change: the 42 test files that load fromwords passed, apart from the two new Blender rows fixed in §3, and so did the fixtures' kn5 digests.
- **No CHANGELOG line:** nothing a player can reach yet. Part 2's menu item carries the player's line (plan §Part 2).

## 2. Tests

**`test/export_gltf.test.js`, 9 rows** (red first: the module did not exist), with **our own strict reader** `test/glb_read.js`. The reader checks:
- the header and length;
- the JSON chunk then the BIN chunk, each a multiple of 4; the JSON padded with spaces only;
- the one buffer;
- every bufferView 4-byte aligned and inside the buffer;
- every accessor aligned, inside its view and counting right;
- POSITION min/max **equal** to the data;
- indices below the vertex count;
- every node's children existing, with one parent at most;
- images as embedded PNGs (signature checked).

The rows:
1. The reader on a textured export.
2. The reader on a scene that NEEDS padding: one triangle, 6 bytes of indices last, under four names so every JSON length mod 4 occurs.
3. Every kn5 mesh is a node of the same name, the same vertex count and **the same Float32 positions**, under its piece group (`tools/kn5.cjs`).
4. The markers: same names, same position and forward (1e-3 / 1e-5).
5. The AI line's points, and **the uvs of a textured closed tube equal to the flow's** (`u, fround(1 − v)`).
6. `writeForBlender`: the files, the README, and the p2-only subset.

## 4. Open, for Part 2 (said plainly)

- **Handedness: inferred, not measured.** Every check here compares our numbers with Blender's import of the same numbers, with Blender's own Y-up → Z-up turn. If AC's frame is left-handed (DirectX), the track shows **mirrored** in Blender while every number agrees. The community's FBX route back to ksEditor would then have to mirror it back. Part 2's FBX test should settle it on an asymmetric lap, for example all left turns: Blender's top view should show the turn the user drives. If it is mirrored, the fix is one sign in gltf.js (negate x on positions, normals and matrices, and reverse the winding), and the FBX settings follow it.
- **Materials:** glTF has no ambient term. The closed tube's dark material (ambient 0.02) comes into Blender like the others. The names keep the difference: `t180b_floor_elysium_dark` and the like.
