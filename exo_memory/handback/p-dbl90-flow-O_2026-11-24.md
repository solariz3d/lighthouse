  - Turn B is 48 m on the real road (about 38 m radius) and 24–32 m on the builds (21–29 m).

- **Flow, his "bumps": he is right there too.**
  - My last hand-back's "within about 3 degrees" was judged only at five stretch marks and does not hold along the road. Between the marks
    the builds are **7.7–8.1°** off (at d 548).
  - They also roll harder than the real road: 1.04 and 1.21°/m at their peak against its 0.85, over the builder's 20 m chord.
- **Fitted to the curve, one piece matches the shape but not the flow.**
  - The fit is one piece, with the as-built check skipped, fitted to the real road every 4 m in plan and in bank.
  - The largest plan gap is 1.7 m and the largest bank gap is 3.9° (rms 1.6°).
  - The peak roll rate over 20 m is 0.89°/m against the real road's 0.85, and the builder's checker draws no red and no amber.
  - **But the roll stops at every bank mark:** 9 stops inside the real road's one long roll. Between the stops it peaks at 1.41°/m over an
    8 m chord, against 1.08 on the real road.
  - These are his bumps, and the builder's checker cannot see them: its 20 m chord averages them away.
- **The cause is one property of the core, not the smoothing.**
  - Every bank change the core lays starts AND ends at zero roll rate. `extend.js` says so: the rounded-linear ramp has "S′ = S″ = 0 at
    both ends", and the same holds for the quintic, the swell and the shares.
  - So following a long, varying roll needs many marks, and every mark is a stop.
  - Five marks: few stops, but 8° off the curve. Thirteen marks: on the curve, but nine stops.
  - **With today's core the shape cannot be made to flow.**
- **Today's core also refuses the fitted piece.** The real `turnby.js` refuses it with `BAD_SECOND`, at the first change after the opening
  ramp (§3).

## 3. The fit to the curve, and where the core cannot follow

**How the fit was set up (`fit.js`).**
- One piece after a 300 m straight lead, family bowl, width 33, `bankShape: 'rounded'`.
- It is laid through a scratch copy of `turnby.js` with only the as-built check stubbed (`checked = (doc, opts, out) => out`, line 162),
  untracked in my worktree.
- Free parameters: a turn extra every 22 m and a bank target every 26 m from d 370. Those are the core's floors: a corner needs at least
  21 m, a bank change 26 m (`BANK_FIT_FLOOR_M`, extend.js:362). That makes 24 parameters.
- Fitted by Levenberg–Marquardt, with the Jacobian taken by finite differences through the core. Every evaluation lays the piece and walks
  its path, about 40 ms.
- Fitted to the real road every 4 m, d 340–712: the bank, and the plan as aligned in §1. The crossing therefore starts at turn A's peak
  and ends inside turn B, as the read does.
- **The fitted parameters stay local** (§6): within 1.7 m, they are that road's layout.

**Other spacings give the same picture:**

| turns every / banks every | marks from | largest bank gap | plan gap | peak, 20 m |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 24 / 28 m | d 452 | 4.4° | 2.1 m | 0.96 |
| 22 / 26 m | d 452 | 4.7° | 1.1 m | 0.88 |
| 22 / 26 m | d 370 | 3.9° | 1.7 m | 0.89 |

**Where the core cannot follow, and by how much.** I split the gap into two parts.
- **The path against the document:** the built road's bank minus the bank the document holds.
  - It is 0.0° through the whole crossing, 480–600: toPath follows the document exactly there.
  - Its largest value is 5.1° at d 376, on the opening ramp, where the adapter re-rounds the first ramp. This is the same re-rounding that
    trips the as-built check, found in the last hand-back.
- **The document against the real road:** what the laid ramps can say.
  - The largest gap is 3.9° at d 548. There the real road changes about 8° in 8 m (−3.4° at 536 to −11.8° at 544). No change of the 26 m
    floor can make that step.
- **The stops (§2) are the larger failure.** It is not that the smoothing is too strong. Each change rests at zero rate at both ends
  (`extend.js` 44–56: "S′ = S″ = 0 at both ends"; the `second` bank is also "ending with slope 0", :15). So a chain of changes is a chain
  of humps.
- **What would flow** (a finding, not a proposal): a bank laid as ONE smooth curve through its targets, with the rate carried across each
  mark, instead of a chain of ramps that each start and stop.

**The real `turnby.js` (check on) refuses the fitted piece:**

    road (the built road rounds a shorter one back over the hold; 106.7 m holds): from at most -50.7, got 30
