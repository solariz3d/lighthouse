### 1c. Getting them in without texconv
- **Offline, in Blender** (`art/tools/textures.py`, headless, Python inside Blender only, item 83):
  - read the downloaded JPG or PNG;
  - make it tile cleanly (both libraries' surface textures are made to tile);
  - downscale to **1024**;
  - build the mip chain;
  - write an **uncompressed DDS** (`R8G8B8A8_UNORM_SRGB` for albedo).
  - A DDS header is a fixed 148 bytes; the script writes it, so no plug-in is needed.
- **In the game:** a DDS reader (a header and mip offsets, a few dozen lines), then `AddTexture` into the reserved range (`handback/p-d659-loader-notes-C_2026-11-11.md` §1b: slots 256-1,279 of the heap, `d659-render-c`).
- **Later, after his yes:** texconv to BC7 makes each map a quarter of the size. Nothing else changes, because the reader takes the format from the DDS header.
- **Memory:** a 1024 RGBA8 map with mips is about 5.6 MB.
  - Six maps a biome (floor, wall, ceiling, frame, door, stair) is about 34 MB; six biomes about 200 MB of video memory.
  - At 2048 it would be four times that, which is why 1024 until BC7.

### 1d. Onto the generator's boxes, with no UVs
- **No UVs needed:** a chunk's triangles carry only a normal and a material (`dz.hlsl`, the chunk path in `Trace`). Every face the generator makes is axis-aligned, so the surface's own plane gives exact coordinates:
  - floor and ceiling: `uv = P.xz / tile`;
  - a wall: `uv = (along, P.y) / tile`, with `along` = P.z or P.x by the normal, as `SurfaceAlbedo` already chooses (`dz.hlsl:152`).
  - `tile` is per material (a carpet repeat of 1-2 m, a terrazzo 3 m), carried with the texture.
- **Two faces of one material:** a slab is one box whose top is a floor and whose bottom is the ceiling below, both material role 0. So a kit material needs two texture indices:
  - `matAlbedo.w` for up and side faces (step 2 put it there);
  - `matEmissive.w` for down faces.
  - The constant buffer does not grow.
- **The mip:** by ray cone, as in the loader notes §1d. The footprint is spread × t / |cos|, divided by `tile` / texture width. Bounce hits take a coarse mip.
- **The procedural layer keeps its job:**
  - D656's grime field (and the skirting band, for walls) multiplies the texture, so a 1 m repeat never reads as a repeat across a corridor;
  - the fine patterns (carpet pile, popcorn) are replaced by the texture;
  - concretely, `SurfaceAlbedo` keeps `shade` and the band for textured kit materials and drops its pattern lines.
- **"Aware of their surroundings" for surfaces,** what the shader can know with no new data:
  - height above the floor (the skirting and scuff band);
  - the grime field.
  - inferred: wear near doorways (a darker path in front of a Door edge) needs the generator to say where doors are. A per-chunk list of door rectangles, read in the shader, is one more buffer. Left for after the first pass, and named here so it is not forgotten.

## 2. Props placed by their surroundings, deterministic per seed

### 2a. How the generator would think about a room
- **What exists:** `gen/src/emit.cpp` builds a chunk storey by storey from `cells[H][H]` with a halo, edges `ex`/`ez` of `EdgeKind` (`Wall`, `Open`, `Door`, `Threshold`, `Rail`, `HighDoor`, `ReprieveDoor`, `HomeDoor`; `gen/src/gen.h:311-322`), cell flags (`kCellCulDeSac`, `kCellRaised`, `kCellRamp`, `kCellMezzanine`; `:290-297`), lamps per cell (lit or in `Chunk::dead_lamps`), and the doors onto a wall.
- **What is missing:** the generator has no room object. A room is the set of `Room` cells joined by `Open` edges, closed by `Wall` and door edges. It can cross a chunk's edge (lane bands are 6 cells, chunks 12).
- **The rule that keeps it deterministic and chunk-independent:**
  1. Each room is found by a flood fill over `Open` edges from any of its cells, through the generator's global layout (the halo already reads outside the chunk). A room is at most a block, a few cells across, so the fill is bounded.
  2. Its **anchor** is its lowest (y, z, x) cell. Every chunk that touches the room computes the same anchor.
  3. **All** of the room's props are decided from `hash3(seed, kSaltFurnish + n, anchor)`, so every chunk decides the same set.
  4. A chunk **emits** only the props whose footprint centre falls in its own cells.
- **Placement is surface-relative:**
  - a wall prop stands against a `Wall` edge of its cell, 2 cm off the wall face;
  - a corner prop sits where two `Wall` edges meet;
  - a centre prop sits on a free cell of the room.
- **What stays clear:**
  - the strip 1.2 m deep in front of every `Door`, `Threshold`, `HomeDoor` and `ReprieveDoor` edge;
  - the walk graph's straight line between a room's doors (its nodes are at cell centres);
  - raised and ramp cells, and stair cells;
  - every existing solid: the same overlap check, after the chunk's other solids exist, that the doors onto a wall use (`emit.cpp`, D656). A prop that would overlap is left out.
- **Drawn only first (`kSurfaceDetail`, its own `SolidKind::Prop`):** nothing collides with a prop and the walk graph never knows it, so the chase and the replays cannot move.
  - The cost of that is that a player can run through a bed. Props hug walls and corners, away from the line of travel, so it is rare.
  - Collision for the big ones (beds, tables, benches) is a later prove-it step: collision, navigation and the chase suites re-measured, the replay fingerprints moving once.
- **"Signs of the missing people"** are rules about **state**, not new kinds:
  - one chair of a set pulled out and turned 10-20°;
  - a TV left on (an emissive screen, and one dim static light);
  - a door propped open by something.
  - Each is a share drawn from the same anchor hash, and kept rare.

**What makes these "aware of their surroundings":** every rule reads something the generator already knows:
- a wall's side;
- where the doors are;
- a room's size and its one way in;
- a lamp that is dead;
- an atrium's rail;
- a junction's count of open edges.

None is scattered at random over the floor.

### 2c. What the props are made of, before models
- **First pass: built from boxes in the generator,** as the fixtures and the doors onto a wall are:
  - a bed is a frame, a mattress and a headboard;
  - a chair is a seat, a back and four legs;
  - a TV is a stand and a screen.
- **Their materials:** the free roles 12-15 of the biome kits that have them, or the shared range 128-255 for things any biome uses (wood, fabric, metal, plastic, screen). That range is why step 1 grew the table.
- **Later, after the loader's yes:** each prop kind is a glTF model, placed as an **instance**, not merged into the chunk.
  - The chunk path is flat-shaded with no UVs.
  - One bottom-level structure per prop model, shared by all its instances; the top-level structure carries the instances.

## 3. The cost against 2.78 ms, and the order

**Where we start** (`handback/p-d656-look-C_2026-11-11.md`, the clean session; 2560x1440, 9 chasers, render scale 0.75): GPU 1.14 ms average, p99 1.49, worst 1.81-1.97. **About 0.8 ms of room** at the worst frame.

**All inferred, to be timed:**
- **Textures:**
  - one texture read per primary hit on a kit surface, and one coarse read per bounce hit;
  - at render scale 0.75 that is about 2.1 M primary hits a frame at 2560x1440;
  - inferred: a few tenths of a millisecond at most, the largest single item here.
- **Box props in chunks:** they add triangles to each chunk's merged structure. A ray's cost grows with log(triangles), so tens of props a chunk are small.
  - The hand lights' and lamps' shadow rays meet more occluders, which costs a little more.
- **Model props:** instances in the top-level structure, which is rebuilt every frame. Hundreds of instances are likely a few hundredths of a millisecond; thousands are the thing to watch.
- **CPU:** the furnishing pass runs in the generator's workers off the frame thread. inferred: a small share of a chunk's generation.

**The order, three steps:**

| step | what | cost class | tier |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Textured kits** | `art/tools/textures.py` (Blender, offline) and the CC0 picks for the apartment first, then each built biome; a DDS reader; `AddTexture`; box projection; two indices per material; ray-cone mips; grime over the texture. Captures with `--capture-look` before and after | **GPU ALU and memory**: a texture read per hit; about 200 MB of video memory at 1024; no CPU per frame | check it, plus the frame cost on D (quiet, both slots empty, watched) |
| **2. Furnishing, drawn only** | the room flood fill and anchor; the per-biome rules above as box props in the shared material range; the overlap check; door strips and the walk line kept clear | **generator CPU, small chunk triangles**; nothing in the chase or the replays | **prove it** (it is the generator): determinism, no overlap, doors and walk line clear, each biome's counts; the chase suites and the fingerprints unchanged |
| **3. Props as models, then collision** | after the cgltf yes: prop models as instances replacing the boxes kind by kind; then collision for beds, tables and benches, with navigation and the chase re-measured | **GPU per instance** (top-level rebuild); collision is **sim-side** | prove it; the replays' fingerprints move once, recorded |
