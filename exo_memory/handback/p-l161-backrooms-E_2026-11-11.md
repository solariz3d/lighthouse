## 1. The origin [ORIGINAL]

- **The thread.** On **12 May 2019** an anonymous /x/ (4chan's paranormal board) thread asked for images that "just feel 'off'". On 13 May a reply posted a photo of an empty, yellow-walled, carpeted room under fluorescent light, with the line: **"If you're not careful and you noclip out of reality in the wrong areas, you'll end up in the Backrooms"** (B1, citing the thread).
- **The photo.** It was taken in **2002** during the renovation of a former furniture store in **Oshkosh, Wisconsin**, into a HobbyTown shop, and posted on the store's blog in March 2003. Discord users identified it in **May 2024** through the Wayback Machine. The room is now an RC racing track (B1).
- **What the original actually holds:** one image, one sentence, a video-game verb ("noclip"), a hum implied by the light, and **nothing else**. No monsters, no levels, no rules. Everything after is addition.

## 2. Kane Pixels' series [KANE]

- **The form.** In **January 2022** Kane Parsons, then 16, posted "The Backrooms (Found Footage)", presented as a **1990s VHS tape** and made in Blender and After Effects (B1). It grew into a series of about 25 shorts (B1) and an **A24 film**: announced 6 February 2023, directed by Parsons, released **29 May 2026** (B1).
- **What it adds:**
  - **An institution with a motive.** The **Async Research Institute** opened a door into the space with the **"Low-Proximity Magnetic Distortion System"**, the **"Threshold"**, through a control room used as a vestibule (B2, B3).
  - **Async's own name for the space is "the Complex".** It pitches it as **"A-Space"**, a cheap answer to housing and storage shortages (B3).
  - The fan wiki dates the door to 1988 (B4, **[WIKI-of-KANE]**, unreliable on dates).
  - **One monster, not a bestiary:** a mould-covered entity that chases people (B3).
- **How it delivers lore:**
  - tapes on a TV with a VCR, ending on "NO SIGNAL";
  - a corporate slideshow ("'A-Space' Prospective Development") and archive stamps;
  - unlisted videos linked from descriptions;
  - single flashed frames ("D TO DECIEVE THE FEDE");
  - an official debrief the viewer is meant to doubt ("Damage Control");
  - conflicting dates, and shorts left unplaced on purpose ("maybe that's the point") (all B3).
- **Its exteriors:** the hills near the facility, a city street in security footage, and "a house taken by the sea" in "I Remember" (B3). So the canon's "outside" is the **real world seen through the institution's cameras**, plus one dreamlike memory-place. It is not a level.

## 3. The wiki's level system and entities [WIKI]

- **The wikis.** Fans added numbered **levels** and hostile **entities**. The Wikidot and Fandom wikis grew them the way the SCP wiki grew (B1).
- **Level 0, "Threshold"**, "The Yellow Hell": a labyrinth "resembling the back rooms commonly found in offices or retail outlets", with buzzing fluorescents and soggy beige carpet. "Falling out of reality is the most frequent way of entering". "It is unknown whether or not entities exist" there (B5).
- **Level 1** is an underground parking lot; **Level 2** is hot, narrow tunnels (B1). The **Poolrooms** are a labyrinth of white-tiled indoor pools (B1).
- **Its "outside" levels**, the closest to his rare biome:
  - **Level 9, "The Suburbs":** "an infinite Suburban Area", always **midnight**, furnished new houses with no power, "each are completely different". It is crowded with entities: Smilers, Hounds, Skin-Stealers, and its own Neighborhood Watch, Observer and Mangled (B6).
  - **Level 11, "The City That Never Sleeps":** an empty-looking metropolis that is actually the wiki's **hub**. It is rated "Safe", "Low Entity Count", with a population of about 129,500 and named factions (the Capital, the M.E.G.). A "Level 11 Effect" calms hostile entities (B7).
  - inferred, from the two: the wiki's outsides drift from **eerie** (Level 9's empty midnight houses) to **civic** (Level 11's factions and survival ratings).

## 4. What fans and critics say made it work, and what made it generic [CRITIC]

- **What worked: suggestion.**
  - Its horror is subtle "wrongness" (Kotaku, via B1).
  - Tama Leaver: it is scary "because [it invites] you to interpret what's not shown" (B1).
  - The genre the image started is the "liminal space", eerie and uninhabited (B1).
- **What wore it down: explanation.**
  - Boing Boing describes lore fans wrapping the vague original in explanations, bestiaries and rules, making the *unheimlich* homely. Canons "bloat, harden, and finally decay" (B8, as quoted in search results; the page itself returned 403 to my fetch).
  - A RIT review: "countless repetitive levels, screaming entities and convoluted lore", and "not everything needs to be overly complex and interwoven with lore" (B9, search results).
  - ABC News, via Wikipedia: with no canonical Backrooms, "drawing a line between authentic storytelling and jokes" got hard, and purist fans split off to r/TrueBackrooms (B1).
- **What revived it** (B8 headline: "the rise, fall and rise again"): Kane's series, which went back to **one place, one institution, one monster, gaps left open**. inferred: that is the cause the critics point to. The article itself was not readable.

## 5. What Dreamzone can learn, read against his decisions (inventory `:56-60,77-78`)

These are suggestions, not a design.

**His "ambiguous lore, drip-fed through rare chronological encounters"** (`:77-78`). This is the Kane lesson more than the wiki's.
- **Keep:**
  - **one** institution or one story spine behind the encounters, the way Async holds Kane's shorts together;
  - **artifacts, not exposition**: a tape, a slide, a flashed frame;
  - **an official account the player can doubt**;
  - **dates that don't quite line up.**
- **Avoid** the wiki's move of giving every entity a page, a danger rating and rules. Each named rule turns a fear into a mechanic the player can stop fearing.
- **A test for each new encounter:** does it add a question or answer one? Kane's best shorts end on an open question ("maybe that's the point").

**His "biomes are the character's psyche"** (apartment, research facility, empty mall, hospital, high school, hotel).
- **This is closer to Kane than to the wiki.** Kane's one non-institutional place, "a house taken by the sea", is a *memory* place. The wiki's levels are a catalogue of anyone's spaces. If his biomes stay his character's places, they keep a meaning a catalogue can't.
- **The risk is the wiki's: numbering them.** Once they are "Level 3: Hospital", they are a list. Unnumbered, they are a mind.
- The original image was a real room in a real Oshkosh shop. The eeriest places are ordinary ones caught empty, not invented architecture.

**His "exceedingly rare outside"** (a liminal neighbourhood door, an unnatural green hill, glitched wilderness).
- **Precedents:** Level 9's endless midnight suburb [WIKI], and Kane's exteriors seen only through cameras [KANE].
- **The lesson from Level 11:** an outside that becomes a **hub with factions and a safety rating stops being outside.** Keep it rare, empty and unexplained. Its power is that it should not be there.
- **The lesson from Level 9:** crowding it with entities makes it just another arena. An outside with **no** chaser, only the sky and the wrong grass, may be scarier and fits "rare".

**His "designed pieces embedded in seeds".**
- **The original is proof:** one *real* designed place (the photo) under an infinite procedural idea. Kane does the same thing: hand-made rooms and tapes inside the endless space.
- **A suggestion:** the designed pieces carry the lore, and the procedural rooms carry the dread. Keep them rare enough that finding one feels like a message.

**What to avoid, in one line each** (all from §4):
- explaining entities;
- numbering places;
- making the outside safe;
- letting volume replace the single uncanny image.
