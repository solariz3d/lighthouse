## 2. What was rendered (the offline variant)

- **The run** (as L192's clip):
  - you sprint at 7 m/s with your own feet heard;
  - the hunter closes from 28 m at 9.5 m/s, its legs at 2.15 strides a second;
  - its footfalls come from the drawn phase, as loud as Keller's force curve;
  - the game's own bed at 0.5, master 0.8.
- **Voice A, recorded footfalls over our low thud:**
  - **The thud:** our 43/46 Hz thud (L192), without its home-made knock.
  - **The takes:** one take from the pack per footfall, played in turn and scaled by the footfall's force. The eight
    are the pack's takes with the most energy under 150 Hz: 15, 20, 22, 18, 16, 12, 09, 10. That rule is ours.
  - **The take gain:** 4.54, solved so the footfall's level above 150 Hz equals the as-built thud's (-37.2 dB at 12-20 m;
    ours). It came out -37.4 above 150 Hz and -32.6 overall (as built -32.4), with a mix peak of 0.61, so no limiting.
- **Voice B, Plaits' analog bass drum (the 808 model), no thud:**
  - The strike is triggered on the footfall's own sample (one sample a call), at the footfall's pitch, scaled by its
    force, through the module's own overdrive and its 0.8 gain (`bass_drum_engine.cc:75-78`, `voice.cc:63`).
  - **The settings are ours:** tone 0.9, decay 0.55, attack FM 0.8, self FM 0.6, drive 0.95, accent 0.7. That is the
    brightest it would honestly go, chosen from three tried; the first had nearly nothing above 150 Hz (-54.6 dB at
    12-20 m).
  - **The gain:** 0.73, matching the as-built thud overall (-32.4 dB at 12-20 m).
  - **On level-matching:** A is matched above 150 Hz, B overall, because each voice's level rule follows its job (A's
    takes carry the upper band; B is one engine). Both land within 0.2 dB of the thud overall.
- **The calm (sound-1, "Duck all but the hunter's footsteps, to a floor"):**
  - A calm input from 0 to 1 stands in for the good entity, as the sound build path's lap 3 plans; no good entity
    exists in code. In the clip it rises over 3-6 s and stays.
  - Everything else falls to 1 - 0.94·calm: the other chasers, the hunter's tone, your feet, the cues, the ambients
    and the bed. The hunter's footfalls fall to 1 - 0.65·calm. Both depths are ours.
  - checked (7-12 s, full calm, against the same run without it): **everything but the hunter -24.4 dB** (registered
    -24.4); **the hunter's footfalls -9.1 dB** (registered -9.1).

## 5. The gap the chair asked about: no CC0 recording is a thunderous footfall

- **The fetch, by a subagent:** OpenGameArt, Kenney, Freesound and Wikimedia Commons were searched, every licence read
  on its own page.
  - **Nothing obtainable without a login is a lossless recording of a giant, thunderous footfall.**
  - Freesound's best CC0 matches ("Stomping Ground Super Bassy", "Big Basey Stomps", "Big robot footstep" and others)
    send their originals behind a login (checked: `/download/` redirects to the login page). Only lossy previews could
    be taken.
- **The closest preview, `giant-step` (Freesound 721601, CC0), measured, is one designed sound, not footfalls:**
  - hits 1-3 are bright crunches (94-99% of their energy over 600 Hz);
  - hit 4 is a thump;
  - hit 5 is a sub boom (95% under 150 Hz);
  - it lasts 1.2 s in all, where the hunter lands a foot every 0.23 s.
- **So voice A is honest about what it is:** a real boot on concrete over our synthesised thud, the recording carrying
  the contact and the thud the weight. A thunderous recorded footfall needs a source beyond CC0-without-login:
  - his `sound-7` answer (Sonniss or attributed Freesound);
  - or the Freesound originals, fetched with an account.
- **Left out:** `mech-stomp` and `yd_landing` are CC0 on their pages, but each was made from material whose licence the
  page does not give. They were left out under the strictest reading.
