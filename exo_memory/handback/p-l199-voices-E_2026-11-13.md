  impact's dirtiness are that move. Roughness itself is not measured.
- **A level that rises as it closes is a warning cue** (Bach 2008, abstract). The mix layer already does this, unchanged.
- **Plaits can stay the engine.** Nothing in the read argues against it: the layers it names (sub, body, crunch, tail,
  several close hits) can be built from the two drum models and a tail.

**Pull against:**
- **A deep boom is not what he hears on small speakers.** Small speakers stop at 100-200 Hz (Moliner 2020), and the
  hearing threshold at 40 Hz is 42.5 dB above 315 Hz's (51.1 against 8.6 dB, ISO 226 Table 1). On a laptop, what carries is the
  impact layer and the boom's overdrive harmonics. **The weights already lean that way:** above 150 Hz, at 12-20 m, they
  read -32.2 / -31.7 / -31.9 dB against B's -41.4. In 150-600 Hz they are -32.8 / -32.4 / -32.6 against everything
  else's -36.5. That is the band his own steps are in, so a loud stomp will also mask his steps upward (Bernstein &
  Trahiotis 2008).
- **Below about 30 Hz, deeper stops being a lower note and becomes flutter** (Krumbholz 2000, abstract: "a pitch limit
  of about 30 Hz"). At this run's force, the booms sit at 36/34 Hz (H1), 30/28 Hz (H2) and 26/24 Hz (H3). So H2's second
  foot and all of H3 are under it. inferred: H3 may be heard as a rumble rather than a lower stomp. That may suit
  "giant", but it is not "deeper".
- **Front from back needs broad content at about 4 kHz and above** (Middlebrooks 2015). Above 4 kHz the weights read
  -61.7 / -60.1 / -58.7 dB, 11 to 14 dB over everything else's -72.4; B adds nothing there (-72.2).
  inferred: whether that is enough to tell "behind" in the game is not measured. The mixer's own direction cues are
  unchanged.
- **Repetition dulls startle** (Putnam & Roth, abstract), and rough sound resists that (Anikin). Every stomp here is the
  same sound at two pitches. Variation from step to step is not built.
- **Not built, named for the next pass:**
  - several close hits in one step ("three to eight impacts over a period of one second or less", Thom 2025);
  - a gap of silence just before the hit (Assells, in Levine 2011);
  - a reverb tail for size (Aadahl 2009).

  At 4.3 footfalls a second each step has 233 ms, so anything added has to fit inside that.
- **The sources' sub sits higher than ours:** Krotos puts the first layer "around 60-100Hz". At 80 Hz the threshold is
  19.6 dB lower than at 40 Hz (31.5 against 51.1 dB, ISO 226 Table 1). Raising the boom is one number in `DZ_STOMP`. It would be more audible and
  less deep.
- **A bright layer alone reads lighter** (Tajadura-Jiménez 2015: only the brightened condition changed perceived
  weight). inferred: the impact layer could pull the stomp toward "lighter". His ear is the test.
