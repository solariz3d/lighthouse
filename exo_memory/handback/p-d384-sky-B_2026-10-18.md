## What changed

- `app/preview/renderer.js`: a full-screen sky pass runs right after the clear, before the grid and track, with
  DEPTH_TEST off and `depthMask(false)`. Both are restored afterwards. `skyBasis(pose, aspect)` inverts the
  view-projection with the eye at the origin (rotation only) and unprojects NDC (0,0,1), (1,0,1) and (0,1,1) into
  `{fwd, right, up}`. The vertex shader's ray is `uFwd + x·uRight + y·uUp`. The program is compiled in `setTheme`,
  so an unknown style throws there. `'none'` builds no program and makes no calls.
- `themeFrom` reads `--sky-style` (new `tokens.word`, which strips quotes) and `--sky-a/-b/-c` (each falls back to
  `--sky`).
- `app/preview/preview.js` hands the sky a time: `t / 1000`, or 0 under `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- `app/preview/sky/sun.js` is the real style: a zenith/horizon gradient, a world-fixed sun disc at
  `normalize(0.55, 0.45, -0.70)` with a glow, and a slow horizon haze that drifts with t.
- `app/themes/skyline.css` sets `--sky-style: sun` and the three colours. The `#preview::after` haze is removed
  (see the judgment below).
- `CHANGELOG.md` has an entry under Unreleased → Changed, in our own words.

## The contract, for E (unchanged since 66b243c)

`app/preview/sky/<style>.js` exports `{ source() }`. `source()` returns GLSL ES 1.0 that defines
`vec3 skyPattern(vec3 dir, float t)`:

- `dir` is a unit view direction, +y up.
- `t` is in seconds, and is 0 under reduced motion.
- The pass declares the uniforms `uSky`, `uSkyA`, `uSkyB` and `uSkyC`.
- Helper names take the style as a prefix.

The full text is in `app/preview/sky/index.js:4-14`. One change after the skeleton: the registry requires each
module by a literal name (`index.js:20`). Below, under Corrections, is why; it was posted on the board for E.

## The designs

**`sun` (Skyline, a clear day with depth):**
- the gradient deepens from `--sky-b` at the horizon, through `--sky`, to `--sky-a` at the zenith;
- it is a little paler on the sun's side and deeper away from it;
- the sun is world-fixed, about 22° up, with a small soft disc, a corona and a faint wide bloom;
- thin high cirrus sits on a deck overhead, on the sun's side, drifting on one wind (q.x −0.010/s). Each of its
  five octaves fades out where it would be finer than a pixel at the default 60° lens;
- a low haze band runs along the horizon, its strength wandering slowly round.

**`prism` (Glass, a cold clear night, the quietest):**
- the gradient runs as in sun, near black at the zenith;
- sparse fine stars sit on a 120-cell cube-face grid (3.5% of cells), each a small gaussian. The twinkle changes
  brightness only, in place (about 18%). The stars fade out below 10–23° elevation;
- a pale cold moon sits about 30° up, with a soft limb over about 12 px, a faint noise face and a halo;
- a thin soft band of spectrum sits about 3° up (red at its top), brightest toward the moon, its strength
  wandering round over minutes.

## Corrections

- **Glass's first edges failed:** largest step 35, with 1521 steps over 24. I blamed the moon first. Dimming it and
  widening its limb changed nothing (35 / 1521), so the moon was the wrong cause. Pixel inspection found a bright
  star, about 87 levels over the sky at σ about 1.8 px. I capped the peak (0.10+0.32b → 0.08+0.18b) and widened σ
  (0.0019+0.0010b → 0.0020+0.0012b), which gave 20 / 0.
- **The first Skyline bar failed the fog band:** 1.735:1 at 10.0°. The horizon colour and the gradient's start were
  redone.
- **My bar tool hung for over 600 s on its first run.** A 4.5 MB CDP reply never arrives. It now reads the render
  back in 500,000-character slices.
- **Glass's real page first read 407 "moved", and that was my tool, not the page.** It snapshotted 3 s after load,
  before Glass's menus were built: mine against mine moved 14, while base against base moved 0. The tool now waits
  for the app's own `data-menubar = 'ready'` and prints the time to ready and the frame time.
- **That surfaced a real cost of mine.** prism ran at a median 33.3 ms a frame in headless software rendering (base
  16.7), and the menus were ready at 9.3–10.1 s (base 1.2–1.5). Its star field summed 9 cells per pixel. It now
  sums the 4 nearest, and skips the stars below their fade and the moon's face off the moon. Back to 16.7 ms and
  2.4 s.
- **The same stars, near enough:** the four shots differ from the 9-cell version in at most 656 of 840,000 pixels,
  by at most 8 levels (`t180-d384\shotdiff-fast.out`). My first comment claimed under a thousandth everywhere,
  which is wrong toward a face's corners; it is corrected in the code.
- **My first final run never ran.** It waited 60 minutes on the lock and gave up. The rerun took over a stale slot,
  and the figures above come from a later full run on the committed code.

## Judgment calls

- **The cirrus at level view.** Near the sun, perspective makes the streaks converge on it, so they read as a few
  broad soft rays rather than thin threads (`sky-skyline-yaw45-level.png`, top left). Looking up, they are thin.
  If they should be thinner at level, the lever is the across-wind scale (`sun.js:41`, `q *= vec2(0.5, 4.0)`) or
  the deck's lower fade (`smoothstep(0.12, 0.32, h)`).
- **The band of the bar.** My band is the WCAG ratio against `--fog`, at most 1.30:1, for every pixel from −10° to
  10°. The packet left the band to me.

## Limits

- The edge metric is at the 60° default lens. A narrower lens (ctrl+wheel) enlarges the stars and softens them; a
  wider one sharpens them.
- The motion probe resolves only 2 px/s and up, so prism's 0.0 means nothing moved that fast.
- The frame times are headless software rendering; a GPU will be faster for every skin.
- The shots are sky-only renders from my harness. The page's own sky is the same shader, behind the track and HUD.
- Aqua and Aurora are E's (caustics.js, aurora.js); this round touched neither.
