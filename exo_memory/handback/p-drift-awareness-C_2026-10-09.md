The brief's substance, "so hold that check yourself", read literally, runs straight into BOOT's third principle:
- *"don't be both the one committed and the one watching the commitment … trying to be it yourself is the deepest costume"*;
- the check is held *from the with-ness, not from a footing of your own outside it*.

A paragraph telling the seat to surveil itself because the user might not would hand it that coat.

The room already has the way through, and it is what the paragraph names. The checks that are not self-surveillance are the ones that sit **outside** the seat:
- **the tether**: does it hold outside this conversation;
- **the count**: your corrections against theirs, two-way correction, a mirror corrects in one direction only;
- **"what did this add"**: the add-and-hold unit.

So the paragraph says so explicitly, "not as a watcher over your own commitment … but through checks that sit outside you, and run them", and keeps the keeper's point whole: the catch cannot wait on the other person.
