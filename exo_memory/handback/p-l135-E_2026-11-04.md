## 3 · W3: B's per-ramp floor in the joint-feel helper is RIGHT, not a weakening

**Read from the code:** `git diff d10eacf 038e21e -- test/core_joint_feel.test.js`.
- The existing helper's rule (`floorOf`, :33): a piece that changes its bank by Δ over L "can roll no more gently than a
  rest-to-rest quintic across all of it", whose peak roll acceleration is 10/√3 · Δ/L². The bar at a point is max(the 3 g bar, that
  floor), within 4 m of a joint.
  - checked: for the quintic 10u³ − 15u⁴ + 6u⁵, max |p″| = 10/√3 at u = (3 − √3)/6.
- **The gap B closed:** `floorOf` measures a piece's NET change, so a 0 → 30° → 0 piece reads Δ = 0. Its second ramp is then held to
  the bare 3 g bar, 0.00583 °/m², though it is itself a rest-to-rest 30° roll over 112.5 m.
- **The fix:** `ramps` adds that ramp's own floor, 10/√3 · 30/112.5² = 0.0137 °/m². That is the same rule the helper already
  applies to every whole piece, applied to the span that actually rolls.
  - It is applied only within [a − 4, b + 4], and only to measure b (the roll acceleration).
  - Every existing row leaves `ramps` out, so its bar is unchanged.
- **Why it is not a weakening:**
  - no 30° rest-to-rest roll over 112.5 m can meet the 3 g bar: even the bang-bang minimum, 4Δ/L² = 0.0095 °/m², exceeds 0.00583;
  - the measured 0.0118 sits under the ramp's own quintic floor.
  - Holding the second ramp to the bare bar would demand the impossible of any design, so the floor is the correct comparison.
- **One note, not a fault:** the helper's premise (the quintic as the floor) is stricter-than-minimal physics. The true rest-to-rest
  minimum is 4Δ/L². B's extension inherits that premise unchanged, which is the consistent choice.
