## 2. FINDING: a failed part leaves the other worker busy, and its late answer lands in the next job
**Reproduced** (`pool.js` case 4, fake workers that process their messages in order like real ones):
1. Job 1 starts. The `rest` worker fails fast (`ok: false`) while the `rays` worker is still computing job 1's part.
2. `fail(err, false)` **keeps** both workers (A's "a failed part keeps them warm") and clears job 1's handlers.
3. Job 2 starts at once on the **same** workers and installs its own handlers.
4. The `rays` worker finishes its OLDEST message, **job 1's**, and posts it. **Job 2's handler takes it as job 2's rays part.** The messages carry no job identity: `id` is always 1 or 2, and the runner never checks it.
5. **Job 2 resolves with `downforce-ray-gap<-job1, fold<-job2`.** Job 2's own rays answer is still queued (`raysQueueLeft: ["job2"]`) and is ignored when it arrives (`settled`).

**Why it matters:** the preview's overlap list for the NEW document then carries the OLD document's downforce-ray gaps and lacks its own. It can say "no overlap" over a track that does overlap, or flag one that doesn't.
- The shell's stale-answer guard (`currentJob.entry !== entry`) can't see it: the wrong data is inside the new job's own promise.
- **Plausibility:** it needs one part to throw while the other is still running (`rays` takes ~5 s on 13 km), then a new preview in that window.
  - The panel's own advice after a failed check is "Cancel the preview and try Close again". Cancel after a settled job calls no `job.cancel()` (`currentJob` is already null), so the workers are NOT replaced, and the retry lands in exactly this window.
  - Rare, because a part has to fail first, but real.

**Fix (either is enough):**
- tag each message with a per-job serial (`id: \`${serial}:${part}\``) and ignore any reply whose id isn't the current job's; or
- call `replace()` on ANY failure, not only on a worker error.

My `pool.js` case 4 is a ready test for it: job 2 must resolve with job 2's parts only.

**The other three lifecycle paths are right** (`pool.js`):
- **Cancel:** rejects `{ cancelled }`, terminates both workers, and the pool is two fresh ones.
- **A newer job mid-run:** the old job is cancelled and its workers terminated; the new one resolves with **its own** document (`downforce-ray-gap<-new, fold<-new`).
- **A late answer after a cancel:** terminated workers can't answer, so it's never delivered, and the job stays rejected `{ cancelled }`.
- The shell side (read, as in my previous look): Cancel, an edit, Undo and a new selection drop the preview and abort the job (`coreshell.js` `:127`, `:131`, `:353`).
