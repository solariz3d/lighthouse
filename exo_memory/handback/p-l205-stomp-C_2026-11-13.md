## 3. The fix that came after the stop note (7b184db), and why it took this shape

**Even the runner's own legs don't carry his hips in our model.** I put Dorn's own angles (his 10% samples) through our
skeleton with the pelvis following the stance leg (`scratchpad stomp/scripts/dornfeas.js`). They give 7.8-9.7% travel,
lowest at the strike. The result swings with the pelvic tilt we assume: 2.7-7.7% at 15 degrees, 12.4-16.1% at 5. So the
hips cannot be read off the legs at speed. inferred: our pelvis is rigid (his pitches through the stride), and our test
skeleton's thigh and shin are equal and it has no heel.

**And a solved stance knee would break the yardstick at Fukuchi's speeds.** A spring-mass pelvis with the knee solved
under the shape's thigh (`stomp/scripts/spring.js`) bends the stance knee 50-73 degrees at 3.5-4.5 m/s. E's landed row
asks for 41 +- 8.

**So two laws** (`game/model_rig.h`, kRunnerHips):
- **Up to 4.5 m/s, where Fukuchi gates the legs:** the pelvis follows the leg whose foot is down. In flight it goes from
  the take-off height to the landing one, plus the throw's rise. This is attempt 1.
- **From 5.5 m/s, where the shapes are OURS:** the hips are the runner's. In stance they run from the strike's height
  to the height the leg can push off from, less a half-sine dip; in flight they go back up with the throw. The dip is
  the recorded pelvis centre's travel less the rise and less the take-off drop.
  - The foot down is planted: its ankle travels back from the shape's strike to the shape's take-off.
  - The thigh and knee are solved to reach it (two bones, knee forward).
  - It pushes off on its ball as the shape pitches it, with the knee at least kPushOffKnee (20 degrees; Dorn's 16-21 at
    toe-off).
  - The leg that leaves takes thigh, knee and foot back to its shape over kKneeHandBack (0.35) of the flight.
- **Between them,** a blend.
- **The thigh's landing:** the swing peak moves to 0.78 past 4.5 m/s (kHipPeakFast). The thigh then lands 43.6 degrees
  pelvis-referenced at 7 and 9.49 m/s (Dorn 42.1 and 46.1) and 42.9 at 5.2 (Dorn 37.3).

**The bound changed to the pelvis centre:** the mean of RASI and LASI with SACR, half and half; standing 1111.7 mm.
It moves 103.3 / 86.3 / 63.2 / 47.2 mm (`stomp/pelvis_centre.txt`, `stomp/scripts/pelvis.js`). The sacrum alone sits
on the back of a pitching pelvis. That centre is highest at the strike at 6.97 and 9.47 m/s, so "highest in flight"
became "in flight or at a strike". A runner takes off lower than he lands, which is why the take-off height above
exists.
