## 2. The fix: three attempts measured, none green (the gate's numbers)

1. **The pelvis follows the leg whose foot is down; in flight, from take-off depth to landing depth, plus the rise.**
   The 27% drop in flight goes; 23.9% remains at 6.97 m/s, now lowest at the strike. A probe of the stance leg
   (`scratchpad stomp/probe.cpp`) shows why: at 6.97 m/s the thigh lands **55.8 degrees** forward of straight down,
   so the foot is far ahead and the hip is only 0.620 m over the floor at the strike, against 0.854 m at mid-stance.
2. **Plus the hip's swing peak moved to Dorn's runner past 4.5 m/s** (0.90 → 0.80 between 4.5 and 5.5 m/s: then this
   shape lands 37.3 / 42.1 / 46.1 degrees flexed at 5.2 / 7.0 / 9.5, Dorn's figures, `sprint_reference/
   simtk_dorn_running.md`). The strike is then right (37.9 degrees, 32 to the vertical, against Bissas 27-30 and
   Miyashiro 33), but take-off is wrong: 21.7% at 6.97 m/s. The thigh swings back to -26 degrees with the knee still
   31 degrees bent, so the hip is 0.65 m over the floor at take-off.
3. **Plus the foot's contact** (the ankle stands on the ball when the foot pitches toe-down, on the heel under the
   ankle otherwise; OURS, the skeleton has no heel): 12.8% at 6.97 m/s, highest at the take-off. That over-lifts, and
   it turns the roll row red (107-123 mm), so it changes row 1's meaning.

**What the three attempts point at, and what the 07:47 addition already said:** "the legs above 4.5 m/s are a jog's
shape played at a sprint's step rate". Measured: the stance share shrinks with speed (0.36 at 3.5 to 0.255 at 7), but the
knee's stance bump (centre 0.174, half-width 0.229) and the hip's swing keep Fukuchi's 2.5-4.5 m/s timing. So at 7 m/s our
knee is 31 degrees bent at take-off (Dorn's runner about 20 degrees at 26.5%), and the hip extends to -30 degrees
(Dorn's -12.6 pelvis-referenced). **The real fix is the legs' shapes past 4.5 m/s, refit to the reference's 5.2 / 7.0 /
9.49 m/s rows**, not a patch on the pelvis. The pelvis-follows-the-stance-leg rule (attempt 1) is still needed with it.
