## 1b · How it works (src/geom/path.js, "BLOCKS AND THE PLACEMENT CHAIN")
- **Blocks.** Every segment is grown ONCE in its own local coordinates: from the origin, at heading 0, at its real start pitch p0 and its start frame R0 in those coordinates. Under the world-up curve model nothing in a block depends on where the segment is placed or which way it faces.
- **One placement chain, for a full build and a sculpt alike.**
  - x(j) = x(j−1) + Ry(th(j−1))·endL(j−1).x
  - th(j) = th(j−1) + endL(j−1).θ
  - s0(j) = s0(j−1) + length(j−1)
  - The next block's local start comes from the previous block alone: p0(j) = endL(j−1).p, and R0(j) = Ry(−endL(j−1).θ)·endL(j−1).R.
  - The same chain on the same floats makes a sculpt **bit-identical to a full rebuild**.
  - World samples are views (x + Ry(th)·local, cached per placement).
- **A sculpt, step by step** (`rebuildPathFrom`):
  1. It regrows the edited range.
  2. It regrows the unchanged tail ONE BLOCK AT A TIME until the next block's (p0, R0) is bit for bit its old one.
  3. It keeps the rest and recomputes only their placements (`path.replaced` counts them).
  4. An unchanged placement keeps its object, so a font edit moves nothing, not even by rounding.
- **Measured, why convergence matters** (scratchpad `probe_block.js`): after a LEVEL turn edit, the next block's R0 differs by rounding noise. Its samples regrow byte-identical, and every block after it has bit-identical inputs. After a turn edit on a 1° climb, every downstream block differs (the twist is real).

  2. (19:0x) It re-placed the tail only when its FIRST block's inputs matched. That regrew the whole tail after a level turn edit; the full suite's mutant run showed the rule was too strict (§7, R6). It was replaced by the convergence above.

## 2 · THE FORK: the ruling's premise, and what makes PITCHED turn edits rigid (posted to the board and raised to the chair, 18:2x; evidence 18:4x)
**Measured** (scratchpad `twist.js`, src/geom/path.js at HEAD):
- The drift is the RMF's twist about T, at rate k·sin p. Measured −8.7249e-6 against predicted −k·ΔL·sin p = −8.7262e-6 (k 0.001, ΔL 0.5 m, p 1°).
- A 360° climbing turn at 10° with roll 0 ends banked **−1.063 rad (61°)** against gravity. Today the document's roll is not the bank the user sees on pitched turns.
- path.js yaws about WORLD up, on purpose. A's `src/doc/connector.js:51` integrates that same model to close loops, and `src/doc/resolve.js` solves jumps in world pitch.

**Option 1, track-relative (the ruling as written):**
- Each word's k and kp are taken in its own frame, so every upstream edit is rigid.
- The language changes: a turn on a slope stops holding its pitch.
- Pitched old documents change shape, with no migration.
- A's connector and jumps move to path.js's integrator.

**Option 2 (recommended), world-up yaw and pitch with the gravity frame:**
- left = (cos θ, 0, −sin θ) plus the explicit roll.
- The document's meaning, the connector and jumps are unchanged; a word's roll becomes its bank against gravity.
- In the block chain every R0 is then exactly (1, 0, 0), so **every edit that keeps the next piece's start pitch is rigid AND exact**, pitched turns included. Pitch edits still re-mesh (the meaning).
- ARCHITECTURE §3's "rotation-minimising" becomes "yaw-pitch frame + roll".
- **Evidence** (scratch copy of the pre-chain path.js; repo unchanged):
  - Δbank → exactly 0; the 10° spiral ends at bank 0;
  - geometry suite 67 pass, 2 fail, 1 todo. The 2 are exactly the tests that pin today's choice (RMF no-spin; the closing twist spread).
