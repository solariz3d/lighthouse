## What I did
- **Two warm workers (the chair's first item), made at page start and kept:** `app/core/overlaprunner.js` makes both workers when the runner is made (page start, `app/index.html`), they load their modules while idle, a job reuses them (they stay after it), and Cancel terminates both and makes two fresh ones. This gains nothing in time by itself (the start was 0.0 s), but it is what makes the next item cost nothing per job.
- **The split (what actually moved the time):** the check is two independent pieces of work on the same mesh. `app/core/overlapjob.js` `overlapPart`: **'rest'** = the mesh with its self-check plus the validator without the road mesh (2.9 s on 13 km), **'rays'** = the mesh without the self-check plus the validator with the road mesh and no folds, keeping its downforce-ray-gap ranges (6.4 s). One part per worker, at once; the time is the longer one. `app/core/overlapmerge.js` `mergeParts` puts the answers together.
- **It is EXACT, not approximate.** `src/validate/index.js` hands its findings to `ranges()`, which orders them by (reason, s, u) ("so the ranges do not depend on the order findings were produced in", its own comment), so merging the two lists and ordering them the same way gives the one-piece answer. Checked: `node probe_parts2.js` → identical (JSON-equal) on all 7 cases: a coil's Close (19 overlaps), a coil's delete (12), a track with a jump (1 red that is not an overlap), a cup track, a tube track, the 46-piece 13.1 km delete, the 14.3 km Close; and `app/test/preview-async.test.js` row 3b asserts the same with `deepEqual` after a structured clone, including with a design speed. Node, same machine, the longer part: 6.4 s against 9.5 s whole (13 km), 7.1 s against 9.6 s (14 km).
- **"Skip what is cheap and safe to skip in a preview": nothing was skipped, on purpose.** The mesh self-check is 1.9 s of the 8 s, not the 5 s I had assumed, and it is not safe to skip: its folds are what the preview reports as "the road overlaps itself" (`self-intersection`). The ray-gap search (5.3 s) is the other overlap finder (`downforce-ray-gap`). Both are in the overlap list the preview shows; skipping either would let the preview say "no overlap" where the export refuses. With the split, the self-check no longer sits on the critical path at all, so nothing needs skipping.
- **What was NOT done, and is still the lever:** the ray-gap search's own cost. `src/validate/raygap.js` welds every vertex through string keys and 27 neighbour lookups; an exact faster weld would cut most of the 5.3 s. That file is the validator the EXPORT also runs, so it is EXPORT tier (touched file's mutants, fixtures 0 of 9). I did not touch it; it is the chair's call whether to open it.
