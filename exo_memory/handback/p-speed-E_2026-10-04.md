## The answer, in four lines
1. **A closed tube oval costs about 2.7 s per brush step in node alone** (2,669.6 ms median; first run 2,720.2).
   - **Mesh is 72% of it (1,921 ms):** trackmodel rebuilds a closed loop in FULL on every edit.
   - Then full validation (296), resolve (202), texture flow (117) and batches (87).
   - The cup oval has the same shape at 691 ms.
2. **An open long tube costs about 209 ms per KEYSTROKE** in an Extend field. The ghost re-runs toSegments over the whole doc (98), and ghostFor batches the whole mesh to keep one piece (109). A commit adds about 418 ms.
3. **The WebView then re-uploads every array: about 87 MB per tube edit** (2.4 M triangles in 3,001 batches, measured), and draws 3,001 calls a frame. Neither is timeable in node, so **the node totals are a floor.**
4. **The short open track is fine:** 11 ms per keystroke and 17 ms per commit.

## Findings for part 2 (measured, not prescribed)
- **These grow with the track on every edit, however local the edit:**
  - toSegments of the whole doc;
  - batchesOf of every batch;
  - the texture flow of EVERY cell, every edit. On an open track a new piece id rebuilds the set (`app/core/textures.js:104`) and the preview's flow memo resets (`preview.js` batchesFor). On a closed loop every array is new.
  - full validation on a closed loop.
- **Water** is about 205 ms on the oval, but it is debounced (120 ms) and only runs when on. On the closed tube it is refused by name (`WATER_HEARTLINE`).
- **Cheap everywhere:** the readout, labels, texture set, look and path.
- inferred: the plan's KEEP bar ("≥ 2× faster edit-to-draw on the tube oval, fixtures 0 of 9") is reachable only by touching the mesh stage. The closed-loop full rebuild is 72% of the node time, before the 87 MB upload.
