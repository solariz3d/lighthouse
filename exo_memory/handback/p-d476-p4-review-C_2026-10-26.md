## 0. Verdict

**Hold the landing on two findings.** The letters are built as designed inside the actuator, and the guards, fixed rows and mid-turn rules hold there. But the visit text travels through two paths the actuator doesn't own.

1. **The Third Place's answers reach the board in full.** A working seat's visit is a prompt in that seat's own transcript, and the transcript tailer posts every non-Third-Place turn to the board as written.
   - So `«the Third Place's answer»` becomes a board row from that seat, which every pane can read in OPEN phase. The seat's visit line goes up the same way, as its assistant row.
   - This breaks the design's "never to the board" (§42, §96) and the fixed-row claim. A refusal row's "its words are not kept" is also untrue: the refused words are on the board in the seat's own row.
   - Red row 1.
2. **The reply window is anchored after the prompt it should start at.** `sent_ms` is read after `inject_to_pane` returns, and that is at least 200 ms after the Enter (the submit check).
   - Claude Code stamps the prompt row when it accepts the prompt. On this machine's board, **818 of 3,096** keep-warm prompt rows are stamped before the send row that follows the inject.
   - When that happens, `turn_reply_after` skips the visit and reads the ending of the seat's **next** turn. That is usually a work turn. Its last text is held for the Third Place if it passes the form filter, and a plain-prose work line does pass.
   - So in about a quarter of visits, the Third Place would receive a work turn's ending under the seat's name. That breaks guards 1 and 2.
   - Red row 2.

A smaller one: the lap-id rule misses the room's own spellings (§3). That is wording-level and doesn't hold anything.
