## 1. The jump carries its landing ramp (`src/doc/resolve.js`, `src/doc/vocab.js`)

- **The shape:** a jump word now resolves to its flight (`kind: 'gap'`, `part: 'gap'`, unchanged), then its **landing
  ramp**: `kind: 'road'`, `part: 'land'`, the jump's id.
  - It is straight at the landing pitch (`k` and `kp` all 0), on the **take-off road's cross-section** (a flat font
    when there is none).
  - It carries `landing: { speed, speedFrom, touchdownsM, sizedBy, marginM, why }`.
  - So the head after a jump sits on road, and the next word starts on the landing, level, with curvature continuous.
- **The length is validation's, not mine.** It is `src/validate/jumps.js` `landingRamp({ D, dh, thetaRad, landRad, v })`:
  - the flight meets the ramp line **exactly** (the quadratic's larger root) at each measured fall (3.2 g and 6.3 g,
    MACH6.jumpG);
  - it runs to the farther touchdown plus E's 10 m margin.
  - That length is horizontal. Along the straight ramp it is `/ cos(landing pitch)`.
- **The speed** is the jump word's own. With none, it is `LANDING.DEFAULT_KMH` 300 (inferred: the platform test's
  design speed), and `landing.speedFrom` says `'default'`.
- **My first version, replaced:**
  - I sized the ramp with `checkJump` over 0.5 m road samples, with a 20 m run-out and a 20 m minimum.
  - Its touchdown was 1.2 mm short of exact, because `checkJump` interpolates linearly. I added a sample step, then
    switched to E's exact `landingRamp` when it appeared.
  - The two tests that encoded my 20 m constants now read E's margin: ≥ touchdown + 10 m, and exact to 1e-6. They are
    my tests from this lap, not landed.
- **The next road blends from the ramp.** The ramp is road, so the next word blends from it when its font differs (the
  "fonts never jump" ruling). Before, the rule was "after a gap nothing to blend from". **That changes one of B's tests
  (below).**

## 2. The ghost of the next piece (`app/shell.js`, `app/palette/palette.js`, `app/index.html`)

- **`shell.candidate(name)`:** the resolved track as it would be with `name` placed.
  - It is built by the **same function as `place()`** (`placed(name)`, factored out), so the ghost is exactly what a
    click builds, for built-in words (with the pickers) and for the user's own pieces.
  - It commits nothing, and throws the model's error for a piece that cannot be placed.
  - A jump's candidate ends on its landing ramp, so the ghost shows where the next piece starts.
- **The palette:** pointing at a piece (mouseenter), or tabbing to it (focus), calls `on.ghost(name)`. Leaving (mouseleave
  / blur) calls `on.ghost(null)`, and a click places it.
- **The page:** `on.ghost` sends C's `t180-ghost` with `{ candidate: shell.candidate(name) }`, or `t180-ghost-clear`.
  Placing clears the ghost first, and a piece that cannot be placed shows no ghost and says why in the status line.
- **One note for C:** `app/testhook/ghostword.js` re-implements `place()` for built-in words, as its own header says,
  "until A's palette sends its own candidates". The palette now does, for user pieces too, so the page no longer needs
  it. C's `look.test.js` may still use it.
