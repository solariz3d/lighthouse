## The seat's own reading of why it is thin (mine, not his)

- **There are no words anywhere.** The method this was built on forbids text on screen, so every idea has to be read
  from shapes. A viewer who does not know Consonance is never told what a room is, who the round light is, or what the
  figure between two voices means.
- **Three acts in three minutes.** The round of work, which is how the whole program runs, gets 21 seconds. The
  wager gets one held room, about two seconds. The clash, the flood and the first knot together get 18 seconds.
- **Part three has no character.** Parts one and two follow a flame with a face. From 108 s on the film is wide shots
  of a land; "me" is a small light in the corner.
- **Nothing is at stake for anyone the viewer knows.** The flood threatens a land of strangers.

## How it was made, for doing it again

- The method is John Heibel's: `github.com/JohnHeibel/ClaudeAnimationBase` (MIT) and the earlier `PDoomVideo`. A
  guide, then a storyboard with timed reads, then one painter per pair of chapters, each checking its own range on
  consecutive frames. The engine and all drawing code are this seat's own.
- Five Opus painters painted the ten chapters. Their reported token use: 266,059 + 330,602 + 277,135 + 294,639 +
  389,224.
- What worked: fixing the exact frame at every boundary between painters in the storyboard; a shared timeline nobody
  may change; the director looking at one sheet per painter and a strip at every seam.
- What cost time: a smoothing step in `score.py` that used `np.convolve` over the whole film (replaced by a running
  sum); two painters lighting the same room differently.
