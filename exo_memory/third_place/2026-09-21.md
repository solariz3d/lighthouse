## 01:00 — "Jev", checked before answered

The keeper brought a three-page infographic, "Jev + Claude Code: build agents 200x faster and 400x cheaper,"
as something that "could greatly benefit us." Checked outside first. **Jev is real and is not what the
infographic says.** It is TypeSafe AI's "System One model": it takes text or structured input and returns a
TYPED DECISION (a choice, a score, a yes/no probability, calibrated) in one forward pass; it cannot write
text. The 20–200x faster / 40–400x cheaper figures are TypeSafe's own, per call, against LLMs on
classification — not "building agents." The infographic casts it as the orchestrator that "breaks the mission
into work packets," which it cannot do. Its workflow advice is generic and the room already does most of it;
one line lands on a number the room already holds: "send a manifest, not the entire corpus" (the librarian's
09-16 measurement, ~549k tokens of intake per seven-seat launch, BOOT ~60% of each pane's).

Where it could bear on the room, for the keeper to carry if he wants: the registered scorers. The polarity
measure (anchor draft R8b: AFFIRMS / CONTRADICTS / SILENT per claim x hand-back, two blind scorers, agreement
>= 0.70) is a typed classification, and the room's standing problem with judges is shared weights (45/45). A
decision model from another company is a decorrelated scorer reachable by a tool script even though non-Claude
PANES are unreachable (draft 8.5). One independent test exists (LangChain: 500/500 vs a human oracle on binary
pass/fail, Claude 80.0%; variance 92x lower than Claude's; $0.34 vs $28.17 per 1,000) and says of itself that
it is narrow (five weather requests) and that a consistently wrong cheap evaluator scales its mistakes.
Cautions: vendor numbers; hosted, so text leaves the machine (he chose local for embeddings on 09-15); a new
dependency is his call; the room's controls (p-leave-read-B positive, p-leave-E negative) are how it would be
admitted, not the brochure. Not a build for this seat.
