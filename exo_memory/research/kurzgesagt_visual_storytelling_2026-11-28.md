# Kurzgesagt's method of visual storytelling, as far as the web will give it up (2026-11-28)

*Written by a research agent briefed by the Third Place seat on L on 2026-11-28, read-only, from the web; nothing in it has been tried yet.*

## Read this first

The honest size of this report: the primary sources are thin. Kurzgesagt have said very little in public about HOW they stage a picture. Their founder said in a German interview that they deliberately "never did behind-the-scenes" (source in section 8). What they have said fits on one page, and it is quoted below.

No source I could reach describes a Kurzgesagt video shot by shot. I could not watch video. So the table in section 1 is a proxy built from two text sources, and it says which columns are missing.

Three labels are used throughout:

- **THEY SAY**: Kurzgesagt's own words (their site, or the transcript of their own video).
- **ANALYST**: a third party's claim. Most of these are marketing or tutorial blogs, not careful studies. Treat them as leads.
- **INFERRED**: my own reading or arithmetic. Not checked against the films.

A note on transcripts: the quotes from their videos come from transcript mirrors (lingq.com, rosetta.to), not from the videos themselves. The rosetta.to transcripts are auto-captions with errors. Wording should be checked against the video before it is quoted onward.

## 0. What they say about the process (verified, with exact words)

From their process page, https://kurzgesagt.org/youtube/ (THEY SAY):

- "Our scripts are the backbone of our videos."
- "For each one we try to find the perfect balance between telling facts and shaping them into a captivating story."
- "This can take about a dozen drafts to cut away unnecessary bit and polish the final result."
- "After deliberating over the best visual metaphors and ideas for each scene in the sketching phase" the illustration begins.
- "Our trademark are quirky and relatable characters" and "if you can't sympathize with our mitochondria and brown dwarfs, we don't know what else will melt your heart."
- "His voiceover provides the timing for the animation team, who bring scenes and characters to life"
- "We combine classic 2D flat animation with 3D elements – whatever is needed to make our puppets dance and planets turn."
- "Each Kurzgesagt gets its own specifically composed soundtrack from Epic Mountain music" to "give it a unique mood and emotional impact."

Correction to the brief: the process page does NOT carry the words "transitions", "200 panels" or "1,200 hours". Those come from the 2020 video, below. The page was fetched on 2026-11-28 and those terms were not on it.

From "How to Make a Kurzgesagt Video in 1200 Hours" (2020), transcript mirror at https://www.lingq.com/en/learn-english-online/courses/689474/how-to-make-a-kurzgesagt-video-in-120-4887128/ (THEY SAY):

- "We try to think of visual metaphors, transitions between ideas, and decide which scenes need the most attention."
- "On average, 200 panels need to be illustrated in Adobe Illustrator."
- "The general idea is to find the right images to the words in the script."
- "In total, two to three illustrators spend 8 to 12 weeks illustrating the video."
- "two to three animators animate scene after scene for about eight to ten weeks in total."
- "every single thing that's moving on-screen has been moved manually."
- "Each character is rigged, so we can move them more naturally."
- "Every song is original music composed to the finished video to create a unique atmosphere."
- "The icing on the cake are the sound effects."
- "Writing is the bottleneck for our videos, and very hard to speed up."
- "But we also want to tell stories and express how we feel about the universe from time to time."

The CERN deck's stage list (research and script, final script, scientists' feedback, illustration, sketches, storyboard, animation) was seen only in a search snippet. The PDF itself would not load (over the fetch size limit): https://indico.cern.ch/event/914586/sessions/348932/attachments/2160174/3644471/Kurzgesagt.pdf

One more line of theirs, from a post on X seen only in a search snippet (the post itself returned HTTP 402), https://x.com/Kurz_Gesagt/status/1814993983023530488: "An animation scene is just like a theater, where all of the stories unfold."

No source gives seconds per shot, words per panel, or cuts per minute. A search for "average shot length" and similar terms with the channel's name returned nothing about them.

### Arithmetic from their own numbers (INFERRED)

- 200 panels over about 9 to 10 minutes is one new illustrated panel every 2.7 to 3 seconds.
- 1,300 words over 200 panels is about 6.5 words per panel. That is less than one sentence per panel: roughly one panel per clause.
- At that rate a 23 minute film would need about 460 to 510 panels.

Cautions on this arithmetic: the word count is from 2019 and the panel count from 2020. A "panel" is an illustrated layout, not a camera cut; one panel can hold while pieces build on it, and one camera move can cross several panels. Their newer videos are longer and their numbers may have changed. Nothing later than 2020 was found.

## 2. How an abstract idea becomes a picture

THEY SAY:

- Metaphors are chosen scene by scene, before illustration: "deliberating over the best visual metaphors and ideas for each scene in the sketching phase" (https://kurzgesagt.org/youtube/).
- Things are turned into characters the viewer can feel for: "if you can't sympathize with our mitochondria and brown dwarfs" (same page). This is their own statement that personifying a non-living thing is a core device.
- On the limits: "we need to find metaphors and stories that capture the true nature of things" and "Don't confuse the description of a thing for the thing itself." Also: "If a simplification is too engaging, if the story it tells is too good, it can distort the true" picture (quote cut off in the source). All from "...And We'll Do It Again": https://www.lingq.com/de/englisch-lernen-online/courses/1301346/and-well-do-it-again-20846000/
- They admit a standard picture can teach a wrong one: colour-coded spinning quarks create "a very wrong image of reality in our heads", and their solar system shows planets that are in truth "super different in size and not close to each other at all" (same source).

ANALYST (a marketing blog, low reliability, names no video titles): the immune system as "a well-organized army of specialized soldiers", atoms as "Lego bricks", the universe as "a massive clockwork machine". https://livecounts.io/blog/case-studies/kurzgesagts-existential-hook-optimistic-nihilism-as-a-branding-strategy

ANALYST (seen only in a search snippet; the Medium page returned HTTP 403): they commit to one long analogy instead of hopping between examples, for example a table tennis analogy held for about two minutes to explain perception, and a "butler" for the brain's automatic processes. https://medium.com/@prismiqpro/creative-spotlight-how-kurzgesagt-transforms-disorienting-science-youtube-analysis-video-editing-tips-e83f3373d600

ANALYST (a style tutorial): "Infographic moments" turn numbers into charts or scales inside the illustrated world, and every sentence gets a metaphor so the picture explains and does not only illustrate. https://cliptude.com/styles/kurzgesagt-style/

The four kinds named in the brief, and what I could source for each:

| Kind | Sourced? |
|---|---|
| A thing personified as a character | Yes, in their own words (mitochondria, brown dwarfs) |
| An idea as an object or machine | Analyst only (Lego bricks, clockwork) |
| A quantity as a crowd or a size | One case from the Optimistic Nihilism walkthrough: a life as weeks ("5200 weeks"), https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20170728-optimistic-nihilism. What the picture looks like was not described. |
| A process as a journey | Not found in any source reached |

## 3. Transitions

THEY SAY: only one sentence, but it matters. Transitions are designed at the sketch stage, together with the metaphors and before any final art: "We try to think of visual metaphors, transitions between ideas, and decide which scenes need the most attention." (lingq transcript of the 2020 video, link in section 0). Note the wording: transitions "between ideas", not between shots.

ANALYST (livecounts.io, link above): "A planet shrinks to become an atom. A cell wall dissolves to reveal a galaxy." The blog credits "narrative momentum" over constant cuts. It names no video, so these two examples are unverified.

ANALYST (a tutorial about the flat explainer style in general, not a study of Kurzgesagt): "The camera glides and zooms between scenes so it feels like one world." and "Smooth easing, no bouncing, no flashy transitions." https://www.videotto.com/blog/how-to-make-a-kurzgesagt-style-animated-explainer

Not found: any statement by them on match cuts, objects carried across, or wipes; any shot-level example with a video name and timestamp.

## 4. Scale

THEY SAY: nothing found on scale as a device.

What the Optimistic Nihilism sources show (the narration is theirs; the reading is INFERRED):

- Scale is climbed in steps, one step per sentence, each sentence a new picture: speck of dust, star, galaxy, cluster, supercluster, observable universe (rows 14 and 15 of the table, and the Gigazine captions after them).
- The same is then done with time: a 100 year life as "5200 weeks", then the life against the age of the universe (Gigazine captions 15 to 18 in https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20170728-optimistic-nihilism).
- The climb in scale is the argument of the first half; the turn of the video comes right after it. Chapter marks: "The Scary Universe" from 0:00, "Introducing Optimistic Nihilism" at 3:13 (https://rosetta.to/u/kurzgesagt/optimistic-nihilism).

ANALYST (videotto.com, link above, general style advice): "Something tiny next to something huge, then zooming out again and again", and use everyday objects as the yardstick (Earth as a peppercorn, the sun as a bowling ball).

Not found: how often they jump scale per video; any statement from them on showing numbers.

## 5. Stand-in characters and humour

THEY SAY:

- Characters are the trademark and their job is sympathy (process page, quoted in section 0).
- Dettmer, in a 2015 Adobe profile mirrored at https://dustindriver.com/in-a-nutshell-kurz-gesagt-explains-it-all/: "Even though we're explaining complex things, I don't think it should be too serious," and "The videos should be fun, and learning something should be a bonus." and "The evil master plan behind the videos is teaching people without it feeling that way."
- Characters are rigged puppets so they can act (lingq transcript, section 0).

ANALYST (livecounts.io): "visual dissonance", meaning cute round figures set against dark subject matter, which the blog says is why "You cannot look away". The same blog claims they do not cut away to unrelated jokes. That is one blog's opinion.

## 6. Build-up in time with the sentence

THEY SAY:

- The voice comes first and sets the clock: "His voiceover provides the timing for the animation team" (https://kurzgesagt.org/youtube/).
- Illustrated scenes are taken apart into pieces so each piece can move on its own: "The first thing to do is take the illustrated scenes apart and organize them." and "The hundreds or thousands of parts are then imported into After Effects." (lingq transcript, section 0).
- Narration is recorded paragraph by paragraph (ANALYST summary of their video: https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20200217-science-animation-making).

INFERRED from the three lines above: a scene is a set of separate parts, and each part has an entrance that can be placed on a word of the recorded voice. That is how a picture can be assembled during a sentence.

Not found: any statement about what moves on which word, or any worked example.

A Skillshare class, "Motion Graphics with Kurzgesagt", taught by one of their senior motion designers, is said to walk through typical scene types (multi-state screens, split screens with simple characters). I saw this only in a search snippet; the page returned HTTP 403: https://www.skillshare.com/en/classes/motion-graphics-with-kurzgesagt-part-1/631970755. It is the most promising unread source for this section.

## 7. Structure of a video

THEY SAY:

- Script first, and it is the backbone; about a dozen drafts (process page).
- The aim is "a new perspective on a topic" that leaves viewers with new insights "without them really noticing" (process page).
- Opinion is labelled as opinion (summary of "Can You Trust Kurzgesagt Videos?", https://rosetta.to/u/kurzgesagt/can-you-trust-kurzgesagt-videos).

What the Optimistic Nihilism transcript shows (INFERRED from https://rosetta.to/u/kurzgesagt/optimistic-nihilism):

- It opens on the feeling, not the topic: "human existence is scary and confusing".
- It first gives the comfort ("this place was obviously made for us ... we were home"), then takes it away one sentence at a time ("we learn that...", repeated).
- The turn comes at about 3:13 of roughly 6 to 7 minutes, near the midpoint.
- The second half answers the first half's pictures in order (Gigazine captions 22 to 27: the stars, the short life, the heat death, each revisited with the new reading).
- It ends on the viewer and on what to do, not on a summary (Gigazine captions 37 to 40).

ANALYST (livecounts.io): a five-part emotional shape, "Hook, Grounding, Crisis, Pivot, Release"; the music changes at the crisis; and "Unearned optimism feels fake", meaning the hopeful ending only works because the dark part was faced first.

ANALYST (Wikipedia search snippet and Nerdist, not opened): "The Last Human" uses branching paths. Not verified.

On hard or sad subjects, from them: nothing found beyond the Dettmer line that it should not be "too serious". The handling of "Loneliness" and "The Egg" could not be sourced. "The Egg" is reported as over two years in the making and slightly adapted from the text for video (search snippet from https://kottke.org/19/09/the-egg-by-andy-weir, not opened).

## 8. What they avoid, and mistakes they have named

THEY SAY (from "Can You Trust Kurzgesagt Videos?", 2019, as summarised from auto-captions at https://rosetta.to/u/kurzgesagt/can-you-trust-kurzgesagt-videos; check wording against the video):

- "the hardest part is being brief while not simplifying too much."
- Of the deleted addiction video: they "simplified an idea so much that it made a great story but became distorting."
- Of the deleted refugee video: made fast and in anger; "absolutely unnecessary to be toxic about it".
- "these two videos don't make us proud."

THEY SAY (from "...And We'll Do It Again", 2021, lingq link in section 2): the warning already quoted, that a story which is "too good" can distort. The project page calls their method "Lies to children, a way to tell a highly simplified story that serves as a gateway to understand the bigger picture." https://kurzgesagt.org/projects/behind-the-lies

THEY SAY (Dettmer, in German, https://www.business-punk.com/deluxe/youtube-kurzgesagt-interview/): "Wir haben nie Behind-The-Scenes gemacht, nie Gesichter gezeigt." (We never did behind-the-scenes, never showed faces.) and "Wir wollen dieser Art von Fankult und parasozialen Beziehungen entkommen." (We want to escape that kind of fan cult and parasocial relationship.) This explains why primary sources on their craft are so few.

Reported by Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurzgesagt: a 2024 exercise video was criticised as oversimplified and was redone with more experts; the addiction video had leaned on one contested source.

The lesson that carries over to a story film (INFERRED): their two named failures were both cases of the story winning over the truth of the thing, not failures of picture making. They have published no list of visual mistakes.

## Rules we could apply

Each rule is one sentence a storyboard can be checked against. The source it rests on follows it.

1. Every sentence of narration has its own panel, and a sentence that lists things has one panel per thing listed. (Inferred from the Optimistic Nihilism table, section 1.)
2. Count the panels: a board with far fewer than one panel per 7 spoken words is sparser than their stated average. (Inferred from their 200 panels and about 1,300 words; section 1.)
3. For every panel, write the one line of narration it is "the right image" for; a panel with no line, or a line with no panel, is flagged. (They say: "find the right images to the words in the script".)
4. Each abstract idea in the script has a named picture chosen for it at the sketch stage, written beside the line before any modelling starts. (They say: metaphors are decided "for each scene in the sketching phase".)
5. Each join between two ideas has its transition drawn on the board as its own entry, not left for the edit. (They say: they plan "transitions between ideas" while sketching.)
6. The board marks which scenes get the most work and which are allowed to be plain. (They say: "decide which scenes need the most attention".)
7. The recorded voice exists before animation, and every entrance of a piece is tied to a word in it. (They say: the "voiceover provides the timing"; scenes are taken "apart" into parts.)
8. Every scene is built from separable pieces that can arrive one at a time, never one finished picture that only holds. (They say: "take the illustrated scenes apart"; the reading is inferred.)
9. Where a thing with no face must carry feeling, it is given a body and behaviour the viewer can feel for. (They say: "if you can't sympathize with our mitochondria and brown dwarfs".)
10. A change of scale is shown as a staircase, one step per sentence and one picture per step, with a familiar thing in frame as the yardstick. (Inferred from Optimistic Nihilism rows 14 and 15; yardstick from an analyst, videotto.com.)
11. A repeated sentence frame ("we learn that...") gets a new picture each time, never the same picture held. (Inferred from the table, rows 9 to 15.)
12. A chosen metaphor is kept and developed across many lines before a new one is brought in. (Analyst, prismiqpro on Medium, seen in snippet only.)
13. Pictures set up in the first half return in the second half with a changed meaning. (Inferred from Optimistic Nihilism captions 22 to 27.)
14. The film opens on a feeling the viewer already has, and the hopeful part comes only after the hard part has been shown in full. (Opening inferred from the transcript; "Unearned optimism feels fake" is an analyst, livecounts.io.)
15. For each metaphor, write down what it gets wrong about the real thing, and drop it if the story it tells is better than it is true. (They say: a story that is "too good" can distort; and the addiction video "made a great story but became distorting".)
