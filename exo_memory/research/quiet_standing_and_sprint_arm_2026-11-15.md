## 1. Quiet standing

### (a) Centre-of-pressure sway, eyes open

- **dos Santos DA, Fukuchi CA, Fukuchi RK, Duarte M (2017),** "A data set with kinematic and ground reaction forces of human balance", PeerJ 5:e3626, doi:10.7717/peerj.3626 [read, via PMC5534162].
  - 49 adults: 27 young (18-40 y, mean 28.1), 22 older (60+ y, mean 67.8). Barefoot two-footed stance on two force plates, rigid or foam surface, eyes open or closed; 3 x 60 s per condition, 12 trials a subject.
  - Licence: article CC BY 4.0, **data CC0 1.0.** Figshare doi:10.6084/m9.figshare.4525082 (about 6.9 GB); a notebook at github.com/demotu/datasets.
  - No sway values in the text; means and CIs only in its Figure 4, not read. **Sway amplitude and frequency can be computed from the raw files.**
- **KINECAL:** Maudsley-Barton S, Yap MH (2023), Scientific Data, doi:10.1038/s41597-023-02375-w; PhysioNet v1.0.3, doi:10.13026/vvkp-ct80 [read]. 90 participants aged 18-92 (33 healthy adults under 65). Kinect V2: quiet standing, eyes open and closed, firm and foam, tandem stance, sit-to-stand, TUG, a 3 m walk. Derived sway metrics included. CC0 1.0. No running.
- **Not found:** body sway (not the centre of pressure) in mm or degrees, or front-back against side-to-side frequency content, with means and SDs.

### (b) Weight shifts between the feet

- **Duarte M, Zatsiorsky VM (1999),** "Patterns of center of pressure migration during prolonged unconstrained standing", Motor Control 3(1):12-27, doi:10.1123/mcj.3.1.12 [snippet, abstract]. 10 young adults standing over 30 minutes. Three patterns: shifting (a fast move of the average position to a new region), fidgeting (a fast, large excursion and return), drifting (a slow continuous displacement). **Counts, sizes and durations are in the full text, not seen.**
- Duarte, Harvey, Zatsiorsky (2000), "Stabilographic analysis of unconstrained standing", Ergonomics [snippet]: no significant effect of load, vision, surface or shoes on the number of patterns.
- A CDC/NIOSH report on prolonged standing (stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/125393) [snippet]: 7 subjects, weight shifting increased with standing time; PDF not readable.

### (c) Resting breathing

- Rate: only generic reference ranges, about 12-20 breaths a minute [snippet, clinical sites]; not a measured mean and SD.
- Chest and abdomen excursion in mm: no source found.
- To open for numbers: an optoelectronic plethysmography study of 83 adults, Braz J Phys Ther 2019, PubMed 30967355 [snippet] (posture changed chest wall motion, not rate); MDPI Sensors 2023, doi:10.3390/s23249736 [snippet].

### (d) Idle head and gaze movements

- No study of glance frequency or head-turn size during idle standing was found.
- Indirect: gaze shifts under about 20 degrees are made mostly with the eyes; larger shifts recruit the head (Becker 1989, via a dissertation chapter) [snippet]. Saccades about 3-4 a second in general [snippet].

## 2. The arm at 5 to 9 m/s in runners who are not trained sprinters

**The agent's plain answer: no peer-reviewed numbers were found for shoulder range, elbow range or hand height in recreational runners at 5-9 m/s.**
- Preece et al. (2016), Gait & Posture [snippet]: 14 elite and 14 recreational runners at 3.3-5.6 m/s; pelvis, lumbar spine and thorax only (recreational runners lean the thorax forward more with speed). No shoulder or elbow values seen.
- Murphy, Clark, Murray, Melton, Mann, Rieger (2021), J Clin Transl Res 7(5):682-686, PMC8580521 [read]: 38 elite 100 m sprinters; lower limb only.
- Pontzer et al. (2009), J Exp Biol 212:523-534 [snippet]: the arms as mass dampers; restricting arm swing did not change cost.
- Gravestock, Tucker, Hanley (2021), Front Sports Act Living 3:702743 [read]: elite racewalkers; correlations only.
- **Mann and Herman (1985),** "Kinematic analysis of Olympic sprint performance: men's 200 meters", Int J Sport Biomech 1(2):151-162, doi:10.1123/ijsb.1.2.151 [snippet]: the 1984 final; the abstract does not give arm values. The coaching article's figures (shoulder excursion about 135 against 118 degrees, elbow about 84 against 67, faster against slower finalists) are secondary and unverified (the page returned 403). No hand-height figure.
