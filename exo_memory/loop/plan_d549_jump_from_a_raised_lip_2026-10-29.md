# D549: a jump takes off from a raised or shifted lip. Librarian, 2026-10-29 21:3x

The user, 21:32: "say you raise the straight with climb a little before adding the jump, it wont place, it has to be flat to spawn in the jump, which is a bug, you should be able to add the climb to create the jump, then place the jump from it".

## What refuses it today (checked in t180-fresh at the 0.4.31 base)

- `src/core/document.js:461` checkDoc: before a flight, the previous road piece's OFFSET channels (`h`, the height brush; `l`, the sideways brush) must be 0 in value AND slope at its end, else `FLIGHT_OFFSET`: "h must fade to 0 (value and slope) before a jump". `app/core/jumpwords.js:11` says it to the user as "the road at the end still carries a height or sideways offset (from the local brush), and a jump can only take off from road where those have faded". So a straight lifted at its end with the Sculpt hill brush (which writes `h`) cannot take a jump. That is the user's case: he raised the end of the straight to make a lip, and the lip is exactly where the jump should start.
- The pitch channel (`kv`, the climb field on Extend) is NOT refused; a piece that climbs by its own route takes a jump. E confirms this with a test in Part 1 so the bug is the one we think.
- Why the rule existed (D243/D258): the adapter builds a flight as one gap segment from the take-off's heading frame (`src/core/adapter.js:343-352`, `src/geom/path.js` `to`), and that frame is the route's end (kh/kv), which does not see the offsets the adapter adds to positions afterwards. With an offset at the lip the flight would start from the unlifted point: a step of h at the lip, so the rule refused it rather than draw the step.

## The change (pane E, PROVE IT: core geometry)

The flight takes off from where the road actually ends. In the adapter, when a flight follows a road piece, the take-off pose is the route's end pose PLUS the offsets at that end: the position moved by `h` along the frame's up and `l` along its left, and the frame tilted by the offsets' end slopes (pitch by atan(dh/ds), heading by atan(dl/ds)) so a lip that rises keeps rising into the air. The landing then starts at that pose plus the flight's `to`, as today, with its own `h` and `l` at 0 (the air resets them, as the cross-section already resets after a flight). `FLIGHT_OFFSET` goes from checkDoc; a non-finite offset at the lip is still refused by the existing checks. The geometry: `flightLength(pitch, to)` takes the tilted pitch; the mesh's last road row at the lip is the lifted one (it already is), so there is no step.

Tests first, red before green (`test/core_jump_offset.test.js`): a 200 m straight whose last 40 m are lifted 2 m by `SC.brush` on `h`, then `jumpHere`: today FLIGHT_OFFSET (the red); after: the flight's gap segment starts at the lifted lip (the path's position at the gap's start equals the road's last row position within 1e-9), the landing's start pose is that point plus the default `to`, the mesh's last road row and the landing's first row are where the poses say, the landing's h and l read 0; the same with a sideways `l` shift (the heading tilts); a lip whose h slope is non-zero (a rising lift) gives a flight pitch equal to the lip's slope plus the piece's pitch; a straight with Extend climb 5° (kv) and no offsets takes a jump today and after, byte-identical; every export fixture 0 of 9 differ; a saved track with a jump after a clean lip is byte-identical (the goldens). Mutation: `test/core_jump_mutation.test.js` whole, with new mutants (the h value dropped from the pose; the slope dropped; the landing's h not reset). Full suite at landing. The checker: a jump from a lifted lip runs through the same jump rules as before (the downforce step, the landing's bank); E shows one checker run on the test track with no new red.

The app side: `jumpwords.js` loses the FLIGHT_OFFSET case (or keeps it for the non-finite case); the Jump button's ghost (`candidateJump`) shows the flight from the lifted lip; A looks once headless if the ghost code needs a change, else E says it did not.

Falsifier: if the user lifts a lip with the brush, places the jump, and the exported track shows a step at the lip in the game or the car's take-off pitch differs from what the lip shows in the builder, the pose composition is wrong (the slope term or the up direction), not the idea.
