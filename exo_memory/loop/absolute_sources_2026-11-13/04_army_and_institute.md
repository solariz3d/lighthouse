## 2. Findings

**The start: GONDOLA WISH and the first contact with Monroe (1977-1978)**

- F1. The 1985 historical overview's first row is "SEP 77 GONDOLA WISH established.", with the comment "Designed as an OPSEC
  support mission to mimic HOIS remote viewers to determine US vulnerability." [read] P20, page image n29, row 1.
- F2. The 1978 assessment report was "Prepared by: 902d Military Intelligence Group". Its office had been "investigating the
  intelligence threat posed by Soviet state-of-the-art parapsychology since September 1977." [read] P1, title page and
  Executive Summary ¶1.
- F3. FAS gives a different home: "a 1977 Army Assistant Chief of Staff for Intelligence (ACSI) Systems Exploitation
  Detachment (SED) effort". The primary papers read here name the 902d MI Group (F2). [summary: FAS] S1.
- F4. The aim was to train MI personnel "so that they can mimic Soviet collectors in an operations security (OPSEC) support
  effort." [read] P1, Executive Summary ¶1.
- F5. The technique chosen was the out-of-body experience, not remote viewing. In an OOBE "the subject perceives himself to
  be actually at the target location"; in remote viewing he is "only observing it". [read] P1, pages 3-4, ¶3.
- F6. How the Army found Monroe: "During March 1978, the name Robert Monroe surfaced during a review of an open source
  document concerning Dr. Elizabeth Kubler-Ross". The report says she had trained in OOBE with Monroe. [read] P1, ¶3.
- F7. Before any contact, DIA learned "that the CIA had shown interest in him in the 1973 - 1974 time frame". That
  assessment "was very casual", and CIA said Monroe was "OK" from a security standpoint. [read] P1, page 4, ¶4.
  *(second reader: the words are found, but DIA did not "learn" the 1973-74 interest; "Individuals at DIA were knowledgeable
  of Monroe, and stated that the CIA had shown interest". What DIA learned by contacting CIA was "that the CIA had no
  interest in Monroe at the present time". P1 ¶4, OCR lines 151-155.)*
- F8. Monroe was interviewed at the institute on 6 April 1978 ("P.O. Box 57, Afton, VA 22920"). On 3 May he got "a
  security indoctrination" and agreed "not to discuss our involvement with other members of MIAS, the press, other
  government agencies, or anyone else." [read] P1, pages 5-6, ¶6 and ¶8.
- F9. He agreed to train three MI personnel in OOBE in October and November 1978 for $2,000; "This is to be a pilot training
  program." BG John A. Smith, Jr., INSCOM, approved the fee on 19 July 1978. [read] P1, Exec. Summ. ¶4 and page 6, ¶8.
- F10. The concept paper's view of the other side: a classified Soviet program "whose funding and control reside largely
  with the Ministry of Defense and possibly with the KGB". It is listed under "ASSUMPTIONS", not findings. [read] P2 ¶2a.

- F12. The chronology records Gateway attendance at the institute: "SEP 80 First GRILL FLAME operative attends Gateway

- F13. Monroe's clearance has two dates in two papers: "On 11 September 1980, Robert A. Monroe was granted a SECRET security
  clearance" (P9 ¶1); "a DoD SECRET security clearance based on a NAC completed on 18 January 1980" (P13 ¶1). [read]
- F14. On 12 August 1980 Monroe phoned the GRILL FLAME training officer "at home at approximately 2100 hours" about a letter
  from LTC Channon: a conference whose subject "is to be the 1st Earth Bn." The signature block reads "FREDERICK H.
  ATWATER / 1LT, MI / Tng Officer" (slashes mark line breaks). [read] P3 ¶1 and signature.
- F15. Why GRILL FLAME wanted Hemi-Sync: to give remote viewers a relaxed state "during the conduct of sessions to stabilize
  their reliability." [read] P4 ¶2.
- F16. MG Stubblebine got his first GRILL FLAME briefing "as CG, INSCOM" on 30 June 1981. He read the customer evaluations

**CENTER LANE and the institute (1983)**

- F19. "19 JUL 83 Contract signed with Mr. Robert Monroe for 'One-on-One' training of CENTER LANE operative at MIAS."
  [read] P20, image n32.
- F20. The price: "Monroe has been paid $24,400.00 (ICF) for this training which will begin in the fall of 1983." The 1984
  funding paper in P20 gives the same sum as "$24.4K". [read] P9 ¶3b.
- F21. The plan had four phases: in-house "Discovery Series"; "Attendance at Gateway Program"; "One-on-one training with
  Monroe under laboratory conditions"; "Application of Hemisync tapes during collection operations." [read] P9 ¶2.
- F22. Phase III was to last ten weeks and include "nonintrusive biomonitoring equipment", Hemi-Sync tapes "custom made for
  project sources", and "environmentally controlled areas". The signature block reads "BRIAN BUZBY / LTC, MI / CENTER LANE
  Project Manager". [read] P9 ¶3a and signature.
- F23. A memo to the general before a seminar: "Six days of intensive tape exercises"; the group is "all 'military
  minded'"; pack "shorts and tee shirt, and carry along a bathing suit". [read] P7, pages 1-2.
- F24. The same memo's CAUTION: "Not all of the people who reside on the property, or those who frequent or visit the
  center are friendly to the U. S. Government or Army." Some knew "U. S. Army Intelligence personnel, were coming to attend
  a seminar." [read] P7, page 3.
