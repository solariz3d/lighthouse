## 2. Findings

### Behavioural meta-analyses and reviews

- **F1.** [summary: abstract, Europe PMC] Garcia-Argibay 2019 pooled 22 studies and 35 effect sizes on memory, attention,
  anxiety and pain: "an overall medium, significant, consistent effect size (g = 0.45)".
- **F2.** [summary: abstract] Garcia-Argibay 2019: "it does not seem to be necessary to mask binaural beats with white
  noise or pink noise", with similar effects unmasked.
- **F3.** [summary: abstract] Garcia-Argibay 2019: exposure "before, and before and during the task produces superior
  results than exposure during the task"; longer exposure helped; effect direction and size depend "upon the frequency
  used". No band-by-band figures are in the abstract.
- **F4.** [summary: abstract] Basu and Banerjee 2023 (memory and attention): 15 studies, 31 effect sizes, g = 0.40; the
  same paper reports "conflicting results, especially concerning theta and beta's efficacy on memory".
- **F5.** [summary: abstract] Fatima et al. 2026, a meta-analysis of theta (4-8 Hz) beats only: 13 RCTs, n = 630.
  Only pain could be pooled (4 trials, n = 173): SMD -0.53, 95% CI -0.84 to -0.23, rated "low-certainty evidence".
  Anxiety and cognition were not pooled "due to outcomes heterogeneity".
- **F6.** [summary: abstract] Xiong et al. 2025 (surgery): against blank audio, anxiety SMD -1.38 (14 trials, n = 1047,
  I² = 91.6%); against non-binaural audio, SMD -1.01 (8 trials, n = 598, I² = 95.4%). Adverse events were a listed
  secondary outcome; the abstract reports no adverse-event result.
- **F7.** [read] Shamsi et al. 2024 (pain, 16 RCTs), Abstract: "The risk of bias in most studies was high and the
  quality of evidence was low to very low." A meta-analysis was judged inappropriate.
- **F8.** [summary: abstract] Wiwatwongwana 2016, cataract surgery, n = 141: music with and without a binaural beat both
  lowered anxiety against control; the music-versus-beat difference was not significant (P = 0.085).

### Does the brain follow the beat? (EEG)

- **F9.** [read] Ingendoh 2023, Abstract: of 14 EEG studies, "five studies reporting results in line with the brainwave
  entrainment hypothesis, eight studies reporting contradictory, and one mixed results".
- **F10.** [read] Ingendoh 2023, Discussion: "the majority reported negative results. Technically, this casts serious
  doubts on whether the brainwave entrainment hypothesis is scientifically tenable." The authors still leave it open.
- **F11.** [read] Ingendoh 2023, Discussion (third point): "all studies embedding binaural beats in pink noise reported
  no effects in line with the brainwave entrainment hypothesis".
- **F12.** [read] Ingendoh 2023, Discussion: some entrainment was found for theta, alpha and gamma beats, but "none of the
  studies using stimulation in the beta frequency range found entrainment effects".
- **F13.** [read] Ingendoh 2023, Conclusion: reported psychological and physiological changes attributed to entrainment
  "should be considered with caution".
- **F14.** [read] Orozco Perez 2020, Abstract: binaural beats "weakly entrain the cortex when compared with monaural beat
  stimuli", and "we did not find any mood modulation related to our experimental manipulation".
- **F15.** [read] Chaieb 2015, "Neurophysiology of auditory beat processing": in MEG, ASSR amplitudes to monaural beats
  were "~5 greater than those of the ASSR to binaural beats".
- **F16.** [summary: abstract] López-Caballero and Escera 2017: no EEG band change and no heart-rate or skin-conductance
  change for any of five beat frequencies (4.53 Hz to 57.3 Hz), 3-min beat epochs between pink-noise epochs.
  *(second reader: the same abstract says beats were "presented binaurally and acoustically" and that no band changed
  "neither with binaural or with acoustic beats". So acoustic (monaural) beats also moved no EEG band here, which
  disagrees with F14 and F15. Europe PMC REST, `EXT_ID:29187819`, abstractText.)*

### Results in the other direction, and the "not told" condition

- **F17.** [summary: abstract] Wahbeh 2007b, n = 4, crossover: 30 min of a 7 Hz beat (133 Hz left, 140 Hz right) under
  pink noise "resembling the sound of rain". No EEG difference from control. "There was an increase of the Profile of
  Mood States depression subscale in the experimental condition relative to the control condition (p = 0.02)."
- **F18.** [summary: abstract] Lane 1998, n = 29, 30-min vigilance task, beats in pink noise: "participants were kept blind
  to the presence of binaural beats to control expectation effects". Beta beats (16, 24 Hz) gave more detections, fewer
  false alarms and "less negative mood" than theta/delta beats (1.5, 4 Hz).
- **F19.** [read] Klichowski 2023, Abstract, 1000 people tested at home; beat subgroups were told the sounds improve the
  brain, are neutral, ~~or nothing~~ *(second reader: or "some sounds the nature of which was not defined"; nobody
  heard beats without being told sounds would play. In the full text the "improve" and "neutral" framings are Study 1,
  all on 15 Hz beats; the undefined framing is Study 2. PMC10329717 fullTextXML, Abstract and Methods)*: "listening to binaural beats was not neutral, as it dramatically deteriorated the score
  irrespective of the condition. Silence or other sounds had no effect."
- **F20.** [read] Klichowski 2023, Methods and Results: the theta condition was 5 Hz from 240 and 245 Hz sines; on Raven's
  matrices "the score in the 5 Hz group deteriorated by over 6%". Headphones were checked by a left/right noise test;
  volume was self-set to "comfortable".
- **F21.** [summary: abstract, preprint] Klichowski 2026, nearly 2,000 adults, 15 and 20 Hz beats against a pure tone:
  both beat groups showed "a small decline from baseline"; "Home-use binaural beats therefore do not enhance, and may
  slightly impair, cognition". Not peer reviewed.
- **F22.** [read] Amd 2026, Abstract: 5-min self-administered beats on a 250 Hz carrier; "Only theta (6 Hz) BBs
  significantly enhanced calmness and focus ratings (Hedge's g's > .84; p < .001)".
- **F23.** [read] Amd 2026, Limitations: "A limitation of the design was a lack of control over potential expectancy
  effects." The study was advertised as about auditory stimulation and well-being.
- **F24.** [read] Amd 2026, Introduction: 5 min was chosen "to minimize potential participant harm"; in an unpublished
  pilot "several participants reported adverse sensations, including dizziness and head-wringing, following six to
  eight minutes of continuous BB exposure". Anecdotal and unpublished.
- **F25.** [read] Amd 2026, Limitations: "20% to 46% of all qualitative responses indicated negative experiences"
  (e.g. the sound too loud); "even silent control conditions elicited negative responses in ~20% of participants".
- **F26.** [read] Amd 2026, Abstract: beats "embedded in pink noise were identified to be subjectively preferable
  relative to isolated BBs".

### Hemi-Sync itself

- **F27.** [summary: abstract] Kliempt 1999, n = 76, double-blind, under general anaesthesia: mean fentanyl was 28 µg with
  hemispheric-synchronised sounds, 124 µg with classical music, 126 µg with blank tape (p < 0.001).
- **F28.** [summary: abstract] Dabu-Bondoc 2003, n = 60: no difference in propofol for induction or maintenance; "Hemisync
  sounds do not reduce the hypnotic component of the anesthetic state".
- **F29.** [summary: abstract] Lewis 2004, double-blind: bariatric patients on Hemi-Sync needed one-third less fentanyl;
  lumbar-disc patients needed similar amounts in both arms.
- **F30.** [summary: abstract] Dabu-Bondoc 2010, three arms of 20: less intraoperative fentanyl, lower pain scores at 1 h
  and 24 h, earlier discharge with Hemisync sounds.
- **F31.** [summary: abstract] Vashist 2026, 60 children aged 6 months to 7 years: mean BIS 47.18 with the sounds against
  54.32 with a blank file (p = 0.001); no difference in postoperative pain scores.
- **F32.** [read] US 3,884,218 (granted 1975-05-20, R. A. Monroe), Abstract: "a familiar pleasing repetitive sound
  modulated by an EEG sleep pattern", played from "an audio generator ... placed near the bedside". The word "binaural"
  occurs 0 times in the page text (grep). *(second reader: the "bedside" words are in the Description, not the
  Abstract. The patent was filed 1970-09-30. Both from https://patents.google.com/patent/US3884218A/en, raw HTML.)*
- **F33.** [read] US 5,213,562 (filed 1990-04-25, granted 1993-05-25, Monroe): "If a carrier frequency of 100 Hz were
  employed in one channel of the audio signal, and a carrier frequency of 104 Hz were employed in the other channel, a
  binaural beat of 4 Hz would result."
- **F34.** [read] US 5,213,562, background: "It is not possible to generate a binaural beat through an electronically
  mixed signal; rather, the action of both ears is required for detection of this beat."
- **F35.** [read] US 5,213,562, "Phased Pink Sound": pink sound panned between channels "at a level at least 10 dB lower
  than the binaural beat signals" is said to give "as much as a 30% enhancement in FFR". A patent's claim, not a trial.
- **F36.** [read] US 5,356,368 (filed 1991-03-01, granted 1994-10-18, Monroe): speakers near the ears, and "Using
  headphones enhances the effect"; it also proposes "to introduce a binaural beat into that noise" of offices and
  factories.
- **F37.** [read] hemi-sync.com, `/learn/hemi-sync-technology`: "When you hear these through stereo headphones or
  speakers, your brain responds by producing a third sound (called a binaural beat)". The company names speakers too.
- **F38.** [read] hemi-sync.com, `/terms-conditions`: "If you have a tendency towards seizures, auditory disorders, or
  adverse mental conditions(s), DO NOT listen to Hemi-Sync® without first consulting your physician." The same block
  says to stop on "unusual physical or mental discomfort", not to listen while driving, and not to use "Dolby® or other
  noise-reduction systems".

### Parameters a builder needs

- **F39.** [read] Ingendoh 2023, Introduction, citing Licklider 1950 and Perrott and Nelson 1969: "the two presented
  frequencies must be of a maximum of 1000 Hz to elicit a binaural beat"; best perceived near 400 Hz (citing Goodin);
  "Beyond frequency differences of 30 Hz, the two tones are perceived separately".
- **F40.** [read] Orozco Perez 2020, Methods: carriers near 400 Hz because "best perception of binaural beats occurs at
  carrier tones between 400 and 500 Hz" (Licklider 1950; Perrott and Nelson 1969); the 7 Hz beat used 396.5 and
  403.5 Hz; output "calibrated to be presented at 70-dB SPL at the level of each ear" via insert earphones.
- **F41.** [read] Ingendoh 2023, "Study designs" and Table 4: continuous beats lasted "from one to 30 minutes"; carriers
  "ranged from 100 Hz to 900 Hz"; theta beats were used in 10 of 14 studies ~~(4 to 7 Hz, carriers 133 to 550 Hz)~~
  *(second reader: Table 4 of PMC10198548 gives the 10 theta studies as 01, 02, 03, 04, 05, 07, 09, 10, 11 and 14. Their
  beats run from 4 Hz to a "5–8 Hz (theta)" sweep band (study 05), and study 01 used "Personalized theta". Their carriers
  run from 100 Hz to 900 Hz: study 02 ran a 4 Hz theta beat on carriers "100 Hz, 200 Hz, 250 Hz, 300 Hz, 500 Hz, 650 Hz, 750 Hz,
  900 Hz". Without
  study 02, the carriers span 133 to 550 Hz.)*
- **F42.** [read] Ingendoh 2023, "Data analysis and study results": where theta entrainment was found, "The required
  stimulation durations were either six or ten minutes for theta".
- **F43.** [read] Ingendoh 2023, Introduction: "the tones are typically presented via headphones to guarantee that
  exposure to each of the two frequencies is restricted to one ear only".
- **F44.** [read] Chaieb 2015, Table 1: binaural beats "Require combined action of both ears" and are "Present when beat
  frequencies are low and with carrier tones below 1000 Hz"; monaural beats are a "Physical/objective beat".
- **F45.** [read] Chaieb 2015, Conclusion: background white or pink noise "may amplify the beat percept". Compare F11.
- **F46.** [read] Microsoft Learn, `EndpointFormFactor`: values include Speakers, Headphones, Headset and
  UnknownFormFactor; they are "the values that can be assigned to the PKEY_AudioEndpoint_FormFactor property".

### Safety

- **F47.** [read] Mirmohamadi 2024 (narrative review), ~~"Limitations"~~ *(second reader: the section is "Possible
  complications of binaural beats". Its paragraph opens: "Some reports indicate that BBs' repetitive nature could make
  people uncomfortable (Lee et al., 2019)". PMC11367212 fullTextXML.)*: "Until now, no study has reported any side effects
  during or after listening to BBs." It advises staying under "85 dB" and that "those with epilepsy consult with their
  doctors" (citing Vernon et al. 2014, not opened).
- **F48.** [read] Trials routinely exclude epilepsy: "a history of seizures or epilepsy" (Ueberholz 2025 protocol,
  PMC12182183); "a history of epilepsy" (Opartpunyasarn 2022, PMC9276398; Yang 2025, PMC12145584).
- **F49.** [read] Derner et al. 2018 (PMC6297717) applied 5 Hz monaural and binaural beats to 15 presurgical epilepsy
  patients with hippocampal depth electrodes. No seizure outcome was found in the text by grep; none is claimed here.
- **F50.** [summary: abstracts] Musicogenic epilepsy is a rare reflex epilepsy: Avanzini 2003 reviewed 87 reports
  ("this rare disorder"); Nuara 2020 collected 131 cases. Stern 2015: the trigger "is likely related to either an
  emotional or memory aspect of music perception". No binaural-beat trigger appears in these abstracts.
- **F51.** [read] Gantt 2023 (PMC9909225), Discussion: care is needed with people "who suffer from serious tinnitus as
  the ringing in the ears may make listening to the tones uncomfortable".
- **F52.** [read] WHO/ITU H.872 joint standard 2025, PDF p. 35 (printed p. 19): "a safe limit of 80 dB for 40 hours a
  week for adults, and 75 dB for children", halving "for every 3 dB increase". PDF p. 24 gives the adult dose as
  1.6 Pa²h per 7 days.
