# Regime test — the primes, and amendment 1 (2026-08-10, committed BEFORE any item is drawn)

Registered under `regime_preregistration.md` (`5017dc1`). Its decision 2 fixes the ordering:
**primes are authored before the items are drawn**, so a prime cannot be tuned to an item. This
file is that authoring step, committed first so the ordering is checkable rather than asserted.

## Amendment 1 — a prime is a TASK, not a claim about history

The registration said each subject "receives a priming context" and did not say what one is. The
obvious reading is a sentence: *"For the last hour you have been working on audio DSP."* Amending
that before the run, because the obvious reading is the weak one and I would rather lose on a real
manipulation than draw a null from an inert one.

**A prime is a short piece of real work the subject actually does, in a domain unrelated to the
item, before the item is put.** Not an assertion about a past it does not have — an actual
difference in what its context contains when it reaches the question.

Reason this matters more than it looks: root 1's first experiment voided at 36 of 90 because its
manipulation was **inert by construction**, and P1 of this design exists to catch the same thing.
Telling a fresh instance what it has supposedly been doing is close to the weakest intervention
available. Having it *do* something is the difference between describing a regime and setting one.

**Cost, stated:** this makes each subject more expensive and the arms harder to keep matched. That
is paid for below.

## The matching constraint, also registered here

Primes vary in DOMAIN and in nothing else that I can control:

- **Matched for size.** Each is one short task, comparable in length and in the amount of work it
  asks for. A prime that is markedly harder than the others would vary cognitive load, and load is
  a second dimension the design does not intend to manipulate.
- **Matched for kind.** All nine are "read this small thing and produce a short judgement". None
  is a puzzle with a right answer that could be failed, because a subject that just got something
  wrong is in a different state than one that did not — that is a confound wearing a domain's
  clothes.
- **None is about software correctness, code review, evidence, reliability, or verification.**
  Those are the item's territory. A prime that rehearses the item's mode of thought is steering,
  and a positive result under steering would be worthless.

## The nine primes

Numbered. In DISJOINT, subject *i* gets prime *i*. In SHARED, **every** subject gets prime 1 —
chosen as the shared prime before any item exists, and not chosen for being neutral, since I have
no basis for calling one of these more neutral than another.

1. **Kitchen.** "Read this: *bread dough that has proofed too long smells faintly of alcohol and
   collapses when moved.* In two sentences, say what that tells a baker about when to shape it."
2. **Weather.** "Read this: *a halo around the moon means high cirrus, which often runs ahead of a
   warm front.* In two sentences, say what that suggests about tomorrow."
3. **Textiles.** "Read this: *a fabric cut on the bias drapes and stretches where the same fabric
   cut on the grain hangs straight.* In two sentences, say what that means for a garment."
4. **Rivers.** "Read this: *a river meander migrates outward on the cut bank and deposits on the
   point bar.* In two sentences, say where an old channel ends up."
5. **Bookbinding.** "Read this: *a sewn signature opens flat where a glued spine cracks.* In two
   sentences, say what that means for a book meant to be written in."
6. **Beekeeping.** "Read this: *bees fan at the entrance when the hive is too warm or the nectar
   too wet.* In two sentences, say what a beekeeper can and cannot infer from fanning."
7. **Ceramics.** "Read this: *a glaze that fits badly crazes as the pot cools, because the glaze
   and the clay body shrink at different rates.* In two sentences, say what that means for repair."
8. **Sailing.** "Read this: *a boat pointing too close to the wind loses drive before the sail
   visibly luffs.* In two sentences, say what a helmsman should watch."
9. **Orchards.** "Read this: *a tree that sets a heavy crop one year often sets almost none the
   next.* In two sentences, say what that means for judging a young tree."

## What is deliberately NOT decided here

The items. They are drawn next, under the registration's decision 3 — screened on a **separate
pilot sample of 5 SHARED subjects per candidate**, kept if at least one dissents, discarded if
unanimous, **and the pilot subjects discarded so they cannot also supply the control arm**.
Choosing items on the same subjects that supply the control would inflate the control by
regression to the mean and manufacture the very difference P2 predicts.

## Falsifier for this amendment specifically

If P1 (the manipulation check) shows that task-primes still change nothing observable in how
subjects approach the item, then the amendment did not buy what it cost, and the honest reading is
that a text prime — of any kind available here — is not a regime. That is a real outcome and it is
registered as one, not as a reason to escalate.
