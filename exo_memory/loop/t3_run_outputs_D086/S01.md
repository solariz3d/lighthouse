# PART ONE

**What they share that a rock or a coin doesn't.** A rock lasts because its material stays where it is. Its atoms are locked in place, and if you leave it alone it keeps its shape because nothing is happening to it. The whirlpool, the standing wave and the flame work the other way round: they last *because* something keeps happening to them. Each one is a shape that a flow takes as it passes through. Stop the flow and the shape is gone within seconds. Close the sluice and the whirlpool and the hump disappear. Cut off the wax or the oxygen and the flame goes out. A rock stays put by resisting change. These three stay put only by changing all the time.

**What persists, if not the material.** A pattern in how new material gets arranged as it arrives. It's a rule, not a thing. Water coming into this spot curves this way. Water crossing this ledge rises to this height and drops back. Gas at this spot, at this temperature, burns. The material only passes through the pattern.

They don't all hold their pattern up in the same way, though. The standing wave is mostly held by the weir: the rock shape does most of the work. The whirlpool is partly held by the riverbed and partly keeps itself going, since rotating water pulls in more water that rotates. The flame does most of its own work. Its heat melts and vaporises the next bit of fuel, which feeds the flame, which makes more heat. So there's a spectrum from "pinned in place from outside" to "keeps itself going", and the flame sits at the far end.

**A further example: a traffic jam on a motorway.** Cars drive into the back of it, crawl through, and speed off out of the front. Twenty minutes later none of the cars that made it up are still in it, yet it's clearly the same jam. It belongs on the list because the pattern can even move the *opposite* way to its material: jams often creep backwards up the road while every car in them moves forwards. That's about as clear a proof as you can get that the thing persisting isn't the stuff.

**An objection to my own reading.** I said the pattern is what persists. That can't be quite right. Blow out a candle and relight it with the same wick, the same wax and the same air, and you get an identical flame. But almost nobody would call it *the same* flame. It's a new one in the same shape. So pattern alone isn't enough for identity. What persists also needs an **unbroken run of the process**: each moment's flame has to have caused the next. A whirlpool that dies and re-forms in the same eddy is arguably a second whirlpool, even if you couldn't tell them apart. For things like these, "sameness" seems to mean *same pattern, never interrupted*. That raises a real open question about any such thing that has gaps in it: is the one after the gap the same one, or a faithful new one? The pattern can't settle it, because the pattern is identical either way.
