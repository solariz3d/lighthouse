# PART ONE

**What the three share that a rock or coin doesn't.** A rock lasts because its material stays where it is. Leave it alone and it goes on existing, because nothing has to keep happening for it to be there. The whirlpool, the standing wave and the flame are different. Each exists only while something flows through it: water in the first two, fuel and oxygen in the flame. Stop the flow and the shape is gone at once, even though no material has been taken away. The rock lasts by holding still. These three last by being continually rebuilt.

**What persists, if not the material.** A pattern persists: a stable way the flow is organized at that spot. Each new bit of water or gas moves through the same arrangement of motion or reaction and then leaves. Two things hold the pattern in place. One is the setting it sits in, such as the shape of the riverbed, the lip of the weir, or the wick. The other is the dynamics of the flow, which pull it back into the same form after small disturbances. Push the standing wave slightly and it returns. The three are not identical, though. The standing wave is held almost entirely by the weir's fixed geometry. The flame partly keeps itself going: its heat melts and vaporizes the wax that feeds it. So the flame is a step closer to something that maintains itself, not just something the surroundings maintain.

**A further example: a traffic jam on a motorway.** A jam can last for an hour and even move backward, against the traffic. Every car in it enters at the back and leaves at the front within a few minutes. What stays is a zone of dense, slow traffic that the flow of cars keeps recreating. It fits the list because it is kept up by throughput, lasts while its contents change completely, and ends when the flow drops below a certain level, not when any particular car leaves.

**An objection to my own reading.** The line between the two kinds may be a matter of degree. A coin also exists because of ongoing processes: its atoms vibrate and its bonds are constantly re-formed. Given enough time, a rock is also replaced, as it weathers, erodes and becomes part of other rocks. Seen this way, "pattern versus stuff" is really "fast turnover versus slow turnover."

I think part of the distinction survives this. The rock and coin are close to equilibrium: cut off every energy supply and they stay as they are. The whirlpool, wave and flame are far from equilibrium: cut off the supply and they disappear. That is a real difference in kind, not only in speed. What the objection does show is that "material" is less solid than it first looks. Even the rock's persistence is its atoms staying in a pattern, not some substance that exists apart from any pattern.
