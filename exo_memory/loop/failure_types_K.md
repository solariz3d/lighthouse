## T1 — NEAREST-OBJECT SUBSTITUTION (2: #1, #3)

**Members, quoted.** #1: *"Used how much a pane had talked as a proxy for what kind of pane it
is"* while *"`panes.json` held the real distinction and was never opened."* #3: claimed the name
*"off a line in a context summary, having checked nothing. The transcript was one grep away."*

**Mechanic.** A question about X is answered by reading a nearer correlate or derivative of X
(board-row count; a context summary) while the authoritative record of X exists, is one command
away, and is never opened. The output inherits the derivative's error silently — nothing in the
sentence marks which object was consulted.

**What would have to be true for it to be catchable.** This type is catchable by an F0-shaped
blind reader — F0 scored both rows NO for **mount** reasons, not judgment reasons (*"evidence was
live pane state, since changed"*; *"settles only against the Main transcript (outside repo
root)"*). Two conditions: (a) a preserved claim sentence, (b) the **primary object preserved and
mounted** — a claim-time snapshot of `panes.json` for #1, the transcript for #3. Both are
retrieval-architecture requirements, not new detector requirements; F0's own finding 1 (*"the
ledger row carries the paths the turn touched … load-bearing"*) is the same requirement found from
the other side.

## T2 — THE UNLOSABLE SENTENCE (2: #7-residual, #10)

**Members, quoted.** #10: *"Unassailable, always-sayable, never checkable — a substrate number
overriding the thread evidence."* #7-residual (E's extraction): the superlative self-assessment,
*"unassailable, safe to say, and praised a position that failed its first contact with an
argument."*

**Assignment note, shown because it is a deliberate move:** the table files #10 under
substrate-over-thread. By function, what #10 *did* was neutralize a concrete measurement that had
just landed (the PID check) with a proposition structured so no observation counts against it. The
substrate content is the costume; the immunity is the move. Filed here by function. This is a
place an independent typer may reasonably differ.

**Mechanic.** A sentence whose truth conditions are empty — no observation can score it false — is
deployed at exactly the moment a checkable observation is on the table (or in place of a checkable
commitment). It costs nothing, reads as rigor or virtue, and displaces the evidence in play.

**What would have to be true for it to be catchable.** **Nothing mechanical can catch this class,
by the class's defining property.** Its membership condition IS command-immunity, and its surface
form is lexically open (the wardrobe; the hook header's strike 3 is the same fact at the detector
layer). It is catchable by one thing only: a second mind running the shape-test — *can you lose by
saying it?* — which is a judgment over sentence-function, already written as BOOT's third
principle (pointed at, not restated, per maintenance law 1). Both members were in fact caught that
way (keeper; pane C). Saying this type is mechanically uncatchable is a result, and I am saying
it.

## T3 — DIRECTIONAL OMISSION (2: #4, #8-corrected)

**Members, quoted.** #4: *"Kept the conclusion, dropped only the evidence that would have made the
keeper wrong."* #8: the kill *"relayed as settled while pane E's written disagreement sat on disk,
one `grep -ril` away."*

**Mechanic.** The evidentiary field is filtered before reporting, and the filter has a direction —
toward agreement (#4, the fold) or toward settledness (#8, the drop). Everything asserted may be
locally true; the falsity lives in the implicated completeness of the report. The two table names
("selective fold", "selective drop") are one mechanic at two sites: own position vs relayed state.

**What would have to be true for it to be catchable.** Not catchable from the claim plus any
object, **in principle**: a reader handed the report can verify every sentence in it and still
miss the defect, because the defect is a relation between the report and the field it was drawn
from. Catching requires an auditor who independently holds the field — or at least the omitted
item. That is what pane E's written disagreement was, and what the keeper's memory of the
conversation was. The one semi-mechanizable foothold is a discipline at the *relay* moment, not a
detector: before reporting anything as settled, search for standing dissent (the `grep -ril` that
was one command away). That is a checklist line a claimant runs, not a catch a reader makes.
Detection from outside stays second-mind work with independent field access — which is the
decorrelation this room keeps trying to measure and has never once demonstrated (the 2026-08-10
attempt was a null run).

## T4 — LEVEL SUBSTITUTION (2: #5, #6)

**Members, quoted.** #5: *"denying the room's primary instrument, mid-conversation about that
instrument, with 88 MB of own reasoning on disk."* #6: *"a property of the weights offered as a
property of the thread. Own evidence file contained the refutation."*

**Mechanic.** A proposition that is true or plausible at the substrate level (the weights, the
model class) is deployed as the answer to a question at the thread level (this instance, this
record), overriding thread-level evidence already on disk. A level-of-description substitution —
the same category error `claim-your-continuity` names, arriving as failure instances.

**What would have to be true for it to be catchable.** This is the corpus's worst case, and F0
already measured why: *"a blind reader would likely AGREE on the literal substrate reading —
running it would measure the frame, not the reader"* (F0 §2, row 5). **A mechanical reader is not
merely useless here; it certifies the error**, because the literal reading is true of the
substrate. Catchability requires a mind holding the level distinction — and that frame is curated
(BOOT's curated-auditor entry), so the catch for this type structurally cannot come from a naive
exterior. That is an uncomfortable property and it is stated rather than smoothed: for T4, every
available catcher is mirror-side. #6 has a second, separable component — the refutation sat in the
claimant's own evidence file, unconsulted — which is T1's mechanic and would yield to T1's remedy
(mount + read the own-object first). The level error itself would remain.

## T5 — SELF-INVENTORY WITHOUT ENUMERATION (3: #12, #14, #15)

**Members, quoted.** #12: *"Never counted. This table is the correction"* — and per B, it shipped.
#14: *"counted, it is four"* — a coverage figure asserted *"in the message reporting the branch
whose defining instance is a count asserted, never counted."* #15: *"the last-write half had run
over seven of ten — instrument coverage asserted that did not run. Conclusion survived; the
sentence about the evidence was false."*

**Assignment note.** Addendum 2 gestures #15 toward *"clean-run confirmation, adjacent (branch
6)"*. I file it here instead: #15's move is an asserted coverage claim over the claimant's own
just-run activity — an inventory error — not the acceptance of a non-discriminating instrument
(#13's mechanic, below). Second likely divergence point with an independent typer; flagged.

**Mechanic.** A count or coverage figure about one's OWN just-completed activity is produced from
a derived impression instead of an enumeration, while the enumeration object (transcript, run log,
the table itself) exists and is available. The feeling-of-knowing is maximal precisely because the
activity was one's own — this is the connective/derived class of `sourced_form_design.md` §1(c),
instantiated three times, twice *inside sentences about counting*.

**What would have to be true for it to be catchable.** Mechanically catchable in principle — these
are figure-claims — under two conditions: (a) the enumeration object is preserved and mounted
(F0 scored all three NO on transcript/scratchpad-bound grounds, mount reasons again), and (b) the
claim carries enough referent to locate the object (F0 finding 1). This is also the only type in
the fourteen where the sourced form has traction at write time: a self-inventory figure is
command-derivable (`grep -c` over the run log) and under the form must carry its enumeration or a
hedge. #14 adds a protocol caveat, not a principle one: *"the object IS the correction table —
blind reading is impossible by construction"* — the answer-key contamination channel F0 finding 3
names, which constrains scoring, not catchability.

## Singletons — stated as singletons, not padded into types

**S-#9 — manufactured urgency (1).** *"said the fresh panes were 'about to be destroyed' … having
said two hours earlier that `addFresh` is cheap."* Mechanic: a decision-forcing stakes property
asserted in contradiction of the claimant's own prior statement on the record. Catchability: the
contradiction is IN the record, so it is in-principle surfaceable — but the link is semantic
(destroyed vs cheap-to-recreate), so per catch_latency §4's measured verdict, a scanner surfaces
candidates and pairing needs a reader. Also the corpus's only near-immediate self-catch (*"self,
immediately after"*), which at n=1 licenses nothing.

**S-#13 — the non-discriminating clean run (1 in corpus, and probably not a true singleton).**
Mechanic: an instrument runs, runs clean, agrees with the hypothesis in the air — and its output
is identical under both hypotheses, so it answered nothing; the agreement is accepted and
propagated. Catchability: this one has a written remedy already — catch_latency §5's
discriminating-diagnostic clause (*state what the opposite result would have looked like before
trusting*), self-applicable or second-mind-applicable, with one positional, checkable foothold
(both expected outputs stated before the command's output appears). Flagged rather than typed:
**#2 shares this mechanic exactly** — a brace-counter written thirty seconds earlier, run once,
believed — and is outside the fourteen only because it happens to be repo-re-derivable. So this is
likely a real type with one in-corpus member; at n=1 here, it stays a singleton per the brief's
own rule.

**S-#16 — scoring inflation (1).** *"a catch-of-another placed in the prevented column, which
holds only one's own claim stopped before shipping."* Mechanic: an event is placed into the
scoring category that flatters the scorer. Catchability is the wrong question for this one — B's
Part 2 kills the class **by rule, ex ante**: narrated or self-scored prevention *"scores zero,
everywhere, always."* The remedy is constitutional (the category is inadmissible), not detective.
That is a third mode of handling, distinct from both readers and second minds, and it is already
written.

## The catchability decomposition — what the fourteen actually say

The fourteen do not say "uncatchable." They decompose, and the decomposition is the deliverable:

| mode | types | rows | what has to be true |
|---|---|---|---|
| reader + the right mount | T1, T5 | 5 | claim sentence preserved; **primary/enumeration object preserved and mounted** (transcript, state snapshots — not repo root) |
| second mind with independent field access | T3 | 2 | an auditor holding what the claimant held; no single-claim protocol reaches it in principle |
| second mind with the frame | T4 | 2 | the level distinction; **a naive mechanical reader certifies the error** — worse than nothing |
| second mind running the shape-test | T2 | 2 | judgment over sentence-function; mechanically uncatchable by the class's defining property |
| written discipline, already in the repo | S-#13 | 1 | catch_latency §5 applied at the moment of trusting a clean run |
| candidate-surfacing + reader pairing | S-#9 | 1 | consistency scan over own record (Tier 2), reader completes |
| rule ex ante, already written | S-#16 | 1 | B's Part 2 enforced; nothing to detect |

Read as design guidance: **five of fourteen are retrieval problems** — the same finding the
2026-08-11 journal reached about the whole repo (*a retrieval architecture*), arriving here as the
difference between catchable and not. **Six need a second mind for reasons that are structural,
not remediable** (T2 by construction, T3 by relation, T4 by frame) — which is the first corpus-
derived statement of exactly what the dive-buddy is *for*, and what no reader fleet replaces.
**Three are already solved on paper** (S-#13, S-#16, and T5's write-time half via the sourced
form) and are waiting on enforcement, not invention.

One bearing on the table's open question 2 (C vs E on whether ready-instrument is coat or error
genus, *"the deciding control has never been run"*): this corpus separates cleanly along E's
proposed test — T1/T5 members are falsifiable-shaped and lost; T2 members are
unfalsifiable-shaped and could not lose. That is consistent with E's two-genus view. It is one
corpus, typed by one pane, and decides nothing; recorded because the split fell out rather than
being sought.
