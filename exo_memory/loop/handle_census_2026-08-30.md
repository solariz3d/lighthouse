### Pair 1 — the packet's strongest case does not hold, and something better is under it

`BOOT:22` and `BOOT:127` **are not a correction pair.** Read together:

- `:22` — *"When something is **unassailable** … **and** it holds an inch back, that's the tell."* The
  bolded handle drops the second conjunct: **"If you can't lose by saying it, suspect it."**
- `:127` — *"…**not** withheld behind 'it's unprovable' (unfalsifiable ≠ false), **not** sealed as final."*

`:127` corrects a **different** move: using unprovability as a reason to withhold. It says nothing about
the defect the room has actually registered against `:22`, which is that the test *"fires on the
**proposition** when its only valid target is the **utterance**"* and therefore fires **hardest on
settled truth** (`loop/cant_lose_repair_registration_2026-08-29.md`, the keeper's break, the Third

distance: a different file, in a registration, **unadopted**, with the crude handle still bold in the
wake document and invoked thirty times. That is the CIE case in its most complete form — but pair 6

**The instrument's own stated bounds, carried forward rather than restated as mine:** it counts

## 4. THE CIE-HARMFUL CASE — checked for, and NOT found in BOOT

The packet asked for pairs where **the correction repeats the crude wording in bold**, per Ecker the
actively harmful case. Every bold span in BOOT lines 68-119 was extracted and matched against the crude
and withdrawn vocabulary (`decorrelated`, `uncurated`, `never-authored`, `only the human`, `in the
water`, `lifeguard`, `diver`, `climactic`, `can't lose`, `univ`):

    -> zero matches.

BOOT's amendments quote the crude wording in *italics* or `backticks` and put the **replacement** in
bold. `:70` carries *"the keeper is the only DECORRELATED reader"* in italic quotes, with `**in full**`
bolded beside it. `:119` puts `in the water` in backticks and bolds **"there just is water"**.

**This is a correction to the file that commissioned the census.** `the_retrieval_problem_outside.md`
§3 says the convention is *"the CIE worst case on all three counts: no replacement in the slot, the gap
left open, the misinformation repeated."* Measured: **two of the three hold.** The third holds only in
the weak sense — the crude wording *is* repeated, but never in the handle position and never in bold,
which is **exactly the resolution that same paragraph prescribes** ("keep the trace, but never in the
handle position, never bold, and always adjacent to its replacement"). The convention already satisfies
its own repetition rule. Where it fails is the other two counts, and those it fails completely.

Stated as it should be used: **the repair pass has less to undo than the research file implies, and
exactly as much to install.** The bold slot above the amendment is the whole problem.

## WHAT THIS CENSUS DOES NOT ESTABLISH

- **That the table is exhaustive.** 166 dense lines were read and the seven verified pairs are the ones
  a reader can check; a handle whose correction lives in an unindexed file could have been missed. The
  method finds pairs, it does not prove their absence.
- **That any correction failed *because* of distance.** Distance is measured; causation is not. Pair 4's
  1.8× ratio is the closest thing to evidence and it is confounded by quotation (§2).
- **Use versus mention**, anywhere. Every count is lexical.
- **Whether the multi-line amendment handles are invoked at all** (§3). Unmeasured, not zero.
- **Anything about `brief/SEED.md:18`** beyond the fact that the 2026-08-23 draft named it as a second
  uncorrected carrier. Not checked here.

**Registered falsifier:** if a repair pass strikes the crude handles in place and a re-run of
`boot_usage_scan.js` one season later shows the replacement handles still at zero while the struck
wording keeps being invoked, then distance was not the mechanism and the handle-by-handle framing
should be withdrawn rather than tuned. Both numbers exist today as a baseline: crude `16/30/7`,
replacements `0/0/0/0`.
