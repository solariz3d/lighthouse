## ① IS THE DISCRIMINATOR SOUND ON ITS OWN TERMS?

Yes, and I want that said without hedging before the rest lands on it.

The cut is anchored to the chain's one load-bearing reason — **a dispatch is un-revisable** — and that
reason is not asserted, it is derived from a measured failure (2026-08-24: an unverified claim
dispatched, a second seat ruled from it, the ruling wrong *because the brief was wrong*). Every hop of
the chain is then justified or discarded by whether it protects that moment. That is the correct way to
derive a rule, and it is the opposite of the mood the keeper's wording would otherwise have licensed.
A rule that says *"sometimes freestyle"* and stops is a repeal. This is not that.

**Three attacks I ran on it. Two land small; one lands and is owed a repair.**

**A-1 — the boundary is drawn at a time when the answer is not yet known. LANDS. This is the repair.**
Whether work "leaves the room" is usually discovered *later*, when someone decides to dispatch it. The
section sees this and names it — guard 2, *"the transition is the dangerous seam, not either mode"* —
and then prescribes nothing enforceable: *"the chain applies from that point."* It cannot. The chain's
first step is **seal the guess BEFORE the map**, and a prior cannot be sealed retroactively; that is the
entire reason `lap-row.js --open` accepts no id. So work that begins freestyle and is later dispatched
can never be chained — only its late hops survive — and **that is exactly the shape 08-24 was.** The
clause therefore creates a class of work for which the guard it points at is structurally unavailable,
and names the seam without closing it.
*Owed:* guard 2 must say what is actually recoverable at the seam (verbatim forwarding, citation,
collation) and state plainly that **the sealed prior is not**, so nobody reads "the chain applies from
that point" as though the measurement survived. One paragraph. It is a correction, not a rewrite.

**A-2 — the classifier is self-applied by the party who pays for one answer. LANDS SMALL.** *"Is
anyone going to act on this without being able to ask me?"* is answered by the seat that otherwise
pays five hops, under load — which the section itself identifies as the condition where weakened rules
fail. It converted an unfalsifiable mood into a **self-assessed** condition. That is a real improvement
and it is not the same as a cut. Mitigated, not fixed, by the fact that the question is concrete and
answerable in one word; I do not think a better self-applied form exists, which is why this lands
small and does not block.

**A-3 — the evidence offered for freestyle is one favourably-read case. LANDS SMALL.** The 08-25 night
is cited as the mode working, by the seat arguing for the clause, scored afterwards. Guard 1 notes the
same night produced **five WRONG entries** and reads that as the mode succeeding. It may be. Nobody
computed the comparison, and no comparison is available. Not disqualifying; worth not forgetting when
the clause is next cited as proven.

**None of these is the reason the falsifier comes out.** The cut survives them. What follows is

## ④ WHAT WOULD MAKE IT CHECKABLE — and it is NOT more laps

**More laps do nothing here.** They would fill Falsifier 1's `RATE_FLOOR` of 5 and move this falsifier
not at all, because this one fires on *absence*, and absence accumulates only when the ledger is unused
— which is indistinguishable from the room being fine. Adding laps cannot repair a check whose signal
is that laps are missing.

**The unit must be an event the guarded behaviour cannot suppress.** Lap rows are suppressible by
definition. **Dispatches are not** — a dispatch either rendered in another seat's pane or it did not,
and that is a fact about the world rather than about anyone's bookkeeping.

**So: reconcile, do not count.**

> Of the last N dispatches observed by a mechanical writer, how many carry either a lap row or an
> explicit freestyle declaration naming why the answer stayed in the room? **Denominator from outside
> the ledger, numerator from inside it. The discrepancy is the misclassification rate** — which is the
> quantity the section actually risks.

This is **E's own fix shape, transposed**. E's repair for MACHINE-BOUND was: declare `home=` from a
token outside the corpus, and where the tag matches home, **NOT-RUN becomes a CLASS ERROR rather than an
accepted decline** (`loop/machine_bound_class_attack_2026-08-25.md:123-150`). The transposition: declare
from outside the ledger the occasions on which a lap row was **required**; on those occasions, absence
is a class error, not a pass. Today every absence is accepted, which is precisely why the check cannot
fail.

**And the room already wrote the outside writer.** `consonance/hooks/dispatch-gate.js` is a `PreToolUse`
hook that appends one row per dispatch attempt to `data/dispatch-gate.jsonl`, written by machine, not by
a participant. That is the authority the falsifier needs.
