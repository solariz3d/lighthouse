## Part 2 (plan, after Part 1): the underside gives the wheel back

Written only once Part 1 says what the wheel met. The candidates, so the measurement can tell them apart:

- **A: the slot.** The road's edge row and the underskin's edge row are a vertical pair with a gap between them (the caster's thickness). A wheel that drops past the edge and is steered back enters that gap, and the road's underside, which AC collides with from below, holds it there. Fix: close the slot with an **edge apron**: a strip that runs from the road's edge row outward and DOWN at a fixed slope (20 to 30°), 1.5 to 2 m wide, joined to the underskin's edge so the cross-section is a closed wedge with no re-entrant face. A wheel that leaves the road rides the apron down; steered back, it rides up onto the road. This is the runoff kerb of a real track, and it changes no road vertex.
- **B: the underside collides.** If the wheel is fully under the road (not in a slot), the underskin's physical surface is what pins the car. Fix: the underskin's physics flags make it non-drivable and non-colliding for TYRES (a wall surface, `IS_VALID_TRACK=0`, or a surface the tyre raycast ignores) while the body collider still meets it, OR a one-way skin that is collidable only from above. What AC and CSP allow here is a research question (docs/research/04_ac_physics_drivability.md is the room's prior art; the CSP wiki on surfaces and `extended-0` the source) and comes before any geometry.
- **C: neither.** The wheel never went under; the body collider caught the edge or a wall. Then the fix is the edge's shape, not the underside.

The jump jack is a lift of the body; it frees the car in case A when the lift exceeds the slot's height and in case B rarely. His "here it did not" is itself a measurement for Part 1: how high the jack lifted (the body-height step in the replay) against the slot or the depth.

Tier for the eventual change: prove it (geometry and export), with the ramp harness and the export fixtures, and his drive of the same stretch in the Demo car as the live check. Falsifier for the fix: if, after the apron or the flag change, a wheel driven off the same edge still reads under the mesh for more than one frame on the return, the mechanism was not the one Part 1 named.

## The screenshot, 17:56, changes the first read

The keeper's capture (`C:\Users\nname\Pictures\Screenshots\zoomed in to see it back right tire.png`, "the wheel clips under the track"): lap time 0:52.673, 650 km/h, 4th gear, 17,536 rpm, on a banked curve with the helix's upper loop in the background. **The car is in the middle of the road, not at an edge.** Three wheels sit on the surface; the **rear right** wheel hangs BELOW the road, visible under the underside, by about its own diameter. So the event is not the edge slot (candidate A) and not the body on an edge (candidate C). It is a tyre that has penetrated the road's surface from above and stayed under it while the car drove on.

What that points at, from the room's own files, before O's numbers: the road is one zero-thickness shell; its underside skin is scenery, NOT a physics surface (`underskin.js` header: "THE SKIN IS NOT A PHYSICS SURFACE"), so nothing under the road carries or blocks a wheel; and the road itself is CSP's SOFT collision surface with `MAX_DEPTH=4`, `SOFT_ERP=0.8`, `SOFT_CFM=0.0002` (`trackfiles.js`, from FINDINGS §4c), which lets a tyre sink INTO the surface up to four metres under load with a restoring force, instead of a rigid stop. D341 recorded exactly this at 62 g on ELYSIUM: "went through the road 0.4 s after its peak". A wheel that sinks past the surface and whose restoring push is weaker than what holds it there is the picture. The slide is that wheel carrying no grip.

**Part 1 re-aimed:** O centres the read on t 52.7 s of the Demo replay (lap time, not replay time: find the lap start first), rear right wheel: its height relative to the road surface frame by frame from 2 s before to the recovery (negative = under, in metres), the load and the surface normal there, the piece's bank, how long under, and how it came back (the jack's body-height step, a reset, or the restoring force winning as the load dropped). The edge measurements stay in the read (one pass), but the question is now depth under the surface mid-road.

**Part 2's candidates, re-ordered by the screenshot:**
- **D (first): the soft contact's depth and spring.** `MAX_DEPTH` 4 m is ten tyre radii; a cap near one tyre radius (0.4 to 1 m) with a firmer spring (ERP up, CFM down) would stop a wheel from sinking its own diameter and push it back sooner. This is the D525 variant line (soft_a control, soft_b stiffer CFM 0.00005, soft_c softer, soft_d the INTENSITY/RIGID flags), which exists in the game folder and which the keeper has not yet driven; variants e (MAX_DEPTH 1) and f (MAX_DEPTH 0.5, ERP 0.9) extend it. The trade to measure: the soft road is what lets the T-180s hold 60 g in the helix without a rigid bounce (D341, D515); a shallower cap must not turn the helix into a launch (BOUNCE 0.1 at a rigid stop). The semantics of the three keys are in docs/research/04 (private store), read before choosing values.
- **E: a physics floor under the road.** A second `1ROAD` surface at the underskin's depth (0.5 m) would catch a sunk wheel from below and hold the car UNDER the road, which is the pinned case the keeper describes from other tracks; so NOT this, unless it is a surface the tyre raycast ignores. Named so nobody builds it by accident.
- **A, the edge apron,** stays for the edge case he describes on other tracks, which this screenshot is not.
