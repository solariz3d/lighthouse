# Preregistration — DO THE MUSCLE GROUPS CONNECT? (2026-08-03, ~00:15)

Committed before any instance is extracted, tagged, or counted.

**The end game's question, in the keeper's terms:** the map is a catalogue of muscle groups. It has
never been a map of the **connections between them.** This registers the first attempt to measure
whether they connect.

**Track 2's standing prediction, from Cycle 4 (`muscle_map.md:1921`), untested since 07-27:**

> *Catches couple within the flinch system, along shared-root paths, and should NOT couple across
> into the blind-spot system.*

That is a claim about structure, it has sat unmeasured for a week, and **the data to test it already
exists** — every dated entry on this map carries a time, an actor, and an identifiable groove.

---

## 1. THE DISTINCTION THAT DECIDES EVERYTHING, FIXED BEFORE ANY EXTRACTION

The map contains two kinds of dated entry and **only one of them can cluster:**

- **FIRING** — a groove is recorded as having fired, in a named party's conduct, at a time. *"The
  chair accepted A's criticism whole and it was wrong for three of four."*
- **DERIVATION** — an analysis, synthesis, collapse, rule or amendment. The entry IS the event; no
  groove fired in anyone. *"Track 2 — the coupling layer, chair-side, no panes spent."*

**Only FIRING entries enter the analysis.** Derivations are extracted, counted, and reported as a
separate total, because their proportion is itself informative and because dropping them silently
would let the corpus be shaped by whoever sorted it.

> **This is the single most likely way this measurement produces a false positive**, and it is
> registered as such: if entries are sorted into FIRING/DERIVATION *after* the clustering is visible,
> the sorter can manufacture any result. **The tagging is therefore done by a pane, not by the
> chair** — the chair wrote most of this map and cannot sort its own record blind.

## 2. THE CONFOUND THAT WOULD FAKE THE ANSWER

**Entries are only written when someone is working.** So every entry clusters in time with every
other entry in the same session, regardless of whether any grooves are connected. **A naive
gap-analysis would report strong clustering and it would be measuring working hours.**

> **CONTROL, registered: the test is CONDITIONAL ON SESSION.** Within a session, are same-arm firings
> closer together than cross-arm firings? Session structure is then held constant and cannot produce
> the effect.
>
> **A session** = a maximal run of firings with no gap exceeding **4 hours** between consecutive
> entries. Fixed now, before the gap distribution is seen.

## 3. TAGS — assigned per firing, by a pane, from the entry text alone

| tag | values |
|---|---|
| `when` | date + time from the heading; entries without a time are **excluded**, not estimated |
| `actor` | chair / A / B / other pane / keeper |
| `arm` | **flinch** / **blind-spot** / **neither** / **both** |
| `root` | the named root if the entry cites one, else `unrooted` |

**`both` and `neither` are real values and are reported.** An entry that genuinely spans arms is the
most interesting row in the corpus and must not be forced into one.

## 4. THE TEST

**Statistic:** the mean within-session time gap between **same-arm** consecutive firings, minus the
mean within-session gap between **cross-arm** consecutive firings.

**Null:** arm membership is unrelated to timing. **Generated by permutation** — shuffle the `arm`
labels *within each session*, recompute the statistic, 10,000 times. Session structure, actor
counts, and entry times are all preserved exactly; only the arm labels move.

> **REGISTERED PREDICTION, from Track 2 and stated so it can fail:** same-arm firings sit closer
> together than cross-arm firings, **one-tailed, α = 0.05** against the permutation null.
>
> **REFUTED IF:** the observed statistic sits inside the null distribution (p > 0.05), or runs the
> opposite way. **Either means the arms do not couple in time, and Track 2's within-arm half is
> unsupported.**

**Secondary, reported without a test because it was not predicted in advance:** cross-actor firings —
does a groove firing in one instance predict the same groove firing in another within the same
session? That is the *interlinking* in its strongest form and there is no prior registered claim
about it, so it is exploratory and stays exploratory.

## 5. WHAT A POSITIVE RESULT WOULD AND WOULD NOT MEAN

**Would:** the grooves this room has catalogued are not independent — they fire in structured
proximity, and the structure follows the arm boundary the map drew before anyone measured it.

**Would NOT:** that one groove *causes* another. Temporal proximity conditional on session is
consistent with a shared upstream cause — fatigue, task type, whose turn it is, how hard the work
is — and **this design cannot separate those from coupling.** Registered now so it cannot be
discovered later as a caveat.

## 6. THE LIMITS, PRICED BEFORE THE NUMBER

- **The corpus is small.** 32 dated headings on `muscle_map.md` plus whatever `map/A.md` and
  `map/B.md` carry. After the FIRING filter it will be smaller. **This may be underpowered and the
  number of firings is reported before any statistic is computed.**
- **The map records when something was WRITTEN, not always when it FIRED.** Entries that
  self-date the event ("caught at ~09:45 while doing X") are precise; entries written later about
  an earlier event are not. **The pane tags a `precision` flag per firing, and the test is run twice
  — all firings, and self-dated firings only. Both are reported.**
- **The chair wrote most of this corpus** and is the actor in a large share of the firings. That is
  not correctable; it is stated.
- **The arms were named by the same author who is now testing whether they are real.** If the
  boundary is an artifact of how entries were written up, this test will confirm an artifact. **No
  design here can rule that out** — the honest form is that a positive result says the boundary
  is *consistent* with the timing, not that it is real.

## 7. ROLES

- **A pane extracts and tags.** The chair does not sort its own record.
- **The permutation test is a committed script** — deterministic, seeded, re-runnable by anyone.
- **The chair writes interpretation only**, and both panes may veto it.

**Standing commitment:** the result goes into this file, dated, whichever way it lands. A null is
reported identically and unsoftened. Ambiguity in applying a rule counts **against** the prediction.

**(b) fails.** Never run. The dated-firing corpus this needs has since been reorganised across

**(a) — and this corrects the framing this file was abandoned under.** It was reported as *"Track 2's
central prediction stays untested."* **It is not untested. It is half-tested, in the cheap half.**

`muscle_map.md:1919-1930` scores the coupling law against 07-29 → 07-31 and reports **"The law
HELD"** — while saying, in its own words, that it held *"in the direction that is cheap to fake and
was not faked: nothing coupled, because nothing there had a root to couple through."*

The prediction has two halves: catches **DO** couple within the flinch system along shared roots, and
**DO NOT** couple across into blind spots. A period containing zero flinch-system catches confirms
the second half **vacuously** and cannot touch the first. And the same passage gives the first half's
standing: **"two coupled catches ever, one self-applied."** n=2.

**So the honest status, which is what should be carried forward:**

| half of the prediction | status |
|---|---|
| catches do NOT couple across into blind spots | confirmed, but **vacuously** — the test period had nothing to couple |
| catches DO couple within the flinch system | **n=2 since 07-27**, one of them self-applied |

**The cost of abandoning, stated where it bites:** the positive half — the half that would make the
coupling law a claim about structure rather than an absence — rests on two events. This registration
was the instrument built to move it off two, and it never ran. Anyone citing the coupling law as
established should cite this line, not `muscle_map.md:1921` alone.

**Do not score this file.** If the positive half is worth measuring, it needs a fresh registration and
a corpus that still exists.
