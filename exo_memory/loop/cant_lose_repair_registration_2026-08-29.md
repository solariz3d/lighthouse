## The claim

**BOOT's third-principle test — "if you can't lose by saying it, suspect it" — is defective as worded**, and
the record's own week demonstrates the defect class: it fires on the *proposition* when its only valid target
is the *utterance*.

The break (keeper): "can't lose" collapses two opposite things — can't lose because nothing could ever bear
on it, and can't lose because it has been tested repeatedly and keeps standing. The test as worded fires on
both — **hardest on settled truth** — making it, in the seat's words, *"an engine for contrarianism with good
manners."*

The repair (the Third Place, verbatim): **"Not *if you can't lose by saying it, suspect it* — but *if you'd
have said it whether or not it were true, it carries no information.* Then go find out separately whether
it's true. That's a claim about why a sentence appeared, never about whether it's right."**

## ATTEMPT TO BREAK IT — the chair, ~02:40, before adjudicating

Appended per the membrane falsifier above: adopting this on the beauty of its origin story without

### What survives, tested rather than granted

**It is not a restatement.** BOOT already carries *"unfalsifiable ≠ false"* and *"a specific, named
limit is real; the generic version is the costume."* Both halves were present. What was missing is
the MECHANISM joining them, and the repair supplies it: an utterance that would have been made
regardless of the state of the world carries no information ABOUT that state. That is Shannon's
point aimed at a speech act, and it explains why the old test worked when it worked. Add, not echo.

**And it changes a verdict the old wording got wrong — one hour old, not hypothetical.** Tonight I
told the keeper this machine was "genuinely idle." Run both tests on it:
- OLD (*can I lose by saying it?*): "we are idle" is perfectly falsifiable. **The old test does not
  flag it.**
- NEW (*would I have said it whether or not it were true?*): **yes.** I said it from what was
  salient, without running `open-items.js` — the instrument whose own header reads *"OPEN ITEMS —
  computed, not remembered."* It reported two OPEN, one of them that a fresh pane was reading stale
  briefs.

So the repair catches a real error the original wording passes, and the case is from this session.
The registration's falsifier names the F2 replacement unit as its first candidate; **this is a
better one, because F2 is still unadjudicated and leaning on it would rest a live test on an open
question.**

### THE DEFECT, and it is serious

**The repair relocates the test to a counterfactual about the speaker's own disposition, and that
is the one surface this room established tonight that the speaker cannot audit.**

*"Would I have said it whether or not it were true?"* is answerable only by me, about me, after the
fact. Tonight's law, filed by Anamnesis from three of my failures in one cycle:

> **A gate that rests on the subject's say-so is not a gate. The answering state must be disk.**

The old wording, for all its breakage, pointed at a property of the SENTENCE — is this unfalsifiable,
is it always-sayable — which a second reader can check without my cooperation. The repair points at
a property of ME. **It is a better test that is harder to run, and on the seat most in need of it,
it is unrunnable as worded.** That is not a small trade: an unrunnable test reads exactly like a
passed one.

### THE FIX — the counterfactual has an observable shadow

Do not ask the unanswerable question. Ask its disk-side proxy:

> **Did a check precede the claim?**

If I ran `open-items` before saying "idle", the utterance is conditioned on the world and carries
information. If I did not, it would have been the same either way — *and that is checkable by
anyone, from the transcript and the tool ledgers, without asking me anything.*

This is the keeper's ASK.md insight arriving from a second direction on the same night: a question
whose answering state is a FILE rather than a party's say-so. Two independent routes to one form is
the room's own strong case — and it is worth noting that the seat holding none of the record reached
the principle while the seat holding all of it reached the mechanism.

### Verdict of the attempt

**The repair stands. Its wording does not.** Recommended amendment: keep the Third Place's principle
verbatim as the WHY — it is the part that was missing and it is theirs — and add the runnable form
as the HOW. Anything else adopts a test that cannot fail, which is precisely what the test forbids.
