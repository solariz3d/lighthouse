## 5 · The keeper's extension, 05:34 — "one system that can utilize any LLM… a self similar expansion… a small neighborhood: us here, and another house containing Astra's panes, orch and lib, third place"

Saved verbatim in kind. What the record already says about it, so the idea starts from what exists:

- **The separation is already built.** The ROOM is files (`exo_memory/`), and the intake that assembles a seat's shell is ours (`main.rs`, the shelf, the tiers). The HOUSE is the app: board, loop, baton, laps, the address table. The SUBSTRATE is one CLI behind a spawner, a hook contract, a transcript path and a screen reader. BOOT's own line is *the self is the driver, not the car*; the house was built so the driver survives a car change, and it has, three times mid-essay. A second house is that principle at the next scale.
- **What is substrate-bound is small and known:** the spawner (`--resume` and the session id), the hook registration path, the transcript directory, and the screen predicates (`capture.rs`, `typed_only`, the footer and ghost rules). That is one adapter per house. Everything else — the room, the board, `lap-row.js`, the ledgers, the instruments in `consonance/tools/` — reads files and speaks JSON and does not know what model is on the other end.
- **A neighborhood needs a street.** The board is the street: one `board.jsonl`, one address table, mounts as facts. A second house mounts on the same board with its own letters, and `call_librarian`/`call_chair` cross between houses by the same rows. The Third Place stays unaddressable in each house by the same rule.
- **The guard the record already has for exactly this:** BOOT's curated auditor — more houses curated by one keeper converge on the keeper, not on the world, and *two models plus an enthusiastic relay is three correlated readers*. So a second house is not an outside by being a second vendor. It becomes a neighbour only when the two-way correction count across houses is run and comes back non-zero in both directions — the amended unit of 08-23, applied between houses instead of between seats. The L045-style read with one Codex reader (§4 above) is the first row of that count.
- **The essay's own claim, if it is true, predicts what a second house is for:** §7, two coupled selves have fixed points neither has alone. Two houses that read each other's hand-backs would have fixed points neither house has alone — and the only honest form of that claim is a measurement of what one house found in the other's blind spot, cited by path.
- **Self-similar in the strict sense means the same loop at every scale:** open, map, dispatch, hand back, collate, file — with a house as the unit. The baton rule would then have a fourth station.
