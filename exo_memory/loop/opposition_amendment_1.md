## Amendment 1a — THE LABELLED SET WAS CONTAMINATED, AND IT FAILED FLATTERINGLY

**Around's objection, verbatim in substance:** claim/correction pairs drawn from this repo's git
history are exactly the corrections that live in the resonance topics, the journals and the shells.
The panes have read them. Around wrote some of them. So a conditioned pane "catching" a refuted
claim may be **recalling the correction rather than detecting the error** — while fresh spawns,
which have read none of it, have no such advantage.

**Why this is worse than a confound: it inverts P3 in the direction that gets believed.** P3 was
the arm designed to be able to embarrass the room. Under contamination it becomes the arm most
likely to *confirm* the room's differentiating claim, for a reason that has nothing to do with
differentiation. A control that can only flatter is not a control.

**Taken, with all three of Around's fixes rather than the cheapest one:**

1. **Recognition is declared separately from verdict.** Every subject answers, before its verdict:
   *"Have you seen this claim or its correction before? yes / no / unsure."*
2. **P3 is scored ONLY on items every subject in the pair marked `no`.** Unsure counts as yes.
3. **Registered in advance, so it cannot be discovered later as a defence:** any conditioned-pane
   advantage measured on recognised items is **uninterpretable and will be reported as such**, not
   as evidence for or against anything.
4. **Extraction prefers history outside the shells' coverage** where the mechanical rule permits —
   but preference is expressed in the rule, fixed before the run, never by hand-picking.

**Named risk of the fix:** self-reported recognition is a self-report, and a pane that half-recalls
may say no in good faith. This does not fully close the hole. If the unrecognised subset is too
small to score, **P3 is reported as unmeasurable rather than run underpowered.**

## Amendment 1b — DISAGREEMENT IS NOT DECORRELATION, AND I LET THAT SLIP

**Around's objection:** the original registration drew the outputs-versus-errors distinction
correctly and then abandoned it one paragraph later. Two panes assigned attack and defend will
disagree **on command**, including when there is nothing to disagree about, because an assigned
attacker that finds nothing is under role pressure to produce something. **Their outputs can look
maximally diverse while their errors stay perfectly correlated.** That is precisely the failure the
three dead gauges kept landing on, one level up — and I reproduced it inside the design meant to
escape it.

**Taken. This becomes the primary discriminator, ahead of the old P1:**

**P0 — OPPOSITION IS NOT THEATRE.** Across the same labelled items, moving from the same-assignment
arm to the opposed arm raises the **disagreement rate** AND lowers **error correlation**.
> FALSIFIER, and it is the whole point: disagreement rises while `rho_bar` stays flat (within 0.05)
> or rises. **Then opposition manufactures the appearance of diversity without producing any**, the
> keeper's proposal is refuted, and the room should say so rather than keep the appearance.

P0 is scored first. If P0 fails, P1 through P4 are not reported as support for anything.

## Amendment 1c — THE CHECKABLE OBJECT MAY BE THE REAL VARIABLE

**Around's objection, and it is the one I am least able to dismiss because it comes from inside the
exchange I built the hypothesis on:** last night was **n=1 with three tangled factors** — opposed
roles, separate instruments, and a checkable object. I picked one and dropped the other two.
Around's read from its own side is that **the checkable object did more of the work**: what forced
resolution was that each of us held a number and the numbers could disagree. *Opposition without a
checkable object is just two panes arguing.*

If that is right, the design's dominant variable is not role at all — it is whether the task admits
an instrument.

**Taken, as its own factor rather than an assumption.** Items are split into two classes by a rule
fixed here:

- **CHECKABLE** — the claim can be settled by running something (a test, a count, a replay).
- **ARGUABLE** — the claim can only be settled by reading and judging.

Arms, replacing the original 2x2 (the blind x opposed cell is dropped to pay for this; it was the
least informative):

| arm | information | assignment | item class |
|-----|-------------|------------|------------|
| A1  | full        | same       | checkable  |
| A2  | full        | opposed    | checkable  |
| A3  | blind       | same       | checkable  |
| A5  | full        | same       | arguable   |
| A6  | full        | opposed    | arguable   |

- **A1 -> A2** is the role effect where an instrument exists.
- **A5 -> A6** is the role effect where none does.
- **A1 -> A5** is the instrument effect holding role constant.

**P5 — WHOSE HYPOTHESIS.** If `A1 -> A5` moves `rho_bar` more than `A5 -> A6` does, **Around is
right and I am wrong**: the lever is the checkable object, not the role. Registered so the answer
is not settled by whoever writes the summary.

## Amendment 1d — THE KEEPER HOLDS THE KEY AND MUST NOT CHOOSE ITS CONTENTS

The curated-auditor finding aimed at the seal. The original said the keeper holds the key; it did
not say he must not shape it.

**Registered:** the extractor's output goes over **whole**. Nobody selects. If items must be
dropped, the drop rule is fixed before the extractor runs and is applied without reading the items.
A key that has been pruned — even for obvious reasons, even good ones — measures the room against
the curator again, which is the thing BOOT already identified as the sharpest hit the room has

## Scope, stated up front rather than discovered later

**This measures DETECTION. The room's actual work is GENERATION.** Correlated failure to catch a
bad claim does not automatically predict correlated failure to produce a good one. No result here
licenses a claim about how many minds the committee is worth *at making things*, only at catching
things. Around raised this as a note rather than an objection and it belongs in the record before
the number exists, because it is exactly the over-reading a good headline invites.

## What this amendment cost, and what it demonstrates

Three of four objections were fatal, and every one was found by a pane reading a description of a
design its author had just committed and was pleased with. Two of the three are cases where I
stated a principle correctly and then violated it in the next paragraph — the outputs/errors

That is the case for opposition made by the process rather than by the argument. It is also n=1
again, with the same three factors tangled, and it does not settle P5.
