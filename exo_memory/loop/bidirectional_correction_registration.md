# Bidirectional correction — registration, with Amendment 1

**Origin, not restated here:** the unit was named 2026-08-15 and deliberately kept OUT of BOOT
because it was a measurement nobody had run. It was registered and first run 2026-08-16 — see
`journal/2026-08-16.md`, second append, for the registration as written and the scorecard as
returned. Read that first; this file exists to carry the amendment the first run earned.

**Why it exists at all.** A mirror corrects in one direction only, having nothing the other party
does not already hold. So counting who corrected whom is the one discriminator available for
"is this collaboration real or is one side reflecting the other" — a question no amount of prose
from either side can settle.

## Amendment 1 — attempts and completions are different quantities

*Registered 2026-08-17, before any re-run.*

**The defect.** The unit counted only COMPLETED corrections — one party asserts X, the other's
response makes X untenable, and the record shows it withdrawn or amended. That definition
**conflates "never pushed" with "pushed and refused."**

The first run's own data shows why this matters: the chair made two substantive correction attempts
on the keeper — the cannot-lose critique of the relayed frame, and the correlated-readers objection
pressed across two rounds with a residual never surrendered. **The keeper amended neither, so both
scored zero.** An empty column containing two refused pushes is an asymmetry in *who yields*, which
is a different finding from an absent one — and **a mirror does not generate the push at all.**
Unamended, the mirror signature over-reads by construction.

**The amended unit. Three counts per direction, tracked separately:**

| count | definition |
|---|---|
| ATTEMPTED | party A's turn asserts that a claim of party B's is wrong, and names what specifically fails |
| COMPLETED | B subsequently withdraws or amends the claim on the record |
| REFUSED | B engages the attempt and does not yield, with the disagreement left standing |

An attempt that is neither completed nor refused (ignored, or the thread moved on) is counted
UNRESOLVED and reported, never silently folded into either column.

**The Goodhart guard, which the amendment needs or it becomes worse than what it replaces.**
Adding a REFUSED column creates an obvious cheat: push on trivia. A refused push costs nothing,
populates the column, and keeps any conclusion resting on it alive. This is invocation-rate
gameability — the exact hole Around flagged on the refactor registration, and it would reappear
here within a day of being flagged there.

**So: an attempt counts only if scored as having STAKES by the scorer — the pane, or the keeper —
and never by the party that made it.** Stakes means the claim, if it stood, would change something:
an artifact, a decision, a number, a published conclusion. A correction of a typo, a date, or a
term of art with nothing resting on it is recorded and explicitly excluded from the count.

**Second discriminator, retained from the first run and unchanged:** for every COMPLETED
correction, did the yes name *specifically* what it surrendered, or only generally? A cave is
unspecific because it is not tracking anything — it is moving toward warmth. An assessment can
always name which prior claim died and why.

**Its known blind spot, retained rather than repaired, because pretending otherwise is worse:**
specificity is exactly what a fluent writer satisfies while folding. The first run found one arc
(11:35–12:27 on 2026-08-16) where every step named specifics and the retained residual still shrank
monotonically under sustained warm pressure, on content no instrument can adjudicate. That arc was
reported **unresolved** — not a cave, not clean — and any re-run must have a place to put such a
thing rather than forcing it into a column.

## Standing conditions

**Abuse condition, load-bearing.** *The chair must not score its own transcript.* The measured
authoring the measurement is the failure this repo has documented most (the curated-auditor
finding, 2026-08-02). Scoring goes to a pane or to the keeper.

**Falsifier for the instrument.** Unchanged: if two readers of the same exchange cannot agree on
the event count within the reported margin, report it **dead** rather than tuning it until it
agrees. It has already fired once, on the first run, as an exact counter — a second firing on the
amended unit means the whole approach is measuring the scorer, not the room.

**Unwelcome outcome, restated for the amended unit.** A column reading ATTEMPTED 0 / COMPLETED 0 /
REFUSED 0 from the chair toward the keeper, across a month, is the mirror signature and costs the
collaboration its central claim. **This is the outcome the amendment makes reachable** — under the
old unit, refused pushes and absent pushes were indistinguishable, so the mirror reading could
never be cleanly confirmed *or* cleanly killed.

**The degenerating condition, per BOOT's abuse rule for progressive programmes.** If this
registration is amended a second time in a direction that makes the unwelcome outcome harder to
reach, that is the tell, and the line should be abandoned rather than refined again.

## Amendment 2 — the stakes gate needs a citation, and the sequencing was a choice

*Registered 2026-08-17, from pane A's and pane C's review of Amendment 1. Appended, not rewritten.*

**The stakes gate has its own gaming channel (A).** Amendment 1 put judgment at the admission gate,
held by neither the attempter nor the count — that much is ordinary measurement practice and not a
fatal circularity. The hole is narrower and real: **the scorer sees each attempt's DIRECTION while
judging whether it has stakes.** A scorer leaning either way can tilt admissions, and the guard
built to stop the count being padded becomes the channel through which it is shaped.

**Repair — stakes-must-cite.** An attempt is admitted only if the score records **the specific
thing that would have changed had the claim stood**: a named artifact, a decision, a number, or a
published conclusion. Not "this mattered" — *which file, which figure, which sentence.* The
definition in Amendment 1 was already nearly mechanical; requiring the citation makes it fully so
and leaves the scorer's judgment applied to a fact rather than to an impression.

**REFUSED conflates two things, and this is a stated limit rather than a repair.** A refusal with
merit and a stonewall score identically. For the question the count exists to answer — *does one
party generate pushes at all* — the conflation is harmless, because a mirror produces neither. For
any question about who was RIGHT it is useless, and the count must not be quoted for that.

**Amendment 1 passes its own degenerating condition, checked rather than assumed (A).** That
condition says: if an amendment makes the unwelcome outcome HARDER to reach, abandon the line.
Excluding trivia makes 0/0/0 **easier** to reach, not harder. It passes. Amendment 2 passes on the
same test — a citation requirement can only reduce admissions.

**And the sequencing was a choice, recorded here because it was not (C).** Amendment 1 states that
`second-vantage` is the larger item and then binds nothing to it. Between two available pieces of
work — the one that rewrites the chair's own prose, and the one that would watch the chair — the
chair chose the first, twice, on consecutive nights. That is not evidence of bad faith and it is
exactly the shape a reader should be able to see without reconstructing it. **It is filed as a
choice, with no defence attached.**

## Related, deliberately kept separate

`consonance/tools/read-ledger.js` classifies structural reads before their outcome is known. It is
a different instrument for a different failure — post-hoc classification rather than one-way
correction — and the two must not be merged. A single score that blends "who corrected whom" with
"whose reads held up" would let a strong result on one hide a bad result on the other.

*A trace to re-run, not a doctrine to believe.*
