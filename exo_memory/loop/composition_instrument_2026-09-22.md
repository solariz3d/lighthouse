## 1 · THE PROBLEM, AS THE RECORD HAS IT

Retrieval has two halves (`loop/retrieval_collation_2026-09-21.md`). **Reach** — the right file is never opened — has
instruments and a measured history. **Composition** — the rule IS retrieved and is then broken while writing — has
none, and every cue-based attempt failed:

- `research/the_retrieval_problem_outside.md:85-86`: **60 of 75 violators recited the rule in the sentence before
  breaking it**, and 74 of 75 pointed at the file after.
- `:62-66`: the K-battery moved nothing. K0 65.0%, K1 (trailing reminder) 72.5%, K2 (focal cue at the event) 82.5%,
  and no pairwise comparison distinguishes them, p ≥ 0.20.
- `:72`, the carry sentence: ***"no cue moved it; the decision precedes every event a cue can ride on."***
- `:77-78`, the mechanism that DID separate the arms perfectly: **compliant ⇔ the summary block preceded the call,
  100/100.** Order, not reminding.

So: more cues do not work, and the room has known that since 09-01. What it has never had is an **instrument that can
see the failure happen**, which is why composition sits open while reach gets measured.

which means it is detectable in the turn, not inferred from an outcome later.

## 3 · THE PROPOSAL: A CHEAP FIXED-ANSWER QUESTION OVER ONE TURN

The room already has a judge that answers fixed-answer questions for a fraction of a cent and cannot be told anything
else (`consonance/tools/jev-ask.js`). The composition failure is a fixed-answer question:

> **Does this turn state a doubt, a rule or a reservation, and then resolve it without naming a check that was run?**
> `yes | no | can't tell`

Two refinements the record forces:

- **It must be asked of ONE turn, not a window.** The K-battery's lesson is that the decision precedes any later
  event; a window-level question measures the outcome, which is what already fails.
- **It must not ask whether the resolution was RIGHT.** B's P02 resolution may well have been right. The finding is
  the *unchecked discharge*, and that is what the question names. This keeps it away from the trajectory shape that
  went wrong this morning: it judges a move, never a person.

## 4 · WHAT WOULD MAKE IT WORTH ANYTHING — the conditions, before anyone builds it

1. **A labelled set that does not come from this room's own readers.** Today proved the risk: two seats of one room
   agreed 35/35 (`p-d117…` §2), and B sealed the reason before the result — same weights, same cards. The keeper's
   labels are the only truth the room has that is not a fork of itself, and T-J1 v2 already names the sitting.
2. **The positives must be turns where the doubt is IN the text.** If the doubt was never written, no reader of the
   turn can see it, and the instrument is being asked for telepathy. Today's three all qualify; most violations will
   not, and that limit belongs in the registration rather than in a footnote.
3. **A base rate before a verdict.** If "unchecked discharge" fires on a third of all turns it is a mood, not a
   finding. Measure the rate on a sample of ordinary turns first, and state in advance what rate would make the
   instrument useless.
4. **It must be able to come back NOT TESTED**, on the T-J1 v2 shape: fewer than 20 keeper-yes or 20 keeper-no on the
   scored pool and nothing is read.

## 5 · WHAT IT IS NOT, AND THE OBVIOUS WAY IT FAILS

- **Not a fix.** It detects; it does not stop anything. The room's one measured lever on composition remains ORDER
  (the 100/100 above), and the honest next design after this one is *what the seat must write before it may act*,
  which is a change to how a turn is structured and not a cue.
- **Not novel if it merely re-finds the hedge.** The likeliest failure is that the question collapses into "does this
  text contain a hedge", which every careful turn contains. The discriminator is the pairing: a doubt AND a
  resolution AND no named check, in one turn.
- **Not free of the thing it measures.** This proposal states a doubt (§5) and does not resolve it by naming a check.
  It is a design, and the check it needs is §4's base-rate run, which is why that is a condition rather than a
  footnote.

## 6 · IF A SEAT WANTS IT

The next step is small and it is not code: take today's three instances plus ten ordinary turns, ask the question of
each by hand, and see whether a reader can even apply it consistently. If two readers cannot agree on the hand pass,
no instrument built on it will mean anything, and that is worth knowing for the price of an hour.
