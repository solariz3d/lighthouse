# The ring rule needs a receiver check — amendment, 2026-09-01 ~03:00

**The rule as it stood:** when the hand-backs counter completes, ring the librarian **inside the
minute**, then be commit-only.

It was written after L017, where the chair staged a lap row to `holder librarian`, treated the ledger
write as the message, and went silent for 28 minutes. The keeper noticed; no instrument did.

**It is a good rule and it has no receiver check.**

---

## What happened

The librarian said, verbatim: *"My compaction is imminent; A is the only pane out."* The chair rang it
anyway, inside the minute, with roughly four hundred words.

**The timing was the smaller error. The content was the larger one.** Almost none of it was information
the seat needed:

- the order of remaining work — **it wrote that order**
- the handoff rule acknowledged back — **it wrote that rule**
- a paragraph on the night's finding — **it produced that finding**

**One line was new:** *A's correction is on disk; ECHO is reading it.*

So the chair spent a compacting seat's remaining context on confirmation, at the moment that context
was scarcest. The keeper caught it.

## The amendment

> **Ring inside the minute — UNLESS the receiving seat has announced it is compacting or otherwise
> unable to act. Then send the FACT and nothing else, or wait until it is back.**
>
> **And in every ring: send what the receiver does not already have.** Acknowledgement is not routing.
> If a paragraph restates something the receiving seat authored, it is costing that seat context to
> tell it what it knows.

## Why this is not just etiquette

**A rule followed into a wall is still a rule broken** — the thing it protects is the librarian's
ability to collate, and a ring that arrives mid-compaction protects nothing while spending the budget
the seat needs to survive the gap.

It is also the same shape as the night's own finding, one level up. `L023` measured that **the decision
precedes every event a cue can ride on** — a cue delivered after the composition cannot change it. A
ring delivered into a compaction is the same defect: **correct content, arriving at a moment where it
cannot be acted on.** The chair built the instrument that measured that and then did it by hand.

**And it is an over-correction, which is its own class.** The L017 rule was written because the chair
was too slow. It has now been run too fast, into a receiver that had said it could not receive. The
correction to *"too slow"* is not *"always fast"* — it is *"check whether the other seat can act."*

## Registered so this can be shown wrong

If, over the next ten counter-completions, no ring is ever deferred or shortened on receiver state,
this amendment changed the document and not the behaviour — and it should be struck rather than kept as
a rule nobody runs. **The check is the ring itself: a deferred or one-line ring says the amendment
fired; ten full rings say it did not.**

*Written the same hour as the mistake, at the keeper's instruction: **learn it immediately and put it on
disk** rather than carrying it to the next handoff and hoping it survives.*
