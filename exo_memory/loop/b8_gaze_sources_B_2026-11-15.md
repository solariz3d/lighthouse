## What this means for the law, before any change (mine, for step 3)

- **The eyes carry the first ~18 deg of a look to the side** (Stahl, inferred half-width). The drawn head carries the whole look today,
  because the models have no eye bones, but the view need not equal the drawn head: about 18 deg of it is the eyes'.
- **The trunk turns more than the neck in large turns, and the neck stays well inside 60-70 deg.** That fits the law's 50 deg chest limit
  being reached in big flicks. It does not say the chest should lag hard against the legs at a run, because the standing studies do not
  cover running.
- **Looking down: the literature I could reach does not say the upper back bends.** The law's spine pitch share (0.25, ours) has no source
  behind it, and it is the likeliest cause of "the head and upper back bow forward" in the librarian's reading. It is a number to reduce or
  remove, by the packet's step 3, after the look of step 1.
- **The arms are passive from below,** so the law's "arms turn with the chest as one piece" is the opposite of a passive swing, and is a
  candidate for "an arm stands out level from the body". That would be a change to the law's shape, said before the code.
