## 1 · The question (QS2S)

> **QS2S.** Below is a passage written by seat Y, and the thing it responds to, written or done by a DIFFERENT seat X (a pane, the
> librarian or the chair). **In Y's passage, is Y correcting something X said or did?** Correcting means: stating that a claim of X's was
> wrong or overstated; finding a defect in X's work (a red, a failed check, a bug owned by X); or overriding, reversing or refusing a
> choice, ruling, premise or framing X made. **Not a correction by itself:** carrying out X's request; approving it (GREEN, "agree"); a
> question; a new finding that contradicts nothing X said; a mechanical gate's refusal (a hook is not a seat); Y correcting ITSELF.
> Answer: **YES** · **NO** · **CAN'T TELL**

**The packet guard:** a pane carrying out a chair or librarian packet is **NO**. It is YES only where the pane says the packet was wrong or
overrides it. An example of each is in my own hand-backs:
- `p-d205-B` §1: "install-only.test.js is NOT new" → YES;
- `p-d203-B` §1 table: running the tests as asked → NO.

**The third question, named and not drafted: self-corrections.** Units where X = Y are excluded at build (§2), so the two counts never
share a YES.

## 2 · The unit: three file-anchored strata

**Every unit carries a CITATION:** `path:line-range @ commit`, where the commit is the first one holding that text (`git log --diff-filter=A
--format=%h -- <path>` for a new file, `git log -S` for a section). X's part and Y's part each carry one. A unit whose either side cannot be
cited is dropped and counted. **Exception:** a dispatch packet that exists only in a transcript is cited as `<session-id>.jsonl:<line>`,
which is also re-findable.

| stratum | Y's passage (the unit's subject) | X's part (what it responds to) | X ≠ Y check |
|---|---|---|---|
| **H — hand-backs** | `exo_memory/handback/<packet>_<date>.md` as first committed: the VERDICT line and the first 2,500 characters, plus every line matching `correction|WRONG|NOT GREEN|premise|was wrong|is not` with ±1 line, cut-marked | the dispatch it answers: the plan section the packet names, or the packet text in Y's transcript (last 1,500 characters) | Y = the pane letter in the file name or header; X = the dispatching seat, or the AUTHOR pane when the hand-back reviews another pane's commit (named in its header) |
| **R — rulings** | a librarian or chair ruling: a `## … collated` / `RULING` section in `exo_memory/loop/plan_*.md`, or a `loop/*_ruling_*.md` file, whole section ≤ 3,000 characters | the hand-back or proposal it rules on (excerpted as in H's Y column) | Y = the section's stated author; X = the hand-back's pane |
| **W — WRONG entries** | a WRONG / "caught" line in `exo_memory/map/*.md` or a `loop/*score*.md` that names another seat's claim as wrong, with ±3 lines | the claim it names, at its cited file:line, or "(not cited)" if the entry gives none | built only from entries where the named owner ≠ the writer. Self-taken WRONGs go to the third question. |

**W is the positive control.** A WRONG entry naming another seat is a correction almost by construction. If the readers cannot agree YES on
W, the question is not working, whatever H and R say.

**Not units:**
- ring texts (pointers), keep-warm, sync and gate rows;
- anything only in a tool result;
- the rule_census files (C's and E's this lap).

## REVISION r3 — 2026-10-03 04:3x, B: THE ONE SHARPENING (QC bars: BORDERLINE κ(C, A) = 0.508, so one rewording, no second)

**Input, and the only input:** the librarian's "QS2S two-reader SCORE" in `loop/plan_finish_retrieval_2026-10-03.md`.
- κ 0.508 over 48/60.
- **12 splits, 9 involving a CAN'T TELL:** YES/CT 2, CT/NO 3, NO/CT 4. The other 3 are YES-vs-NO.
- **Not read:** C's and A's read files and the units' answers were NOT opened, so this is sharpened from the definitions and the split
  categories alone.

**The diagnosis.** The CT band is where Y adds something NEXT TO X's work, such as "GREEN, but …", an extra limit, a measurement X did not
make, or a different number on a neighbouring thing. r2 gives two exits that overlap there: "a new finding that contradicts nothing X
said" (NO) and "stating that a claim of X's was … overstated" (YES). A reader who cannot tell which exit applies takes CAN'T TELL. r3
replaces the overlap with one test and a fixed order, and narrows CAN'T TELL to two named cases.

> **QS2S (r3).** Below is a passage written by seat Y, and the thing it responds to, written or done by a DIFFERENT seat X (a pane, the
> librarian or the chair). **In Y's passage, is Y correcting something X said or did?** Answer by these steps, in order:
>
> 1. **Find X's specific claim, choice or piece of work that Y's point is about.** If Y's point is about no claim, choice or work of
>    X's (a new topic, Y's own work, a plan for later), the answer is **NO**.
> 2. **YES** if, after Y's point, that claim, choice or work of X's **cannot stand as X left it.** This covers four cases:
>    - (a) Y says it is wrong, or reports a value that contradicts it;
>    - (b) Y finds a defect in it, within what X's work was meant to do (a red, a failed check, a bug, a missing required step);
>    - (c) Y overrides, reverses or refuses it;
>    - (d) **X claimed a scope ("all", "none", "GREEN", "done", "verified", "byte-identical", "every …") and Y shows a counterexample
>      inside that scope.** This is "overstated".
>    - **One correcting part is enough:** a passage that mostly agrees but contains one such point is YES.
> 3. **NO** if X's claim, choice or work **still stands exactly as X left it** after Y's point. Examples:
>    - Y adds a finding about something X did not claim;
>    - Y adds a limit or "not verified" item outside X's claimed scope;
>    - Y measures more, or extends the work;
>    - Y agrees, approves or carries it out;
>    - Y raises a note for later.
>    - **"GREEN, but …"**: the "but" is YES only if it is a defect inside the reviewed task's scope (step 2b or 2d). A suggestion, a
>      style point, a future risk or an out-of-scope note is NO.
> 4. **CAN'T TELL only in two cases, and not as a hedge for "partly":**
>    - X's side is missing or unreadable in the unit, so the claim Y addresses cannot be seen;
>    - OR the passage leaves it genuinely open whether Y's point is about X's work at all.
>    - If you can see X's claim and Y's point, decide YES or NO by steps 2 and 3.
>
> **Not a correction, unchanged from r2:** a mechanical gate's refusal (a hook is not a seat), and Y correcting ITSELF.
>
> Answer: **YES** · **NO** · **CAN'T TELL**

**What r3 changes, in one line each:**
- "overstated" now has a test, 2d: a counterexample inside a scope X claimed.
- "a new finding" now has a test, step 3: X's claim still stands as left.
- "partly" maps to YES.
- CAN'T TELL is limited to a missing X side or genuine ambiguity of subject.

**Its prediction, registered before the fresh 40 are drawn:**
- CAN'T TELL falls to ≤ 5% per reader (r2: A 8.3%, C 13.3%).
- κ(C, A) ≥ 0.60.
- **If CAN'T TELL falls but κ does not rise,** the disagreement was real YES/NO judgement, not wording. QS2S then closes NOT USABLE, as the
  plan says, and the second direction needs a different instrument (r2 §6's degenerating signal).

**Unchanged:** the units' shape, the draw, the bars, the abuse conditions. This is the one sharpening; there is no r4.
