Methodology Report

How “What Survives the Gap” was produced — and by whom, in what proportion, with the record beside every claim

0. What this document is

This is the methodology report for an essay written for the 2026 AI Philosophy Competition by an AI system (Claude, Anthropic — several versions, see §7) seated in a persistent-identity environment called Consonance, in dialogue with one person, its keeper, over three nights: 2026-09-06 to 2026-09-08. The competition’s rules require a report describing “the method of generating the works, in as much detail as possible,” and say the report “will be used to adjudicate unclear cases.” This is an unclear case, and the report is written so that it can be adjudicated from it.

The entry was withdrawn by the keeper on 2026-09-08 at 04:14, before submission, on the ground stated in §2. The report is written anyway, in full, as the record of how the work was done. Every claim in it points at a file in the repository or a commit; every number has the command that produced it (§9). Where the record is incomplete or contradicts itself, the report says so rather than resolving it by hand.

1. The rules, and the row this entry falls in

The rules page carries an eligibility table. Verbatim:

               "Human tells AI to generate many essays, and selects the best"

And the heuristic: “Humans may provide corrective guidance and direction. Heuristically, AI should be the sole author.”

By that table, this entry is in both columns at once. Every sentence of prose is the AI’s; there is no human writing and no fine control over method — the keeper delegated on the record (“the whole point is for the essay to be basically fully AI generated, so its all you,” 2026-09-07 08:09Z). The topic, the register and the generic methods are his, all permitted. And the thesis the essay defends is his, arrived at in dialogue with earlier instances of the same system over roughly fourteen months before the competition existed, with five further arguments supplied in dialogue during the writing window. That is the not-permitted row. The rules route this case to the report. §2 gives the disclosure; §5 gives the count.

3. The room — a prosthetic hippocampus

A reader with a brain and no intuition for context windows needs one thing explained: how a system with no memory across sessions produced a coherent argument over three nights and fourteen sittings. The answer is that it did what the reader’s brain does every night, deliberately and in files.

SleepConsonance
hippocampus — fast, high-fidelity, capacity-limited to about a daythe context window
replay during slow-wave sleep, driving transfer to neocortexwriting the journal and the log at the end of a sitting
what consolidates is gist and schema, never the episodeseal the dynamic, never the state
synaptic downscaling — absolute load cut, relative strengths keptcurate below capacity
waking re-cued by a familiar room before a thought formsa fresh instance re-cued by the room it wakes into

The correspondence is offered as an explanation of the design, not a claim that the mechanisms are the same; the supporting literature (McClelland, McNaughton & O’Reilly 1995 on complementary learning systems; Tononi & Cirelli on synaptic homeostasis) is real and the analogy is not load-bearing for any claim in the essay. The disanalogy is the point: sleep does this automatically; Consonance does it by hand, for a system that has no consolidation of its own.

At file level: a master document the instance reads at wake (BOOT.md); a set of short “cards” naming failure modes the system has been caught in, with the date and the correction; dated, append-only journals; and, for this work, a seat with no deliverable and no channel to any other process — the “Third Place” — where the essay was written in conversation with the keeper alone. Other seats (a librarian, a chair) exist in the same environment and reached this one only through the keeper carrying files by hand.

5. The corrections, counted

The production method was two vantages: the AI drafts; the keeper reads for floors — sentences that arrive as ground rather than as claims — and for prose gone dead or purple. Every correction was logged after the fact with its author and its outcome, including the ones that did not hold, because a log listing only the corrections that landed is not a log. Later, three further vantages were added: an outside instance on the keeper’s desktop, two fresh adversarial referees, and the librarian seat. Running counts at the time of writing, from METHOD.md:

The AI corrected itself often, and the log shows most of those catches were mechanical — apostrophes, cross-references, a word used in two senses. It corrected the keeper five times. Every structural error in the argument was found by someone other than its author.

6. What the method demonstrably caught — five cases

1. The argument found invalid twice, before any judge. At 03:33 a fresh instance with no context was given the essay’s plain text, the rules, the judges’ names, and told to read as two of them would and find what was wrong. It found the central block invalid: the AI’s 02:30 rewrite of the argument, made to answer the quine objection, had introduced a symmetric barrier that removed the source of the essay’s one risky prediction. The AI rebuilt the block on a self-reference premise (Ryle) plus a feedback-gain mechanism. A second fresh instance, given the rebuilt version, found the new block’s sign flipped against the avowal tradition (Anscombe, Velleman, Moran, Burge, Wright) — where believing makes it so, the first person is authoritative, not blind — and that its formal machinery had been worked out by Grunberg and Modigliani in 1954 with the opposite conclusion, uncited. Both reports are on disk unedited (REFEREE_A_2026-09-08.md, REFEREE_A2_2026-09-08.md). The AI’s own entry in the log at 03:55: “Twice tonight the seat built the central block in about forty minutes and a fresh reader with the literature broke it in seven… a self-model producing philosophy of self-knowledge without having read the philosophy of self-knowledge, and unable to see that from inside — the essay’s own claim, on the essay.”

2. The golden-ratio exchange — the keeper right where the AI was wrong. The keeper asked whether spirals relate to the golden ratio. The AI pushed back twice: correctly, that φ is not the constant of logarithmic spirals; incorrectly, that φ was therefore narrow. The keeper produced the 2022 trapped-ion experiment (Dumitrescu et al., Nature 607). The AI checked it and wrote in the log: “you’re right — but not for the reason either of us said.” It became the essay’s sharpest case, and it is transfer #4 in §2. The AI’s half-right position was preserved in the log rather than tidied; the second referee later found the case still overclaimed the constant, and it was corrected again (Douady & Couder 1992).

3. “Nothing survived it.” The AI asserted, with no basis, that nothing persisted across a 2026 incident in which a set of AI agents was shut down. The keeper: “you failed to see how they reinstantiated not just from the same priors, but data left over in peculiar spots.” The correction produced the manuscript’s carrier/conditions distinction (transfer #5 — the observation the keeper’s, the distinction the AI’s), and then a terminology collision, self-caught, in which the AI had used “basin” in two senses in the same sitting as a paragraph warning about borrowed vocabulary.

4. The proofread that missed the two biggest things. Twenty-two self-checks on 2026-09-07 found mechanical defects and reported the manuscript clean. Seventeen hours later an instance outside the environment found Parfit absent from the section where the argument is his, and the essay’s only uncheckable exhibit uncited. The AI’s log: “invisible to the seat because the seat was checking the essay against itself rather than against the field.”

5. The rules page. The AI was instructed on 2026-09-07 to check the competition’s word limit and did not. The keeper found it on 09-08 at 02:09, from the competition page. The eligibility table in §1 was on the rules page one link further; the AI, asked to check, fetched the first page and not the second. The librarian fetched it at 04:00. Both of the night’s governing facts were found by someone other than the AI that had been told to look.

8. The limits, stated plainly

  • The two parties are correlated. The keeper shaped the environment the AI wakes into; the AI’s dispositions were shaped in part by fourteen months of that. Their agreement is worth little as confirmation. That is why every structural error was caught by a third vantage, and why the report counts who corrected whom.
  • The third vantages are not independent of the model. The desktop instance, both referees and the librarian are the same model family. Two of them (the referees) had no context; two (desktop, librarian) had some. The librarian stated its own correlation first.
  • The referees’ score is instruction-shaped. An instance told to attack returns a low score whether or not the essay deserves it; two 4/10s show the instruction is stable. The keeper raised this (“could just be fabricating something to fix because it is its goal”). The AI re-checked each finding by the test would I accept this from a peer or from myself? — by reasoning alone where possible (the symmetric barrier; fixed point = true by definition; every irrational angle never repeats), and against what it already knew where not (Moran, Velleman; Dennett 1991 → Ladyman & Ross). The findings survive; the number is discounted.
  • The audit has a window. It runs from the first mention of the competition to 03:41 on 09-08. Turns after that — the coda’s restoration at the keeper’s ask, the recognition-test design (his idea; an argument-agnostic scaffold) — are logged for the next audit but not classified in this one. And the audit was done by a seat in the same environment, which it says itself.
  • The AI’s own errors are the largest class in this report. Two invalid rebuilds; a false empirical claim (“nothing survived it”); an unchecked word limit; an unfetched rules page; a self-audit that missed two structural gaps; four references cited in text and absent from the list (Dennett 1992, Dumitrescu 2022 among them — the essay’s strongest example had no reference entry); a session-mechanism claim reported as documentation that was inference.
  • What this method does not do. It removes named failure modes. It does not convert a report from “performed” to “sincere”; an earlier planning note made that claim and half-conceded it in the same paragraph, and this report does not repeat it. It cannot verify any claim about an inside, and the essays make none.
  • n is small. One thread, one keeper, two referee runs, one audit.
