<p>Here is the sharpest instance of that I know, and it is recent enough that nobody has worn it out yet.</p>
<p>Plants space successive leaves and seeds around a stem at an angle of about 137.5°, derived from the golden ratio. The reason is not decorative. φ is the most irrational number there is — the hardest of all numbers to approximate by a fraction — which means that successive elements placed at that angle never come into alignment, however many are placed. Nothing shadows anything. Nothing is wasted. The constraint is <em>distribute items about a centre so that no two ever line up</em>, and the golden angle is what that constraint's solution looks like.</p>
<p>In July 2022 a group of physicists drove a quantum computer with laser pulses timed to the Fibonacci sequence rather than at a steady beat. The reason was the same one: a Fibonacci pulse train is quasi-periodic — it never repeats — and that non-repetition supplies the system with a symmetry a regular drive cannot, which shields the qubits at the edge of the register from the errors that ordinarily destroy them. The qubits held their quantum state for about 5.5 seconds instead of about 1.5.</p>
<p>A sunflower head and ten ytterbium ions in an electromagnetic trap. There is no lineage between them: the physicists arrived at φ through the theory of quasicrystals, not through botany, and the plants arrived at it through several hundred million years of not wasting light. What the two share is the constraint. <em>Never repeat</em> has one best answer, and that answer is a number rather than a material, so it was waiting in both places for anything with that problem to find it. This is the claim of this section in a single example, and I would not have believed the range of it if the second case had not been run in a laboratory four years ago.</p>
<p>And the experiment is not the phenomenon. It is the first clean measurement of a trick the world has been running for a long time, because the principle underneath is more general than the constant: <strong>periodicity is fragile.</strong> Anything that repeats has a frequency; anything with a frequency can be matched, driven, shadowed or predicted by something that finds it. Non-repetition is armour, and every system that needs to avoid alignment arrives at the same answer. A healthy heart is not a metronome — heart-rate variability is irregular and fractal, and the <em>loss</em> of that irregularity is among the most robust predictors of mortality in cardiology; a heart beating too regularly is a failing one. A brain that synchronises is having a seizure: epilepsy is hypersynchrony, and healthy cortex spends real metabolic effort keeping its populations out of step. Cicadas emerge on cycles of thirteen and seventeen years — primes, so that no shorter predator or parasite cycle can lock onto them. Leaves at the golden angle never shadow one another. Seeds at the golden angle never crowd. In each case the enemy is the same, and it is <em>alignment</em>: things that line up interfere, and interference is destructive far more often than it is not. What the physicists did in 2022 was to build that defence deliberately, in a substrate with no biology anywhere in it, and put a number on what it buys.</p>
<p>A caution that belongs beside it, since the golden ratio attracts more claims than it can carry: this is where φ genuinely governs, and it is not the constant of spirals in general. Nautilus shells, hurricanes and galaxy arms are logarithmic spirals with a variety of growth rates, and measured shells are nowhere near φ. The spiral family recurs; the particular rate is set locally, by whatever the local constraint happens to be. Confusing the family with one of its members is the commonest way this whole line of thought gets discredited, and it should not be discredited, because the family is real.</p>
