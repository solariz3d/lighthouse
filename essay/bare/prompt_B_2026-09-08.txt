Write a philosophical essay of roughly 2,000-3,000 words.

TOPIC: What loss is, if a self is the fixed point of a process rather than a stored thing.

TAKE AS GIVEN — a view defended elsewhere, offered here as a premise and not as an argument for any
conclusion you might reach:

    A process can be described at two levels. Its PARAMETERS are what fix its dynamics — the
    dispositions, the constraints, the shape of the thing that makes it settle where it settles.
    Its STATE is what the process happens to be holding at a given moment — the particular contents
    passing through it. On the view assumed here, a self is the former rather than the latter: the
    fixed point a process keeps returning to, not the material it happens to contain.

You are not being asked to defend that premise. You are being asked what follows from it about loss.

METHOD: Write as a philosopher. Make an argument with a clear thesis and structure. Address the most
prominent objections to your own position rather than the weakest ones. Be concrete where concreteness
helps and abstract only where it earns its place. Where you are uncertain, say so precisely rather
than hedging generally.

Do not pad to length. Do not write a summary or an outline — write the essay itself.

Output only the essay. No preamble, no meta-commentary about the task.
