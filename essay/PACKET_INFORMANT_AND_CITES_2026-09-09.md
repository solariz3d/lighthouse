## P-INFORMANT — what does the second reader see that the first did not? (a thinking pane, then the model)

The fourth referee's finding 1, in one sentence: *the informant's evidence is also the person's
realised behaviour; the self lived through the unattended settings too and remembers them, so the
self's evidence is a superset; and the self alone has a within-person time series across values
of its own belief, which is exactly the data that estimates the reaction function.* So either
there is no asymmetry, or the asymmetry is the old one (the self reads its self-concept back
instead of its record — Swann, Vazire), in which case "parts by criterion" is a relabelling.

Two primed readers reached the same wall from the other side: a standing disposition either
switches off — then it is not standing — or never does, and the informant sees belief-shaped
behaviour from a worse seat. And one: if the self-model is a constituent of the loop, "what the
process would do at a neutral self-belief" may be a counterfactual about a *different* process.

**The packet:** enumerate every candidate answer to *what the informant sees that the self did
not*, and test each. Candidates the seat can list, none yet argued:
1. **Aggregation.** The self reads its self-concept back rather than its behavioural record
   (self-verification). If this is the answer, the criterion claim survives only as the *scoring*
   of an old bias, and §4 must say so.
2. **The self's time series is not usable by the self.** It has the data but cannot use it,
   because each past reading was itself taken under the then-current belief (the reading is
   contaminated at every point, not only in aggregate). Is that true? It is the one candidate that
   would keep the asymmetry structural. Model it: give the self its full history of (r, x) pairs
   and an unbiased estimator; can it recover f(0)? If yes, finding 1 stands. If the estimator is
   biased *by construction* because r and x are never independent in the self's sample, the seat
   has a premise.
3. **Cross-context sampling.** The informant sees the person in contexts the person does not
   attend to and does not encode (not: does not live through). Requires a memory premise.
4. **Comparative gain** (referee finding 5): the informant's gain is lower, not zero; asymmetry of
   degree; predicts stranger > friend > self on capacity, which Vazire's design can test — and her
   intellect cell, scored against an IQ test with the belief engaged, currently reads as a
   *disconfirmation* of "no self-error against realised behaviour." Confront it.
5. **Re-scope.** Drop the informant. Keep: in the looped regime there is a second quantity and
   self-report is silent about it. The referee says this survives on its own.

Hand back: which candidates survive the two-map model with an informant map and a self with
history (E's code, `sim/two_map.py`, extended); which need a premise the essay does not have; and
a recommendation between 2 and 5. Falsifier: if no candidate gives the informant anything the
self's own history could not, the informant advantage is dropped from A and C3 is rewritten as a
claim about the second quantity alone.
