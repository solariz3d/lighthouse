## A. STRUCTURAL

### 1. The P3–P4–C2 step is invalid: indeterminacy is not inaccuracy. At a fixed point the self-representation is true *by definition*.

(a) "Where the gain is high, several descriptions are each self-consistent, the fact under-determines which is reached, and the self-model settles where its own dynamics take it rather than where the truth is. That is where a self cannot see itself." / P4: "A representation that changes its object is accurate only where it is a fixed point — true once believed." / C2: "a self is unreliable about a variable in proportion to the gain."

(b) P4 says accuracy obtains *at* a fixed point. High gain, on the essay's own account, yields *several* fixed points and the system settles at one. But whichever one it settles at, the representation there is true once believed — i.e. accurate. So the conclusion licensed by P3+P4 is "at high gain, which true self-description obtains is under-determined by the prior fact," not "at high gain the self is unreliable." The essay slides from *not fact-tracking* to *inaccurate* in one sentence ("rather than where the truth is" — but at a fixed point the representation *is* the truth). The only way to get inaccuracy is for the system to be held off any fixed point (transit, oscillation, basin-switching), and nothing in the block supplies that. Worse, the attractor metaphysics of §2 says the opposite: attractors are where a system *stays*. So C2 does not follow, and prediction three below (instability) contradicts P1.

There is also an unstated mathematical error: "high gain" (large ∂v/∂r) does not entail multiple fixed points. A monotone map with slope > 1 can cross the identity exactly once, or never. Multiplicity depends on the *shape* of the response, not its magnitude. The sentence "several representations are self-consistent" is asserted, not derived.

(c) Fix: define the objects. Let v be the variable, r the self-representation, v = f(r) with gain f′. Fixed points are r* = f(r*). Then say what "unreliable" means — (i) r ≠ v at time t, or (ii) r not determined by the pre-representational fact, or (iii) low test-retest — and pick one; they are three different claims and the essay uses all three (C2 uses (i)/(ii), prediction 3 uses (iii)). If the claim is (ii), retitle it: it is a claim about *authority without tracking*, not about opacity, and it is an old claim (finding 3).

### 2. The best objection, unanswered: the highest-gain self-representations are the ones we know *best*. Intention, decision, avowal.

(a) "Represent yourself as intelligent, or as someone who finishes things, and what you do next changes… That is where a self cannot see itself."

(b) Take gain to its maximum: "I am about to raise my arm," "I intend to finish this," "I hereby decide." Here representing makes it so — gain ≈ 1, the representation is *the cause* of what it represents — and self-knowledge is at its most secure. This is Anscombe's practical knowledge ("the cause of what it understands"), Velleman's self-fulfilling intentions (*Practical Reflection*, 1989; "Epistemic Freedom," 1989), Moran's deliberative authority (*Authority and Estrangement*, 2001), Burge's self-verifying judgments (1988), and Wright's extension-*determining* judgments (1989), which enjoy authority precisely *because* they constitute rather than track their objects. Every one of these says: where believing makes it so, the first person is authoritative and the third person is derivative. The essay's C3 says the exact opposite, at the same location, and cites none of them. A referee who knows this literature will read C3 as the known result with its sign flipped and no argument for the flip.

The essay's own example betrays it: "I am about to change my mind" is listed as a high-gain variable the self is opaque about. But announcing a change of mind is one of the paradigm cases of avowal — saying it is doing it.

(c) Fix: distinguish *constitutive* gain (avowal/intention — believing is the making; unique fixed point; authority) from *causal* gain (trait beliefs that shift behaviour probabilistically over time; possibly multiple fixed points; possible drift). The interesting claim, if there is one, is about the second only, and it needs an argument that causal gain produces basin-switching rather than lock-in. State that and cite Moran/Velleman/Wright as the position being departed from.

### 3. The originality claim is inaccurate. The formal core (reflexive representation + fixed point) was worked out in 1954 with the opposite conclusion; the "location" claim is not new either.

(a) "What none of them supplies — and what the attractor view does — is a location." / "What I add is narrower… not that a self cannot see itself, which is old, but where."

(b) Three separate problems.
- **Grunberg & Modigliani (1954), Simon (1954).** Public predictions that alter what they predict; the question "can such a prediction still be true?"; answered via Brouwer: a correct reflexive prediction *always exists* (continuity assumed). This is the essay's P3–P4 machinery, in the essay's own vocabulary ("fixed point"), and its conclusion is *possibility of accuracy*, not opacity. Henshel (1995) and the "performative prediction" literature (Perdomo et al. 2020) extend it. Not cited. A judge who knows Fitelson's corner of the field will know Simon 1954.
- **Merton (1948), Hacking's looping kinds (1995), Elster (states essentially by-products, *Sour Grapes* 1983), Rosenthal's Pygmalion, labelling theory.** All are "representation with causal gain on its object," several at the level of persons and traits. Not cited. Hacking in particular gives a *location* — human kinds loop, natural kinds do not — which is the essay's low-gain/high-gain cut one level up.
- **Carruthers does supply a location.** The ISA theory says attitudes are opaque (interpreted) and sensory/perceptual states are transparent (directly accessed). That is a principled where-and-why. Metzinger's location is global; Ryle's is the act in progress. So "none of them supplies a location" is false of Carruthers, and the essay's claim should be "a *different* location, by a different principle" — which is a smaller claim and needs to be argued against Carruthers' rather than beside it.

(c) Fix: one paragraph acknowledging Grunberg–Modigliani/Simon and the avowal tradition, stating precisely what the gain view says that they do not (candidate: that gain is a graded, per-variable quantity and predicts a *distribution* of accuracy across variables rather than a binary attitudes/sensations cut). That is defensible and would be new. The current framing is not.

### 4. C3 does not follow from C2 + P5. The second conjunct smuggles an observability premise.

(a) C3: "…and reliable about itself where a second self is blind — on low-gain states the second cannot observe."

(b) Nothing in P1–P5 links low gain to low observability. Heart rate is the essay's own zero-gain example, and it is observable with a stethoscope. Conversely, an anxious mood is low-gain *and* low-observability, but only contingently. The "exactly where" symmetry of C3 requires gain and observability to be anti-correlated, and that is not argued anywhere; it is read off Vazire's data after the fact and then presented as derived. This matters because it is precisely the "self is best on low-observability" cell of SOKA, and the essay's C3 has nothing of its own to say about it — it borrows Vazire's variable and calls it a prediction.

(c) Fix: either drop the second conjunct of C3 (then C3 is only "others are better on high-gain variables") or add P6: "low-gain self-variables are typically low-observability to others" and defend it. It is not obviously true.

### 5. P5 is false for exactly the variables that matter. Other people's representations of you have gain too — and not only "through what it communicates."

(a) P5: "A second self's model of the first has, on the first's variables, no such gain except through what it communicates. Its representations are effects of the first and not causes."

(b) Evaluative, socially-embedded traits — intellect, competence, conscientiousness — are the paradigm cases of *other*-gain: teacher expectancy (Rosenthal & Jacobson), stereotype threat, labelling, Merton's original examples. A friend who believes you are capable hands you the harder task, waits for your answer, introduces you differently — gain through action, not only communication. And the "except through what it communicates" clause swallows the premise: friends communicate constantly. If other-gain on evaluative traits is comparable to self-gain, C3's asymmetry disappears on the essay's own mechanism. The essay needs either an argument that self-gain is systematically larger (plausible — the self is always in the loop, the friend intermittently) or a weaker C3 stated in terms of *relative* gain.

(c) Fix: rewrite P5 as a comparative — "the first self's gain on its own high-gain variables exceeds any second self's" — and give the reason (continuous vs. intermittent coupling). That is defensible and keeps C3, weakened.

### 6. "Fixed point" is used in three non-equivalent senses and the argument's location claim rests on the one the metaphysics does not use.

(a) §2: "a tree is not literally the fixed point of a map… the attractor sense, used broadly." / P4: "a fixed point — true once believed."

(b) Sense (i): fixed point of a map. Sense (ii): attractor of a dynamical system (the essay's admitted extension; the self is one of these). Sense (iii): a self-fulfilling representation — a fixed point of the map from belief-content to truth-value. (ii) and (iii) are different objects: the self is an attractor of the whole process; the "true once believed" description is a fixed point of a sub-map. The essay's title, §2 and §5 run on (ii); the "location" claim in §4 runs on (iii); the transition is made by the word. Dorr will see it in the first read.

Related: "self" is likewise unstable. P1: the self *is* the attractor. P3/C2: the self *has* a self-model that represents variables and is "unreliable." Attractors do not have beliefs; models do. And if at high gain the process has *several* attractors, P1's definite article ("the attractor") is undefined — which one is the self?

(c) Fix: two words for two objects. Keep "attractor" for the self, use "self-verifying" or "self-consistent" for the description, and say explicitly that the second is a property of the self-model's contents, not of the attractor.

### 7. Formal block: P2 and C1 do no work in the derivation and should not be in it.

(a) "C3 is the claim. It is not… Ryle's elusiveness restated — Ryle locates the blind spot at the act in progress, and C1 keeps that."

(b) C2 is derived from P3, P4; C3 from C2, P5. P2 and C1 are cited by nothing downstream. They are there to let the essay say Ryle is kept. In an argument the essay flags as "the one place where something can be settled," decorative premises read as padding to a logician. Also, "settled rather than shown" is wrong about its own status: C3 is an empirical distribution claim and premises cannot settle it — the essay concedes as much three paragraphs later ("It is settled by whether the distribution C3 predicts is the one that is found").

(c) Fix: move Ryle to the prose; keep the block as P1, P3, P4, P5 → C2, C3. Drop "settled."

### 8. The empirical section: the three divergent predictions are, respectively, (1) not divergent, (2) already refuted by existing data by the essay's own falsification criterion, (3) inconsistent with the attractor framing and equivocal on "reliable."

(a) "It parts from it in three places…"

(b)
- **Prediction 1** ("self-opacity on high-gain variables that are not evaluative — I am someone who finishes what I start… where a motivational account predicts nothing"). "Finishes what I start" is conscientiousness, which is evaluative on Vazire's own scale, so it is not a clean non-evaluative high-gain case. And SOKA is not merely "a motivational account": it has two axes, observability (informational) and evaluativeness (motivational). For a low-observability, non-evaluative trait SOKA predicts *self* advantage; so it does not "predict nothing," it predicts the opposite of C3 — which is fine, but the essay misdescribes the rival. "I am about to change my mind" is not a trait and cannot be scored in a SOKA design; see also finding 2.
- **Prediction 2** ("self-accuracy on evaluative facts with zero gain — a recorded score, a height — where a motivational account predicts distortion") and the falsifier "or that evaluative zero-gain facts are misread, and C3 is refuted." This is already answered, and against the essay. Self-reported GPA and test scores are systematically inflated, most among low performers (Kuncel, Credé & Thomas 2005, meta-analysis, N ≈ 61k — checked). Self-reported height is over-reported and weight under-reported in large validation samples (Gorber et al. 2007 review). These are zero-gain evaluative facts and they are misread in the self-favouring direction. By the essay's own stated test, C3 is refuted — or, more charitably, motivational distortion exists *independently* of gain, so gain cannot be what explains SOKA's evaluativeness cell, and the view is at best a complement to SOKA, not a rival. This is the most damaging thing in the essay because the essay itself named the test.
- **Prediction 3** ("self-ratings on high-gain variables less stable across time than others' ratings"). Two problems. First, an attractor is by definition where the system *stays*; multiple fixed points give hysteresis and lock-in, not wandering. Instability is what the view should predict *against*. Second, "reliable" here is test-retest consistency; in C2 it was accuracy. If self-ratings track a variable that genuinely moves (because the self keeps re-settling), the self is *accurate* and the stable friend-rating is *wrong*. Without a criterion measure (which Vazire had — behavioural tests, IQ), the prediction cannot distinguish "self is noisy" from "self is right and the variable moves."

(c) Fix: drop prediction 2 or reverse it (the view now predicts *distortion* at zero gain if a motivational term is added — say so and lose the rivalry claim). For prediction 3, specify the criterion and state whether the view predicts lock-in or drift, with the mechanism. For prediction 1, choose a variable that is high-gain, low-evaluativeness, and non-trait-ambiguous — e.g. "I will keep this resolution" against a behavioural record — and say what SOKA predicts for it.

### 9. Section 5 answers Vacariu by asserting the thing in dispute, and the analogy equivocates token identity with type recurrence.

(a) "Two forms, arrived at by different physics in different materials, that turn out to be one spiral are not two things in agreement. They are one attractor, reached from two directions." / "they are descriptions of one fixed point, and a fixed point has one shape."

(b) The shell and the galaxy are two *tokens* of one form *type*. Brain-description and mind-description, on dual-aspect, are two descriptions of one *token*. The move from "same type recurs in two media" to "one token under two descriptions" is the whole of the argument and it is a category shift. And "they must agree because they are descriptions of one fixed point" presupposes that there is one fixed point — which is what dual-aspect asserts and what Vacariu denies. It is not a mechanism where Vacariu had a word; it is the same word ("one") with "fixed point" attached. §5 adds nothing to Spinoza/Russell/Feigl and a judge will say so. (Also: Feigl was an identity theorist and Russell a neutral monist; "dual-aspect" fits Spinoza only.)

(c) Fix: cut §5 to a paragraph that says the view is a dual-aspect view and does not pretend to derive the correspondence; or give the actual argument for why two descriptions of one attractor must be *complete* descriptions that agree, which is non-trivial (two projections of one attractor can be in one-to-many relation).

### 10. The stored-parameters / regenerated-state distinction undercuts the essay's own slogan and its advance on Parfit.

(a) "the parameters of a process can be stored… but the state is not." / "You are not retrieved. You are re-arrived at." / "What survives the gap… is the fixed point. Not the stuff."

(b) Given the dynamics, the attractor is *determined by the parameters*. So what makes tonight's re-arrival *you* rather than some other self of the same process type is the stored parameters — the synapses. Then what survives the gap is the parameters, and the fixed point is re-derived from them each morning. The essay has relocated Parfit's psychological connectedness (carried by stored connections) into the phrase "parameters," and then said the parameters are not what survives. Either the parameters are part of the self (then "not retrieved" is false — most of you is retrieved) or they are not (then the essay owes an account of why two processes with the same dynamics and different parameters are different selves, and "fixed point" cannot supply it, since the fixed point *is* the parameter-dependent thing). §6's substrate anecdote makes the same slip: what carried across the model swaps was the stored context, i.e. parameters.

(c) Fix: say "a self is a stored parameter-set plus the attractor it generates under a dynamics"; then Parfit's continuity is the parameter-continuity, and the essay's addition is the claim that the *state* must be regenerated — a genuinely smaller but honest claim.

## B. OBJECTIONS NOT ANSWERED (the best one is finding 2; these are the others)

11. **Graded self-representation dissolves the multiplicity.** Grunberg–Modigliani's result depends on continuity; real self-representations are graded ("I usually finish things," "70% likely"). A graded representation has a fixed point under almost any gain. The essay treats self-descriptions as binary points, which is what manufactures indeterminacy. No answer.

12. **Anticipation.** A self-model that knows it has gain can correct for it (the "I know I over-promise" move). The essay's picture is of a self-model blind to its own gain; but sophisticated self-knowledge routinely includes a gain estimate. Where it does, the fixed point becomes unique and the second self loses its advantage. Not addressed.

13. **The shift from "cannot see" to "is unreliable."** Ryle/Metzinger describe *structural* inaccessibility. C2 describes *statistical* unreliability. The essay presents the second as a sharpening of the first, but they are different genera; a variable can be perfectly accessible and yet unreliably reported (motivational bias), or inaccessible and reliably inferred. The lineage claim ("I take all three to be right… what none supplies is a location") joins a structural thesis to a statistical one without noting the change of kind.

## C. FACTUAL / CITATION

14. **Goldberger is miscited and the citation cuts against the thesis.** (a) "the loss of that variability is among the most robust markers of disease and age there is (Goldberger et al. 2002)" / "Health, in a process, looks like coming back without coming round again." (b) Goldberger's claim is loss of *fractal complexity / long-range correlation*, and he is explicit that pathology has two faces: excessive periodicity (Cheyne–Stokes, heart-failure oscillations) *and* uncorrelated randomness (atrial fibrillation — maximally aperiodic, and disease). So "aperiodic = healthy" is precisely what the cited paper denies. What Goldberger calls healthy is *correlated* aperiodicity, i.e. fractal, i.e. the property the essay explicitly declines two sentences earlier ("I will not say strange"). "Among the most robust markers there is" is inflated in any case. (c) Either accept "strange"/fractal (and lose the disclaimer) or drop the physiological support.

15. **"Aperiodic attractor that is not strange" is a quasiperiodic torus — which *does* come round again, componentwise.** (a) "The mathematical name for that is an aperiodic attractor — I will not say strange… the Fibonacci drive of the last section is aperiodic without being strange." (b) A bounded aperiodic non-chaotic attractor is quasiperiodic: a superposition of periodic motions with incommensurate frequencies. Each component recurs. That is the opposite of "never once in the same state" in the sense the essay wants. Also, a *drive* is not an attractor — the Fibonacci pulse train is an input sequence, so it is no example of the category. (c) Say "quasiperiodic" if that is meant, and check whether the clinical vocabulary (rumination = limit cycle) survives; or accept chaotic.

16. **The golden-angle constraint does not select φ; every irrational angle satisfies "never align."** (a) "The constraint is distribute items about a centre so that no two ever line up, and the golden angle is what that constraint's solution looks like." (b) Any irrational multiple of 2π never exactly repeats. What φ optimises is *uniformity* of spacing (three-distance theorem; worst-case gap minimised). The stated constraint under-determines the solution, which is the same flaw the essay identifies in its own quantum case one paragraph later ("any sufficiently irrational drive would have protected the qubits"). Having conceded that, "one of them reached for the same member of it" is not a convergence — the essay says Fibonacci was canonical, i.e. conventional, not constraint-selected. The paragraph refutes its own headline case. (c) State the real constraint (maximise minimum angular separation over all n) and drop "same member."

17. **Phyllotaxis "through several hundred million years of not wasting light."** (a) That sentence. (b) The golden angle in phyllotaxis arises from the mechanics of primordium placement (Douady & Couder 1992, physical and numerical demonstration; auxin-transport models since) — it is a dynamical attractor, not a selected optimum. Ironically this is *better* for the essay's thesis (form from process) than the adaptationist story it tells. (c) Cite Douady & Couder and say so.

18. **Dumitrescu et al. 2022.** Checked: 10 Yb ions, edge-qubit coherence ≈ 5.5 s under the Fibonacci drive — confirmed via coverage of the Nature paper. The "≈1.5 s" periodic-drive figure is from press coverage; give the paper's own measure if citing precisely. "That non-repetition supplies the system with a symmetry a regular drive cannot" is loose: the quasiperiodic drive is viewed as a projection of a 2-frequency (two-time-dimension) drive, and the protection is a dynamical *topological* phase of the edge modes; "any sufficiently irrational drive would have protected" is an inference the paper does not make. Flag as unsupported, not false.

19. **Ryle.** "(Chapter VI, 'The Systematic Elusiveness of I')" — Chapter VI is "Self-Knowledge"; the elusiveness passage is §7 of it. Minor. More substantive: Ryle's point is *deflationary* — the elusiveness is an indexical feature like "today," not an opacity of a self. Enlisting him in an opacity lineage with Metzinger and Carruthers strains him; "Metzinger made the same point from the other side" is not the same point (phenomenal transparency vs. logical type-difference).

20. **Carruthers.** "with no privileged channel" — true of *attitudes* on ISA; Carruthers explicitly grants privileged access to sensory and imagistic states. The essay's sentence restricts to attitudes, so it is defensible, but see finding 3: this *is* a location, and the essay says there is none.

21. **Structural realism.** "What would refute the view is a bottom: something that persists and is not the fixed point of any process." That is not OSR's refutation condition (which concerns individuals with intrinsic natures/haecceities); OSR does not claim everything is a process fixed point. The essay has substituted its own thesis for OSR's in stating what would refute it. "Every time anyone has dug for stuff, they have come up with structure" is rhetoric. "Some forty orders of magnitude, from the molecular to the cosmic web" — molecular (10⁻⁹ m) to cosmic web (10²⁵ m) is ≈34.

22. **Dual-aspect lineage.** Feigl (1958) is an identity theorist; Russell a neutral monist. Only Spinoza fits "dual-aspect" cleanly.

23. **Vacariu.** "one per kind of observer, one per mind" — these are different theses (kind-relative vs. token-relative worlds); pick one.

## D. MECHANICAL

24. "φ governs where the constraint is never align" — unreadable without quotation marks or italics around *never align*; same for "the problem never align" in the next sentence.
25. "and one of them reached for the same member of it" — "same" has no antecedent (same as what? neither reached for the *other's* member). See finding 16.
26. Abstract: "differs from the model that predicted it in three ways" — "it" refers to the pattern, "the model" to SOKA; reads as if the view differs from a model that predicted the view.
27. "It's a fold that includes the fold." / "a self is a fold that includes itself" — the image is doing argumentative work it cannot bear; a fold that includes itself is not a defined object.
28. Repeated self-announcing tic: "I want to be exact about what is," "I should be exact about what converges," "I will be careful, because it is easy to fit," "I am going to take it slowly," "Now look at what just happened." Five in 5,000 words. Style is not graded, but each one is an unkept promise where a definition should be, and judges notice the substitution.
29. "What I claim is… that everything in this essay follows from the structure alone." False on its face: the three predictions require empirical gain assumptions (which variables are high-gain), which are not structural.
30. "Each is its own argument, made in a companion essay." Judges have no companion essay; this reads as a claim to work not before them. Cut.
31. §6 first paragraph: "that click… should be trusted exactly as far as its gain is low" — the essay uses its own theory to validate the reader's assent to the essay. A judge will read this as the theory certifying itself.
32. "The two images are not similar. They are the same shape." — they are similar; "same shape" is the thesis, not an observation. Minor, but it is the first substantive sentence and it asserts the conclusion.

## E. VERDICT

**What is genuinely good and must not be touched.** The idea of *gain* as a graded, per-variable quantity that predicts a *distribution* of self-knowledge accuracy — rather than a binary attitudes/sensations cut (Carruthers) or a global opacity (Metzinger) — is a real proposal, and prediction 3 (indeterminacy rather than flattery; reliability rather than bias, tested against a criterion) is a real, cheap, discriminating experiment that could be run on existing panel data. Keep those two things and the honesty of the falsification paragraph. The §2 parameter/state distinction is also correct and useful, if the essay follows it where it leads (finding 10).

**Score: 4/10** as a competition entry read by Dorr and Fitelson. The prose is strong and the ambition is right, but the central derivation does not go through (finding 1), the best-known literature on the exact mechanism says the opposite and is uncited (findings 2–3), and the one empirical test the essay names as decisive is already failed by data it did not look up (finding 8). A judge who reaches finding 8 will stop scoring.

**The one change that would raise it most.** Rebuild the core around *constitutive* vs. *causal* gain (finding 2), concede the avowal tradition and Grunberg–Modigliani/Simon, and make the claim: *causal-gain variables with multiple self-consistent descriptions show lower self-report stability against a criterion than informant reports, controlling for evaluativeness.* That is one precise, new, testable claim; drop predictions 1 and 2, cut §5 to a paragraph, and give the reclaimed words to defining "gain," "fixed point," and "unreliable" so that P3–P4–C2 is actually valid. Done well, that is a 7.
