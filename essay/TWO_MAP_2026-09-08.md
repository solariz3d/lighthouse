## 0 · THE ANSWER, FIRST

**Item 3 — does self-error at equilibrium appear from lag alone, with no bias term?**

> **No. In the model the third referee specified, lag alone produces exactly zero self-error at
> equilibrium — to machine precision, at every gain, every learning rate and every delay swept.
> The falsifier the brief registered has fired.**

The reason is one line and it is analytic, not numerical. Equilibrium requires `r* = g(f(r*))`.
With `g` unbiased, that is `r* = f(r*) = x*`. **The learning rate and the delay do not appear in
the fixed-point equation at all** — they are stability parameters, not location parameters. There
is no value of either that can move an equilibrium off the diagonal. Measured: max `|r* − x*|` over
all 201 fixed points of all 121 gains = **2.776e-17** `[2.1]`; over 287 settled integrations
spanning six learning rates and eight delays = **2.105e-15** `[2.4]`.

Bias does move it, and moves it by exactly itself: `|r* − x*| = |b|`, deviation from that identity
over the 61 × 121 (gain, bias) grid = **5.551e-17**, *independent of gain* `[2.7]`.

**So the referee's diagnosis is confirmed and his proposed repair is not.** "The structure does the
settling and something else must do the erring" — correct. But lag is not that something. On this
model lag can do only two things, and §4 must choose between them:

| what lag can do | what it costs |
|---|---|
| destroy the equilibrium (oscillation) → persistent error, **no lock-in**, and the error is *unsigned* | C3 loses "locks against contrary evidence" |
| leave the equilibrium alone → **lock-in**, and *zero* error | C3 loses the inaccuracy |

`[4.5]`: cells in the whole (gain × delay) sweep showing lock-in **and** error, at `b = 0`:
**0 of 847.** Not few. None.

**And a second result the brief did not ask for, which costs §4 more than item 3 does.** In the
specified model, when the informant is given exactly the self's lag, `max |r_t − o_t|` over the
entire gain grid is **2.220e-16** `[8.6]` — the informant is wrong in precisely the same way, at
every gain. **The whole self–informant asymmetry in this model is lag difference; gain contributes
nothing to it.** Worse for the essay's direction: over positive gain the self/informant error ratio
correlates with gain at **−0.835** `[8.4]` — higher gain makes the self *relatively better*.

**There is a route that works, and it is not lag.** Part 9 (labelled throughout as going beyond the
specification) adds the one thing the referee's own words require and the specified model does not
contain — a criterion the informant reads "in contexts where `r` was not an input". Score both
readers against **capacity** `c = f(0.5, a)`, the behaviour that would be realised at a neutral
self-representation, rather than against realised behaviour `x`. Then, **with no lag and no bias
whatsoever, at a static equilibrium**, `|r* − c|` is **0.000e+00 at zero gain** and rises
monotonically to **0.268** at gain 3 `[9.4] [9.5]`; an informant carrying the *self's own lag*
still beats the self, by a ratio that climbs **1.00 → 13.47** across the gain range `[10.4]`.

That is structural, non-motivational, gain-sorted, present at equilibrium, and compatible with
lock-in. **It is also a different premise from the one the handoff's step 2 names.** The extra
premise §4 needs is not *the self updates late*; it is *the self and the informant are scoring
different criteria* — which is exactly the ambiguity the referee flagged in finding 1(b)'s last
paragraph and which the essay has never resolved.

## 1 · THE MODEL, AND WHY IT IS NOT A REFUSAL

Per packet §6 I had to decide first whether lag and bias are separable here — whether "no bias term"
is a state the model can occupy. **They are, and it is**, so I did not refuse.

```
  x_t     = f(r_t; k, a_t)          f(r;k,a) = 1/(1+exp(-(k*(r-0.5) + a)))
  r_{t+1} = (1-α)·r_t + α·( γ·(x_{t-d} - 0.5) + 0.5 + b + ξ_t )
  o_{t+1} = (1-α_h)·o_t + α_h·( x_{t-d_h} + ξ^h_t )
```

- **f** — representation → fact. Logistic, so S-shaped per Henshel. `f(0.5) = 0.5` when `a = 0`, so
  `r* = 0.5` is always an analytic fixed point and every numerical root-finder can be checked
  against it `[0.4]`.
- **gain** `G = f′(r) = k·f·(1−f)`. Swept as `Gmax = k/4`, its value at the inflection. Local `f′`
  at each equilibrium is what the stability calculation actually uses.
- **lag** — the pair `(α, d)`. `α = 1, d = 0` is no lag.
- **bias** — `b` (additive) and `γ − 1` (sensitivity). `b = 0, γ = 1` is no bias.
- **h** — the informant. Reads the same `x`; never enters `f`, per the brief.

**Separability:** `b` and `γ` enter the *value* `g` returns; `α` and `d` enter only *when* it is
applied. `b = 0, γ = 1` with `α < 1` or `d > 0` is well defined and is what parts 2–8 run.

**Stability** is the referee's `|f′g′| < 1` generalised to hold the delay exactly. Linearising gives
`λ^(d+1) − (1−α)λ^d − αγf′(r*) = 0`; I take `max|λ|` over all roots. At `α = 1, d = 0` this reduces
to `|γf′| < 1`, the referee's condition.

### The instrument checks itself before it is believed — `--part 0`

| check | result |
|---|---|
| `[0.1]` `d=0` stability vs hand-derived `\|(1−α)+αγf′\|`, 400 random cells | max deviation **0.000e+00** |
| `[0.2]` `α=1` stability vs `\|γf′\|^(1/(d+1))`, 400 random cells | max deviation **5.440e-15** |
| `[0.3]` linear verdict vs what the simulation actually does, 300 random cells | agree **233**, disagree **0** (67 boundary cells skipped) |
| `[0.4]` root-finder recovers the analytic `r* = 0.5`, 200 random gains | max distance **0.000e+00** |
| `[0.5]` `simulate()` vs an 8-step trajectory stepped by hand | max diff **0.000e+00** |

**`[0.1]` exists because it caught a real bug in this file.** `max_eig` built the characteristic
polynomial by assigning `coeffs[1]` and `coeffs[-1]`; at `d = 0` the array has length 2, so those
are the same slot and the `(1−α)` term was silently overwritten. **Every `d = 0` stability verdict
in the first run of part 3 was wrong**, and wrong in both directions at once: for `α=0.4, d=0` it
reported unstable intervals `[−3.00,−2.52]` and `[+2.50,+3.00]`, where the truth is `[+1.00,+3.00]`
and *nothing* on the negative side. So it **invented** a negative-gain instability that does not
exist and **hid** the real positive one between +1.00 and +2.50. Found by checking one printed row
against `J = (1−α) + αf′` computed by hand. Fixed at `two_map.py:106-115`, and part 0 now pins it.

## 2 · THE SWEEP RANGES, AND WHY THOSE

Stated here because a range chosen after seeing the result is not a sweep. Every one of these was
fixed by an analytic landmark **before** the first run, except the two marked otherwise.

| parameter | range | why |
|---|---|---|
| gain `Gmax` | **−3.0 … +3.0**, step 0.05 (0.02 in part 3) | `Gmax = 1` is the analytic pitchfork and `\|Gmax\| = 1` the stability boundary; the range covers a full unit either side of both. **Symmetric because the sign of the gain is the thing that decides whether delay can destabilise at all** — and the essay only ever discusses positive gain (believing yourself capable makes you more so). Negative gain is the self-defeating loop, and it is Grunberg–Modigliani's own "suicidal prediction" case. Spot-checked to ±6: the branches saturate, nothing new appears. |
| delay `d` | **0 … 12** in the maps, **0 … 20** in the exhaustive scans | `d = 0` is the no-lag control. Delay-induced instability in a first-order loop appears at small `d` if it appears at all; `d = 20` is far past any boundary at these `α`. |
| learning rate `α` | **0.05, 0.1, 0.2, 0.4, 0.7, 1.0** | `α = 1` is instantaneous update — the other no-lag control. `α = 0.05` gives a relaxation time of 20 steps, comparable to the fastest drive period swept. |

| drive period `T` | **20, 50, 100, 200, 500, 1000** | `T = 20` is comparable to the self's relaxation time (fast); `T ≥ 500` is the **quasi-static control**, where tracking error must vanish, and does `[6.6]`. |

`py essay/sim/two_map.py --part 3` and `--part 4` · **Figs 3, 4**

If lag cannot move an equilibrium, the only remaining route to error without bias is to destroy the
equilibrium. Part 3 asks where it can, exactly.

Read down a column: **a slower self is a more stable self** (small `α` is *harder* to destabilise).

|---|---|

| `[4.2]` | `d = 0`: **no** cell at any gain exceeds 1e-6. `d = 12`: 40 cells do, all with `Gmax ≤ +1.00` |

finite run leaves a residual that reads as persistent error. `[10.1]` shows the residual falling by
a factor of ~200 as `T` goes 30000 → 1000000. Part 4 now runs that survival test itself rather than
leaving it to a later part, and the figure is not written because there is nothing to draw.

the snippet at the end of §11; it imports `simulate` from the module.)* **So where lag alone errs,
it errs symmetrically about the truth** — an oscillation around the fact, not a stable misestimate
of it. §4 cannot get "a belief that you are worse than you are" out of it in either direction.

## 6 · ITEM 2 — lock-in and basin jumps

`py essay/sim/two_map.py --part 5` · **Figs 6, 7**

This item comes out **exactly as the referee predicted**, and it is the half of §4 that survives.

the trajectory in the other basin, by bisection to 1e-6:

| `Gmax` | fixed points | upper stable | `δ*` | distance to the middle unstable point |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.2 | 0.17072, 0.5, 0.82928 | 0.829285 | **0.329285** | 0.329285 |
| 1.5 | 0.07072, 0.5, 0.92928 | 0.929280 | **0.429280** | 0.429280 |
| 2.0 | 0.02125, 0.5, 0.97875 | 0.978752 | **0.478752** | 0.478752 |

| 3.0 | 0.00255, 0.5, 0.99745 | 0.997451 | **0.497451** | 0.497451 |

`δ*` equals the distance to the separatrix to six figures in all five rows — an independent check
that the bisection and the root-finder agree. **Basin depth grows with gain**, which is the
quantitative form of "reports lock against contrary evidence", and it is real.

`[5.4]` hysteresis, ramping the exogenous `a` from −4 to +4 and back over 2 × 40000 steps:

| `Gmax` | jump up at | jump down at | loop width | max single-step \|Δr\| |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.5 | −4.0000 | −0.0013 | *(no loop — see below)* | 0.0027 |
| 1.2 | +0.1375 | −0.1375 | **0.2750** | 0.0209 |
| 2.0 | +1.0861 | −1.0861 | **2.1723** | 0.1075 |
| 3.0 | +2.6260 | −2.6260 | **5.2519** | 0.1722 |

The `Gmax = 0.5` row is **not** a hysteresis loop: below the pitchfork there is one fixed point and
no jump, so "largest single-step change" picks up the steepest part of a smooth curve
(`|Δr| = 0.0027`, two orders below the genuine jumps) and the reported "width" is an artifact of
asking a jump-detector where there is no jump. **I am flagging that row rather than deleting it:**
the detector has no no-jump verdict, which is a defect of my instrument, and the honest reading is
that the loop width is undefined there. The three rows above the pitchfork are genuine and the
width grows monotonically with gain.

**So item 2 delivers.** Lock-in, hysteresis, discontinuous jumps, all from the S-shape, all
gain-graded, none needing a bias. **The half of C3 that the loop supplies is the lock-in.** It is
the *inaccuracy* that the loop does not supply.

from this model, and mild counter-support.**

**THIS GOES BEYOND THE MODEL THE REFEREE SPECIFIED, and I am saying so rather than quietly

exactly this fork and calls either horn fatal. It is not fatal, but the essay has to pick the horn
and defend it, and the horn it must pick is the one it currently gestures at and never states.**

## 10 · WHAT THE MODEL CANNOT REPRESENT ABOUT THE THING IT STANDS FOR

Per the packet's fifth bar. These are limits of the model, not of the run.

1. **One scalar `r`, one scalar `x`.** A person's self-representation is not one number, and the
   essay's own sort is *per-variable*. Nothing here can speak to interactions across traits, which
   is where the interesting clinical cases (the referee's finding 5 on anxiety straddling regimes)
   actually live.
2. **The informant has exactly zero gain, by construction.** The brief specified "no feedback into
   `f`", and I obeyed it. But the referee's finding 6 is that this is *false of the informants in
   Vazire's data* — friends are the outsiders with the highest gain on you. A model in which `h`
   feeds back into `f` is a different and harder model, and every asymmetry reported in §7 would
   need re-deriving in it. **§7's results are an upper bound on the informant's advantage.**
3. **`c = f(0.5, a)` is a stipulation, not a measurement.** Part 9's whole result depends on there
   being a fact about what you would do at a neutral self-representation. In a real person that
   counterfactual may not be well defined, and if it is not, part 9's error is not an error.
   **This is the extension's soft spot and the essay should say so rather than let a referee find
   it.**
4. **Discrete time, one delay, one timescale.** Real update lags are distributed, not a single `d`.
   A distributed lag is a low-pass filter and would smooth the oscillations in §5; the *equilibrium*
   results are untouched by this, since no lag structure of any kind enters the fixed-point
   equation.
5. **Nothing here is evidence about persons.** It is a consistency check on an argument. It can show
   that a conclusion does not follow from a premise set — which is what it did — but it cannot show
   that any human self-report behaves this way. **The essay must not cite these numbers as findings
   about self-knowledge.** They are findings about the model.
6. **`f` is monotone.** Non-monotone reaction functions (believing yourself very capable makes you
   complacent past a threshold) would give richer dynamics, including the negative-gain regime
   arising *locally* on a variable whose gain is positive elsewhere. Not swept.
7. **No estimation.** The self here does not infer; it low-passes. Any real account in which the
   self runs an inference — and so can be *right about what it cannot observe* — is outside this.
