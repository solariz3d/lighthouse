## A. STRUCTURAL — the P1–C3 block

### 1. The informant has no route to "capacity" that the self lacks. P4 and P5 together either give no asymmetry or collapse into the bias account the essay says it is not.

(a) "the self has no evidence about capacity, because every piece of evidence it receives is its own realised behaviour, which its representation helped produce. A second person can see the same individual in settings where that representation was not in play."

(b) Every piece of evidence the *informant* receives is also the person's realised behaviour, produced by the same loop. The only thing that could put the informant closer to f(0) is the existence of settings where the self-representation is switched off and the informant's sampling of them. But the self lived through those settings too and observed its own behaviour there; the self's evidence set is a superset of the informant's. Not attending to who you are while acting does not delete the memory of what you did. So P5's second clause is false as an access claim. To rescue the asymmetry you need a further premise — that the self's *aggregation* of its own behaviour is dominated by its self-concept (it reads the concept back rather than the record) or by belief-engaged episodes — and that premise is a claim about self-concept-driven bias in self-perception, i.e. Swann's self-verification and the cognitive side of Vazire, not a new criterion. Dilemma: no asymmetry, or the old asymmetry. The block does not close this and the prose does not notice it.

Worse, the self has a piece of evidence no informant has: a within-person time series. Anyone whose self-belief has changed across a life has observed their own behaviour at several values of r, which is exactly the data needed to estimate the reaction function and therefore f(0). "The self has no evidence about capacity" is not merely undefended; on the essay's own model the self is the only observer positioned to have it.

(c) Either (i) state the aggregation premise explicitly as P5′, defend it, and concede that it is the same mechanism Swann and Vazire use, so that what is new is only the *criterion* the bias is scored against; or (ii) drop the informant altogether and keep the honest core: in the looped regime there is a second quantity, and self-report is silent about it. That claim survives; the informant advantage does not.

### 2. Vazire's own data disconfirm the essay's stated prediction on the paradigm looped variable, and the essay reports the opposite.

(a) "A motivational account predicts self-error against realised behaviour … The loop account predicts none there." And: "C3 recovers it — … intellect is looped, since believing yourself capable alters what you attempt."

(b) Vazire 2010 scored intellect against an IQ test (checked: the study's criteria included an intelligence test and a leaderless group discussion). Taking a test is realised behaviour, and one where the self-belief is engaged if it is engaged anywhere — this is what the stereotype-threat and self-efficacy literatures are about. The self was *worse* than friends against that criterion. That is self-error against realised behaviour on a looped variable, which the loop account says should be zero. So on the essay's own criterion distinction, the intellect cell is a disconfirmation, not a recovery. The only escape is to reclassify the test as a "capacity criterion", which would concede that "conditions where the self-belief is not engaged" cannot be told apart from ordinary performance and so that C3 is not operationalised.

(c) Confront it. Either argue that an IQ test is a non-engaged setting (and say why, given the literature), or concede that Vazire's intellect finding is evidence against the pure loop account and that the essay's prediction is a difference-in-criteria to be tested, not a pattern already recovered. Do not keep "recovers it".

### 3. "Capacity" is not a well-defined quantity as stated; the essay slides between three different ones.

(a) "its capacity, the range it could meet if its belief about itself were not one of the makers" (abstract); "what the process would do at a neutral self-representation" (§4, P4); "blind to what it could do".

(b) Three quantities: (i) f(0), behaviour at a *neutral* representation; (ii) the *range*, i.e. the set of behaviours reachable across all r; (iii) *what it could do*, which in English means the maximum. These are different, the informant reads none of them, and only (i) is what the model computes. Further problems with (i): the neutral point is a choice of origin, and the gap f(r*) − f(0) depends on where r* sits, not only on gain; when r* is *above* neutral, realised behaviour exceeds "capacity", so the belief has *opened* a range, not closed one, and the essay's rhetoric ("a range your own belief had closed") assumes a sign the model does not supply. "Grows monotonically with gain, with nothing else in the system" is true of a linear map x = a + k·r and not in general; with the discontinuous, multi-equilibrium reaction functions the essay endorses via Henshel one paragraph earlier, the gap can be non-monotone in gain and can be zero at high gain if the locked-in equilibrium happens to sit at f(0). The essay cannot both lean on Henshel for lock-in and on the linear model for monotonicity.

(c) Pick one quantity, name it something other than "capacity", define the neutral point (e.g. the behaviour under an uninformative self-belief, and say what that is for a standing disposition), state the monotonicity claim as a property of a named model class, and drop "what it could do" unless you mean the maximum and are prepared to show the informant reads the maximum.

### 4. The extension to standing dispositions destroys the operationalisation of the capacity criterion.

(a) "extended from the occurrent brain-processes he had in view to standing dispositions"; then C3: "performance in conditions where the self-belief is not engaged"; then P5: "where the person was not attending to who they are".

(b) A standing disposition is precisely a belief that acts without being attended to. "I am someone who finishes things" does its causal work while the person is thinking about the task, not about themselves. So "not attending to who you are" does not set the gain to zero, and there is no natural population of settings where a standing self-belief is "not engaged". The two halves of the originality claim — standing dispositions, and a checkable informant prediction — pull against each other. And the two test variables offered ("a resolution — to call people back, to be up by seven") are *resolutions*, i.e. intentions, which the essay's own taxonomy puts in the made regime where the informant is derivative. The one worked example of the test lives in the wrong cell.

(c) Either restrict the prediction to occurrent, prime-able self-beliefs (where "engaged/not engaged" can be manipulated) and drop "standing dispositions" from the originality claim, or give an operationalisation for standing dispositions (implicit measures? incidental behaviour coded blind? behaviour before the self-concept formed?) and name it in C3. Replace the resolution example with a genuinely looped, low-evaluativeness trait self-concept.

### 5. P5 is false of real informants, and the essay's own coda concedes it.

(a) P5: "A second self's representation enters the first's process only through what it expresses"; §4: "nothing they believe about you feeds your loop except what they say." Then §6: "two coupled selves have fixed points neither has alone."

(b) Teacher-expectancy (Rosenthal and Jacobson), behavioural confirmation (Snyder, Tanke and Berscheid), Swann's own work on partners negotiating identity, and Hacking's looping — which runs through institutions and practices, not through what anyone says — all show that an informant's representation is a *standing cause* on the target through channels the informant does not control or notice. Friends — Vazire's informants — are makers. So the informant is a second constituent of the loop, "accurate about what the pair does", and reads no closer to f(0) than the self. The last section's "fixed points neither has alone" is exactly this and is stated as a consequence rather than the refutation of a premise. The abstract says the second self's representation "does not feed the loop"; P5 says it feeds it through expression; §6 says the two are coupled. Three incompatible statements of the same premise.

(c) Restate P5 as a comparative: the informant's gain on the target is *lower* than the self's on the same variable. That is defensible for strangers, doubtful for friends, and gives you a prediction that actually distinguishes stranger from friend informants — which Vazire's design can test and which the current essay throws away.

### 6. The made regime is formally backwards, and Moran is misdescribed inside a single paragraph.

(a) "Where the representation constitutes the fact … the gain is total and the fixed point is unique: to represent it is to make it so." And: "My self-ascription does not track those facts, it makes them. Constitutivists … hold that this is what first-person authority consists in; Moran reaches the same cell by deliberation and rejects the constitutive reading."

(b) If representing r makes the fact r, then *every* r is a fixed point of represent-then-read — maximal multiplicity, not uniqueness. Uniqueness is what you get at *zero* gain (the fact fixes the reading). The two limiting cases have been swapped. Separately, a constitutive relation is not a large derivative; identity is not gain = 1 on a scale with 0.3 on it. Putting Moran in the cell as its authority and then in the next sentence saying he rejects the reading that defines the cell is a contradiction, and "my self-ascription makes them" is the constitutivism Moran explicitly argues against — his authority comes from deliberation settling the question, with the avowal expressing the result. Finally, estranged attitudes are not "near-zero gain": rumination on one's resentment feeds it. Moran's axis (deliberative vs. theoretical stance) is orthogonal to gain, and the essay maps one onto the other.

(c) Rewrite the made cell as: the representation and the fact are not two variables, so gain is undefined rather than total; self-authority there has a different source (Moran's), and the informant is derivative for that reason. Drop "unique". Drop the Moran-as-zero-gain gloss on estrangement or argue for it.

### 7. P1 and P2 do no work, and P2 contradicts the read regime.

(a) "so every self-representation has some gain … On a view in which the self-model observes the process from somewhere, the gain is zero everywhere; that is what P1 contributes." Then: "Where the gain is zero — a sensation, the anxiety you have right now, a heart rate."

(b) No one holds that self-beliefs have zero causal effect on all self-variables; Merton's self-fulfilling prophecy predates MacKay. So P1 is not needed to license nonzero gain, and the attractor view contributes nothing to the argument from P2 onward — which the essay itself half-concedes in §3 ("the argument of the next section does not depend on it"). That makes §§1–3 (roughly 2,000 words) inert with respect to the philosophical content the competition grades. And P2's "every self-representation has some gain" is flatly contradicted by the first regime.

Also: C1's "accurate at equilibrium" is definitional once equilibrium is *defined* as reading = behaviour; "I checked this in a minimal two-map model … no lag, delay or learning rate produces any self-error at equilibrium" is a check on a tautology and the model is not specified in the essay. Judges will read an unspecified simulation as rhetoric.

(c) Replace "every self-representation has some gain" with "gain is not zero by fiat and varies by variable". Either give the two maps in one line (x = a + k·r; r′ = x, say) or cut the sentence. Then decide whether §§1–3 earn their words (see E).

### 8. C3 does not follow from C2, and C2 contradicts C3 on the read regime.

(a) C2: "the self is the better instrument in the read and made regimes." C3: "On read variables neither differs." C3 also: "informant error does not [rise with gain]", "(From C2.)"

(b) C2 says the *margin* grows with gain; that is consistent with both readers' error rising, the self's faster. A zero slope for the informant is not derived anywhere. "At fixed evaluativeness" appears in C3 without any premise about evaluativeness in the block — it is a control on Vazire's alternative, not a consequence. And C2's blanket "self better in the read regime" is contradicted both by C3 and by the essay's own gloss on Vazire (visible traits: everyone equal), which forces the admission that observability, not gain, decides the read cell — i.e. Vazire's first axis, unchanged.

(c) State C3 as a difference-in-slopes prediction and mark the evaluativeness control as a control. Amend C2: in the read regime the self is better *only where observability is low*, which is Vazire's, not yours.

### 9. Henshel is cited for the wrong consequence.

(a) "Grunberg and Modigliani (1954) and Simon (1954) showed such a fixed point exists when the reaction function is continuous; Henshel (1995) argued that for persons it usually is not, so there may be several, and a self can lock into one."

(b) Brouwer gives existence, not uniqueness; multiple fixed points are compatible with continuity. Discontinuity threatens *existence*, not uniqueness. Henshel's discontinuity therefore licenses "there may be none", not "there may be several". The inference is reversed.

(c) "…exists when the reaction function is continuous, and need not be unique; Henshel argued that for persons it is often discontinuous, so it need not exist at all — and where several exist a self can lock into one."

### 10. The originality claim is stated against the wrong neighbours.

(a) "MacKay's interference condition made into a single quantity, extended … to standing dispositions, given a third cell he did not have, and turned into a prediction about self-report against an informant that can be checked."

(b) Uncited, and the judges will know them: Bandura's self-efficacy (1977; 1997) *is* the thesis that belief in capability changes what is attempted and achieved, with the belief–performance gain measured for forty years and explicitly distinguished from capability; Velleman (*Practical Reflection*, 1989; "Epistemic Freedom", 1989) put the constitutive cell beside MacKay-style self-fulfilling expectation, in philosophy, decades ago — the "third cell he did not have" has been in the literature since then; Ismael (*The Situated Self*, 2007; "Decision and the Open Future", 2011) works MacKay's indeterminacy directly into a self-model account of agency; Rummens and Cuypers (2010, *Erkenntnis*) formalise the MacKay/Scriven paradox of predictability; Jussim's reflection–construction model (1991) is exactly the decomposition of a perceiver's rating into accuracy and self-fulfilling construction that P5 needs and denies; Funder's Realistic Accuracy Model (1995), which SOKA is built on, already frames informant advantage as availability of relevant behaviour. The specific combination — a counterfactual-at-neutral-belief quantity as a scoring criterion for self–other asymmetry — may be new, but the essay claims the parts, and the parts are not. Fitelson would send it back for Bandura alone.

(c) One paragraph: cite Bandura, Velleman, Ismael, Jussim; narrow the claim to the criterion (scoring self- and informant-report against a counterfactual rather than a realised criterion) and nothing else.

## B. OBJECTIONS a strong referee raises that the essay does not answer

**The best one — the evidence-symmetry dilemma (finding 1).** The informant reads the same realised behaviour the self does. Either there are no settings where a standing self-belief is switched off (finding 4), in which case both readers are equally blind to f(0) and there is no asymmetry; or there are, in which case the self observed them too and has strictly more data, including the within-person variation in r that estimates the reaction function. The only way the informant wins is if the self's report is a readout of its self-concept rather than of its behavioural record — which is self-verification, a motivational-cum-cognitive account, and then the essay's "parts from them by criterion" is a relabelling. A judge will put it in one sentence: *what does the informant see that the person did not?* The essay has no answer.

Others, in descending order:

- **The informant is a maker (finding 5).** Friends' expectations construct as well as reflect. The essay's own coda says so.
- **"Neither differs against realised behaviour" is already refuted by Vazire's IQ-test cell (finding 2).**
- **What is gain for a categorical self-concept?** "I am a finisher" is not a scalar; Henshel's discontinuities are the norm, and the essay has no account of the derivative it needs across a threshold.
- **Why the zero slope for informants?** A SOKA theorist can absorb the entire predicted pattern by adding "observability of the counterfactual" to her availability axis; the essay does not show that its prediction is one SOKA cannot make.
- **Occurrent read-regime variables are not zero-gain.** Attending to your anxiety changes it; attending to your heart rate changes it (biofeedback). The regime the essay uses as its anchor for gain = 0 is one where gain is measurably nonzero. The "read" cell is an idealisation presented as a datum.

## C. FACTUAL and CITATION errors

### 11. Dumitrescu et al. 2022 — author list is wrong.

(a) "Dumitrescu, P. T., Bohnet, J. G., Gaebler, J. P., Hankin, A., Gresh, D., Vasilyev, A., Potter, A. C., et al."

(b) Checked against the Nature record (s41586-022-04853-4), ADS and OSTI: the authors are Dumitrescu, Bohnet, Gaebler, Hankin, Hayes, Kumar, Neyenhuis, Vasseur, Potter. "Gresh" and "Vasilyev" are not authors; Hayes, Kumar, Neyenhuis and Vasseur are missing. Two invented names in an essay flagged as AI-authored is the single most damaging thing a checking judge could find.

(c) Replace with the correct nine-author list. Audit every other entry the same way before submission.

### 12. The abstract's "for the same reason" is contradicted by §2, and the mechanism given fits neither case.

(a) Abstract: "converging on aperiodicity for the same reason". §2: "What converges is not the constant … It is the kind of solution … Anything that repeats has a frequency, and anything with a frequency can be matched, driven, shadowed or predicted by something that finds it."

(b) The sunflower's aperiodicity is spatial: an irrational rotation number gives even packing. The ion trap's is temporal: a quasi-periodic drive gives an emergent symmetry that protects edge modes (a dynamical topological phase). Neither is "armour against being found"; the sunflower is not defending against anything. §2 correctly retreats to "the same class of answer"; the abstract still says "same reason". A physicist judge reads the abstract.

(c) Abstract: "converging on aperiodicity as a class of solution" — and cut the "can be matched, driven, shadowed" sentence, which is not the mechanism of either system.

### 13. The attractor vocabulary in the "first refinement" conflates three things.

(a) "I will not say strange, which requires a sensitivity to initial conditions … what has been shown, in physiology, is fractal variability, and that is the property I mean."

(b) "Strange" means fractal *geometry* of the attractor in phase space; sensitivity to initial conditions is what makes an attractor *chaotic*; strange non-chaotic attractors exist (Grebogi, Ott, Pelikan and Yorke 1984). Goldberger's "fractal dynamics" is scale-invariant *temporal correlation* in a time series (1/f-like), which is a third thing and does not by itself say anything about the geometry of an attractor. And "aperiodic attractor" includes quasi-periodic tori, which are not what the essay wants. The refinement uses the right-sounding words for the wrong distinctions.

(c) Say: the self is bounded, non-repeating, and shows long-range correlated variability across scales; drop "strange", "aperiodic attractor" as a technical term, and the sentence about what "strange" requires.

### 14. Swann — the wrong citation for the finding.

(a) "Swann (1983) showed people who saw themselves as submissive and were told they were dominant became more submissive."

(b) That experiment is Swann and Hill (1982, *JPSP* 43, 59–66). Swann 1983 is the review chapter.

(c) Cite Swann and Hill 1982 for the finding; keep Swann 1983 for the theory if wanted.

### 15. MacKay — attribution of degrees and the hair/teeth example is ambiguous; quotations unpaged.

(a) "Not all such interference nullifies the statement; it comes in degrees. One's hair and teeth are unaffected by what one believes about them; one's anger is not."

(b) The paragraph is in MacKay's voice and this reads as his; if it is the essay's illustration, say so; if it is MacKay's, page it. The three direct quotations carry no page numbers in a 10-page article. Fitelson will want to check the sentence "this section is built on".

(c) Page every quotation; mark the example as yours or his.

### 16. Douady and Couder — overstated.

(a) "each new primordium settling into the widest gap left by the last (Douady and Couder 1992) … the golden angle is where the dynamics settles."

(b) The golden angle is the attractor only in the limit of small plastochron ratio; at larger values the same dynamics settles into distichous (180°) and other modes through a bifurcation sequence, which is the paper's actual result. "Widest gap" is Hofmeister's rule, which D&C implemented via repulsion. Minor, but it is the essay's exemplar of "where the dynamics settles".

(c) "…the golden angle is where the dynamics settles as growth slows" and cite Hofmeister for the rule if you keep it.

### 17. "The state was not [stored]" overnight is not true of brains.

(a) "the parameters were stored, the synapses held — but the state was not, and the process has to resume and return to its attractor."

(b) Sleep does not reset dynamical state; mood, working memory residue, consolidation and circadian phase all carry through. This is the only motivation given for "re-arrived at", and a judge who knows any neuroscience will read it as figurative. §3's "no bottom" has the same shape: a field being described by equations does not make it the fixed point of any process, and by the essay's own stored-parameter/regenerated-state distinction the couplings and masses are exactly a bottom of stored parameters. The author says §3 is not load-bearing; the abstract nevertheless leads with it.

(c) Weaken to "much of the state", or drop the morning image and let P1 be a stipulation.

### 18. Small checks.

- Hacking 1995: verify page range (351–383 vs 351–394).
- "cracks … which is the branching network drawn backwards": cracks propagate outward from a point like everything else in the list; the phrase does not say anything.
- "spirals, where something grows at a constant rate about a centre": constant *proportional* rate gives the logarithmic spiral; constant linear rate gives Archimedes'. The essay means the former.

## E. What is good, score, the one change

What must not be touched: the move from MacKay's "not wrong, only indeterminate" to *accurate about the wrong quantity* — that in a loop the self's report can be exactly right about realised behaviour and silent about a counterfactual, and that a rival account is to be separated by *criterion* rather than by the sign of the error. That is a precise, defensible, original-enough claim, and the essay's refusal to let the sign do the work (the Swann concession) is the kind of honesty judges reward. Keep it, keep the three-regime sorting as a heuristic, keep the falsifiers paragraph.

Score as a competition entry: **5/10.** The good claim is real but is surrounded by a premise (P5) the essay itself contradicts, a criterion ("capacity") that changes meaning three times, a data section that reports a disconfirmation as a recovery, a fabricated author list, and two thousand words of morphology and self-reference that the content rules do not grade and that a hostile judge will read as decoration.

The one change that would raise it most: cut §§1, 3, 5 and 6 to a page between them, and spend the freed 2,000 words on the informant premise — state exactly what the second reader sees that the first did not (or concede that nothing, and re-scope the claim to the second quantity alone), confront Vazire's IQ-test cell head-on, and cite Bandura, Velleman, Ismael and Jussim as the neighbours the claim is narrower than. That turns a decorated essay with a good idea inside it into an argument.
