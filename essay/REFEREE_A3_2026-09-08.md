## A. STRUCTURAL

### 1. The loop, as modelled, cannot produce self-error at equilibrium — so C2 and C3 do not follow from P1–P4.

(a) *"the representation is accurate only at a fixed point of the function that runs from representation to fact"* (§4); *"On looped variables, a self's reports lock against contrary evidence ... the second self is the better instrument in the looped regime"* (C2, C3).

(b) Take the essay's own model. Let r be the self-representation and x = f(r) the fact. The essay defines accuracy as r = f(r), a fixed point. But then **at any fixed point the self is accurate by definition**, and an informant reading x reads the same value. The regime's two structural features pull in opposite directions:
- *Several fixed points* (Henshel) gives lock-in — but each fixed point is an accurate self-report. A person at the "unflattering" equilibrium believes they finish nothing and, at that equilibrium, finishes nothing. That is not "a belief that you are worse than you are"; it is a belief that is exactly right. The informant has no advantage.
- *No fixed point* gives permanent self-error (r ≠ f(r) everywhere) — but a loop with no fixed point does not "settle" or "lock"; it cycles or wanders. "A loop of that kind does not track; it settles" is the opposite of what the no-fixed-point case delivers.

C3 needs lock-in **and** inaccuracy on the same variable at the same time, which the model cannot supply. The only way to get a stably *wrong* self-report is an extra premise about the updating map g (evidence → r): that it is sticky, biased, or blind to some of the evidence the informant sees. That is a premise about the self's belief formation — precisely the motivational/cognitive kind of premise the essay claims to have replaced with structure. The "structural, not motivational" contrast is therefore unearned: the structure does the settling; something else does the erring.

Note also that the essay never says what "the criterion" the informant tracks *is*. If it is the realised behaviour f(r), the self is accurate at equilibrium (above). If it is an underlying capacity that r does not move ("what you actually do is also fixed by history, capacity"), then the gain on the criterion is ~0 and the variable belongs to the read regime. Either way the looped regime collapses.

(c) Fix: write the two maps explicitly — f: r ↦ x and g: x ↦ r′ — define gain as f′, and use the standard stability condition |f′·g′| < 1 for a stable equilibrium. Then derive, separately: (i) with a sigmoidal f (thresholds, à la Henshel, but continuity is not even needed — a continuous S-shaped reaction function already gives multiple equilibria, hysteresis and jumps), lock-in and discontinuous change follow; (ii) self-error requires stating what the informant reads that the self does not, e.g. the informant observes x across contexts where r was not an input, or g is slow relative to f. Say which extra premise you are adding and why it is not motivational. Without this, C2/C3 are asserted, not derived.

### 2. P1 is idle. "Gain" does not come from the attractor view; it comes from mental causation, which every rival view has.

(a) *"On a view in which the self-model observes the process from somewhere, that amount is zero everywhere; that is what the attractor view adds, and everything below follows from it."*

(b) False. Any view on which beliefs cause behaviour — dualist, functionalist, folk — gives self-beliefs a causal effect on their objects. Merton's self-fulfilling prophecy (1948), the direct ancestor of Grunberg–Modigliani and Simon, needed no ontology of selves at all. So P2 does not need P1, and nothing in §§1–3 (~2,300 words, over 40% of the essay: morphology, fixed points, the sunflower, the ion trap, structural realism) is a premise of the block the essay says it should be judged on. The essay half-concedes this for structural realism ("The argument of the next section does not depend on it"); the same is true of the other two sections. A judge will notice that the "central" argument begins at line 63 and that the first half is scene-setting for a conclusion it does not entail.

(c) Fix: either show something in P2–C3 that requires P1 (e.g. that on the attractor view gain is *ubiquitous and unavoidable*, whereas on an observer view it is incidental — then argue for ubiquity), or cut §§1–3 to a page and spend the words on finding 1.

### 3. The originality claim ignores the nearest predecessors, and one of them refutes a stated prediction.

(a) *"What none of them supplies, and what the attractor view does, is a single quantity that sorts all of these cases and predicts the sorting."*

(b) Uncited, and each closer to the thesis than Hofstadter/Dennett/Parfit:
- **Hacking** (1995, 1999), looping effects of human kinds / interactive kinds — a representation of persons changing the persons represented is his named phenomenon; the essay's "looped regime" is his word.
- **D. M. MacKay** (1960, *Mind*, "On the logical indeterminacy of a free choice"; 1967) — a self-representation that alters the state it represents, argued with exactly the fixed-point structure, a decade before Hofstadter. **Ismael** (2007 *The Situated Self*; 2016) rebuilds this as a self-modelling dynamical system and draws the self-prediction limit.
- **McGeer** (1996, *J. Phil.*) — self-knowledge as self-regulation: avowals are made true by the agent's subsequent self-governance. That is the made/looped distinction, twenty years earlier.
- **Coliva** (2016, *The Varieties of Self-Knowledge*) — the direct rival: pluralism, self-knowledge sorted by attitude type with different epistemologies per type. An essay claiming one scalar sorts everything must say why pluralism is worse.
- **Wright** (1998) and **Bilgrami** (2006) — the constitutive view of first-person authority. The "made regime" is theirs; see finding 7 on why it is not Moran's.
- **Schwitzgebel** (2008) — introspection is unreliable *on sensations and current emotions*, i.e. on the essay's read regime, where P3 says the self "reads it as well as its channel allows."
- **Swann's self-verification theory** (1983 onward) — a *motivational* account on which people with negative self-views seek and retain confirming evidence. This directly falsifies *"A motivational account has no room for a stable, evidence-proof belief that you are worse than you are."* Beck's schema model of depression is a second counterexample. The essay's second "divergent prediction" therefore does not divide loop accounts from motivational accounts; it divides them from Vazire's particular self-enhancement mechanism.
- **Rosenthal & Jacobson** (1968) and the expectancy literature — see finding 6.

(c) Fix: cite and position. The residual novelty — a scalar gain, a three-way sort, and an informant prediction that diverges from SOKA — is real but small, and it survives only if the essay says what it adds over Hacking + MacKay + SOKA. Drop the "no motivational account" claim; replace with "Vazire's self-enhancement mechanism is one-signed."

### 4. "Gain" is not a single quantity, and it is never measured independently of what it predicts.

(a) *"Where the representation constitutes the fact ... the gain is total"*; *"intellect is looped, since believing yourself capable alters what you attempt."*

(b) Two problems. First, constitution is not the limit of causal influence. Moran's authority over what I believe about the weather is not "gain = 1"; it is not a causal relation from representation to fact at all. So the sort uses *two* things — a magnitude of causal gain and a constitutive/causal distinction — and "a single quantity" is false as stated. "Total" and P4's "intermittent" are never defined. Second, no measure of gain is given. Traits are assigned to regimes by intuition after the data are in: anxiety is read because Vazire found the self wins there; intellect is looped because friends win there. Until gain can be estimated for a trait without looking at the self–informant result, C3 is unfalsifiable and the "recovery" of Vazire is post hoc.

(c) Fix: define gain operationally as the causal effect of an exogenous shift in self-belief on the criterion (false-feedback and self-efficacy manipulation paradigms already exist), classify traits before running the informant comparison, and treat constitution as a separate, non-scalar category rather than a value of gain.

### 5. The three regimes are not exclusive, and the essay's own examples straddle them.

(a) *"Where the gain is zero — a sensation, the anxiety you have right now, a heart rate — representing the state does not move it."* … *"Rumination is another [basin]."*

(b) Anxiety is the textbook looped variable: believing you are anxious raises anxiety (anxiety sensitivity; Wells's metacognitive model of worry). Rumination — the essay's paradigm looped fixed point — is an anxiety/depression phenomenon. So the same trait is "read" when recovering Vazire's first cell and "looped" when supplying the essay's sharpest prediction. Attending to heart rate changes heart rate (panic, interoceptive attention). "Morning person" is largely chronotype: low gain, high observability — a bad example for a looped, informant-advantage cell. Gain is a continuum; the essay names three points on it and then places every actual trait at one of the points.

(c) Fix: admit the continuum; state the prediction as monotone in gain (informant advantage grows with gain, bounded by observability), which is both more honest and more testable than three bins. Pick looped non-evaluative examples where gain is plausibly large and observability moderate (e.g. procrastination, punctuality).

### 6. P4 is false for the informants that matter.

(a) *"A second self's representation of the first has zero or intermittent gain on the first's variables — it enters the first's process only through what it expresses."*

(b) "Only through what it expresses" is the whole channel. Teacher expectancy (Pygmalion), labelling, stereotype threat, and Hacking's looping are all documented, large causal effects of a second person's representation on the first's variable. Vazire's informants are *friends* — the outsiders with the highest gain on you. So the asymmetry in C2 is one of degree (whose gain is smaller), not of shape ("in every regime it is a reading"), and the essay never argues the comparative claim.

(c) Fix: replace P4 with "a second self's gain on the variable is typically smaller than the first's, because it acts through a lossy channel," and then say what "typically" rests on. Or restrict the informant to strangers — but then Vazire's data (friends beat strangers on intellect) no longer supports you.

### 7. Moran is mis-mapped twice.

(a) *"my self-ascription does not track the fact, it makes it"*; *"Call this the made regime; it is Moran's"*; *"Moran's alienation ... is the self in the looped regime discovering it is not in the made one."*

(b) (From memory, but I am fairly confident.) Moran grounds first-person authority in the deliberative stance and rational agency, and explicitly distances his view from constitutive accounts on which avowal *makes* the attitude so (he discusses Wright's extension-determining view critically). The "made" gloss belongs to Wright/Bilgrami, or to Velleman's self-fulfilling intention — which the essay cites in the same sentence and then attributes to Moran. Second, Moran's estranged attitudes — the phobia or resentment one disavows on reflection — are precisely the ones deliberation *cannot move*: the representation has near-zero gain on them. On the essay's own scheme that is the read regime, not the looped one. The sentence mapping alienation to the looped regime gets the sign of the gain wrong.

(c) Fix: attribute the made regime to constitutivists and Velleman; say Moran's authority is *deliberative*, which is a different route to the same cell; re-sort estrangement into the read regime, where the essay's "third-person stance on ourselves" gloss actually fits.

### 8. "Fixed point" equivocates between §2 and §4, and the equivocation produces a contradiction about health.

(a) §2: *"I am extending them: a tree is not literally the fixed point of a map."* §4: *"The mathematical name for that is an aperiodic attractor ... the loss of that structure in either direction — toward lockstep, or toward randomness — is a marker of disease."* C3: *"a self's reports lock against contrary evidence."*

(b) §2 uses "fixed point" broadly (any attractor). P3 invokes literal fixed-point theorems (Brouwer via Grunberg–Modigliani). §4 says the healthy self is aperiodic, never twice in the same state, and that stuckness is pathology. C3 then predicts lock-in — a stable fixed point of self-report — as the *normal* condition of looped variables. So lock-in is either the disease (Goldberger) or the default (C3). Further, "rumination as a fixed point" is really a repeating *thought*, a limit cycle, which is the periodic end the essay calls dangerous — not a fixed point of the representation→fact map. And Goldberger's fractal heart-rate variability is a property of a time series that stochastic processes without any attractor also produce; it cannot underwrite "aperiodic attractor" as a claim about persons.

(c) Fix: use "attractor" in §2 and reserve "fixed point" for P3; state that lock-in on a *variable* is compatible with aperiodicity of the *state*; drop the rumination-as-fixed-point line or reclassify it as a cycle.

### 9. The two divergent predictions and their testability.

(a) *"Show that self–informant discrepancies on looped variables are one-signed — all flattering — and the motivational account suffices."*

(b) No data set is all-flattering; that falsifier cannot fire. The first prediction (informant advantage on non-evaluative looped variables) is the genuine divergence from SOKA — SOKA predicts parity on high-observability, low-evaluativeness traits — but it depends on finding 4 (an independent gain measure). "Lock-in" vs noise: self-report test–retest reliabilities of 0.7–0.8 already look like stability; distinguishing lock from reliability requires a criterion series and a comparison of self-report vs informant *responsiveness to criterion change*, which the essay does not specify. And informants also update sluggishly — they see you rarely — so "an informant's reports track the criterion continuously" is not a safe assumption on the informant side either.

(c) Fix: state the statistic. E.g. in a multi-wave panel with self, informant and criterion measures: regress change in self-report and change in informant report on change in criterion; the loop account predicts a smaller slope and a heavier-tailed change distribution for self-report on high-gain traits, symmetric in sign. Replace "one-signed" with "the negative-residual tail is less stable across waves than the positive tail."

## B. THE BEST UNANSWERED OBJECTION

Finding 1, stated as a judge would: *"On your model, accuracy is r = f(r). At every equilibrium the self is right. Where does the self-error come from?"* The essay's answer would have to be a premise about how the self updates — and then the account is not structural, it is structural-plus-a-bias, and the bias is doing the work Vazire's motive did. Everything the essay claims as its contribution (the informant advantage, the two-sided error, the divergence from SOKA) hangs on the answer, and none is given.

## C. FACTUAL / CITATION

### 10. The ion-trap experiment is not an instance of the thesis it is offered as the sharpest instance of.

(a) *"Here is the sharpest instance I know of the first of those three — that a form belongs to the process and not to the medium."* … *"Two systems ... reached for the same class of answer."*

(b) The Fibonacci pulse train was *chosen by the experimenters* as an input; the ions settled into nothing. There is no process of which the aperiodic drive is the attractor. The sunflower is a genuine attractor (Douady & Couder); the ion trap is an engineered analogue, and "reached for" is false of it. Two smaller points: the abstract says the two converge "for the same reason" while §2 says "What converges is not the constant" — but the Fibonacci drive's quasi-periodicity *is* the golden ratio's irrationality (the Fibonacci chain is the φ-quasicrystal), so disowning the constant while keeping the aperiodicity is confused. "In July 2022 a group of physicists drove a quantum computer" — the paper appeared online in June 2022 (the print issue is July, from memory), the experiment was earlier, and the title says "quantum simulator." The physics gloss (emergent symmetry protecting edge qubits; ~1.5 s periodic vs ~5.5 s quasi-periodic) is acceptable.

(c) Fix: keep the sunflower; either drop the ion trap or present it as a designed analogue that borrows the sunflower's number-theoretic reason (badly-approximable φ), which is a nicer point than the one made.

### 11. Ontic structural realism is not process ontology.

(a) *"What would refute the use I make of the view is a bottom: something that persists and is not the fixed point of any process. Nobody has found one."* … *"That is what a spiral is, what a field is."*

(b) OSR says there are no intrinsic natures below structure; it does not say everything persisting is regenerated by a process. A field is not the fixed point of anything; a stable particle persists by conservation law, not by being re-made. So a "bottom" in the essay's sense arguably has been found: the electron. The slide from "structure, not stuff" to "process, not stuff" is unargued. Also, Ladyman & Ross do build on Dennett's real patterns — that part is correct.

(c) Fix: claim only what OSR gives ("pattern, not substance") and drop "what a field is" from §6.

### 12. Smaller checks.
- Douady & Couder 1992 (PRL 68, 2098): correct; the gloss "widest gap left by the last" is fair. "Of all angles, the golden angle spreads successive elements most evenly" is stronger than the result (optimality is in a specific gap-ratio sense); minor.
- Goldberger et al. 2002: correct citation and correct claim (loss of fractal correlation toward either periodicity or randomness). The essay says "in either direction" and then only explains one.
- Grunberg & Modigliani 1954, Simon 1954: correct. The theorem also needs a bounded (compact convex) outcome set, not continuity alone; minor.
- Henshel 1995: title and venue plausible. "Henshel showed that for persons it usually is not" — he *argued*, about social aggregates reacting to *public* predictions; the transfer to one individual's self-belief is a substantive step the essay does not make.
- Ryle 1949, ch. VI §7: correct.
- Vazire 2010: N = 165, neuroticism/extraversion/intellect design, self best on neuroticism, friends best on intellect, parity on extraversion — correctly reported. "Anxiety" for the neuroticism cell is acceptable.
- Text credits "Varela" alone; the reference is Maturana & Varela 1980.
- Marks-Tarlow 1999, Vacariu 2008, Chakravartty 2003, Worrall 1989, Dennett 1991/1992: no problems found.

## D. MECHANICAL

13. *"Three consequences follow, and they do most of the work in this essay."* Consequences two (self-similarity) and three (persistence = generation) are never used in §4. Self-similarity does not reappear at all.

14. *"it is not Ryle's elusiveness, which C1 keeps as a fact about acts and does not use."* C1 does not mention Ryle or acts. The cross-reference points at nothing.

15. *"the gain is total and the fixed point is unique"* — uniqueness is asserted, not argued; a decision can have several self-consistent outcomes. "Zero or intermittent gain" (P4): "intermittent" undefined.

16. *"the self reads it as well as its channel allows, while a second person has only outputs to go on"* — for heart rate a second person with a cuff beats the self; the read-regime claim should be about *unobservable* zero-gain variables, which is what Vazire's observability axis already says.

17. §5 asserts nothing a judge can assess (*"I will not pretend the fixed point proves this"*), and §6's *"once seen it does not go back ... They have been shown where they were standing"* asserts the conclusion rhetorically after conceding the felt click settles nothing. Style is not graded, but a paragraph whose content is "you now agree" is content, and it is not argument. The AI-provenance paragraph is handled modestly and can stay.

18. Abstract: *"converging on aperiodicity for the same reason is the sharpest case"* is ambiguous about which of the three consequences it is a case of, and conflicts with §2's "not the constant" (finding 10).

## E. WHAT IS GOOD, AND THE SCORE

What must not be touched: the one sentence — *a self is the better instrument on itself where it is the maker outright, and where it is no maker at all; it is the worse instrument exactly where it is one of the makers* — together with the move of importing Grunberg–Modigliani/Simon/Henshel into the self-knowledge literature and recasting the self–other asymmetry as a loop property with an informant prediction that diverges from SOKA. That is a real, unobvious, cross-field idea and the essay's honest core.

Score as a competition entry: **5/10.** The core idea is worth a 7–8; the derivation from it to C3 does not go through (finding 1), the first half is idle (finding 2), and the nearest predecessors are missing (finding 3). The one change that would raise it most: rewrite P3–C3 as an explicit two-map model with gain as the slope of the reaction function and a stability condition, derive separately which condition gives lock-in and which gives self-error, name the extra premise that produces error and defend it as non-motivational — and pay for those words by cutting §§1–3 to a page.
