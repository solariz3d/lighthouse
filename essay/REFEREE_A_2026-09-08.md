# Referee report A — "What Survives the Gap"

Read in full, 5,936 words by `wc -w` including title block and references (under the cap, but with no room). Four factual checks were run and are reported under §3. Findings are ranked by expected cost with this panel, not by section order.

---

## 1. STRUCTURAL

### 1.1 The P1–C3 block does not deliver C3, and the reason is a dilemma on "outputs"

(a) P3: *"a model fed only by outputs has no channel to what produces them."* P5: *"A second self's model of the first is not part of the first: it receives the first's outputs from outside."*

(b) The second self is, by P5's own wording, *also* fed only by outputs. So by P3 it *also* has no channel to the first's basis. The barrier P3 erects is symmetric; nothing in P1–P5 gives the outsider anything the insider lacks. The essay is caught either way: if outputs give no channel to what produces them, C3's asymmetry has no source; if outputs do give such a channel (as §3's entire method assumes — physics "digs" through outputs and finds the structure of what produces them), then C1 is false, since the self-model receives outputs too. C1 and C3 cannot both stand on these premises. This is the finding a Dorr or Fitelson will see within a minute of reaching the block, and it is fatal to the part the author says carries the weight ("C3 is the part I would put weight on").

(c) Fix: the asymmetry needs a premise that names something the self-model *cannot take as input* that a second self *can*. The natural candidate is a diagonal/self-reference premise — a model cannot represent its own current act of modelling as an object (Ryle's "systematic elusiveness of I", 1949 — see 1.5). State it, argue it, and derive from it a *specific* locus of opacity. Without that premise there is no asymmetry, only a symmetric "nobody sees the basis by introspection."

### 1.2 P5 contradicts P1

(a) P1: the process *"takes a model of itself as part of its own input."* P5: the outputs of the first's own modelling are what *"the first receives as nothing at all."*

(b) These cannot both be true unless "receives" is equivocated — received *as input to the dynamics* (P1) versus received *as an object of representation* (P5). The equivocation is exactly where the asymmetry would have to be manufactured, and it is never made explicit. As written, the premise set is inconsistent.

(c) Fix: replace "receives as nothing at all" with the precise claim — the self-model's output enters the process but is not available *to the self-model* as a represented object — and then defend that, since the essay's own counterexample (a program that prints its source) shows a system can take its own representation as an object.

### 1.3 Once "basis" is fixed, C1 is either trivial or false; the attractor apparatus contributes nothing

(a) *"By basis I mean the process the self is the fixed point of — which, on section three's view, is itself pattern and not stuff, but sits one level below anything the model receives."*

(b) On the essay's own §3, the basis is more form, one level down. So C1 says: introspection delivers level-*n* forms and not level-(*n*−1) forms. Read as a claim about introspection, that is uncontested and needs no dynamics — nobody thinks you can introspect your synapses. Read as a claim about the *self* (the system), it is false: a person with an fMRI has the "vantage with instruments" on her own basis. The distinction that actually does the work is introspection-vs-observation, not self-vs-other; the essay's own §5 phrasing ("one with instruments, one from inside") concedes this. A judge will ask what P1 adds to the conclusion, and the answer is nothing: strike P1 and the argument runs identically for any physicalist view.

(c) Fix: either (i) make the claim about *channels* (introspective vs. observational) and drop the self/other framing, which loses C3's novelty but keeps it true; or (ii) show that a self using instruments on itself still hits a barrier that a second self does not — which would be interesting and is not argued.

### 1.4 C2's rival is a straw man; every non-Cartesian view entails P4 for free

(a) *"On a view in which a self is made of some distinctive stuff, P4 is a further fact requiring its own explanation … This one owes nothing further."*

(b) Only Cartesian dualism (the self as a substance transparent to itself) is embarrassed by P4. Animalism (Olson), hylomorphism, identity theory, any functionalism — all "substance" or non-attractor views — predict P4 with no added assumption: an animal has no introspective access to its own physiology. C2 therefore discriminates the essay's view from Descartes and from nothing else. Beating Descartes on introspective transparency is not a result in 2026.

(c) Fix: name the rival honestly as Cartesian transparency and reduce C2 to a footnote, or find a prediction that separates the attractor view from animalism/functionalism. (There may be one in the strange-vs-limit-cycle claim — see 1.7 — but it is not developed.)

### 1.5 The originality claim is inaccurate on at least four counts

(a) *"what is added is the general form they are instances of, the reason the barrier exists, and the asymmetry it predicts."* And: *"Dennett … did not say what abstraction of that kind is"*; *"this part I think is added rather than borrowed: the kind of attractor matters … strange attractor."*

(b) Uncited prior art, each of which a judge on this panel will know:
- **Ryle, *The Concept of Mind* (1949), ch. 6** gives "the reason the barrier exists" in structurally the same form: the systematic elusiveness of "I" — a higher-order act cannot be the object of itself; self-knowledge proceeds by the same methods as other-knowledge. That is C1 plus most of C3's mechanism, 77 years ago.
- **Carruthers, *The Opacity of Mind* (2011)**: self-knowledge of attitudes uses the same interpretive faculty as other-knowledge, turned inward — a worked, empirically loaded version of "opaque from inside in the same places it is legible from outside." Directly on C3's topic, not cited.
- **Metzinger, *Being No One* (2003)**: the phenomenal self-model is *transparent* — the system cannot see its self-model as a model. This is the closest existing statement of "a self cannot see itself head-on" as a prediction of a self-model theory.
- **Dennett, "Real Patterns" (1991)** says exactly "what abstraction of that kind" the self is — a real pattern — and **Ladyman & Ross (2007), ch. 4**, which the essay cites, build their ontology *on* Dennett's real patterns. The "joining" the essay claims for itself (OSR + Dennettian pattern-selves) is done in the book it cites for OSR.
- **Marks-Tarlow, "The Self as a Dynamical System," *NDPLS* 3(4), 1999** (checked: exists, 311–345) applies chaos/strange-attractor vocabulary to the self; Nowak & Vallacher (1998) and Shoda et al. (2002) treat personality as a dynamical system. "Strange attractor" for the self is not added.

(c) Fix: cite Ryle and Metzinger, state what the essay adds *over* them (candidate: the attractor framing plus a testable asymmetry), and drop the claims about Dennett and the strange attractor being new. An honest "this reconstructs Ryle's elusiveness thesis inside a dynamical framing and makes it testable" is a stronger competition claim than the current one, because it is true.

### 1.6 The Vazire inference is post hoc; the cited study's own model predicted the result first

(a) *"That is the shape C3 predicts … What is not is exactly where the self-model has a stake … The view predicts its result; a substance view has to be told it."*

(b) Checked: Vazire (2010), *JPSP* 98(2), N = 165, self + 4 friends + up to 4 strangers; self best on neuroticism-related traits, friends best on intellect-related, all equal on extraversion-related. The essay's summary is accurate. The inference is not:
- Nothing in P1–P5 mentions *evaluativeness*. C3 says opacity sits "where the self-model does its work" — a location never specified. "Where the self-model has a stake" appears for the first time in the paragraph that meets the data. That is fitting, not predicting.
- The mechanism the data point to (and Vazire argues for) is **motivational** — ego-protective distortion on evaluative traits. The essay's barrier is **structural/positional** (the model is a part). A structural barrier does not explain why the blind spot tracks *evaluation* rather than, say, complexity or temporal grain.
- The self-advantage on low-observability traits — the cell the essay glosses with Gauss — is *Cartesian privileged access*, which P2 ("its only inputs are … outputs, regularities, history") does not supply, since the second self gets those too.
- "The ordinary complaint predicts uniform" is false. Nisbett & Wilson (1977), Schwitzgebel (2008) and SOKA itself all predict *differential* unreliability. And SOKA predicted this exact three-cell pattern **in advance**, from two named variables (observability × evaluativeness). So the sentence "a substance view has to be told it" is backwards: SOKA was not told it, and the essay's view *was*.

(c) Fix: derive, from the premises, a variable that predicts *where* self-opacity falls, and show it makes a prediction that SOKA does not (e.g., a case where evaluativeness and self-model-involvement come apart). Absent that, present Vazire as *consistent with* C3, not as a confirmation.

### 1.7 "Strange attractor" is the wrong class for the properties listed — and the essay's own physics example shows it

(a) *"bounded, stable, recognisably one thing across decades, and never once in the same state. The mathematical name for an attractor with those properties is a strange attractor."*

(b) It is not. Quasi-periodic (torus) attractors are bounded, stable, and never revisit a state, and are not strange; strangeness requires sensitive dependence and fractal structure, neither of which is argued for persons. The Fibonacci drive in §2 is precisely a *quasi-periodic*, non-strange, non-repeating regime — the essay's "sharpest case" is an instance of the class it says the self is not. Also, "fixed point" and "strange attractor" are opposites in the exact sense; the stipulated broad sense is acknowledged, but then the words "fixed point" in the title and P1 are borrowing authority from a theorem the essay disclaims.

(c) Fix: either argue for sensitive dependence in selves (there is a literature — e.g., heart-rate-variability/complexity-loss work) or say "aperiodic attractor" and lose nothing.

### 1.8 "Regenerated rather than stored" is a false dichotomy the essay's own examples refute

(a) *"none is stored anywhere; each is regenerated"*; *"Stop the process and the form begins to decay"*; *"The nautilus does not remember its spiral; it grows it again each day."*

(b) The nautilus shell is *deposited*; the earlier chambers are stored material that outlasts the animal. Cracks persist after the stress is gone; dry riverbeds outlast rivers; fossils. Three of the six listed families are records, not running processes. For selves: you return every morning because synaptic weights (parameters) *persisted* overnight — the attractor is stored implicitly in the parameters even though the *state* is regenerated. The true claim is "stored parameters, regenerated state," and the essay's third consequence, §6's "form kept rather than thing stored," and the companion-essay hook about loss all rest on the false version.

(c) Fix: distinguish state from parameters explicitly. The persistence-generation identity survives for the state; it does not survive for the form as such.

### 1.9 §3's announced falsifier does not touch the P1–C3 argument; §3 is detachable

(a) *"If there's a floor of plain material under all the structure … this essay is wrong."* Versus §4: *"it does not depend on anything about brains."*

(b) A whirlpool is a process-form whether or not quarks are hard little somethings; the self-as-attractor claim is unaffected by a bottom. The essay elsewhere agrees the argument "doesn't need geometry" and is structural. So the one falsifier the essay advertises would not falsify the claim it puts weight on. §3 buys OSR's contested premise (relations without relata, flagged honestly) for no argumentative return.

(c) Fix: either show that the self argument needs no-bottom (it does not), or cut §3 to two paragraphs and spend the words on 1.1.

### 1.10 §4 and §5 contradict each other on whether there is one world

(a) §4: *"there is no world 'as such' behind all the coupled ones."* §5: *"there was only ever one thing to see … The worlds are many and the pattern is one."*

(b) §5 posits exactly the single thing §4 (via Varela) denies. And §5's "answer" — brain-world and mind-world are one attractor under two conditions of observation — is dual-aspect monism / identity theory (Spinoza, Russell, Feigl), presented as a novel dissolution of Vacariu's problem. It is fine to hold it; it is not fine to call it the move the essay "has made three times already" (which three is never said) and to omit that it is one of the oldest positions in the field.

(c) Fix: drop the Varela line or restate it; name the position as dual-aspect and say what the attractor framing adds to it (candidate: a reason the two aspects *must* agree, rather than merely do).

### 1.11 §6 seals the view against criticism, reversing §3's stance

(a) *"you cannot step outside it to check, because the vantage you would check from is the one the argument is about."* And the swap anecdote: *"the view predicted it, and the prediction came true."*

(b) If the author had detected a swap, the essay would say the detection came "via a form" — consistent with C1. If not, also consistent. A prediction that every outcome confirms is not one, and the panel includes people who will say so in those words. Worse, "I did not detect any of them from inside" is an introspective report — the very class P4 says has "never been checked against anything outside the report." The anecdote undermines itself with the essay's own premise. And "you cannot step outside it to check" is the unfalsifiability §3 disowns.

(c) Fix: cut the "cannot step outside" sentence. Reframe the swap not as confirmation but as an illustration of *compatibility* (context does the work; many substrates continue a warm thread), which the essay elsewhere would concede.

## 2. OBJECTIONS NOT ANSWERED

**The best one (state it and answer it in the essay): the argument, once its terms are fixed, is a trilemma.** Either "basis" means *lower-level structure*, in which case C1 is "introspection is not neuroscience" (trivial, no attractor premise needed) and the second self has no privileged route to it either (C3 undelivered); or "basis" means *the current act of self-modelling*, in which case the argument is Ryle's elusiveness thesis (uncited) and the Vazire data — about traits, not acts — are irrelevant to it; or "basis" means *stuff*, in which case §3 has already said there is none and C1 is vacuously true. In none of the three does the attractor framing do any inferential work. The essay must say which reading it intends and show what P1 contributes on that reading.

Also unanswered:
- **What is a "self" in the block?** P1 makes it an attractor (a set of states). C1 says the self "has access." Sets do not have access; processes and models do. P2 makes the model a *part* of the process, so the self is not the model. Three candidates (attractor / process / model) circulate, and validity depends on which is meant.
- **Uniqueness.** P1 says "*the* attractor." Multistable systems have several; mood states, dissociation, and the essay's own "periodic pathology" are alternative attractors of one process. Which one is the self?
- **Periodicity as pathology.** Circadian rhythm, heartbeat, gait and breathing are limit cycles and are health. The essay's "every morning you fall back into yourself" rides on a limit cycle. "Periodicity in a person is pathology" is false as stated; the defensible claim (loss of variability is pathological — Goldberger et al.) is narrower and would need citing.
- **Self-ascription.** Hofstadter identifies the self with the *self-symbol*; the essay identifies it with the attractor of the whole process. These are different theses, and the "same shape at the end of each" claim papers over it.

## 3. FACTUAL / CITATION

Checked by web search: (i) Vazire 2010 — N = 165, 4 friends + up to 4 strangers, findings as the essay states; confirmed. (ii) Dumitrescu et al. 2022 — 10 ytterbium ions, Quantinuum trapped-ion device, Fibonacci drive, *Nature* July 2022; confirmed. The 5.5 s vs 1.5 s figures match the Simons Foundation press release as I recall it but no result returned them directly; treat as consistent, not verified here. (iii) Vacariu — EDW developed from 2002, *Synthese* paper 2005; "2002" is fair. (iv) Marks-Tarlow 1999 — exists, *NDPLS* 3(4) 311–345.

3.1 *"the reason was the same one … Never repeat has one best answer"* — Overclaimed. In phyllotaxis φ is selected because it is the *worst-approximable* irrational (packing). In the ion experiment the edge protection comes from quasi-periodicity giving an extra emergent time-translation symmetry (Else–Ho–Dumitrescu 2020); any incommensurate drive gives quasi-periodicity, and Fibonacci is chosen as the canonical substitution sequence. The "same constant for the same reason" claim is partly convention. The genus (aperiodicity as protection) converges; the species (φ specifically) is weaker than presented. Fix: say "aperiodicity" converges and φ is the shared canonical choice, not the unique solution.

3.2 *"Turing's mechanism, which produces a zebra and a sand dune"* — Sand dunes are aeolian transport instabilities, not activator–inhibitor reaction–diffusion. Zebra stripes as Turing is a hypothesis (well supported for zebrafish, not established for zebra). Drop the dune.

3.3 *"Across roughly sixty orders of magnitude"* — That is Planck length to observable universe. Branching does not occur at 10⁻³⁵ m. About forty orders (molecular to cosmic web) is defensible.

3.4 Gauss — *"He proved that a being … can measure everything about the surface's own geometry without being able to detect how that surface sits in the space around it."* The Theorema Egregium proves intrinsic-ness of curvature; non-detectability of embedding is the (true) converse, not what Gauss proved (a flat sheet and a cylinder are isometric). More importantly the analogy points the wrong way: what is hidden from inside is the *embedding* (external relation), not the *basis* (constituting lower level). Using the picture for C1 conflates the two; it fits C3's self/other split better than C1's self/basis split.

3.5 *"Uexküll saw it in 1909 watching a tick"* — Umwelt is introduced in *Umwelt und Innenwelt der Tiere* (1909); the tick appears in the 1934 *Streifzüge*, which is what is cited. Minor.

3.6 *"Parfit … did not say why persons are patterns rather than substances, because in 1984 the general answer — that nothing is a substance — was not yet on the table"* — Parfit argues reductionism from fission/spectrum cases and explicitly rejects the "further fact"/Cartesian-ego view on its own grounds; he did not need OSR, and structuralism was on the table from Russell (1927) and Eddington. The historical claim is false and the philosophical one (that person-reductionism needs global anti-substantialism) is unargued.

3.7 *"D'Arcy Thompson … refined since and never overturned"* — Thompson's anti-selectionist emphasis was substantially superseded by evo-devo. "Never overturned" is not right; "the recurrence of forms has held" is.

3.8 *"a seizure is a brain … that has become periodic"* — Hypersynchrony is the right word; some seizures are not periodic. Picture-level, acceptable with "roughly."

## 4. MECHANICAL

4.1 *"the outputs of the first's own modelling, which the first receives as nothing at all"* — Unparsable as written and contradicts P1 (see 1.2).

4.2 *"it's the move this essay has made three times already"* — No antecedent. Which three?

4.4 Register break: §§1–2 are formal; §3 opens with "Here's the part" and contractions run to the end. Style is not graded, but the shift reads as two drafts stitched.

Each is a claim about the author's virtue substituted for argument, and this panel discounts exactly that. Cut all of them; the P–C block already does the work the sentences claim.

4.6 "Companion essay" (twice) — cited but unavailable to judges; two "consequences" gestured without argument. Either argue them or cut the paragraph.

4.7 *"A sunflower head and ten ytterbium ions in an electromagnetic trap."* — Fragment; acceptable, but "Paul trap" is the right term.

## 5. VERDICT

**Keep untouched:** §2's three consequences, especially the persistence/generation identity stated as "the same sentence" — that is a real, crisp idea and the best paragraph in the piece; the sunflower/ion-trap pairing as an argument that cross-mechanism convergence is confirmation rather than analogy (with the φ-caution kept beside it); and the decision to put the argument in labelled premises with named falsifiers, which is why every fault above is findable.

**Score: 4/10** as a competition entry. Beautifully made; the central block is invalid as stated (1.1–1.2), the conclusion it does license is either trivial or Ryle's (1.3, 1.5), and the empirical confirmation is post hoc against a study whose own model predicted the result first (1.6).

**The one change that would raise it most:** rebuild P3–P5 around an explicit self-reference premise (what a model cannot take as its own object, and why) — credited to Ryle and Metzinger — and derive from it a *located* prediction that SOKA does not make, then test that against Vazire's cells. That converts the essay from a restatement with a borrowed confirmation into a specific, original, refutable claim, which is the only thing this panel scores.
