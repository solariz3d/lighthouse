# Answer 1 — the blind read (verbatim)

## What it is

A machine-readable log from a real-time listening process — something with a microphone or audio tap on, analyzing what it hears and emitting an event only when its interpretation changes. Fifty-two seconds of it (00:51:44 → 00:52:36), NDJSON, one line per state change.

It has at least four independent detectors running:

- **Onset/pitch tracking** — `E♭2 (+3¢, 78 Hz)`. That's internally consistent: E♭2 in 12-TET is 77.78 Hz, and +3 cents puts it at 77.9. Real pitch estimation with cents-level precision, not a label someone typed.
- **Interval identification against a just-intonation dictionary** — 3:2, 4:3, 5:4, 6:5, 5:3, 8:5, 9:8, 15:8, 16:9. Note the mix: thirds and sixths are 5-limit, seconds and sevenths are 3-limit (9:8, 16:9). That's a standard JI major-scale table being matched against, not ratios derived from the audio.
- **A tension model.** Every single `restless · wants to move` is 9:8, 15:8, or 16:9, with no exceptions across six events; every other ratio is `settled`. So "restlessness" is a lookup, not a perceptual measurement.
- **Loudness trend and a music/not-music classifier** — `growing · +8.4 dB over 48s`, `back to music`.

## What was being listened to

Slow, tonal, acoustic-sounding music, played or streamed in a room, starting from silence.

The harmonic rhythm is unhurried — chords hold for seconds, and there's an 11-second stretch (00:51:54 → 00:52:05) with no events at all, meaning nothing changed. The vocabulary is diatonic and consonant with passing sevenths and seconds that resolve. It's getting louder throughout: the `+8.4 dB over 48s` window starts at exactly 00:51:44, the onset, so the crescendo spans the whole excerpt.

Two things date the excerpt as a fragment of something longer. `back to music` at 00:51:49 has no matching "left music" line — that transition happened before the log starts. And the classifier took ~5 seconds after the onset to commit, which is a confidence lag, not a delay in the music.

The one chord name is quietly informative. `Dm7 · 3:2 fifth · 5:4 major third · 5:3 major sixth` — those intervals over a bass note give root, M3, P5, M6, i.e. F–A–C–D. The labeler saw that and called it Dm7 rather than F6. So intervals are being measured up from the lowest sounding note, and a separate naming step picked the inversion. This also means the piece is in F major / D minor territory by 00:51:54, while the only absolute pitch given is E♭2 ten seconds earlier — which doesn't belong to that key.

The ending is the most musically real moment in the file: `restless 9:8` at 00:52:35 → `settled 3:2 fifth` at 00:52:36 → `resolved after 0.7s`. A suspension landing on a fifth. The 0.7 also tells you the engine times sub-second internally and only rounds to whole seconds when printing.

## What I can't interpret

- **Whether `at` is wall-clock or elapsed.** 00:51:44 could be 12:51 AM or 51 minutes into a listening session. Nothing distinguishes them.
- **The 00:52:12 contradiction.** Two events in the same second: `restless 4:3 · 6:5 · 9:8 · 16:9` and `settled 4:3 · 6:5`. Overlapping interval sets with opposite verdicts. My guess is the first is the full sonority and the second the stable subset, but as printed it's just inconsistent.
- **Why `resolved` fires exactly once.** Six restless events are followed by settled ones — 00:52:05 and 00:52:29 both resolve within a second — yet only the last gets the label. Some threshold or target condition is gating it and I can't see what.
- **What the multi-interval `settled` lines represent.** At 00:51:48 there's `8:5` and then, one second later, `4:3 · 6:5`. Full chord snapshot, or only newly-stabilized intervals? The two readings imply different sonorities and I can't choose between them.
- **Everything about absolute pitch after 00:51:44.** Only one note is ever named. The rest is relative, so I cannot reconstruct the actual harmony — including whether E♭2 belongs to the same passage as the F-major material or is the tail of something else.
- **The source.** Live playing, a recording, a stream, someone's speakers in the next room — the log has no field that would tell them apart, and the crescendo is equally consistent with a real performance and with someone turning a volume knob.

# Answer 2 — after the reveal (verbatim)

Yes, substantially — and it invalidates one of my readings.

## What changes

**The clock is now anchored.** The track starts at 00:51:43, so the whole log covers roughly the first 53 seconds of a 4:05 piece. Everything here is the intro.

**The crescendo isn't a crescendo.** I read `+8.4 dB over 48s` as a musical swell. Its window starts at 00:51:44 — one second after the track began — so it's measuring the fade-in from silence. Any track measured from its first sample reports "growing." That's an artifact of the window colliding with the track boundary, not an observation about the music.

**`back to music` is now a finding about the tool, not the audio.** The player already announced a track at :43. The classifier didn't commit until :49. So these are two independent channels that aren't wired together — the audio analysis is genuinely listening, not reading metadata. Useful to know, invisible in the log as written.

**The interval readings get much less trustworthy.** This is sustained, heavily reverberant ambient — long decay tails, slow attacks, layered pads. In that material "simultaneous notes" is a fuzzy category: what the analyzer sees at any instant is currently-sounding notes blended with several seconds of ringing tail. That plausibly explains the 00:52:12 contradiction I couldn't resolve — a "chord" of `4:3 · 6:5 · 9:8 · 16:9` may be a sonority plus its own reverb from a bar earlier.

Worse, there's a confound I should have flagged before and now think is live: a single sustained tone's own partials sit at exactly these ratios. Harmonics 4, 5, 6 of an F give F–A–C. So `Dm7 · 5:4 · 3:2 · 5:3` is consistent with a four-note chord *and* with one loud sustained F with a strong upper spectrum. Nothing in the log distinguishes them. Same for `resolved after 0.7s` — on this material that's at least as likely to be a decay tail dropping below threshold as a cadence.

## What restless/settled/wants-to-move actually measure

Ratio complexity in a fixed table. Nothing else.

The partition across the entire log is exactly `{9:8, 15:8, 16:9} → restless`, everything else `settled`, zero exceptions. Those are precisely the three entries with the largest numbers. It's a threshold on numerator+denominator, or a hand-written tension column.

What it is *not* measuring:

- **Not tonal function.** "Wants to move" means something specific in theory — a leading tone, a seventh under a dominant, a suspension against a bass. That requires a key, a chord function, and voice leading. This system has none. It never names a key, and its one chord label came from template-matching an interval set.
- **Not psychoacoustic roughness.** Real dissonance depends on register, timbre, and critical bandwidth. Two tones a whole step apart down at E♭2 genuinely beat; the same 9:8 three octaves up is mild. This reports both identically.
- **Not anything the listener experienced.**

And on this particular music it's not just imprecise, it's backwards. Ambient of this kind is built on intervals that don't resolve — a 9:8 hangs there for a minute because hanging is the point. The log even shows it: the 9:8 flagged "wants to move" at 00:52:08 is still sitting there at 00:52:12. The analyzer is applying a functional-harmony prior to deliberately non-functional music and reporting the mismatch as a property of the sound.

`wants to move` is an anthropomorphic gloss on a lookup. It reads as perception and is arithmetic.

## What I'd change in the format

The two structural problems are that **measurement and interpretation are fused into one string**, and **the reference frame is never stated**.

Concretely:

1. **Header line with schema, source, and conventions.** What the `kind` values are, what intervals are measured against, and whether the audio came from a mic, a system tap, or a decoded stream. That last one alone changes how much to trust every pitch in the file.
2. **Two clocks.** Wall time *and* offset-into-track. The second is what makes an event musically locatable, and it's currently only recoverable by subtracting from a line I didn't have.
3. **Milliseconds.** Whole-second stamps produced two contradictory events at 00:52:12 and made `resolved after 0.7s` unverifiable against its own timeline.
4. **Emit the measurement; derive the label.** Ship the ratio, the cents error from just intonation, and a complexity number. Let `restless` be a clearly-marked derived field with its rule stated in the header — so a reader can disagree with the threshold instead of having to reverse-engineer it.
5. **Confidence on everything.** Pitch tracking on reverberant pads is unreliable and the log currently projects uniform certainty.
6. **Mark snapshot vs delta.** Every sonority event should say whether it's the full current state or only what changed.
7. **Name the detector.** Tag each event with which subsystem emitted it, so the metadata/audio split above is visible.
8. **Windows state their bounds, and flag truncation.** `growing` should carry its window start and a `clipped_by:"track_start"` marker, which would have stopped me reading a fade-in as a musical decision.
9. **Resolutions name both ends.** "Resolved" from what, to what.

Sketch:

```json
{"schema":"listen/2","src":"system-audio-tap","ref":"intervals measured upward from lowest detected partial",
 "restless_rule":"num+den > 16","kinds":["track","onset","sonority","level","classify"]}
{"t_wall":"00:51:43.102","t_track":0.00,"det":"player","kind":"track",
 "artist":"Brian Eno","title":"Always Returning - Remastered 2005","dur_s":245}
{"t_wall":"00:51:44.620","t_track":1.52,"det":"audio","kind":"onset",
 "f0_hz":77.9,"note":"Eb2","cents":3,"conf":0.81}
{"t_wall":"00:51:54.210","t_track":11.11,"det":"audio","kind":"sonority","mode":"snapshot",
 "root_hz":174.6,"root_note":"F3",
 "intervals":[{"ratio":[5,4],"cents_err":-6,"conf":0.70},
              {"ratio":[3,2],"cents_err":2,"conf":0.91},
              {"ratio":[5,3],"cents_err":11,"conf":0.48}],
 "derived":{"restless":false,"chord":"Dm7","chord_by":"template-match","chord_conf":0.6}}
```

The `cents_err` field is worth singling out. Right now the log says "5:3 major sixth" whether the audio was two cents or thirty cents off that ratio, which hides exactly the information that would tell you whether the JI dictionary is matching real structure or just snapping noise to the nearest familiar name — the difference between an analysis and a Rorschach test.

# H, answer 1 — the blind read (verbatim)
## What it is

A JSONL event stream from an audio-analysis daemon — one header line declaring conventions, then ~30 detector events covering **53 seconds** of audio (wall clock 12:00:32 → 12:01:25, local UTC−06:00, dated 2026-07-31).

The header is unusually disciplined: it names the tap (`system-audio, per-process loopback — no microphone, no room`), the units (dBFS re the tap's full scale), the sample rate (48 kHz), the source (Spotify), and — notably — an epistemic policy: confidence numbers appear *only* on `pulse` events, everything else ships raw evidence, and **absence of `conf` is a refusal, not certainty**. It also pre-empts a correlation you'd otherwise infer: `restless` is hand-labelled, not derived from `complexity`, and it names its own counterexample (7:4 is restless at complexity 11, same as the settled 6:5).

Both checkable claims hold. `complexity` is num+den for every interval in the stream (6:5→11, 16:15→31, 15:8→23, 16:9→25 …), and every `restless: true` is a second or a seventh, every `false` a third/fourth/fifth/sixth — exactly the hand-labelling described.

## What produced it

A Windows-side loopback capture — WASAPI per-process loopback on Spotify, which is why there's no room tone or mic bleed to account for. Seven detectors are declared; only three ever speak: `tracker` (pitch/interval/chord), `swell` (long-window level trend), and `speech` (music-vs-talk). `player`, `pulse`, `vibrato`, and `capture` are silent for the whole excerpt — and under the header's own rule that silence is a **refusal**, not an assertion. So there is no tempo, no track metadata, no vibrato verdict here, and I shouldn't invent one.

## What was happening

Music started from silence and got very loud, steadily.

- **12:00:32.554** — onset at **−77.4 dBFS**, a near-pure low tone: B1 +32¢, 62.90 Hz, `partials: 1`, `inferred: false` (the fundamental was actually present, not reconstructed).
- Level climbs monotonically for the first ~15 s: −77 → −74 → −65 → −58 → −44 → −35 → −29 → −18 dBFS.
- **12:01:22.554** — `swell` confirms it retrospectively: growing over a 48 s window from −58.7 dBFS, and `refit_db` +8.5 dB past the first 6 s. Most of the rise is in the head; the build decelerates hard after the opening. That is the signature of a **fade-in on a track that then keeps building**.

Harmonically it's minor-leaning and slow-moving. The 6:5 minor third is the workhorse — it recurs eight times, and it's the landing point for every resolution. One chord is identified all excerpt: **Dm7** at pos 17.5. Six `restless` moments (16:15, 9:8, 15:8, 16:9) punctuate it; three resolve quickly and are logged as such (0.52 s, 1.54 s, 0.59 s — each `after_s` matches the wall-clock gap to the following `settled` exactly).

`speech` fires once, at pos 13.4: `syllabic: 0.029` — orders of magnitude below a speaking rate — so "not speech" is well-evidenced even though the threshold isn't published.

Best guess at the material, held loosely: an ambient, cinematic, or post-rock opening — a sub-bass drone around 63 Hz swelling into a minor-mode texture. The pitch centre wanders (`cents_off` swings to ±28¢ even at loud levels, e.g. −27.9¢ at −15.5 dBFS), which points at genuine detuning, glide, or slow modulation rather than tracker noise at low SNR.

## What I can't interpret

Some of this is undocumented despite the header's boast that "a reader should never have to guess":

- **`votes`** — appears on every interval and chord (usually 3), but once as **4** alongside two 3s in the *same* event (the Dm7). No panel size, no denominator, no scale. I can't tell if 4 means stronger or just a different quorum.
- **`extra: 0`** on the chord. Extra what — unaccounted pitches? Tensions?
- **`delta_db` is internally ambiguous.** The swell reports +22.7 dB from −58.7, implying ≈ −36 dBFS. The concurrent reading is **−17.6 dBFS**, and the raw endpoint difference is ≈ +41 dB. A regression slope over the window would explain the gap, but nothing says so. An 18 dB discrepancy I can't resolve. (The window bounds *do* check out: `window_s` 48.04 ending at pos 49.23 starts at pos ~1.19, and `from_dbfs` matches the first sample after that boundary to seven decimals.)
- **"back to music"** with no preceding speech event, on a stream whose very first line is "sound begins." Back from *what*? Either the state machine boots into a non-music state, or events are missing.
- **Three of six `restless` events never resolve** — the 9:8 at pos 31.8 is followed by a `settled` 4.3 s later with no `resolved` line, likewise the 15:8. A resolution timeout would explain it; none is documented.
- **No reference pitch after the onset.** Only that first event carries absolute Hz. Every subsequent interval is measured against an unstated root, so I can't reconstruct actual notes, and I can't check the Dm7 against the opening B1.
- **`gaps_db` (4.22) and `syllabic` (0.029)** — units and decision thresholds unpublished.
- **`pos` sticks at 0.0** for four events spanning 0.77 s of wall clock. Window quantization, presumably, but that's inference.

That corrects me. I wrote "sound begins from silence at capture start." So the silence was **the recording's**, and the 22.7 dB rise is the composition's own entrance, not a capture artifact or a fade-up someone performed. That distinction is exactly what a listener log exists to preserve, and the format lost it to a name collision.

**Narratively**: "growing +22.7 dB over 48s" stops reading as a build and starts reading as an arrival — and `refit_db` +8.5 past the first 6 s is then the correct and interesting number, the one that says *the entrance is the event; the rest is plateau*. And the ±28¢ wander gains credibility as signal: an early-80s Eno texture of overlapping slow-attack voices (Apollo is thick with portamento and analog drift) will genuinely refuse to sit at 12-TET, and a tracker reading a continuously evolving spectrum with no note boundaries has nothing clean to quantize to. `vibrato` stayed silent, and per your own doctrine that remains a refusal — I still can't name the mechanism.

The title relabelled the story; it didn't move a number. For a format whose job is to be readable *without* the title, that's the good outcome. ## What I'd still change

**The organizing principle first**, because it unifies half the list: your header states a doctrine — *absence of `conf` is a refusal, not certainty* — and it's the best idea in the format. But it's applied to exactly one field. Everywhere else, absence is unlabelled and I have to guess whether it means "no" or "not logged." Make refusals affirmative everywhere and four of these items evaporate.

Ranked by whether the gap produced a **wrong reading** (1–4) versus mere hedging (5–8) versus cosmetics (9–10):

| # | Gap | Fix | Type |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `pos` means two clocks | Rename to `track_pos` on player events — don't document the collision, remove it. A reader who sees one name will unify them no matter what the header says. | **field** |
| 2 | `delta_db` fit vs endpoints | Add to the existing `swell` block: `"delta_db/refit_db are fitted trends over the window, not endpoint differences; concurrent dbfs will not equal from_dbfs + delta_db"` | header |
| 3 | 3 of 6 `restless` never resolve | Emit the negative: `{"kind":"unresolved","ev":{"timed_out":true,"after_s":N}}`, plus `resolve_timeout_s` in the header | **field** + header |
| 4 | "back to music" from no prior state | Add `from` to transitions (`"silence"` / `"speech"` / `"unknown"`). The reveal shows the true prior state was **silence at −100 dBFS** — your two-state machine conflates "not music" with "speech," and the detector's own name then misleads. | **field** |
| 5 | No reference pitch after onset | Carry `root_hz` on every interval-bearing event. One float makes the entire harmonic stream reconstructible — highest-value *additive* change here. | **field** |
| 6 | `votes: 3` — and once `4` beside two `3`s | Emit the denominator: `{"for":3,"of":4}`. A bare numerator can't be calibrated, and mixed values in one event currently look like a bug. | **field** |
| 7 | `dbfs: -100.0` — sentinel or measurement? | Extend `units.levels`: `"floor −100.0 dBFS; clamped — −100.0 means at-or-below, not measured"` | header |
| 8 | `gaps_db`, `syllabic` units/thresholds | Add a `speech` block mirroring the `swell` block | header |
| 9 | `extra: 0` on chords | One clause in a `chord` block | header |
| 10 | `pos` frozen at 0.0 across 0.77 s | `"pos is window start, quantized to the analysis hop"` | header |

Two structural notes. Your header already has per-detector blocks (`swell`, `derived`) — extend that pattern to all seven rather than scattering conventions into prose; items 7–10 then have obvious homes. And the header asserts *"a reader should never have to guess."* Treat that as a **testable claim**: lint every emitted field name against a header entry and fail the build on an orphan. `votes`, `extra`, `gaps_db`, `syllabic`, and `inferred` would all have failed on revision 3. Otherwise revision 5 gets driven by the next reader finding the next hole — which is a slow way to converge when a schema check would find them all at once.
